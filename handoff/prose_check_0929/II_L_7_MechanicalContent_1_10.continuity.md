# Continuity check: II.L.7/MechanicalContent#1-10 (A Plethora of Paladins)

Judged against the revised style contract (marginalia add no facts; every Record sentence must rest on words in the entry's own data).

## Coverage
- Canon entities: 10. Chapter entries: 10. Names and order match. No missing entries, no extra entries.
- Shelfmarks, Magnitude ("unassayed") and Attestation ("Transcribed") match canon on all 10. No assay decimals. All Threads read "pending the entanglement pass". No placeholder violations there.
- Severity counts: HIGH 3, MEDIUM 10, LOW 8.

## HIGH

H1. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "do not seek to destroy lawful beings but to challenge the rigidity of order"
- Canon: "struggling in behalf of chaos against law"; the original Fantra features "included detection of and bonus damage against lawful creatures".
- The claim is invented and pulls against the canon. Nothing in the description says these paladins refrain from destroying lawful beings.

H2. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "often aligning with entities that embody disorder"
- Canon has no allies, alignments or entities of disorder. This is an invented relationship.

H3. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "inverts the traditional alignment of chaos and law, casting chaos as a force for liberation rather than destruction"
- Canon says only that paladins are "dedicated to struggling in behalf of chaos against law" and may disregard good and evil. "Inverts" and "liberation rather than destruction" are unsupported. The liberation theme belongs to the Oath of Anarchy entry (freedom against tyranny), not to this one.

## MEDIUM

M1. Class field, 8 entries: "Class: Archetype" (Oath of Anarchy) and "Class: Archetype Feature" (7 entries).
- The contract's Class list has no such values. Entries 9 and 10 use "Praxis" for the same canon type, "Archetype Feature", so the chapter is inconsistent with itself.

M2. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "abilities... such as detecting and damaging constructs and elementals—entities often associated with rigid, unyielding structures of existence"
- Canon: Detect Law and Strike the Inflexible are optional features "can be included". The Record says the Oath "grants" them.
- "Structures of existence" is invented. Canon says only "obdurate".

M3. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "draws inspiration from the Fantra class introduced in *Dragon #106*"
- The canon description does state this. Rule 4 says a publication has to be replaced by its in-universe counterpart. This is a frame break at the source level, not an invention.
- "similarly embraced chaos as a thematic and mechanical force" is an added embellishment.

M4. Chaos Versus Law (Record): "reinforcing the archetype's affinity for primal, untamed forces"
- Nothing in the Chaos Versus Law description says primal or untamed.

M5. Dances with Wolves (Record): "a more visceral and instinctual connection to their power"; "not bound by the rigid structures of celestial or fey influence, but rather by the raw, unfiltered energy of the natural world"
- Canon states only the beast-instead-of-celestial/fey/fiend choice for find familiar and find steed. The rest is invented characterisation.
- Also "different nature than those typically associated with divine magic" (LOW-MEDIUM), and the fiend option is dropped from the final sentence.

M6. Aura of Impunity: "unimpeded by obstacles". Quickfooted: "unimpeded by obstacles", "reflects the paladin's agility".
- Canon: "unaffected by ... difficult terrain" (Aura); Dash or Disengage with a bonus attack (Quickfooted).
- Obstacles and agility are not in canon. The Quickfooted sentence is pasted from the Aura entry. Quill and Moth repeat the "freely/unimpeded" claim.

M7. Limitless Power (Record): "ultimate mastery over chaos and their ability to transcend the limitations of the natural world"
- The description names no chaos and no natural world. Quill's "becoming a force of chaos and freedom" repeats it.

M8. Oath Spells (Record): "These spells reflect the oath's themes of chaos, nature, and freedom... whether by manipulating nature, communicating with beasts, or disrupting the order imposed by authority"
- Canon is a bare level/spell table. This adds thematic and functional claims for the spells, and the "manipulating nature" and "disrupting order" gloss is invented.

M9. Marginalia adding out-of-data referents:
- Chaos Versus Law, MOTH: "beings from the Outer Planes". "Outer Planes" is nowhere in canon.
- Chaos Versus Law, AVAR: "rare and provocative choice"; "traditional paladin play". "Play" is also a frame break.
- Oath of Anarchy, MOTH: "a mirror to the Instrument's own principles". A banned shape, with no grounding in canon.
- Dances with Wolves, AVAR: "rejection of the traditional magical hierarchy". A hierarchy is not in canon.

M10. Game-system frame breaks, throughout.
- Chaos Strike: "roll on a d6", "1d6" (Rule 4 bans dice).
- "3rd/7th/15th/18th/20th level" and "paladin's level" in Chaos Strike, Channel Divinity, Aura, Quickfooted and Limitless Power.
- "bonus action", "Dash or Disengage" in Quickfooted.
- "class" in Oath of Anarchy AVAR/QUILL.
- Intro: "class feature", "mechanics".
- The canon text itself is mechanical, but the contract asks for in-universe counterparts such as "the practice" and "the observed regularity".

## LOW

L1. Intro: "delves" is a banned word. "foundation for those who would swear to it" is a flourish.
L2. Oath of Anarchy (Record): "guardians of this natural order". Canon casts them as keepers of chaos and defenders of disorder. Also "chains that separate people from their own desires"; canon says separate people "from each other" and bind them against desires (a small conflation).
L3. Channel Divinity (Record): "harness its chaotic forces". "Harness" is banned, and Primeval Speech is not a chaos feature.
L4. Chaos Versus Law: Instrument line reads "beings, not practices". The contract fixes the wording as "things" for Praxis.
L5. Limitless Power AVAR: text corruption, "the Oath of An, Anarchy".
L6. Marginalia repetition, entries 5-8 (Primeval Speech, Aura, Quickfooted, Limitless Power).
   - AVAR's note is a clone: "a clear example of how the Oath of Anarchy interacts with the natural world". No canon links Aura, Quickfooted or Limitless Power to nature.
   - MOTH's notes also share a template ("fascinating study... interacts with the natural world").
   - The Unnamed Hand's "not merely a tool but a reflection" appears five times, and "Beware" opens every one of its notes. This breaks Rules 5, 6 and 7.
L7. Banned vocabulary in prose and notes: "embodies" (Quill, Chaos Strike), "underscores" (Avar, Dances), "resonate" (Moth, Chaos Versus Law), "compelling" (Avar, Oath of Anarchy; Quill, Channel Divinity), "not merely" (Unnamed Hand, entries 2-8), "what if chaos is not the enemy, but..." (Quill, Chaos Versus Law).
L8. Formatting drift, entries 9-10: no bold names or Hand tags, and "⌁ Threads" is used in entry 10 only. Not a canon issue.

## Vocabulary and unverifiable claims
- "fantra" is used consistently and matches canon. "Fantra class" (Chaos Versus Law) refers to the separate original class in canon.
- Not verifiable from canon, and therefore unsupported: the intro's "philosophy as much as a class feature"; Avar's claim that the Oath is a "radical departure from the structured paladin paths"; Quill's "refreshing take".

## Verdict
Every entry is faithful on hard data: levels, ranges, spell lists, damage numbers, durations and tenets. The main risk is Chaos Versus Law, which invents relationships and reverses its own source's stance on lawful creatures. Beyond that entry, the pattern is embellishment: nature and chaos glosses that the data does not carry, plus boilerplate marginalia. The chapter does not pass the revised contract until H1-H3 and M4-M9 are cut.
