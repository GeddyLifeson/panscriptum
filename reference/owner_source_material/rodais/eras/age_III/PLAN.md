# Age III, the Holy Age (An Aois Naomh, FE 1 – 2,960): volume plan

Architect's plan for the era legendarium of Age III. Read with WRITERS_GUIDE.md, TELLS_FOR_WRITERS.md, the master
Book `legendarium/book/age_III.md` (the spine: every scene it tells must be kept and may be deepened), the master annals
`eras/age_III/annals/age_III_master.json`, `eras/specs_draft/age_III.json`, NAMES.json. Cast: `PLAN_people.json`.
New names: `PLAN_names.json` (every `dt` tested with normalize() and check_agreement()).

## 1. Title, epigraph, premise, question

**Làn an Dà Bhois** — *The Fill of the Two Palms*

> *Cha tèid an fhèith a roinn. Gearr i, agus silidh i fuil.*
> The vein will not be parted. Cut it, and it bleeds.
>
> — Aodh Crom, eldest keeper of the slab, in the gathering-house below Dùn ìseal

**Premise.** Ailean Mòr falls alone at the ford because his kin were quarrelling over what he would leave. Seven
hundred and twelve heirs, some of them thousands of years old, stand up to claim. They cannot agree on anything except
that none of them shall have the vein, and out of that grudging oath comes the Binding: a flame no heir may hold, kept
by people of no one's blood. The kingdom is cut on a knotted cord into shares, and the shares into shares, and the
long-lived heirs feud for five and a half centuries, remembering every insult because they live to. The Hall of the
Flame outlasts them; a blind Keeper fills a stone cup by touch, and the handful becomes law; carriers make roads and a
peace of their own; the shares gather again under a granary-lord the households call rìgh; the long years drain out of
the king's blood generation by generation until, in the last centuries, every life on the island runs the same length.
The one folk-practice splits into eight companies. The volume closes on a winter morning in FE 2,960 with a young
queen in the red hall, the cup on the threshold at Dùn ìseal, and the sea at the island's back uncrossed.

**The question the volume answers.** When the one hand that held everything is gone, what holds a people together?
Its answer, told in scenes: the thing no one is allowed to hold (the flame and the vein), a measure that is the same
for every hand (the cup), roads with a peace on them, and grain shared before the storms; and last, the passing of the
long years, which makes every hand the same.

## 2. Point of view and voice

The high recollection voice of the master Book III, with a frame line where apt (*"At Dùn ìseal the knot-keepers tell
this…"*; the smiths of Muileann chiar; the carriers at the long table; the reapers of Achadh mhòr; the clerks of the
red hall; the scholars of the stag at Muileann chaol). About half the chapters are lived close to one person (POV,
past tense, sight, cold, speech), half told as recollection with scenes inside. The book is told as at the close of
the Holy Age, by the island's own tellers and clerks; there is no Library yet and no later age.

**Principal figures (a relay; the age is 2,960 years long).**
1. **Niall Òg** (grandson of Ailean; ~900 at FE 1, dies FE 1,109): the laughing heir, "owed twice", the spine and
   frequent teller of Books 1–9. He watches the heirs he stood among die one by one.
2. **Eithne nic Ailein** (daughter of Ailean; ~1,900 at FE 1, dies FE 1,122 aged 3,021): the sharp one; her knife cuts
   Ciorstaidh's knot; she swears first at the Peace of the Threshold; the last of the nine.
3. **Sìne nan Snaidhm and her line, the knot-keepers of Dùn ìseal** (FE 1 – 1,320): Sìne ties the cord of the heirs;
   her daughters keep the Hall's memory on cords and notches until letters come; the last of them, Cairistìona nan
   Snaidhm, is among those who write the Keepers' roll down in FE 1,320 (II-0113).
4. **The Keepers of the flame** (a relay): Beathag Bhàn, Fionnlagh Dall, Eilidh nic Ìomhair, Seonaid Chiar, Dùghall mac
   Raghnaill, Seumas Bàn, Catrìona nic Mhuirich.
5. **The line of Ìomhar and of Goraidh** (the rulers): Ìomhar Dearg, Goraidh Mòr, Mòrag nan Rathad, Iain Garbh,
   Beathag Mhòr; the long years fade visibly in this line (each ruler lives less long).
6. **The clerks' line of Tormod**: Tormod Scrìobhaiche, Peigi nic Tormoid, Gilleasbuig Mòr ("a grandson of the clerks'
   line"), the house of Tormod down to Mòr nic Coinnich and Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill.
7. **The askers of the stag**: Sìleas nic Coinnich and Donnchadh mac Iain.
8. **A place as chorus**: Mòr nic Dhùghaill's house on the carriers' road (the jugged buffalo, the road-peace), revisited
   in Books 5, 8, 12.

## 3. Structure

Six Parts, 40 chapters, ~190k words (the per-chapter targets below add to 207k: treat them as ceilings and write about nine-tenths of each). Each chapter is its own file `book/NN_<slug>.md` (NN = the chapter number,
two digits; slug from the chapter title), opening `# <chapter title>`, the date line, an optional epigraph, and `##`
sections inside. The "Book" groupings below are editorial units only (they fix hand-offs and back-matter duties). The
first chapter of each Part puts one italic line under its date line: `*Part One: The Cord*` (and so on). Word targets are per chapter.

### Part One: The Cord (FE 1 – 4)

**Book 1: Of the Ford, the Bier and the Binding (FE 1).**

**Ch 1. Of Ìomhar Dearg and the Bier of Spears.** POV Ìomhar Dearg. FE 1, 2–12 an t-Sultain. Masters: I-0098a. 4,200 w.
Open exactly where Book II of the master closes: the third night, the light gone out of the river, the host of the east
standing in the dark water, the saying going from fire to fire, the lad of the host at an east-bank fire. Cut to Ìomhar
riding east in the grey before dawn, a son of the king ~1,400 years old, who stayed three days in the valley arguing
for river-grazing and rode late, ashamed, with his household. He finds the river dammed by the dead, the meadow
drowned, his father on the west bank by his stone, the host spent in the heather. He falls on them; by evening he holds
the ford. In the following days he takes Dùn dhearg, burns the halls within its double bank, and takes its living under
his own hand (the lad among them; no household will own the blow). He has a bier made of the host's spears and sends his
father up the valley road; he himself stays nine days. Turn: shame becomes possession; the first share is won with the
sword before any is given. End: Ìomhar washing his hands at the ford, as his father did.
New events: Ìomhar comes to the ford the morning after (FE 1, 3 an t-Sultain); the spent host broken at the ford (FE 1);
Dùn dhearg taken and its halls burned (FE 1); the people of Dùn dhearg taken under Ìomhar's hand (FE 1); the bier of
spears sent up the valley (FE 1).

**Ch 2. Of the Runner at the Spring.** POV Sìne nan Snaidhm. FE 1, 6–20 an t-Sultain. Masters: I-0098a (aftermath). 4,000 w.
Frame: the knot-keepers tell it with a cord across their knees. Keep every beat of master §I: Sìne at the spring on the
fourth evening; the herd-boy Donnan, who ran the long way round over the eastern hills because the host held the road
(the ford is only a few miles from the valley: make the heirs' failure to come feel that near); "There are sixty camps
in this valley that call themselves the king's sons"; the steward in the blue cloak asking "Who holds the hill now?";
the keening from the fire of the white-haired daughter Mairsile; the bier coming up, the young face, the wounds all in
front, two grey sons behind. The burial and its quarrel: the tellers of Dùn ìseal and a reciter of the Mason's line
(Seathan Crom of Clann na Ceiste, down from Tobar dhìreach) each point to a different mound; leave it unresolved. On the
ninth day Ìomhar comes up as lord of Dùn dhearg. Runners go to every coast. End: Aodh Crom at Sìne's door: "Not the
households. The heirs."
New events: the tidings at Dùn ìseal (FE 1, 6 an t-Sultain); the king laid in the earth (FE 1); Ìomhar comes up as lord
of Dùn dhearg (FE 1); the heirs sent for from every coast (FE 1).

**Ch 3. Of the Binding of the First Flame.** POV Aodh Crom. FE 1, 20–23 an t-Sultain. Masters: II-0001. 4,400 w.
Aodh Crom is the eldest of the nine keepers of the slab at Tobar dhìreach, ~180 years old from a thin strain of the
long-lived blood, and younger than the youngest of the king's sons. The heirs' first quarrel is over the hill, because it
stands over the vein: Murchadh Mòr by the eldest, the lines of the dead against the living, the western cousin's "a
gallery each"; Eithne and Murchadh nearly draw blood on the first day. Aodh Crom rises: "Cut the vein, and it bleeds"
(the volume's epigraph), asks what the keepers asked Catrìona Mhòr, and more: the vein and the hill in no share, no one
of the blood to hold it. Silence; Eithne's laugh; each would sooner nobody had it than a cousin. On 23 an t-Sultain the
heirs swear the Binding, Brìde's undying flame kept over Crom's stone (the Mason's-line reciters say it was lit from the
mason's fire; keep the two fires apart). Gille-Brìde of Somhairle's line comes up from the high valleys, swears, takes a
coal home, asks for nothing, and no knot is tied for him. End: the flame in a rush-lamp in the keepers' hut, the hill in
no share, the heirs in their tents not yet speaking of anything else.
New events: the quarrel over the hill (FE 1); Aodh Crom asks that the vein be in no share (FE 1); Gille-Brìde carries a
coal of the First Flame to the hills (FE 1).

**Ch 4. Of the Knots.** POV Sìne. FE 1, c. 2–13 an Dàmhair. Masters: II-0001a. 4,200 w.
The Gathering of the Heirs at the door of the gathering-house: each heir says the line of the claim and Sìne ties a
knot. Murchadh Mòr ("Ailean's son"), Eithne with her own knot-keeper Fionn Beag on the step (sent home on the fourth
day), Niall Òg ("I'm owed twice"), the child in arms, Ìomhar's knot and the four lines' muttering. A steward offers Sìne
a cow for a tight knot; she has nothing she wants. Eleven days; 712 knots; the cord laid along the floor from hearth to
door and back, and the heirs in good cloaks counting the knots either side of their own. Turn: the cord becomes the
kingdom's first record, and Sìne sees they look at their neighbours' knots, not their own. End on the floor of knots.
New events: the knotting begins (FE 1); Fionn Beag sent home (FE 1); the bribe refused (FE 1); the cord laid on the
floor (FE 1; list it before the II-0001a marker, same day).

**Book 2: Of the Four Rules, the Knives and the First Blood (FE 1 – 4).**

**Ch 5. Of the Four Rules.** POV Niall Òg. FE 1 an Dàmhair – FE 2 an Dàmhair. Masters: (gap II-0001a → II-0001b). 5,000 w.
The winter and summer of sitting; the grass of the valley that did not grow back for three years. The four rules, each
with a hundred heirs behind it: the eldest, the living, the dead (the king's dead children outnumber the living), the
lot (only the youngest want it). Mairsile, the white-haired daughter who began the keening, dies in the valley in the
cold (FE 2). Niall, laughing, carries words between Eithne and Murchadh. The Red Share: four lines protest; nobody can
see how to take it from Ìomhar. The clause that none shall be called rìgh, sworn with private reservations. The
settlement: all four rules at once: nine great shares for the nine eldest lines with living heirs, *Cuid nam Marbh* out
of all nine, the Red Share let stand. End: the agreement to cut the cord on the night of the handful.
New events: the four rules put (FE 1); Mairsile nic Ailein dies in the valley (FE 2); the four lines protest the Red
Share (FE 2); the clause against the name rìgh (FE 2); the nine eldest lines named (FE 2).

**Ch 6. Of the Night of the Handful.** Recollection, told by Niall Òg in later centuries (frame), with the scene. FE 2,
1 an t-Samhain. Masters: II-0001b. 5,500 w.
Oidhche an Dòrlaich, every hearth burning its measure at once; smoke in the gathering-house; nine knives; each head walks
the cord with a lamp twice. Murchadh cuts first, where he meant to. Eithne kneels long and cuts through a knot:
Ciorstaidh of the salt shore, a cousin of Niall's, hand over her mouth, "I'll take both." Eleven knots cut through, the
first double oaths. Seats: Murchadh at Baile Mòr mhòr (north), Eithne at Cathair mhòr (the lowland plain), Fionnghal at
Ros dhomhain (west), Artair at Baile ghorm (salt shore), Cormac at Caol shean (east); Ùna, Coll, Gormlaith and Tadhg for
the north-west, the Abhainn fhionn, the south-east and the Abhainn ìseal (the tellers name these four in different
orders; say so once). The Red Hill's holder: sworn to Ailean, Ailean dead, so sworn to nobody; three shares claim it
that evening. The Hall's ground in no share. End at dawn: heirs carrying their lengths of cord out of the house.
New events: Ciorstaidh cut through (FE 2); the eleven double-sworn (FE 2); the Red Hill declares itself sworn to nobody
(FE 2); three shares claim the Red Hill (FE 2); the lengths of cord carried home (FE 2).

**Ch 7. Of the Stones and the First Blood.** POV Ruairidh Glas. FE 2 an t-Samhain – FE 4 an t-Ògmhios. Masters:
II-0001c, II-0001d. 5,300 w.
The clachan-crìche cut with share-signs, set up, moved at night. Dùn thais on the line: a stone on each side of the
bank. 17 an Giblean FE 3: rain, cattle bawling, mud over the shoes, Aonghas Bàn with a herding-stick, Ruairidh's spear
already up from fear. "I have killed a thousand years." He never returns to Dùn thais. Aonghas's brother Eachann Ruadh
swears on the body; Aimhreit nan Oighrean begins. Then the shares of shares: countries, provinces, counties, holdings,
households sworn to three people; the mill on the Abhainn dhomhain whose three lords quarrel over the eels. The knot-
keepers count on cords nine great shares, forty-four countries, a hundred and thirty-one provinces, and give up at the
counties (no writing exists yet; see §4). The heirs go home. End: carriers learning to walk by the stones and ignore the
signs; the Hall's ground the one place a feud may not follow a man.
New events: stones on both sides at Dùn thais (FE 3); Eachann Ruadh's oath (FE 3); the Hall's ground declared a place no
feud may follow (FE 3); the three lords of the mill (FE 4); the heirs go home from the valley (FE 4).

### Part Two: The Flame without a Hand (FE 4 – 380)

**Book 3: Of Beathag Bhàn, the Cairns and the Luck-Stream (FE 4 – 68).**

**Ch 8. Of Beathag Bhàn.** POV Beathag Bhàn. FE 4–15. Masters: II-0002, II-0003, II-0004. 5,300 w.
The choosing on 28 an t-Ògmhios FE 4 as in master §V (mud on her shins, barley parting like a hare through it, the lit
rush held like water, "Hold it for Brìde, and answer to Crom", her silence counted in her favour). She is of no heir's
blood; that is the whole reason. Her first seasons: learning the slab and the galleries from the old keepers; the nine
hearths taking the flame by turns (FE 10); Niall Òg comes with a gift and she gives it back. Talla na Lasrach raised of
dry stone and turf, one door to the vein, none to the sea (FE 15). End: the flame carried in at midwinter; at Samhain two
heirs who have not spoken since Dùn thais stand side by side on the Hall's ground.
New events: the flame kept in the keepers' hut (FE 4); first season of the nine hearths (FE 10); Niall Òg's gift
returned (FE 12); the flame carried into the Hall (FE 15).

**Ch 9. Of the Cairns and the Unentered Temple.** Recollection with scenes; POV Mòrag nan Càrn, weather-watcher, then
Beathag. FE 16–44. Masters: II-0005, II-0006, II-0007, II-0008, II-0009. 5,300 w.
The hearths' reverence takes a name (Seann Spioradan nan Dia-thìreach): ancestors, the sìth, the vein, the weather,
no priests. The cairns above Cnoc ghlas; a wrong reading of the cloud costs the mountain a season (FE 29) and the
watchers are chosen more carefully than the Keeper after. The ban on fire in sight of the Doire uaine hills; herders
eating cold. Beathag walks alone to Làrach an Teampaill, finds the altar already turned toward the mountain, and comes out
saying it is neither Brìde's nor Crom's (FE 34). Her death (FE 44), laid facing the vein with her back to the sea though
the dead go west over it. End: the nine hearths choose her sister's son Muireach Liath.
New events: the wrong reading at the cairns (FE 29); the herders' cold winter on the Doire uaine slopes (FE 33); the
keepers' vow never to enter the temple (FE 34); Muireach Liath chosen (FE 44).

**Ch 10. Of Muireach Liath and the Luck-Stream.** POV Muireach, then Niall Òg. FE 45–68. Masters: II-0010, II-0011,
II-0012, II-0013, II-0014. 5,300 w.
The heather basket, one to a household, filled once a season. Allt an Àigh and its moon-quarter; the night path from Caol
shean; Muireach rules the stream belongs to the ancestors and the weather, not the vein: the first visible parting of
the Keepers' rite from the older reverence, and both sides quote it. The sick carried to the warm springs of Muileann
ruadh, the heat coming up without a hand breaking the stone (Dian Cècht). Gille-Brìde dies in the high valleys at 224
(FE 53); his son Ceallach keeps the coal of the First Flame on his hearth. Murchadh Mòr dies at Baile Mòr mhòr close on
three thousand years old (FE 64); Niall watches the north cut among eleven sons with eleven knives, and the feud with
Ìomhar's line flare. End: the north a floor of counties that cannot raise twenty spears.
New events: Gille-Brìde dies (FE 53); Murchadh Mòr dies (FE 64); the north parted among his eleven sons (FE 65); a raid of
Eachann Ruadh on the Red Share's cattle (FE 67).

**Book 4: Of the Silver, the Stag and the Stone Cup (FE 69 – 148).**

**Ch 11. Of the Silver of Muileann chiar.** POV Fearchar Airgeadach. Frame: the smiths of Muileann chiar, waiting for
the metal to run. FE 69–96. Masters: II-0015 – II-0020. 5,300 w.
The seam on the mountain's flank a few miles north of Dùn ìseal (burg 419; not the far-eastern Muileann chiar of the
stag): the first thing out of the ground that is surely not coal. Fearchar, a basket-carrier of the nine hearths, smelts
it with wood and peat: the first fire that answers to nobody in the Hall. Muireach turns a ring in his fingers:
*"Chan eil fuil san airgead…"*. The ring to Goibhniu. The two cousins of the eastern share who claim the county: a ring
to each, and dig on. Rings for salt fish and sealskin at Ros fhionn, one ring a boatload. Ceallach dies at 140 (FE 79),
the last of Somhairle's line in whom the long years showed; his children go down into the valleys. Muireach dies keeping
the flame (FE 89); Gormshuil nic Mhuirich is chosen over three of other lines; Sliochd Mhuirich named (FE 96).
New events: the two cousins' claim to the silver county (FE 75); a ring paid to each (FE 76); Ceallach dies (FE 79);
the three passed-over candidates (FE 89).

**Ch 12. Of Sìleas and the Stag.** POV Sìleas nic Coinnich. FE 97–125. Masters: II-0021 – II-0024. 5,300 w.
A cutter from the far-eastern Muileann chiar (burg 78) who sees the coal break bright under some picks and dull under
others and will not let it be: *"Carson a tha an fhèith a' freagairt…"*. Teaching in Coille Naomh Muileann chiar; the
stag that stands through a lesson; her hearers call themselves the stag's people (do not use the name Camaran; the
Flidais telling only as something later tellers add). Gormshuil shuts the galleries to them (FE 125). Counterpoint:
Ìomhar Dearg is killed by Eachann Ruadh at a ford of the Abhainn dhomhain (FE 121), the vengeance for Aonghas Bàn, one
more child of the king to die as Iain Dubh died; the Red Share passes whole to Dùghall Dearg, the only wise thing that
line did, for the wrong reasons. End: the stag's people walking east from the mountain.
New events: the druid school's first lesson in the wood (FE 103, as II-0021); Ìomhar Dearg killed at a ford (FE 121);
the Red Share passes whole to Dùghall Dearg (FE 121); Eachann Ruadh flees to the Hall's ground (FE 122).

**Ch 13. Of the Smoke and the Stone Cup.** POV Fionnlagh Dall. FE 126–148. Masters: II-0025 – II-0029. 5,300 w.
Gormshuil dies; the blind Fionnlagh chosen (FE 128), led by the boy Calum Beag. The hard winter at Cnoc dhubh: basketful
burning, the smoke lying like a sick dog, five dead (FE 132); the season of argument. The scene of master §VII kept whole:
threshold found by the foot, the stone cup re-cut to two palms, "Give me the coal", "Enough". The Small-Burning Law
(FE 142), the first law to run in every share alike; the lords grumble and burn their handful. First sentence: the
household of Seann Tarr (FE 145) and its petitions. No coal on beacons (FE 148): the headland at Tobar dhearg on
driftwood and seal fat. End on the cup on the threshold.
New events: death of Gormshuil nic Mhuirich (FE 127); Fionnlagh Dall chosen (FE 128); the grey stone quarried for the cup
(FE 138); the lords of the shares swear to the Law at Samhain (FE 142).

**Book 5: Of the Carriers' Roads, the Bridges and the Road-Peace (FE 149 – 380).**

**Ch 14. Of the Carriers and the First Bridges.** POV Ailean Clachair. FE 149–163. Masters: II-0030 – II-0034. 5,300 w.
The Law makes the Hall a giver-out, and what is given out must be carried: the stepped path to Ceann chaol. The flood
on the Abhainn dhomhain takes eleven carriers (FE 154); the Hall mourns the coal too (the carriers' joke). Fionnlagh asks
for a crossing the river cannot take. Ailean Clachair and forty masons of the nine hearths throw the single span above
Ros fhionn (FE 160); the hearth-sign in the keystone. The oath on the stone cup: no span closed in the dark, none paid in
coal. The lodge by the western crossing in the country of Ros dhomhain. End: a carrier refusing an unsigned bridge.
New events: the steps at Ceann chaol finished (FE 151); the names of the drowned (FE 154); Ailean cuts the first
hearth-sign (FE 160); the masons' lodge raised (FE 163).

**Ch 15. Of Mòr nic Dhùghaill's House and the Blind Keeper's Last Road.** POV Mòr nic Dhùghaill, then Calum Beag. Frame:
the carriers at the long table. FE 164–189. Masters: II-0035 – II-0044. 5,300 w.
The waypoint houses; Mòr, widow of Doire shean, and her jugged buffalo; the turf longhouse with two hearths. The road-peace
(FE 175): a feud-chase stopped at her door; heirs who will not speak in the valley sit at one table and do not speak
there either (Niall Òg among them). Settlers keep the old names at Seann Mhorn and Seann Warr (song). Fionnlagh dies
(FE 184); at his own asking he is carried to the old graves near Cnoc dhìreach: twenty miles in eleven days, because every
hearth on the way asks to see him. Tuam Cnoc dhìreach hallowed; the grave-keepers paid a handful each, the only payment
in coal the Law allows. End: Calum Beag laying the stone.
New events: the first feud stopped at an inn door (FE 176); Niall Òg at Mòr's table (FE 177); Mòr nic Dhùghaill dies
(FE 186); the bearers' road (FE 184).

**Ch 16. Of the Unforgotten Wedding.** Recollection with scenes; POV Niall Òg. FE 190–380 (master gap). Masters: none
(II-0044 → II-0045). 5,400 w.
The strife of the heirs in its long middle: men who began feuds live to remember them. An insult at a wedding (FE 214,
a grandson of Fionnghal's line against Artair's) carried for centuries. Heirs killed seldom and bitterly: a death is a
thousand lost years. The old children of the king die one after another (Fionnghal FE 262, Artair FE 298, Cormac FE 331;
Eachann Ruadh in his bed FE 377). Eimhir nic Mhuirich keeps the flame in the middle years. More heirs every generation,
living shorter lives, hating no less. Niall tries a peace at the long table and fails (FE 352). The households go on:
the handful, the tallies, dues to whichever lord comes first. End: "the island was never so well governed as when all
its governors were busy with each other."
New events: the wedding insult (FE 214); Eimhir nic Mhuirich keeps the flame (FE 236); death of Fionnghal (FE 262); death
of Artair (FE 298); death of Cormac (FE 331); Niall Òg's failed peace (FE 352); death of Eachann Ruadh (FE 377).

### Part Three: The Long Strife and the Threshold (FE 381 – 634)

**Book 6: Of the Four Houses and the Reverent Centuries (FE 381 – 542).**

**Ch 17. Of Eilidh nic Ìomhair and the Four Houses.** POV Eilidh nic Ìomhair. FE 381–397. Masters: II-0045 – II-0049. 5,300 w.
The first Keeper outside the house of Muireach, and of the king's blood, which the Binding forbade: her friends' answer
(a drop of the king in every household) and its uselessness. Sliochd Mhuirich refuses a season, then yields on one
condition: the cup never leaves the Hall. The Four Houses sworn (FE 385): Muireach, Ìomhar, Tormod (claiming Tormod
Liath), Raghnall (claiming Raghnall Ceannfhionn). The hall and shrine at Seann Dunn with the always-fullest lamp; Seann
Tarr's boats; Tormod Ruadh steers round Eilean dhubh (FE 397) to see a coast, and brings back rock, birds and nothing
else. End: Eilidh watching the boats come in.
New events: Sliochd Mhuirich's season of refusal (FE 382); the cup sworn never to leave the Hall (FE 382); Tormod Ruadh's
return (FE 397).

**Ch 18. Of the Plunderers and the Thing under the Boat.** POV Anndra Cìobair, one of the two
swimmers of Baile chrom. FE 398–421. Masters: II-0050 – II-0055. 5,300 w.
Households stripped for a third offence take to boats (spùinneadairean). The boat of Baile chrom lost in a flat calm,
something rising under it; the swim home; the wake kept every year; the beacon relit with driftwood as the Law requires.
Seann Bhrenn resettled on its silted quay; the cairn at Cathair naomh begun. The panthers' crossing over the southern
road; Eilidh's ruling, which costs the carriers only time. End: Anndra at the wake, not saying what he saw.
New events: the plunderers' first raid on a strand (FE 403); the wake first kept (FE 406); Anndra Cìobair dies (FE 440).

**Ch 19. Of the Reverent Centuries.** POV Coinneach Mòr mac Tormoid. FE 422–542. Masters: II-0056 – II-0064. 5,300 w.
The granaries of Dùn dhearg and people who owe the house their bread; Baile Mòr dhomhain at the foot of the pass; the
same measure mined and burned, generation after generation, whatever lord's stones a household lives between. Coinneach
keeps the flame longer than anyone and changes nothing; his walkers list the ruins and say who does not own each; the
