# Words of An Aois Choigreach

The age that began with a cup and a loaf held up across a table ended with two peoples who could each say the other's word for most things and still did not always mean the same by it. Gormshuil nic Fhearchair and Samuel Wren made some hundreds of words on each side in their first season, near enough even by the council clerk's count, and the island went on making words for the strangers' things, and for what the strangers did, through all the hundred and fifty years. This appendix gives the words of the age that the rolls and the camps kept, as they were used at its close. Words the island already had are given only where the age changed their meaning.

## The forty colours

On the morning of the basket Gormshuil laid broken coal on the table between herself and Wren and named its colours one by one, as any child of the hills could have done: the green that lies under the black like deep water under weed, *uaine*, and the word for that green and no other, which is not the green of grass nor of the sea; the red at a fresh break that deepens if you breathe on it; the grey-violet of the old cuttings that no dyer has matched; the brown that shows a piece came from near the surface; the blue that runs along a split edge like the light on a mackerel's back; colours like heather in Lùnastal, the dark of a blackberry and rust on a nail left out in the rain; the ember before it goes grey and the ember after; a hair-line like gold; and a sheen like a pigeon's neck, with a word for each of the two colours it shows and a third for the piece that shows both. She gave forty. There were more. She stopped because Wren had stopped writing. He tapped the whole heap with his pen and wrote one word for all of it.

The first leaf of Muireall's fair copy holds the forty words in the left-hand column and, in the right-hand column where Wren's words should stand, nothing but a note at the foot saying what happened. It is the most handled leaf in the cellar. Nine years later Raghnall, guiding the prospectors, named the colours at a fresh break for Hugh Mercer, *gual donn-ruadh* where the red ran into the brown and *gual airgid* for the sheen, and Mercer nodded the way a man nods at a child who has shown him a pebble. Aonghas mac Coinnich's question at Dùn thais, why the humans cannot see what every Dia-thìreach child sees, was never answered in the age.

## Words for the strangers and their things

**an Tìr Thall** · "the land beyond". The strangers named their homeland in their own tongue, but no scribe of the island wrote the word down. From the first season the scribes called it an Tìr Thall, and its people simply the humans.

**an Eaglais** · the humans' faith, from the word their priests used of the whole company of their believers. The son of their god the scribes wrote *Crìosd*, as the clerics said it.

**A' Chompanaidh** · the Company, the trading house that Edmund Harrow and Thomas Pike founded under licence, which held the pits.

**Riaghaltas nan Coigreach** · "the government of the strangers", the Administration.

**An t-Àrd-mhaor** · "the high steward", the Commissioner; his house above Ros dhomhain was **Taigh an Àrd-mhaoir**, the Residency.

**Puingean a' Chalaidh** · "the points of the harbour", the Harbour Articles of the nineteenth year, which fixed the dues and anchorages of Ros dhomhain in A' Chompanaidh's favour. Gormshuil refused them.

**maor** · "steward". Under A' Chompanaidh, a Dia-thìreach set over Dia-thìrich crews, with a staff and a slate roof. The byname Raghnall Maor was given at a mill door by a woman who said, loud enough to carry, "There goes Raghnall Maor to his castle," and the hill kept it for four generations.

**mòine** · peat. The island's old word, which Wren learned over a brazier; beside it Gormshuil wrote what he told her of *the black stone the humans dig in their own country and burn on their hearths*. It was one line among two hundred, and she thought nothing of it.

**seachdain** · the week, which came with A' Chompanaidh's contracts and the prayers of Teachdaireachd na h-Eaglaise. The island had never before divided time by seven.

**Eadrach** · the between-moon. Wren could find no place for it on his wheel and wrote it in the margin with a question after it.

**eun-iarainn** · "the iron bird", A' Chompanaidh's flying machine, which went the length of the working districts in the winter of the hundred and forty-seventh year. By evening of the day it first flew over Muileann chrom every hearth had the word, borrowed from nowhere and understood at once. The old women said a bird that did not beat its wings had no soul in it and could carry none away.

## Words the age made for itself

**fuil-ghuail** · "coal-blood". The plain word the custodians agreed at the shrine of Cill ghlas, fit for the physicians' book, for the change that had come upon some children of the camps, so that they did not grow as others do. The humans rendered it *coal-touched*. The custodians wrote it in their columns against four names by the close of the age, and reported none of them to Taigh an Àrd-mhaoir.

**nighean an rìgh** · "the king's daughter". What the washing-women of Achadh dhomhain called the girl of the spoil-houses who did not grow, meaning Ailean Mòr, whom the gods crowned in the grove; half in jest, and as the seasons went by, half not.

**Aisling-aois** · "Aisling-age". The camps' word for anyone who kept their youth past its season, the kind of word boys use for a thing they are half afraid of and wholly certain will never touch them.

**Tìodhlac a' Choimhdich** · "the Keeper's gift". What the camps called the change, holding that it was the gift of Coimhdeach na Fine woken in the first king's line. The rolls do not use the phrase. The copyists of Muileann chaol found that the line itself stood written, seven names back.

**A' Phlàigh Uaine** · "the green plague", the Green Death of the seventy-third year, named for the greenish pallor it gave its dead.

**Gorta Baile chrom** · the Great Hunger of the north-west, named for the town where it was worst, whose people could see the new beacon from their empty fields.

**Tiormachd Achadh shean** · the Long Drought, named for the town whose wells failed first.

**Scàineadh Cill ghlas** · "the Rending of Cill ghlas", the collapse of the hill under the old cutting in the hundred and thirty-seventh year.

**Buidheann an t-Seabhaig Luaidhe** · "the company of the leaden hawk", the seven of Cuan dhearg who carried the council's letters to Oirthir an t-Seabhaig, each with a hawk cast in lead from the cartridges of Muileann chrom at his throat.

## Names the age gave

The rolls of the age named its places, papers and griefs in Dia-thìris, as the island names everything; the humans' own names for the same things are given here beside them, once.

**Dath a' Ghuail** · "the colour of the coal". What every Dia-thìreach child sees in broken coal and no human ever did; the name the copyists of Muileann chaol gave their fair copy of the age's rolls.

**Linn an Acair** · "the era of the anchor", the Anchor Era. The count of years the custodians kept from the first ship's anchor off Cuan shean; the rolls of the age are dated by it beside the year of An Diosal.

**Coinneachadh nan Long** · "the meeting of the ships". The two boats of Achadh àrsaidh meeting Na Trì Longan off the north-west cape, where the age begins.

**Clàr nan Dà Fhichead Dath** · "the list of the forty colours". Gormshuil nic Fhearchair's first leaf, the forty words for the colours of broken coal, with the humans' column left empty.

**Taigh nan Dìlleachdan** · "the house of the orphans". The house at Cill thais where the orphans of An Casad Tioram were kept at the custodians' own cost.

**Scoil na Teachdaireachd** · "the school of the Mission". The school of Teachdaireachd na h-Eaglaise at Ros dhomhain, where Barabal nic Dhùghaill and Iain Maor learned the humans' letters.

**Cairt Chathair gheal** · "the charter of Cathair gheal". The market charter of the concession town, the only one of the age that names no custodian.

**Crann-tomhais a' Chalaidh** · "the weighing-beam of the harbour", the steelyard at Ros dhomhain. From its day a hewer's work was what the beam said it was.

**Sìol-lann na Companaidh** · "the Company's granary". The granary at Muileann chrom built against a stoppage after Stad-oibre Baile thais, with its rule of a week's ration and no more.

**Cùmhnant an Airgid** · "the covenant of the silver", the Silver Compact. The agreement Ùna nic Choinnich signed with Arthur Lisle in both tongues, which left the silver to the guild; it lay in the guild chest under three locks.

**Comann an Adhlacaidh** · "the fellowship of burial", the burial club. The society of Baile thais and Cnoc chaol that bought ground and a box for the dead of the pits, under whose cover Faire nan Lòchran met.

**Riaghailt Dunstan** · Dunstan's rule, a human's name kept as written. The ruling of the magistrate Oliver Dunstan in the case of Thomas Hale, that customary rights descend only through the line Riaghaltas nan Coigreach owns; under it eleven customary cuttings passed to A' Chompanaidh, and Mairead nic Aoidh stopped entering marriages in the roll.

**Lagh na h-Aon Loidhne** · "the law of one line". The humans' rule that land passes down a single line of heirs, against the island's way of holding by the whole kin.

**Slighe a' Ghuail** · "the way of the coal", the coal line. A' Chompanaidh's iron road from the pits at Muileann chrom by Cathair dhearg to the deep-water quay at Ros dhomhain; its engines burned the island's own coal, and the carters of Doire chaol lost the long haul.

**Na Trì Teaghlaichean** · "the three households". The three copying families of Muileann chaol who kept the cellar's rolls, winter on winter.

**Seilear Muileann chaol** · "the cellar at Muileann chaol". Where the copies of the custodians' rolls were kept from the reach of Taigh an Àrd-mhaoir, and where Cailean mac Eachainn found the line.

**Sloinneadh Aisling** · "the line of Aisling". The seven names of Aisling's father's line found written in the roll of Àth ìseal, as the custodians write a marriage.

**An Acair Uaine** · "the green acre". The plague-field beyond the spoil-heaps of Achadh dhomhain, named by no one on purpose.

**An Dithis a Nigh Còmhla** · "the two who washed together". The camps' name for Anne Colby and Beitris Bhàn, who washed the dead of A' Phlàigh Uaine side by side.

**A' Chiad Athchuinge** · "the first petition". The first written grievance the custodians laid before Riaghaltas nan Coigreach acting together and not settlement by settlement, drafted over three days at Ceann àrsaidh; it was not answered, and An Dàrna Athchuinge followed it.

**Leabhar Baile thais** · "the book of Baile thais", the book of grievances. The book of complaints set down at Baile thais by many hands over some years: wages, deaths, the granary, the stream at Doire mhòr. It went to Muileann chaol in the four chests at the close of the age.

**Coinneachadh an Fhearainn** and **Coinneachadh a' Mhonaidh** · "the meeting of the land" and "the meeting of the moor". The standing stone on the moor road where the custodians of Muileann dhomhain lent the dispossessed their poorest common land, and the place above Cnoc naomh where the crew-leaders of the northern pits fixed the week of their rising and left a cairn of spoil-stones.

**Cnoc an Rabhaidh** · "the hill of the warning". The hill above Doire mhòr where the beacon of Là Stad nan Sloc was lit.

**an Spiorad Naomh** and **am Bìoball** · "the Holy Spirit" and "the Bible". Words of An Eaglais the scribes took as they heard them; the priests of Manannan found the wild goose of an Spiorad Naomh in their own harbours.

**An Dealachadh** · "the parting", the Severance. The closing of An t-Aiseag at the end of the war, when the last ship went north and the humans' way over the sea was shut; the rolls also call it a' Scaradh.

## Sayings

*Cha tàinig long.* "No ship came." The line a keeper wrote in the daybook on a night with no ship to note, first at Baile chrom and then along the whole coast. By the last winter of the age it was habit more than grief; the sentence had outlived the asking it once answered.

*Loisc iad an leabhar, ach cha do loisc iad an cnoc.* "They burned the book, but they did not burn the hill." The verse the camp folk of Muileann chrom made while the steelyard ledgers were still burning green, sung by the market women of Ros dhomhain within the week without knowing where it came from.

*Further than a man can walk in one day.* Abel Stone's words for the black seam, the only words of the prospectors' walk the humans kept.

*Put what happened.* Gormshuil's answer to Muireall, asking what to write in the empty column beside the forty colours. The copyists of the cellar took it for their rule.

*I never told you a man lived four thousand years. I told you whose son he was.* Said by an old woman of Clann na Ceiste at Tobar dhìreach, of a line kept by people who had stopped expecting anyone would come asking for it; carried to Muileann chrom by a copyist.

*A debt kept on paper can always be raised again by whoever next holds the paper.* Said by a woman of Ceann àrsaidh whom the telling would not name, as the last ledger went on the fire.

*Write down that they were gentle about it.* Said by an old man lifted from his own doorstone at Doire mhòr under Àithne a' Ghlacaidh.

*We keep the sky.* Hannah Webb's answer when Teachdaireachd na h-Eaglaise asked what she kept in the fields at night.

*One town may be a trial, but two would be a rule.* The custodian of Inis thais, declining the joint council of Baile dhìreach.

*An empty seat answers no letters and signs no papers.* Said in the council, in the hearing of no clerk of Taigh an Àrd-mhaoir, in the years it chose no head.
