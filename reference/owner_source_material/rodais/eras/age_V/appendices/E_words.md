# Words of the Age of Strangers

The age that began with a cup and a loaf held up across a table ended with two peoples who could each say the other's word for most things and still did not always mean the same by it. Gormshuil nic Fhearchair and Samuel Wren made some hundreds of words on each side in their first season, near enough even by the council clerk's count, and the island went on making words for the strangers' things, and for what the strangers did, through all the hundred and fifty years. This appendix gives the words of the age that the rolls and the camps kept, as they were used at its close. Words the island already had are given only where the age changed their meaning.

## The forty colours

On the morning of the basket Gormshuil laid broken coal on the table between herself and Wren and named its colours one by one, as any child of the hills could have done: the green that lies under the black like deep water under weed, *uaine*, and the word for that green and no other, which is not the green of grass nor of the sea; the red at a fresh break that deepens if you breathe on it; the grey-violet of the old cuttings that no dyer has matched; the brown that shows a piece came from near the surface; the blue that runs along a split edge like the light on a mackerel's back; colours like heather in Lùnastal, the dark of a blackberry and rust on a nail left out in the rain; the ember before it goes grey and the ember after; a hair-line like gold; and a sheen like a pigeon's neck, with a word for each of the two colours it shows and a third for the piece that shows both. She gave forty. There were more. She stopped because Wren had stopped writing. He tapped the whole heap with his pen and wrote one word for all of it.

The first leaf of Muireall's fair copy holds the forty words in the left-hand column and, in the right-hand column where Wren's words should stand, nothing but a note at the foot saying what happened. It is the most handled leaf in the cellar. Nine years later Raghnall, guiding the prospectors, named the colours at a fresh break for Hugh Mercer, *gual donn-ruadh* where the red ran into the brown and *gual airgid* for the sheen, and Mercer nodded the way a man nods at a child who has shown him a pebble. Aonghas mac Coinnich's question at Dùn thais, why the humans cannot see what every Dia-thìreach child sees, was never answered in the age.

## Words for the strangers and their things

**an Tìr Thall** · "the land beyond". The strangers named their homeland in their own tongue, but no scribe of the island wrote the word down. From the first season the scribes called it an Tìr Thall, and its people simply the humans.

**an Eaglais** · the humans' faith, from the word their priests used of the whole company of their believers. The son of their god the scribes wrote *Crìosd*, as the clerics said it.

**A' Chompanaidh** · the Company, the trading house that Edmund Harrow and Thomas Pike founded under licence, which held the pits.

**Riaghaltas nan Coigreach** · "the government of the strangers", the Administration.

**An t-Àrd-mhaor** · "the high steward", the Commissioner; his house above Ros dhomhain was **Taigh an Àrd-mhaoir**, the Residency.

**Puingean a' Chalaidh** · "the points of the harbour", the Harbour Articles of the nineteenth year, which fixed the dues and anchorages of Ros dhomhain in the Company's favour. Gormshuil refused them.

**maor** · "steward". Under the Company, a Dia-thìreach set over Dia-thìrich crews, with a staff and a slate roof. The byname Raghnall Maor was given at a mill door by a woman who said, loud enough to carry, "There goes Raghnall Maor to his castle," and the hill kept it for four generations.

**mòine** · peat. The island's old word, which Wren learned over a brazier; beside it Gormshuil wrote what he told her of *the black stone the humans dig in their own country and burn on their hearths*. It was one line among two hundred, and she thought nothing of it.

**seachdain** · the week, which came with the Company's contracts and the Mission's prayers. The island had never before divided time by seven.

**Eadrach** · the between-moon. Wren could find no place for it on his wheel and wrote it in the margin with a question after it.

**eun-iarainn** · "the iron bird", the Company's flying machine, which went the length of the working districts in the winter of the hundred and forty-seventh year. By evening of the day it first flew over Muileann chrom every hearth had the word, borrowed from nowhere and understood at once. The old women said a bird that did not beat its wings had no soul in it and could carry none away.

## Words the age made for itself

**fuil-ghuail** · "coal-blood". The plain word the custodians agreed at the shrine of Cill ghlas, fit for the physicians' book, for the change that had come upon some children of the camps, so that they did not grow as others do. The humans rendered it *coal-touched*. The custodians wrote it in their columns against four names by the close of the age, and reported none of them to the Residency.

**nighean an rìgh** · "the king's daughter". What the washing-women of Achadh dhomhain called the girl of the spoil-houses who did not grow, meaning Ailean Mòr, whom the gods crowned in the grove; half in jest, and as the seasons went by, half not.

**Aisling-aois** · "Aisling-age". The camps' word for anyone who kept their youth past its season, the kind of word boys use for a thing they are half afraid of and wholly certain will never touch them.

**Tìodhlac a' Choimhdich** · "the Keeper's gift". What the camps called the change, holding that it was the gift of the kin's Keeper woken in the first king's line. The rolls do not use the phrase. The copyists of Muileann chaol found that the line itself stood written, seven names back.

**A' Phlàigh Uaine** · "the green plague", the Green Death of the seventy-third year, named for the greenish pallor it gave its dead.

**Gorta Baile chrom** · the Great Hunger of the north-west, named for the town where it was worst, whose people could see the new beacon from their empty fields.

**Tiormachd Achadh shean** · the Long Drought, named for the town whose wells failed first.

**Scàineadh Cill ghlas** · "the Rending of Cill ghlas", the collapse of the hill under the old cutting in the hundred and thirty-seventh year.

**Buidheann an t-Seabhaig Luaidhe** · "the company of the leaden hawk", the seven of Cuan dhearg who carried the council's letters to the Hawk coast, each with a hawk cast in lead from the cartridges of Muileann chrom at his throat.

## Sayings

*Cha tàinig long.* "No ship came." The line a keeper wrote in the daybook on a night with no ship to note, first at Baile chrom and then along the whole coast. By the last winter of the age it was habit more than grief; the sentence had outlived the asking it once answered.

*Loisc iad an leabhar, ach cha do loisc iad an cnoc.* "They burned the book, but they did not burn the hill." The verse the camp folk of Muileann chrom made while the steelyard ledgers were still burning green, sung by the market women of Ros dhomhain within the week without knowing where it came from.

*Further than a man can walk in one day.* Abel Stone's words for the black seam, the only words of the prospectors' walk the humans kept.

*Put what happened.* Gormshuil's answer to Muireall, asking what to write in the empty column beside the forty colours. The copyists of the cellar took it for their rule.

*I never told you a man lived four thousand years. I told you whose son he was.* Said by an old woman of Clann na Ceiste at Tobar dhìreach, of a line kept by people who had stopped expecting anyone would come asking for it; carried to Muileann chrom by a copyist.

*A debt kept on paper can always be raised again by whoever next holds the paper.* Said by a woman of Ceann àrsaidh whom the telling would not name, as the last ledger went on the fire.

*Write down that they were gentle about it.* Said by an old man lifted from his own doorstone at Doire mhòr under the edict of seizure.

*We keep the sky.* Hannah Webb's answer when the Mission asked what she kept in the fields at night.

*One town may be a trial, but two would be a rule.* The custodian of Inis thais, declining the joint council of Baile dhìreach.

*An empty seat answers no letters and signs no papers.* Said in the council, in the hearing of no clerk of the Residency, in the years it chose no head.
