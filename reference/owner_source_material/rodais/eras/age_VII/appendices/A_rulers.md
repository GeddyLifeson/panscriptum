# Appendix A — The Rulers, the Council and the Offices of the Age

Rìoghachd Dia-thìr kept in An Aois Dhubhain the shape it had given itself in Linn an Dealachaidh: a crowned ruler at Cathair dhearg, a council of twelve that governed in the ruler's name, and around them the offices that the vein had made and that outlived it, Seanchaidh na Rìoghachd, the keeper of the great library, Àrd-choimheadaiche, Comhairle nam Mèinnean and its master. To these the age added one, Ceannard nan Rèisimeidean, and one office it did not make but could not ignore, the speaker of Tional nan Tuathach at Caol mhòr, which sat for the north and had no standing in the kingdom's law. This appendix gives each office as the rolls, the open chronicle and Tional nan Tuathach's minute-books give it at the close of the twenty-seventh year, with the years and ages of those who held it. Years are counted from the first working dubhan, as the council ordered in {{year:VI-0049a}}; years before it are given in the count of An Dealachadh. Ages are given at the taking of an office and at its end, or at the close of the present year for those still in it.

## The crown

The crown passed once in the age, in its third year, and without dispute. The ruler of Rìoghachd Dia-thìr is crowned by Macha's rite on the temple steps at Cathair dhearg, and the two horses of the rite, Liath Macha and Dubh Sainglenn, are led out before the new ruler as they were before the first kings. The crown carries the sealing of the council's acts, the naming of its members when a seat falls empty, and the coinage. It does not carry the command of the regiments, which the council gave in the twentieth year to a soldier and seated on its own benches three years later.

**Mairead**, ruler from {{date:V-0199}} to {{date:VI-0006}}. She was born in the twentieth year of Linn an Dealachaidh and was thirty-eight when she was crowned, over a kingdom whose founding wealth was plainly failing. She was fifty-three when her reign ended, in the third year of the new count, while Dubhan's first trials still failed more than they succeeded. Eachdraidh na Rìoghachd of her last years gives the lamp-houses of the capital letting their lamps go dark and Comhairle nam Mèinnean's two surveyors sent north, and her name on neither. The record does not give her death.

**Cathal**, ruler from {{date:VI-0006}} and in the present year. He was born in the forty-fifth year of Linn an Dealachaidh and was twenty-eight when he was crowned at Lùnastal, the grey mare pulling at her rope at the temple steps, over a kingdom that did not yet know how near its remedy lay. The mint cut new dies for his coinage that month, the first not cut by a pupil of Fearchar Bonn's workshop, with the coal ink still pressed into the seal; in the fifteenth year his council pegged the coin to the making of dubhan, proclaimed the new count of the years and had the plain seal cut. The war, the sealing of its record, the seating of the regiments' commander and Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt were all done in his name. He is fifty-two at the close of the present year. An Clàr Foscailte gives no word of his own on any of them.

## The council of twelve

The council sits at Cathair dhearg, twelve members named by the crown for life or until they lay the seat down, most of them from the great houses of the capital's country and the southern ports. It governed the kingdom through the whole age: it heard Comhairle nam Mèinnean's report on the northern fuel in the second year and filed it without minute; it proclaimed Linn an Dubhain; it received Ruth Calder's letter and Tional nan Tuathach's petitions and filed them; it called up the regiments by their old names in {{year:VI-0075}}; it held over Tagradh na h-Èirig until it died; and at its third sitting on the petition it granted Dubhan's Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt. An Clàr Foscailte names few of its members. Two are named in it by their acts.

**Anna nic Phàil**, councillor for Cathair dhomhain in the war years and after. She was born in the fortieth year of Linn an Dealachaidh. She gave the speaker of Tional nan Tuathach the smallest incline of the head across the council table when Athchuinge Dheireannach an Tionail was carried in, on the day of an Nollaig in the twentieth year, and in {{year:VI-0137}}, at fifty-five, she brought Tagradh na h-Èirig on Athchuinge Dheireannach an Tionail and spoke for it once. It was held over morning after morning for eight days and let die without a vote. In the present year she asked at the council, while Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt was heard, how the vein was to be kept out of the trade the charter opened. She is fifty-seven at its close.

**Somhairle mac Lachlainn**, seated on the council on {{date:VI-0124}}, the first soldier to sit there since the captains of Cogadh nam Beann. His offices are given below.

The master of An t-Ionmhas sits with the council without a seat on it and answers to it for the roll; An Clàr Foscailte does not name him in the age. It was he who asked, when Tagradh na h-Èirig was laid, what figure the motion came to, and his roll for the present year stands at 9,568 purses.

## The royal chroniclers

Seanchaidh na Rìoghachd keeps the kingdom's year-book at Leabharlann Muileann chaol, the day's page written in the chronicler's own hand, and since Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh's time adds at the foot of each year a note of what the record could not learn. The office is given by the council and held for life or until it is laid down. Two held it in the age.

**Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh**, royal chronicler from {{date:V-0220}} to {{date:VI-0074}}. She was born in the twenty-first year of Linn an Dealachaidh, took the office at forty-four when Tormod Pinn died at his desk, and was the first to write the foot-note of the year. It was she who first noted, in the first year of the age, that a fuel made without the vein was reported from the north and that she had not seen it; she re-headed fourteen years of Eachdraidh na Rìoghachd by lamplight when the new count was proclaimed, and wrote that she had never before had to move a year she was living in. She laid down the office at sixty-nine and went to live at Àth dhìreach, where she watched An dàrna rèisimeid's camp pitched below her garden wall. She died there in {{year:VI-0144}}, at seventy-five.

**Sìleas nic Rath**, royal chronicler from {{date:VI-0074}} and in the present year. She was born in the forty-ninth year of Linn an Dealachaidh and came up the library road at thirty-two in the eleventh year as the chronicler's clerk, paid from the keeper's own fund for binding; she kept the slip-box of the fuel's spellings and signed for Comann nan Gualadairean' goods when the guild dissolved. She took the office at forty-one, eight months before the war. Hers is the record of the war that the library sealed in {{year:VI-0128}}; she carried its four boxes to the sealed room herself. Of it there stays open only her list of places, the regiments present and the counts she was allowed. She is forty-eight at the close of the present year, and wrote at its foot that the kingdom's record is whole in every part but two.

## The keeper of the library

**Raghnall mac Mhuirich**, keeper of Leabharlann Muileann chaol from {{date:V-0235}} and in the present year. He was born in the twenty-fourth year of Linn an Dealachaidh and became keeper at forty-six, after thirty years of Mòrag nic Coinnich's keepership had made him her deputy. He counted the shires' chests into the new wing in the first year of the age, a thousand and six with Seann Tarr's entered as sent to the fire; he found the money for the chronicler's clerk from his own fund; he answered Ellen Marsh's request that Sceulachdan nan Tuathach be kept beside the record that it awaited the council's view on the standing of spoken witness, and laid it on the table. He built the sealed room and turned its lock, and wears its key on a cord under his shirt. When An dàrna rèisimeid set a guard at the library in the twenty-sixth year he wrote to the council that the library had not asked for one. He is seventy-three at the close of the present year.

## The high custodian of the Rite

**Alasdair mac Ìomhair**, Àrd-choimheadaiche, high custodian of the Rite, at the house of Brìde at the southern Seann Skell through the whole of the age. He was born in the twelfth year of Linn an Dealachaidh. His letter of the fourth year, Litir an Àrd-choimheadaiche, told every house of Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail that the new fuel was not forbidden, since it was not coal, and not blessed, since it was not coal. He blessed the empty lantern after the last coal flame of the coast went out on Brìde's altar in the sixth year; he rode the first carriage without a horse in the ninth; he protested the plain seal in the fifteenth, and the council answered that the kingdom had never said it held the vein sacred. In the present year, at eighty-five, he drew by lot among Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail the four baskets of altar-coal cut by Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail's twelve hewers at An Sloc Mòr.

## The Mine Board

Comhairle nam Mèinnean at Cathair mhòr, in the old stone house of Mac Ùisdein, holds the kingdom's right in the vein, takes the fifth, and in this age became the kingdom's agent in the west. Its master in the age was **Fearghas mac Aonghais**, born in the twenty-fifth year of Linn an Dealachaidh. Under him Comhairle nam Mèinnean bought An Sloc Mòr's last private share in the thirteenth year, called for the northern returns in the eighteenth, and declared the western seams the kingdom's under Breith Leòid in {{year:VI-0064}}; it sent its enforcers west, capped the western seams by its order after the war, and in the twenty-sixth year closed An Sloc Mòr, one face left in Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail's keeping. He is seventy-two at the close of the present year.

Of Comhairle nam Mèinnean's surveyors the record names two. **Iain Ruadh mac Nèill**, born in the fifteenth year of Linn an Dealachaidh, the senior surveyor, was sent north in the second year with **Seumas mac Eachainn**, born in the forty-eighth, to inquire into the northern fuel; their report was bound in Comhairle nam Mèinnean's grey board and filed without minute. Seumas mac Eachainn became in time Comhairle nam Mèinnean's northern surveyor, and his report of the eighteenth year left out the carrying.

## The commander of the regiments

**Somhairle mac Lachlainn**, Ceannard nan Rèisimeidean, commander of the regiments from {{date:VI-0076}} and in the present year. He was born in the forty-sixth year of Linn an Dealachaidh and rose among Comhairle nam Mèinnean's enforcers. He was given the western command at Cathair mhòr at forty-two and chose forty men who had served under him before; he built the enforcers' depot at the western Seann Skell that the priests of Manannan would not bless, and wrote three drafts of his report on it. He was given the command of the new-formed regiments at forty-four, when the rolls of Buidhnean an Dealachaidh were brought out of the council's chests to name them, and was seated on the council at forty-seven. He read the sixty-one names at the unveiling of Clach nan Rèisimeidean. When he was asked at the chamber door whether the sum for amends was too great he answered that he had never been asked whether the towns could bear the cost of raising the rolls he had read. He is fifty-one at the close of the present year.

## The speakers of the Moot

Tional nan Tuathach, sits at Caol mhòr and keeps its minute-books unbroken from Walter Hale's first meeting. It has no standing in the kingdom's law and has never asked for any. Its speaker is chosen by the delegates of the northern towns and keeps the key of Tional nan Tuathach's press on the speaker's ring. Three held the chair in the age.

**Daniel Frayne**, speaker from {{date:V-0189}} to his death on {{date:VI-0020}}. He was born in the fifth year of Linn an Dealachaidh, the first speaker born after An Dealachadh, a pupil of Agnes Pike's school at Doire ghlas and master of a copying school at Muileann leathan. He took the chair at fifty and died in it at Muileann leathan at seventy-three, and was buried in its churchyard with a length of binding thread laid in his grave.

**Martha Greaves**, speaker from {{date:VI-0020}} to {{date:VI-0139}}. She was born in the nineteenth year of Linn an Dealachaidh, a mill manager of Doire ghlas, and took the chair at fifty-nine. Under her Tional nan Tuathach had its minute-books rebound, voted to take up the new fuel, stood apart from the quarrel of Comhairle nam Mèinnean and the western towns, filled its granaries beyond the wont, and fed the families of the west. She carried Athchuinge Dheireannach an Tionail to the council in person on the day of an Nollaig in the twentieth year. She laid the chair down at seventy-six, in the year Tagradh na h-Èirig was let die, and went home to Doire ghlas.

**Peter Hale**, speaker from {{date:VI-0139}} and in the present year, chosen by acclamation without a show of hands. He was born in the fortieth year of Linn an Dealachaidh, a great-grandson of Tional nan Tuathach's first speaker, and was fifty-five when he took the chair. He had carried the rebound minute-books from Muileann leathan and Athchuinge Dheireannach an Tionail in its leather folder, and told Tional nan Tuathach that a filed letter was better than a refused one, since a refusal closes a door and a filing leaves it shut. He is fifty-seven at the close of the present year.
