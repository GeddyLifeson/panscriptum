# Appendix C — The Dubhan Trade, the Guilds and the Dues of the Kingdom

The coal made the kingdom and the kingdom made its trade in the coal's image: a vein held by a few houses under Comhairle nam Mèinnean's fifth, a guild of hewers sworn on an oath-stone, carters and a coal line to carry it, lamp-houses to burn it in the capital's lanes, and a coinage whose seal was inked with it. In twenty-seven years every one of those things changed hands. This appendix gives Malairt an Dubhain, as it stood at the close of the present year: the fuel and its making, the works and depots, the guilds that rose and the guilds that went, the carrying, the peg of the coin, the dues the kingdom takes, and Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt. It is drawn from the Board's returns and An t-Ionmhas's roll as the library holds them, from Cùirt na Fèille's books at Muileann òg, and from Cuimhne-coinneimh an Tionail.

## The fuel

Dubhan is made from what the mines throw away. The first working fuel was made on {{date:VI-0001}} by the man whose name it now carries, a Tuathach of the north, rough but good to burn; it was no trade then, but one man's answer to a need that those in power had not yet faced. What came south in the first years was a black cake, sold by the basket at the markets of Caol mhòr and Cnoc ghorm beside the peat, which burned without the coloured edge of coal. The Board's surveyors, sent north in the second year, burned a cake beside a thumb of An Sloc Mòr coal in an inn grate at Caol mhòr and reported what they saw, and the council filed the report without minute.

The trade's fuel is finished in retorts: the spoil is heated in closed furnaces and the fuel drawn off and barrelled. It is burned in lamps with a burner of wick-rings and a warming coil, in the engines of boats, lorries, carriages and the line, under pans and boilers, and in the forges. By the present year every lamp, engine, ship and lorry on the island runs on it. The word for the fuel was the man's name before it was the thing's; the library's slip-box found it spelled four ways in the council's letters before it settled.

## The works

**Obair-dhubhain Seann Skell**, the works of Comann nan Sciobairean, the company of shipmasters, on the salt-sheds of the southern Seann Skell, founded {{date:VI-0008}}, the first on the island outside the north. On what terms the shipmasters had the making of it is not told. Its chimney has been the tallest thing on the coast since its first year, when it sold its making to the south-coast boats and the lamp-houses of Cathair dhearg. Its making-shed is kept under a padlock the size of a man's fist; its retort furnaces carry the baffle Peigi nic Ascaill sent round unsigned after the retort burst of the fourth year. Its hulls wear the company's black ring on their bows.

**The northern works**, the Tuathaich makings of the first years, which supplied the cake of the markets and the fuel carted to Taigh-solais Muileann bheag.

**The works of the east and north** of the present year, named in the Board's returns among the works where dubhan is made; the Board gives their making and not their owners.

## The depots and pumps

Dubhan is sold at depots, each with its pumps, and from the twenty-first year a clerk of An t-Ionmhas sits beside every pump. The first depots were the harbour tanks of the south coast and the lamp-houses' stores at Cathair dhearg. Comann nan Gualadairean' hall at Achadh dhomhain became a depot in {{year:VI-0040}}, the guild's oath cut in the lintel above its pumps. The Tuathaich depots were opened at Caol mhòr, Doire ghlas and Ceann mhòr on {{date:VI-0048}}, supplied by sea; Martha Greaves wrote in Tional nan Tuathach's minute that the north now bought its fire from the south, and did not say whether this was good. The tank-yard of the eastern ports at Inis uaine is the largest store of dubhan outside the southern works, and An t-ochdamh rèisimeid guards it. The inns sell it too: An Taigh-òsta Grianach near Cnoc dhìreach was the first on the island to sell fuel as well as beds, in {{year:VI-0029}}, and the drove-inn at An Sligeanach Fad' às kept its trade by selling it to the drovers who had bought lorries.

## The guilds that went

**Comann nan Gualadairean**, Comann nan Gualadairean, which bound the hewing families' sons and daughters on its oath-stone of coal at Achadh dhomhain. Its day of binding passed empty in the first year of the age. It sold its hall in the ninth year and dissolved itself on {{date:VI-0038}}; its last master, Tormod mac Dhonnchaidh, brought its rolls, its sick-roll and Clach-mhionnan nan Gualadairean, the oath-stone, to the library, and asked no receipt but that it be said they came whole and not begged for. His letter said that the guild had outlived the mountain's patience. Its bath-house at Fuarain Theth Muileann ruadh below Muileann ruadh passed to Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail.

**The coal carters**, never a guild, who carried the coal on Rathad na Fèille, the north road and the old mining roads. The last carter of Rathad na Fèille sold his cart in the third year; the last to bring coal down the north road carried a load for the Taigh-seinnse Reòta's kitchen in the twenty-sixth. The last coal on Slighe a' Ghuail went the last stretch from the goods-yard at Cathair dhearg behind carters' horses in {{year:VI-0148}}.

## The guilds that stayed and the guilds that came

**Comann nan Saor-luinge**, Comann nan Saor-luinge' Guild, whose first rule is Cha tollar an druim, the keel is not bored. When the boatwrights of Caol fhiadhaich bored their keels for engines in the fifth year it called the work unlawful, struck them and fined their yards; they returned their papers. Its court at Ros dhomhain ruled on {{date:VI-0013}} that the fitting of an engine was no shipwright's work and so outside its charter, and readmitted them. They declined.

**Comann nan Lòchran**, the lamp-makers of Muileann mhin, the first craft of the kingdom to turn to the new fuel wholesale, on {{date:VI-0019}}. They asked leave to set their guild mark on the new lamps, and two Tuathaich fitters signed their petition; after a year's argument the guild court allowed it, and the flame over the hammer was kept. Their lamps were on the roads by the ninth year.

**Comann nan Sciobairean**, Comann nan Sciobairean of the southern works, which is a company and not a guild, and makes the fuel.

**Comann nan Goibhnean-ceangail**, chartered at the southern Seann Skell on {{date:VI-0045}}, twelve masters sworn in the works' long shed under a banner of a black swan on grey. The fitters of dubhan engines had worked outside every guild; the charter gave them one, and Riaghailt na Cairte kept its rolls, like those of every guild before it, to Dia-thìrich only. The Tuathaich fitters who had turned the lights and the first engines were let go. Peigi nic Ascaill, a master of the new guild, paid Joe Ashby a month over. The fitters let go went home to the northern towns and fitted engines there without a guild, so that within a few years Caol mhòr held more dubhan boats than any port of its size in the south. On {{date:VI-0132}} the guild was given the contract for the regiments' engines and the fleets' hulls and doubled its rolls; all its new fitters are Dia-thìrich.

**The forges** of Àth naomh in the Cuan shean shire and Caol ìseal on the north coast, which smelt the bog-iron of their districts, took the kingdom's contract for the regiments' small arms and field-guns on {{date:VI-0132a}}; the forge-towns of the old vein country followed, and arms became the dearest common goods in every market.

## The carrying

Comhairle na Slighe-iarainn's line from Achadh dhomhain by Muileann chrom to Cathair dhearg and Ros dhomhain took its first dubhan engine on {{date:VI-0022}} and laid up its coal engines on {{date:VI-0030}}. In {{year:VI-0037}} its timetables dropped the word coal from the line's name, and everyone else went on calling it Slighe a' Ghuail. It carries dubhan, wool, salt and soldiers, and ran its last coal in the twenty-sixth year. The first lorries carried salt from Àth bheag inland in {{year:VI-0023}}; the drovers of the east and of Achadh mhòr sold their oxen and bought lorries; buses and hired cars came to the capital in {{year:VI-0041a}}. At sea the south-coast boats took engines first, then the patrol boats at Ros dhomhain, which outran the raiders; Taigh Mhic Ùisdein put its money into dubhan shipping in {{year:VI-0042}}, and An treas cabhlach's tank-hulls carry the fuel up the west coast to the Tuathaich depots, Tional nan Tuathach paying the freight.

## The coin

On {{date:VI-0049}} the council pegged the coin to the making of dubhan, at the same sitting that proclaimed the new count of the years. On {{date:VI-0050}} the mint ceased to press coal ink into the seal of the coinage; the new seal is plain, and Fearchar Bonn's first dies went to the library, in the case beside Clach-mhionnan nan Gualadairean. The coin is reckoned in purses. At the fair of Muileann òg Cùirt na Fèille had kept a painted coal ceiling on its board by the toll-house door; it was scraped off in the third year, since no seller kept it.

## The dues

**The fifth**, the kingdom's old right in the vein, taken by Comhairle nam Mèinnean from those who held seams. The western holders ceased to pay it, they said, when the Board ceased to carry their coal; under Breith Leòid the Board declared the right lapsed and the seams the kingdom's. Ruth Calder offered to pay it in coal if the Board would carry it. With the seams capped and An Sloc Mòr closed, the fifth is taken on nothing in the present year.

**Deachamh a' Chogaidh**, one part in twelve of every sale of dubhan, laid for a time on {{date:VI-0086}} and taken at the pumps by a clerk of An t-Ionmhas; within a month Cùirt na Fèille at Muileann òg recorded the first complaint against it. It was never lifted. On {{date:VI-0131}} An t-Ionmhas's accounts carried it forward as a standing line under the regiments' pay, and no minute of the council records a choice to keep it.

**The market-due**, fixed on {{date:VI-0131a}} at seventeen in the hundred on every sale a town makes into its market and on every sale between markets, taken by a clerk of An t-Ionmhas in each of the sixteen market towns. Caol ruadh, whose harbour victualled the garrisons and An dàrna cabhlach through the war, was granted the sixteenth and last market right on {{date:VI-0120a}}.

**The head-due**, taken at eighteen purses on every five thousand heads.

**An t-Ionmhas's roll** for the present year, on {{date:VI-0154a}}, stands at 9,568 purses: 6,375 from the head-due and 3,193 from the market-due. Deachamh a' Chogaidh is carried on its own line under the regiments' pay. An Oirthir Ghaothach pays neither due.

## The charter of export

Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt was won on {{date:VI-0154b}} by Dubhan, who made the first working fuel and was then in his sixty-ninth year, after a year of letters and three sittings of the council at Cathair dhearg. It is the first leave the kingdom has given to sell dubhan beyond the island. Its terms are narrow: every barrel measured by An t-Ionmhas's measure; loading at Ros dhomhain only; the tithe taken on every barrel; the home depots served first; and every hold searched and sealed by the custodians of Òrd Bhrìde before a hull sails. The first tank-hull under it, An Eala Dhubh, was loaded at Ros dhomhain over three days, a clerk of An t-Ionmhas marking the first barrel and the last and every fifth between, and stood out on the morning tide past the north-western capes with the seal on her hatches. At the southern Seann Skell the priests of Manannan said over her the prayer to keep the sea shut.
