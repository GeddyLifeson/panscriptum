# Appendix D — The Faiths in the Age of Dubhan

The orders of An Creideamh Sean came into the age as they had come through An Dealachadh: Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail of the custodians, whose Àrd-choimheadaiche sits at Brìde's house at the southern Seann Skell and who kept the vein holy while it lasted; Òrd Mhacha at the capital, which crowns the rulers and keeps the horses; Òrd Mhanannain in the harbours; Òrd an t-Seabhaig of the north-central country; Scoiltean nan Draoidhean at Caol shean; and the houses of Na Seann Spioradan in the east. The Tuathaich of the north keep An Eaglais, the church of Naomh Breandan and A' Chiad Churach, and at Baile chrom Oidhche Glaise. None of them was put down in the age, and none was asked what it thought of the fuel before the kingdom took it up. What each said, and what each did with an engine when one came to its door, is given here as the letters, Eachdraidh na Rìoghachd and Sceulachdan nan Tuathach give it.

## The Rite and the new fuel

Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail had kept the vein sacred for as long as there was a vein, and the coal flame on its altars was the vein's flame. The fuel made without the vein put to it a question it had no teaching for, and its Àrd-choimheadaiche, Alasdair mac Ìomhair, answered it in {{year:VI-0009}} in a letter to every house of Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail: the new fuel was not forbidden, since it was not coal, and not blessed, since it was not coal. Litir an Àrd-choimheadaiche was read in the houses and argued in Scoiltean nan Draoidhean at Caol shean, which disputed it the same winter. It governed Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail's dealings with dubhan for the rest of the age. Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail blessed no lamp, engine or depot of the new fuel, and forbade none; its custodians rode behind an engine from the ninth year, when the fitters of the southern works set a lorry's engine under the Àrd-choimheadaiche's closed carriage and the carriage's matched greys were retired.

**The last coal flame.** When the five lights of the coast were turned to dubhan by order in the sixth year, the custodians of Tobar dhearg asked that the flame of its light, which had burned coal-lit for more than a century, be given to Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail. On {{date:VI-0015}} the keeper Ciorstaidh nic Artair bore it in a closed lantern on the walk from the cape to the southern Seann Skell and down the hall of Brìde's house between custodians of four shires, and it was let burn out on the altar. The Àrd-choimheadaiche blessed the empty lantern. The song of the last flame was first sung at the southern Seann Skell that year.

**The bath-house.** Comann nan Gualadairean' bath-house at Fuarain Theth Muileann ruadh below Muileann ruadh passed to Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail with the guild's other goods on {{date:VI-0039}}. Its custodians kept the old keeper and the same sick, and opened it to the Tuathaich; the first Tuathaich family was bathed there that year. In the war they took in wounded officers at the council's asking.

**The protest on the seal.** When the mint ceased to press coal ink into the seal of the coinage in the fifteenth year, the Àrd-choimheadaiche protested to the council that the kingdom had taken the vein out of its own coin. On {{date:VI-0051}} the council answered that the kingdom had never said it held the vein sacred. Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh copied both letters into Eachdraidh na Rìoghachd.

**The altar-coal.** When An Sloc Mòr was closed in {{year:VI-0144}}, one face was kept in Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail's keeping, and on {{date:VI-0145}} twelve hewers were sworn at Achadh dhomhain to cut the altar-coal. They are the last of their trade to work the vein on the island, and Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail gave them its own oath, not the dissolved guild's; one of them, an old man, had sworn the guild's oath in his youth. In the present year the coal they cut was carried south in four baskets, and on {{date:VI-0155}} the Àrd-choimheadaiche drew by lot in the rain in Brìde's yard which of Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail should have the four baskets. A novice of fifteen held the bowl. The southern house drew its own basket, so that one basket went no farther than across the yard. The coal is sold at the fair only by the lump, for altars.

**Òrd Bhrìde.** The custodians of Brìde search and seal every hold that sails under Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt, and set their seal on An Eala Dhubh's hatches at Ros dhomhain in the present year. It is the only office in Malairt an Dubhain that Deas-ghnàth a' Ghuail holds.

**Caol ruadh.** The house of Brìde at Caol ruadh keeps the orthodox rite of Brìde's flame. It bought two years' altar coal ahead in the sixth year, lest the coal fail before the rite did, burned the last of it in the eighteenth, and wrote to the Àrd-choimheadaiche asking where the next was to come from.

## Òrd Mhacha

Òrd Mhacha crowns the rulers at Cathair dhearg and keeps the two horses of the rite, Liath Macha and Dubh Sainglenn, which were led out at Cathal's crowning on {{date:VI-0006}}. It has always blessed roads and those who set out on them. When Comhairle na Slighe-iarainn's first dubhan engine was hauled to Muileann chrom on a wagon by twelve of the order's horses, a priest of Macha walked at the leaders' heads and stood in the ford to his thighs to speak them out of it, and before the first dubhan train left the yard he said over it the blessing for a horse setting out on a long road, there being none made for an engine. The order has made no blessing of its own for an engine since.

## The order of Manannan

The priests of Manannan bless boats, harbours, roofs and carts against the sea and the rain, and keep houses at Seann Bhral and the western Seann Skell and in the harbour towns. Their houses were slow to bless the engine-boats. In {{year:VI-0066}} the house at the western Seann Skell refused to bless Comhairle nam Mèinnean's enforcers' depot: a young priest told Somhairle mac Lachlainn that the house blessed a boat and a roof against the sea and the rain, and would not pretend the same of a depot built for Comhairle nam Mèinnean's lorries. The roof was blessed at last by a stranger's hand. The house at Seann Bhral heard of the refusal and sent no word either way. At Àth naomh in the Cuan shean shire the harbour carts are blessed every spring, horses and all, and a dubhan lamp on a tail-board is blessed because it is on the cart. When An Eala Dhubh sailed under Cairt na h-Às-mhalairt in the present year, the priests of Manannan at the southern Seann Skell said over her the sailing prayer that asks the sea to stay shut.

## The Hawk houses

Òrd an t-Seabhaig of Cnoc bheag and the north-central country keep their own rite, older than the kingdom. When An siathamh rèisimeid was raised at Cnoc bheag on {{date:VI-0101}} they blessed its colours by that rite, and the colours hang in their hall between musters.

## The druid school and Na Seann Spioradan

Scoiltean nan Draoidhean at Caol shean disputed the Àrd-choimheadaiche's letter in the fourth year, and in {{year:VI-0036}} its masters, writing to the library of their own school's revival, made the word dubhan-tùsail, of a first attempt that seemed a dead end and proved otherwise. The school sends a teacher once a year to give the one lecture that remains at the druid house of Muileann dhearg, which was given to the town's caretaker with the graves when the pits closed. Pilgrims have gone up to its landing by engine since the ninth year.

The house of Na Seann Spioradan at Inis uaine added An t-ochdamh rèisimeid to its prayers when the regiment's guard on the eastern tanks was made lasting on {{date:VI-0141}}.

## An Eaglais of the north

The Tuathaich keep An Eaglais, the church of Naomh Breandan, whose telling of A' Chiad Churach, Breandan's voyage to Inis nam Beannaichte, is the north's own telling of how its people came. The Dia-thìrich in office call it folklore. Its hymns are sung in the humans' tongue that the Tuathaich keep, and its services are kept on the Sunday. The five western towns kept an Nollaig together at Àth leathan's chapel in the nineteenth year; in Sèist Muileann àrsaidh Weir kept the Sunday service through the whole of it and the graves were dug behind the chapel; before Àth leathan the men of Tobar dhearg of the hills kept the chapel and sang low along their line in the words of An Eaglais. At Ceann mhòr, in the quartered year, Corporal Dùghall mac Iain heard the hymns of Barbara Keir's house through the wall of his billet. At Doire ghlas, on the shortest evening of the present year, Ellen Marsh told the eldest children the telling of A' Chiad Churach and of the fire its crew carried in a clay pot through the mist, and a boy asked her whose fire it was. The rhyme of the north asks the same.

An Eaglais has no office in the kingdom and has asked for none. It was not asked for its witness when the war was sealed, nor was its register of the dead of the western towns kept at the library; Clàr nam Marbh is nan Leòinte is kept by Tional nan Tuathach.

## The Grey Night

Oidhche Glaise sect at Baile chrom keeps its vigil once a year with every lamp in the town out and every door shut. On {{date:VI-0108}} the regiments quartered along the coast reported a night when the whole town was dark and silent; a file of An ceathramh rèisimeid searched it and found nothing. The house of Margaret Crane on the lighthouse road kept the vigil alone; a soldier of the file set his lamp down in her passage rather than carry its light past her. The vigil is kept every year as it always was.
