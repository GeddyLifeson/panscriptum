# Appendix B — The Hosts of An Cogadh Fada

The kingdom had kept no army since An Dealachadh. What it had in the first twenty years of the age was Comhairle nam Mèinnean's enforcers, a few score men who guarded Comhairle nam Mèinnean's yards and depots and went with its clerks where a clerk was not welcome, and the patrol boats of Ros dhomhain, which kept the raiders off the southern trade. An Cogadh Fada was fought in two summers, the twenty-first and twenty-second years, and in it the kingdom raised nine regiments and four fleets, each named for the town where it is stationed. All of them stand in the present year. This appendix gives the hosts as the regimental lists and An Clàr Foscailte give them: the enforcers and their depot, the regiments and fleets in the order of their raising with their musters at the close of the present year, the companies of the western towns as Sceulachdan nan Tuathach count them, the campaign in outline, the counts An Clàr Foscailte allows, Clach nan Rèisimeidean, and the posts and passes that the war left. Of the war's full record, which lies in the sealed room at Leabharlann Muileann chaol, nothing is given here, because nothing of it can be.

## The enforcers

Comhairle nam Mèinnean's enforcers were given a western company in the eighteenth year, when the western seams were declared the kingdom's and the pits ordered shut. Somhairle mac Lachlainn, who had risen among them, was given forty men and chose men who had served under him before. They posted two men at each pit-head of the five towns with Comhairle nam Mèinnean's order nailed to the winding-gear, to report twice weekly whether coal came up. It came up. In {{year:VI-0066}} the company built its depot at the western Seann Skell, whose roof the priests of Manannan would not bless, and brought up the first two lorries the town had seen. From the autumn of that year its wagons met stones and trenches on the roads into the hills. None of the skirmishes of that autumn is written down, and none was great enough to name.

## The regiments

The regiments were called up in the twentieth year by the names of the companies of An Dealachadh, whose rolls were brought out of the council's chests at Cathair dhearg and read in the old drill hall, the dead towns struck through. They were formed and armed openly, months before any in power would own that war was coming, and their command was given to Somhairle mac Lachlainn. Every regiment has foot, horse, riflemen and guns; every one of its carts and lorries runs on dubhan, which is carried up to it by Slighe a' Ghuail, the western branch and An treas cabhlach's tank-hulls. The musters below are those of the winter of the present year.

**A' chiad rèisimeid**, A' chiad rèisimeid, raised at Inis mhòr on {{date:VI-0088}} from the capital's own companies. It guards the council, the mint and the bridge over the Abhainn dhomhain. It fought at Muileann ghlas and marched the released prisoners north to the boundary stones. It musters 248 foot, 174 horse, 119 riflemen and 4 guns: 545 men.

**An dàrna rèisimeid**, the second, raised at Àth dhìreach in the east on {{date:VI-0102}} to guard Slighe a' Ghuail and the library road. Its camp was the first soldiery Muileann chaol had seen, and since the twenty-sixth year it keeps a guard at the library over the sealed room. It musters 259 foot, 70 horse, 146 riflemen and 3 guns: 478 men.

**An treas rèisimeid**, the third, raised at Cathair mhòr on {{date:VI-0092}} under the market cross of Taigh Mhic Ùisdein. It fought at Muileann ghlas, its four guns hauled the last two miles to the field by hand. It musters 220 foot, 91 horse, 94 riflemen and 2 guns: 407 men.

**An ceathramh rèisimeid**, the fourth, raised at Caol bheag in the north-east on {{date:VI-0103}}. It went down the Abhainn ghorm to Ros chiar in coal barges, was shipped round the north coast in hired dubhan hulls, and was quartered in the old magistrate's house at Ceann mhòr for four years before it went home. It musters 205 foot, 80 horse, 109 riflemen and 2 guns: 396 men.

**An còigeamh rèisimeid**, the fifth, raised at Seann Warr on the lake shore west of the capital on {{date:VI-0093}}, and sent north to relieve the regiments before Muileann àrsaidh. Its stone barracks at Seann Warr were the first lasting quarters any of the nine had. It musters 184 foot, 108 horse, 96 riflemen and 4 guns: 392 men.

**An siathamh rèisimeid**, the sixth, raised at Cnoc bheag on {{date:VI-0101}}, its colours blessed by Òrd an t-Seabhaig by their own rite. It holds the northern road between the Tuathaich shires and the middle of the island, and keeps the posts by the boundary stones. It musters 149 foot, 58 horse, 92 riflemen and 2 guns: 301 men.

**An seachdamh rèisimeid**, the seventh, raised from the levies of the west coast at the western Seann Skell on {{date:VI-0084}}, the day of Àth leathan. It holds the road behind the army and Àraich Àth leathan, stood by at every capping of the western seams, and keeps a company at Freiceadan Àth leathan, its post on the road below the town. Its rolls ran short of the order in its second week. It musters 131 foot, 67 horse, 69 riflemen and 2 guns: 269 men.

**An t-ochdamh rèisimeid**, the eighth, raised at Inis uaine on the Abhainn chaol on {{date:VI-0106}} to guard the dubhan depots and tanks of the eastern ports. Its guard was made lasting in the twenty-fifth year. It musters 142 foot, 28 horse, 84 riflemen and 1 gun: 255 men.

**An naoidheamh rèisimeid**, the ninth, raised at the southern Seann Skell on {{date:VI-0110}} to guard the shipmasters' works; the regimental clerks tell the two Seann Skells apart by river and coast. Its man won Còmhrag Ceann leathan at Ceann leathan in the twenty-sixth year and lost the championship in the present one. It musters 135 foot, 20 horse, 96 riflemen and 3 guns: 254 men.

Together the nine regiments muster 1,673 foot, 696 horse and 905 riflemen, some three thousand three hundred men, with twenty-three guns serviceable.

## The fleets

**A' chiad chabhlach**, A' chiad chabhlach, raised on {{date:VI-0089}} from the south-west harbours and stationed at Àth gharbh, its hulls lying in the harbour of Doire shean. It was sent round the western cape to stop the coal going out by sea from the Tuathaich ports. It has two hulls.

**An ceathramh cabhlach**, An ceathramh cabhlach, raised at Cuan shean on {{date:VI-0090}}. With the first it closed the western Seann Skell and Cnoc bhàn to every boat but fishing boats, and searched those, and the boats of Caol mhòr and Cathair gheal on every day they put out. It is kept on the Tuathaich coast since the war. It has one hull, the Bratach Ghlas.

**An dàrna cabhlach**, An dàrna cabhlach, raised at the southern Seann Skell on {{date:VI-0099}} to keep the eastern and southern waters while the first and fourth were in the north-west. It ran down one of the raiders who took the salt-boats off Àth bheag in the war, and the rest of them off Baile ìseal in {{year:VI-0133}}. It has two hulls.

**An treas cabhlach**, An treas cabhlach, raised at Baile ghorm on {{date:VI-0107}}. It carried the army's dubhan up the west coast in tank-hulls to the regiments in the hills, and since the war runs dubhan to the Tuathaich depots on the council's contract, Tional nan Tuathach paying the freight. It has one hull, and the tank-hulls it hires.

## The companies of the western towns

An Clàr Foscailte names the holdouts only as Muinntir gun Siorrachd. Their companies are known from Sceulachdan nan Tuathach, from Ruth Calder's list at the pit-shed of Àth leathan as the families at Doire ghlas remembered it, and from Comhairle nam Mèinnean's own list, which named her and Tom Varley among them. By the eve of Àth leathan the muster there counted near nine hundred souls with their carriers, in five companies: Muileann àrsaidh near two hundred, Cnoc thais a hundred and sixty, Àth leathan ninety, Àth naomh sixty and Tobar dhearg of the hills forty. They had among them some sixty firearms, most of them fowling pieces older than any man carrying them, a dozen rifles, picks and short axes, and four kegs of blasting powder from the pits. They had no guns, no horse and no fuel but their own coal.

The column of the second summer, which left the western hills and marched south-east through the middle of the island toward the capital, is given in An Clàr Foscailte as some hundreds strong. Who led it is named only in Sceulachdan nan Tuathach, as Owen Barrow. Why it marched is not told.

## The campaign in outline

*The first summer.* The regiments set out from Cathair dhearg by the western road on {{date:VI-0080}}, their carts and lorries on dubhan, and joined the enforcers at the western Seann Skell. On {{date:VI-0081}} they met the five towns' companies below the spoil-heaps of Àth leathan, the first great battle of the war. The holdouts gave up the southern pits and fell back into the hills about Muileann àrsaidh and Cnoc thais; the pits at Àth leathan were taken without a fight and flooded. The regiments laid siege to Muileann àrsaidh on {{date:VI-0091}}; the town held for weeks on stored coal and potatoes while its six pit-heads were taken one by one. Cnoc thais was taken on {{date:VI-0098}}, Tom Varley firing his own two shafts, and some of its people went on beyond the hills onto an Oirthir Ghaothach of the far north-west; the regiments followed to the edge of the shire of Seann Vell and no farther. Àth naomh and Tobar dhearg of the hills opened their roads without a siege on {{date:VI-0109}}.

*The quartered year.* Through the twenty-second year An ceathramh rèisimeid patrolled the Ceann mhòr road, the sixth held the northern road, and the seventh the western hills, and the northern office issued passes. In the autumn the column went south by Àth bheag in the west, the smaller Muileann mhin and the edge of the capital's country, and camped on the fields by Muileann ghlas, a day's walk from Cathair dhearg. The first and third regiments were brought down Rathad na Fèille from their camp on the fair-ground of Muileann òg to meet it.

*The second summer's end.* On {{date:VI-0115}} the column was broken at Muileann ghlas, the second great battle of the war and the last; sixty came out of the byres at dusk under a white cloth. Word of it reached the hills, and on {{date:VI-0117}} the holdouts still there came down to the posts of An seachdamh rèisimeid on the Àth leathan road and were disarmed. The war was declared ended that day.

## The open record's counts

An Clàr Foscailte counts the kingdom's dead at Àth leathan and not the holdouts'; Sceulachdan nan Tuathach put theirs in the hundreds. At Muileann ghlas it lists the first and third regiments on the field and gives no count of the column's dead. Of Sèist Muileann àrsaidh it gives the day the town fell and nothing else. Of Cnoc thais it gives forty-one taken. Of Àth naomh and Tobar dhearg it names no holdouts taken at either. The regiments' surgeon's tally for the night after Muileann ghlas, which the regiments allowed, stands at twenty-three of the regiments buried or dying and near ninety of the column's wounded brought in. Some four score holdouts taken at Muileann ghlas were held in the capital's gaol until {{date:VI-0123}}, when A' chiad rèisimeid marched them north and handed them to Tional nan Tuathach at the boundary stones, and Tional nan Tuathach entered their names in a column of their own in Clàr nam Marbh is nan Leòinte, its register of the dead and maimed.

## The stone at Cathair mhòr

The regiments' dead are named on a stone set up at Cathair mhòr on {{date:VI-0126}}, beside the market cross of Taigh Mhic Ùisdein. It is cut in two columns under a single heading, the regiments' dead, and gives neither battle nor date. It carries sixty-one names: thirty-eight of Àth leathan and twenty-three of Muileann ghlas. Among them are two brothers of Seann Bhral who enlisted together and died within the same hour of the same morning. Somhairle mac Lachlainn read the names at the unveiling. No stone names the dead of the western towns. Their cairn stands on Àraich Àth leathan, built by the Tuathaich families without leave in {{year:VI-0130}}, and grows by a stone or two every time the patrols pass it.

## The posts and the passes

After the war the regiments stayed on their stations, as the council ordered on {{date:VI-0120}}. The seventh built Freiceadan Àth leathan on the road below Àth leathan in the autumn of the twenty-third year and keeps a company there; the town calls it something else. The sixth keeps posts on the northern road by the boundary stones, where from the twenty-sixth year the Tuathaich going south show Cead an Rathaid, the pass of the northern office, printed at Cathair dhearg on grey card with a space for a name and none for a trade, and good for a time only. The second keeps its guard at the library, the eighth at the eastern tanks, and An ceathramh cabhlach on the Tuathaich coast. The war's cost is carried on An t-Ionmhas's roll as Deachamh a' Chogaidh, one part in twelve of every sale of dubhan, laid for a time in the twenty-first year and never lifted; it stands on its own line under the regiments' pay.
