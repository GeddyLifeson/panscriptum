# Age VII: the plan of the volume

*An Aois Dhubhain, the Age of Dubhan. Linn an Dubhain, DE 1 – 27 (19 an t-Ògmhios DE 1 to 21 an Dùbhlachd DE 27,
the present). 166 master events, VI-0001 … VI-0157. Architect's plan for the lead and writers W01–W12.*

## 1. The volume

**Title.** *Cò leis an Teine?* · **Whose Is the Fire?** (Book spine: *An Aois Dhubhain*.)

**Epigraph** (for `front.json`), the north's rhyme, heard there in both tongues:

> *Dubh an gual, 's dubh an dubhan,*
> *ach cò leis an teine?*
> Black the coal, and black the dubhan;
> but whose is the fire?
>
> — a rhyme of the Tuathaich north

**Premise.** On a midsummer night in the north a Tuathach whose people were pushed to the island's edge makes a fuel
out of what the mines throw away. The kingdom that the vein built has been failing with the vein, and within fifteen
years it takes the fuel for everything: its lights, its roads, its rails, its coin, and at last the count of its years.
Everything the old coal carried changes hands, and the people who make the change are not the people who gain by it.
The Tuathaich fitters who turn the lights are let go under a charter rule. The pit towns of the western hills will not
hang their fires on one man's fuel, so the kingdom calls up the regiments of the Severance by their old names and
breaks them in two campaigns, with lorries that run on the fuel a Tuathach made. It seals the record of what it did,
keeps the regiments on their stations and the tithe on the pumps, lets the motion for amends die, and in the last
autumn of the age sends the fuel out past the north-western capes under its maker's narrow charter. The volume is told
as the Library's teller at the close of DE 27 tells it, from the open record, the towns' reports, the Moot's minutes and
the Tuathaich tellings; what lies in the sealed room is not told.

**The question the volume answers:** *whose is the fire?* When the vein fails and a new fire is made, who owns it: the
man who made it, the north that made him, or the kingdom that took it up? The answer the age gives in law is plain
(the Crown's: the coin is pegged to it, the tithe is taken on it, the regiments run on it). The answer the north gives
is the *seann-chunntas*, the old reckoning no one settles and no one strikes out. The book ends with both answers
standing.

## 2. Point of view and voice

**Voice.** The high recollection voice of the master Books, but in scenes. Roughly half the chapters are told close to
one person (past tense, sight, cold, smell, speech), half as recollection with a teller placing the telling ("So the
old people of Doire ghlas tell it…", "The Moot's minute says…"). The later the book, the quicker and drier the texture:
this is the age of lorries, buses and council minutes, and its wit is the wit of clerks. Months are the civil Gaelic
months. The humans' descendants speak both tongues; mark it once in a scene and move on.

**The frame, and why it matters for the war.** The teller writes at the close of DE 27 and has NOT read the sealed
archive. So:
- Before DE 21 and after DE 22, kingdom-side interiors (the council, the Board, Somhairle mac Lachlainn) are open to
  the teller through minutes, letters and returns, and may be told close.
- In the war years (DE 21–22) the kingdom's side is told only through what is open: Sìleas nic Rath's list of places,
  regiments and counts, the regiments' returns, the towns' reports, and what men said afterwards. The holdouts' side
  comes from the Tuathaich tellings and Ellen Marsh's accounts, and is told close.
- Say the silence plainly once in the whole volume, in ch. 23: *What is told here of the war comes from the open
  record, the towns' reports and the Tuathaich tellings; the rest is sealed.* After that, don't apologise for it.

**Hard rules for every writer of this volume**
1. **The owner's characters** act only as the master annals say. *Dubhan*: he makes the first working fuel (VI-0001,
   told as the north's tellers tell it, a man at a fire, no speech, no thoughts); he wins the charter of export
   (VI-0154b: a year of letters, three sittings, an old man in his sixty-ninth year, tells no one his buyers). Nothing
   else: no invented words, feelings, kin, pupils, rivals, home town or deeds. Others may speak *of* him. *Seachran*
   and *Brean* may be named once as his closest allies and do nothing. *Cathal* is crowned (VI-0006) and reigns; the
   council, the Board and the Treasury act, never Cathal. *Mairead*'s reign "ended"; give no cause. Aisling, Fionnan,
   Lorccan and Cian do not appear; "the coal caste" and the census line on the coal-touched stay as the master gives
   them. No invented person is parent, child, spouse, lover, rival, teacher or pupil of any of these.
2. **What stays unresolved:** Ruth Calder's fate after 11 an Giblean DE 21 (her POV ends before the fighting); why the
   column went south-east, and what Owen Barrow meant by it (no POV inside the column; he is seen from outside, named
   only "in the tellings"); how the Seann Skell shipmasters came by the making of dubhan; where the Moot's second set of
   minute-books is hidden; what the sealed archive holds; who buys Dubhan's fuel; what the town calls the Àth leathan
   post. Nobody in the book knows these, so no POV can think them.
3. **Scale** (WRITERS_GUIDE §13; specs_draft/age_VII.json). Towns are small: Àth leathan was a pit village of a few
   hundred before the war and is 34 souls in DE 27; Muileann àrsaidh 650, Cnoc thais 960, Àth naomh 650, Tobar dhearg of
   the hills 200 with one pit and forty hewers; Caol mhòr 2,100; Doire ghlas 2,000; the capital's precinct 514; the
   western Seann Skell 3,700, the southern 2,900. The nine regiments and four fleets muster some 3,300 men, 23 guns and
   six hulls in DE 27. Fixed figures for the whole volume: at Àth leathan the kingdom had about 1,400 men in the field
   and the holdouts some 900 with their carriers; the stone at Cathair mhòr bears **61** names (38 of Àth leathan, 23 of
   Muileann ghlas); the column was about 400; the holdouts' dead are "in the hundreds" and never counted exactly.
4. **Geography** (map units × 0.14 mi): capital–Caol mhòr 34 mi (two days by road, one by dubhan bus after DE 13);
   capital–Àth leathan 29 mi; Àth leathan–western Seann Skell 5 mi; Àth leathan–Muileann àrsaidh 10 mi, –Cnoc thais 10
   mi; capital–Library at Muileann chaol 69 mi (the coal line and the library road); capital–Muileann òg 15 mi south-east,
   Muileann ghlas close beside it. The column's road (VI-0112) runs from the western hills by Àth bheag (burg 248),
   south-east past Muileann mhin (burg 137, the lamp-makers' town) and the edge of the capital's country, to the fair
   country south-east of the capital: some 55–60 miles in five weeks. Two Seann Skells: the western river port (489,
   the seventh regiment, the enforcers) and the southern coast (431, the Rite, the works, the ninth regiment and second
   fleet); always say which. Two Tobar dheargs: the south-coast light (marker 16, burg 398) and the hill pit town
   (burg 408). The fourth regiment goes down the Abhainn ghorm to Ros chiar and round the north coast to Ceann mhòr
   (VI-0103). Ships for beyond the island go north-about, past the north-western capes.
5. **Faith.** The Tuathaich are Christian: the churches of the Mission's line (largest house Cnoc ghorm), the old
   congregations of the north coast (Doire ghlas, the First Canoe), the Grey Night vigil at Baile chrom. Christmas
   (*an Nollaig*) is kept in the north and not by the Dia-thìrich (VI-0071, VI-0077). The Dia-thìrich keep the Old Faith
   and its orders; Òrd Bhrìde (the Rite) at the southern Seann Skell.
6. **Forward only; no meta; no obsidian; NAMES.json forms only**; new forms in `PLAN_names.json` (lead to confirm).
   Every chapter ends on its own day; no chapter hints at the war before DE 18.

## 3. Structure: eight Parts in twelve Books, forty chapters

**Files.** Each chapter is its own Book file, `book/NN_<slug>.md` (NN = chapter number, two digits), opening with
`# <chapter title>` (e.g. `# Of the Fire Banked in the North`), the date line (`*DE 1*`), and, only for a chapter that
opens a Part, an epigraph block carrying the Part's title and epigraph as given below. Inside, 2–5 scenes as `## I.`,
`## II.` … sections, each titled in the "Of …" style. New events go in `events/NN.json` per CHAPTER_BRIEF; the foreman
compiles the annals (§6 ranges). Chapter numbers run 1–40. "Masters" lists the master events a chapter must carry (a
contiguous run, so the book moves strictly forward). Era events are proposals; add what the chapter needs.

### Part One — *Of the Fire in the North and the Five Lights* · DE 1 – 7
Epigraph: *Loiscidh e, tha fàileadh dheth, agus chan e gual a th' ann.* "It burns, it stinks, and it is not coal." — the
Board's surveyors, DE 2.

**1. Of the Fire Banked in the North** · recollection, then Beathag · DE 1 · VI-0001, VI-0002 · 4,500 w
The north's tellers open the age at a banked fire on a midsummer night: a man who has failed for ten years, a crucible
of pit waste, and in the morning a black stuff that burns (Dubhan seen from outside; no speech). Then the kingdom as it
stood that summer: Mairead reigning, coal sold dear, the poorer streets of the capital unlit, the Guild of Hewers
binding no apprentices, the fourth floor at Muileann chaol empty. Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh at her desk in the Library
through the autumn; a carrier's rumour, a Tuathach pedlar's sample she is not shown; Raghnall mac Mhuirich says the
Library keeps, it does not chase. The turn: she will not write what she has not seen, and will not leave it out. The
chapter ends at the foot of the year, on 21 an t-Samhain, with the first mention of dubhan in the royal record, the word
spelled three ways in two lines.
*Era:* a Tuathach pedlar carries "the black cake" to the Caol mhòr market (DE 1); Beathag writes to the maor of Caol
mhòr for a sample and has no answer (DE 1); the price of coal-fuel at the fair holds above the ceiling (DE 1); the
Library's copyists count the shires' chests catalogued, the fourth floor still empty (DE 1).

**2. Of the Two Surveyors** · POV Seumas mac Eachainn · DE 2 · VI-0003, VI-0004 · 4,000 w
Seumas, a young surveyor of the Mine Board, rides north with old Iain Ruadh mac Nèill to learn whether the new fuel
burns as men say. The boundary stones; the northern road; Caol mhòr's harbour; Tuathaich who answer in two tongues and
say little in either. They are shown the fuel burning in a mill yard (Martha Greaves's, at Doire ghlas; she meets them
civilly and tells them nothing) and never shown who made it. The turn: Iain Ruadh wants to write "unproven"; Seumas
writes the three plain clauses of the report. Back in the capital on 27 an Dàmhair, the lamp-houses leave the poorer
streets dark to save coal; Seumas walks home through them and counts the unlit lamps.
*Era:* the surveyors cross the boundary stones on the northern road (DE 2); the Board files the surveyors' report
without minute (DE 2); a lamp-lighter's family of the capital petitions for work and is refused (DE 2); first winter of
the dark streets, a robbery on the bridge noted by the watch (DE 2).

**3. Of the Last Cartload and the Crowning** · POV Ùna nic Ailein; recollection of the crowning · DE 3 · VI-0005 –
VI-0007 · 4,000 w
Ùna, clerk of the fair court at Muileann òg, writes up the last sale of coal-fuel by the cartload in am Faoilleach, and
the chapter follows the fair's year through her ledger: coal by the sack, then by the lump, for altars. At Lùnastal she
goes up to the capital to see the crowning by Macha's rite: Cathal crowned (told as the crowd saw it: the regalia, the
two horses of the rite; Cathal has no words). At month's end Gille-Easbaig Bàn, the first die-cutter not of Fearchar
Bonn's workshop, cuts the reign's dies and presses coal ink into the seal as it always was. The turn is quiet: Ùna's
ledger has no line for the new fuel, and she rules one.
*Era:* the fair court's first entry for "black cake" sold by a Tuathaich pedlar (DE 3); the coal ink bought for the new
dies costs a year's wages of a die-cutter (DE 3); the first coal-lump sold for an altar at the fair (DE 3).

**4. Of the Shipmasters' Works** · POV Peigi nic Ascaill · DE 4 · VI-0008 – VI-0010 · 4,000 w
Peigi, engineer to the Lighthouse Board, comes to the southern Seann Skell to see the works a company of shipmasters has
raised; she asks where they learned the making and is given wine instead of an answer. The works' Tuathaich hands (among
them Joe Ashby, first seen here, sleeves rolled, saying nothing). The high custodian Alasdair mac Ìomhair's letter in
an t-Samhain, read in the Rite's houses: not forbidden, since not coal; not blessed, since not coal. At year's end the
works sell their first year's making to the south-coast boats and the capital's lamp-houses, and the chimney is the
tallest thing on the coast. The turn: Peigi buys twenty barrels on the Board's account without leave.
*Era:* the shipmasters' company takes a lease of the old salt-sheds at Seann Skell (DE 4); the druid school at Caol shean
disputes the high custodian's letter (DE 4); the first dubhan barrels landed at the capital's lamp-houses (DE 4); Peigi
nic Ascaill's twenty barrels entered to the Lighthouse Board (DE 4).

**5. Of the Hundred Nights** · POV Peigi, with Murchadh mac Coinnich · DE 5 · VI-0011 · 4,000 w
In the yard below Taigh-solais Baile ìseal on the eastern cape Peigi fits a dubhan burner to a spare lamp and runs it
a hundred nights. Old Murchadh mac Coinnich, retired from the light, keeps the log: weather, wick, smoke, what the
boats said. Storm nights, a cracked chimney-glass, a burner that clogs, a fishing skipper who comes in by the trial lamp
and not by the tower. The keeper Seonag nic Thòmais will not let the thing up her stair. The turn falls on the
seventieth night, when the tower's coal fails in a gale and the trial lamp does not. The chapter ends on the hundredth
night with Murchadh's last line in the log.
*Era:* the trial lamp's first night (DE 5); the tower's coal flame fails in a gale and the trial lamp holds (DE 5);
Peigi nic Ascaill's report to the Lighthouse Board (DE 5).

**6. Of the Boatwrights of Caol fhiadhaich** · POV Sorcha nic Dhùghaill · DE 5 – 6 · VI-0012, VI-0013 · 4,000 w
On the south-west coast Sorcha's yard fits dubhan engines to the first fishing boats. The Shipwrights' Guild calls it
unlawful for its members; the boatwrights leave the guild and keep the work. A season at sea with an engine: the boats
come home against the wind. At Ros dhomhain in an Giblean the guild court rules that fitting an engine is no
shipwright's work at all, and readmits them. They decline. The turn is Sorcha's refusal, spoken in the court.
*Era:* the first engine-boat of Caol fhiadhaich brings in a catch against a south-westerly (DE 5); three yards of the
south-west coast follow (DE 6); the guild's fine on Sorcha's yard remitted (DE 6).

**7. Of the Last Coal Flame** · POV Ciorstaidh nic Artair · DE 6 · VI-0014, VI-0015 · 4,500 w
By decree the five beacons are turned to dubhan in an Cèitean. Ciorstaidh, grey, keeper of Taigh-solais Tobar dhearg on
the south coast, will let no one else carry the lantern: the walk from the tower to the Rite's house at the southern
Seann Skell, the custodians of four shires in their rows, the flame on Brìde's altar burning down to a blue bead and a
thread of smoke. The master Book's scene, told whole and slowly, with Alasdair mac Ìomhair and Peigi among those who
watch. The chapter ends with the south coast's song (*Dh'fhalbh an lasair às an tùr…*) in its shortest form.
*Era:* the decree turning the lights read at the Lighthouse Board (DE 6); Ciorstaidh nic Artair refuses a deputy for the
lantern (DE 6); the song of the last flame first sung at Seann Skell (DE 6).

**8. Of the Five Towers** · POV Joe Ashby; Peigi · DE 7 · VI-0016 – VI-0019 · 4,000 w
The other towers turned, one by one: Muileann bheag on the Tuathaich north coast fed by cart from the north;
Taigh-solais Àth dhearg on the north-east cape, where the tank will not go up the stair and a crane from the rope-walk at
Seann Tarr lifts it outside; and Baile chrom, where the Board has no fitters to send so far and Joe Ashby and three
other Tuathaich fitters of Caol mhòr do the work in a week of rain. The keeper's report names none of them. At year's
end the lamp-makers of Muileann mhin begin to make dubhan lamps and ask leave to set their mark on them. The turn: Joe
reads the keeper's report on the notice-board at Caol mhòr and looks for his name.
*Era:* the crane walked from Seann Tarr to Àth dhearg (DE 7); the Tuathaich fitters paid by the Moot, not the Board
(DE 7); Peigi takes service with the shipmasters' works at the southern Seann Skell (DE 7); Joe Ashby hired at the
works (DE 7).


### Part Two — *Of the Roads, the Rails and the Plain Seal* · DE 8 – 15
Epigraph: *Cha b' fheudar dhomh riamh roimhe bliadhna a ghluasad 's mi beò innte.* "Never before have I had to move a
year I was living in." — Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh, DE 15.

**9. Of the Speaker and the Binders** · POV Martha Greaves · DE 8 · VI-0020, VI-0021 · 4,300 w
Daniel Frayne dies at Muileann leathan in am Faoilleach. The Moot at Caol mhòr chooses Martha Greaves, mill manager of
Doire ghlas, who did not ask for it. Her first act is his last wish: his old pupils, the binders of Muileann leathan,
rebind the Moot's minute-books from Walter Hale's first meeting. She signs the minute that says a second set is made and
hidden elsewhere, and does not ask where (she does not know; neither does the reader). Peter Hale, a young delegate,
carries the books. Ellen Marsh, teaching at Doire ghlas, lends the binders her schoolroom.
*Era:* Daniel Frayne's burial at Muileann leathan (DE 8); Martha Greaves's first sitting as speaker (DE 8); the rebound
set laid in the Moot's press at Caol mhòr (DE 8).

**10. Of the Coal Line and the Salt Road** · POV Eachann mac Ruairidh · DE 8 – 9 · VI-0022 – VI-0024 · 4,300 w
Eachann, an engine-driver of the yard families at Muileann chrom, learns the dubhan engine in a month. On Bealltainn he
drives the Rail Board's first dubhan engine down the coal line to Cathair dhearg carrying no coal, only dubhan for the
lamp-houses, and brings it home empty. The yard rows, counted apart; the coal engines idle in the siding. Winter: salt from
Àth bheag goes inland by lorry past Baile ìseal, and the drovers of the east sell their oxen; in spring the drovers of
Achadh mhòr buy lorries and the drove-inn at An Sligeanach Fad' às sells them dubhan. The turn: Eachann's father, who
drove coal forty years, will not ride the new engine.
*Era:* the Rail Board's first dubhan tank at Muileann chrom (DE 8); a yard family's son killed under a wagon in the
changeover (DE 8); the last ox-drove on the eastern salt road (DE 9).

**11. Of the Wick over the Door** · recollection (the roads) with Oighrig nic Iain · DE 9 – 10 · VI-0025 – VI-0029 ·
4,500 w
A carrier's year along the old mining roads as the inns hang dubhan lamps. At An Leòmhann Buidhe the landlord keeps one
wick lamp over the door, and the custom spreads. In an t-Ògmhios the fitters at the southern Seann Skell (Peigi's shop)
set a lorry engine under a closed carriage for the high custodian, the first carriage without a horse. Oighrig nic
Iain, landlady of Am Buabhall Fortanach, is the last of the great inns to hang the lamps and writes the cost in her
book as a year's takings. An Taigh-seinnse Mòr lets its new hall to the Guild of Hewers, whose own hall is sold; An
Taigh-òsta Grianach sells fuel as well as beds. The turn is Oighrig's reckoning.
*Era:* the high custodian's first ride in the horseless carriage, to Brìde's house at Ros dhomhain (DE 9); the Guild of
Hewers' hall at Achadh dhomhain put up for sale (DE 9); a carriers' song of the wick lamp (DE 10).

**12. Of the Engines Laid Up and the Pit Quarter Emptied** · POV Eachann; Tormod mac Dhonnchaidh · DE 10 – 11 ·
VI-0030 – VI-0034 · 4,500 w
The coal engines are laid up in the siding at Muileann chrom and never run again; the yard families are taught the new
engines, such as will learn. An Taigh-seinnse Reòta's landlord writes that the reek of dubhan drives away his guests,
then puts in the lamps. In an Dàmhair the last face under Muileann dhearg is shut; Tormod mac Dhonnchaidh, master of the
Guild of Hewers, walks the families to the Hewers' Row at Seann Toll on the Abhainn uaine. Few find work on the boats;
most go to the dubhan tanks at the river landing. The druid house at Muileann dhearg goes to the caretaker with the
graves. The turn: the last shift comes up and there is no one to hand the lamps to.
*Era:* Eachann mac Ruairidh passed as driver of the dubhan engines (DE 10); the last shift at Muileann dhearg (DE 10);
the Hewers' Row built at Seann Toll by the Board (DE 11).

**13. Of a Name that Went before the Fuel** · POV Sìleas nic Rath (clerk); Tormod · DE 11 – 13 · VI-0035 – VI-0040 ·
4,500 w
Men begin to say "dubhan" for the fuel and not the man. Sìleas nic Rath, Beathag's young clerk, keeps a slip-box of the
word's spellings. A letter of the druid school at Caol shean coins *dubhan-tùsail*. The Rail Board drops "coal" from its
line in its timetables, and nobody else does. In an Cèitean DE 12 the Guild of Hewers dissolves itself; Tormod brings
its rolls, its oath-stone of coal and its sick-roll to the Library, and Sìleas signs for them. The bath-house at the hot
springs below Muileann ruadh passes to the Rite and opens to the Tuathaich; the guild's old hall becomes a depot with the
oath still cut in the lintel over the pumps. The turn: Tormod's letter, "the guild had outlived the mountain's
patience", and Beathag copying it in.
*Era:* Sìleas nic Rath taken on as the chronicler's clerk (DE 11); the Hewers' oath-stone set in the Library's case (DE
12); the first Tuathaich family bathed at the hot springs (DE 12); Tormod mac Dhonnchaidh dies at Seann Toll (DE 13).

**14. Of the Charter Rule** · POV Joe Ashby; Coinneach mac Ùisdein · DE 13 – 14 · VI-0041 – VI-0047 · 5,000 w
The trade turns wholly to dubhan; the coal caste says nothing. Buses run from the capital to Inis mhòr and Cathair mhòr
and hired cars wait at the bridge (*bus*, *tacsaidh*). Coinneach mac Ùisdein sells his house's last share in the Sloc
Mòr and buys dubhan hulls at Ros dhomhain. The patrol boats, refitted, outrun the raiders; the crews are tried at the
court of the sea, Tuathaich sent home. Then in an Lùnastal DE 14 the Fuel-Fitters are chartered at the southern Seann
Skell, rolls open to Dia-thìrich only. Peigi, a master of the new guild, lets her Tuathaich fitters go; she pays Joe a
month over and cannot look at him. Joe goes home to Caol mhòr and fits boats without a guild. In an t-Samhain the Moot
votes to take up the new fuel, and the western pit towns' delegates walk out; Ruth Calder is among them.
*Era:* Peigi nic Ascaill sworn among the first masters of the Fuel-Fitters (DE 14); Joe Ashby's first engine-boat at Caol
mhòr (DE 14); Ruth Calder's first speech in the Moot (DE 14); the house of Mac Ùisdein's first dubhan hull launched
(DE 13).

**15. Of the Fifteenth Year** · POV Beathag · DE 15 · VI-0048 – VI-0051 · 4,500 w
Depots open at Caol mhòr, Doire ghlas and Ceann mhòr; Martha writes that the north now buys its fire from the south. On
5 an Cèitean the council pegs the coin to the making of dubhan and proclaims the Dubhan Era, counted from the first
working fuel. Beathag re-heads fourteen years of her chronicle by lamplight, and writes the line about moving a year
she was living in. Gille-Easbaig Bàn cuts the plain seal; Fearchar Bonn's first dies go to the Library beside the
Hewers' oath-stone. The high custodian protests; the council answers that the kingdom never said it held the vein
sacred; Beathag copies both letters. The turn: the kingdom says plainly what it is, once.
*Era:* the council's proclamation read in the market towns (DE 15); Beathag's re-headed pages sent to the copyists (DE
15); the last coin struck with coal ink kept back by the mint (DE 15).


### Part Three — *Of the Holdouts of the Western Hills* · DE 16 – 20
Epigraph: *B' fheàrr leinn a bhith fuar còmhla ri ar nàbaidhean na blàth leinn fhèin.* "We would sooner be cold with our
neighbours than warm alone." — the delegates of Àth naomh, DE 16.

**16. Of the Meeting at Muileann àrsaidh** · POV Ruth Calder · DE 16 · VI-0052 – VI-0056 · 4,800 w
In am Màrt the western pit towns meet at Muileann àrsaidh, in Sarah Dunmore's kitchen and then the pit-shed, apart from
the Moot. Ruth, a pit captain's daughter of Àth leathan, drafts the agreement: work the seams, sell to any northern
household that will buy. Àth naomh joins with the line about being cold with its neighbours. By an Cèitean the five

## 4. Continuity

**Opening state** (where the Sixth Book closes, 18 an t-Ògmhios SE 71 → DE 1): a night near midsummer; Dubhan at his
fire in the north, ten years at his work, unknown to the capital and the Library. Mairead reigns. Coal-fuel at the fair
has doubled and the court cannot hold its ceiling; Board watchmen at Cnoc fhiadhaich see nothing; the Guild of Hewers
binds no apprentices; the lowest galleries of the Sloc Mòr are flooded ("a pause"); the Board has closed the branch to
Àth leathan and takes the western pits' fifth as arrears under the Leòid ruling; the census is published; the Library has
doubled its holdings and its fourth floor stands empty for what was not sent. Beathag nic Fhionnlaigh is chronicler,
Raghnall mac Mhuirich keeper, Daniel Frayne speaker of the Moot; the Severance companies are names on a list in the
council's chests.

**Closing state** (the present, 21 an Dùbhlachd DE 27; no later age exists to open): as VI-0157 and the specs snapshot.
Cathal reigns; council of twelve with Somhairle seated; nine regiments and four fleets on their stations paid from the
tithe; western seams capped; passes on the northern road; coin pegged to dubhan; Treasury roll 9,568 purses; the charter
of export, the first tank-hull gone north-about; the Moot under Peter Hale; the war sealed for fifty years under the
second regiment's guard; Ellen's books kept in the north; the Rite's twelve hewers and four baskets; *seann-chunntas*.
