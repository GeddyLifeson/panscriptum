# Age II, An Aois Ailein (Linn na Doire, GE 1 – 4,603): volume plan

Sources used: legendarium/book/age_I.md (close), age_II.md, age_III.md (opening §I–IV), eras/age_II/annals/age_II_master.json
(186 events), eras/specs_draft/age_II.json, appendices A, B, C, E. Companion files: `PLAN_people.json` (cast),
`PLAN_names.json` (new proper nouns, all tested with normalize() and check_agreement()).

## 1. Title, epigraph, premise, question

**An Làmh nach do Dh'fhàg an Teine**, *The Hand That Did Not Leave the Fire*.

Epigraph (for front.json):
> *Bidh e leatsa, agus cha bhi e aig duine às do dhèidh.*
> It will be yours, and no one's after you.
> — the Keeper to Ailean at the vigil, as Clann na Ceiste keep it

**Premise.** A lad of Clann na Ceiste keeps seven nights by seven fires, is crowned by the Young God, and walks south to take a
hill. He never grows old, never tells what went into him, and never gives the kingship out of his hand. For four thousand six
hundred years the island grows up round him: his mortal daughter goes grey at the gate while he stays young; the Old Ones count
their dead and fall silent; the hill-folk make, without any law, the handful, the lot, the cup, the sharing and the saying; his
children live thousands of years and their children less. He agrees with customs and does not make laws; he gives shares and
takes them back; after the last Stone King he names no one. His kin learn to wait. When the east comes down to the ford below
Dùn dhearg, he goes to meet it alone, and the kin stay by their fires counting shares.

**The question the volume answers:** why did the kin not come to the ford? (And beneath it: how did the island's customs grow
up with no law, under a king who would not make one?)

## 2. Point of view and voice

- The high recollection voice of the master Books, with close past-tense POV scenes; about half and half. Each chapter opens
  with one italic line placing the telling ("*At the salt-pans of Baile ghorm the rakers tell this.*"). Speech is welcome.
- **The king is never a POV.** We see Ailean only through others: a warmth like an opened kiln, spears that will not be
  thrown, water warm round him, a low dark shining light in fire, a stone greyed under his hand. He never explains the power.
  His speech is plain, short and dry.
- **A relay of witnesses**, one central figure a chapter. The long-lived give the spine across millennia: **Somhairle** (the
  son who keeps the lines, GE 38–3,025), **Beathan** his son, **Murchadh Mòr** (born GE 1,705), later **Eithne, Ìomhar Dearg,
  Niall Òg, Gille-Brìde**. Mortal witnesses carry single chapters. Principal figures: Ailean Mòr (seen, never inside),
  Somhairle, Gormshuil, Beathan, Oighrig nic Dhòmhnaill, Murchadh Mòr, Catrìona Mhòr, Niall Òg.
- Motifs to carry: the warmth; the "two names" of the blood's line (the second always Ailean's); wives buried "one in every
  century"; the gaps in the lists; hands (the cupped hand, the hand on the hearthstone, hands between the king's hands).

## 3. Structure: 6 Parts, 12 Books, 40 chapters

Each chapter is its own file `book/NN_slug.md` (NN = chapter number, 01–40) and prints as one Book; the chapter that opens a Part carries the Part heading (Parts: ch 1, 8, 15, 22, 29, 35 open Parts One–Six). The "Book 1 … Book 12" groupings below are the original writer blocks, kept only as id-range and hand-off groupings for compiling. Each file: `# Chapter title`, italic date line, optional Ròdais epigraph, `##` sections. Words are targets (4,000–6,000).

Legend: **POV** · **GE span** · **masters** · synopsis · *new era events (proposed GE year)*.

### Part One: The Grove and the Hill (Books 1–2, GE 1 – 590)

**Book 1 (W01): "Of the Seven Nights and the Taking of the Hill", GE 1 – 106**

**1. Of the Seven Nights** (5,000) · Coinneach mac Shuibhne, Ailean's younger brother · GE 1 · I-0080b.
Opens exactly where Book I closes: dusk, the seven fires lit, the Keeper standing in the ring. Coinneach has followed Ailean north
with food his brother will not eat and camps outside the oaks, forbidden to come nearer. The vigil is told as the Line of the
Mason keeps it (the Keeper's words, the promise "when strangers hold this island", verbatim from the master Book), cut against
Coinneach's nights outside: colours through the trunks, hunger, the fifth night's voices he half hears. On the eighth night the
fires go out and the grove fills with light; Coinneach sees shapes moving between the trunks and his feet will not carry him in.
The turn: at morning Ailean walks out and Coinneach, taking his arm, finds it warm as a hearthstone. "They named me king." No
more. Ends with the brothers turning south, Ailean not stopping at the high valleys, gathering kin as he goes.
*Coinneach finds his brother warm at dawn (1); the six pots left cold under the oaks (1); Ailean passes the high valleys and
eleven of his kin walk south with him (1).*

**2. Of the Knapper's Son** (4,500) · the knapper's son (13, unnamed, as in the master) · GE 1 – 2 · I-0081.
The flint-knapper's pit, the lads on the lip of the hill, the twelve walking up the valley road. The taking as the master tells
it (spears that will not be thrown, the elder sitting down, the water, "Take it back to him… I shall want a great many more").
Then what the master only names: the valley households coming in one by one; the first judgement (two households and a strip of
grazing: the king rules in a sentence and everyone is afraid and a little glad); the first winter, the girl who stood warm by
him. The lad becomes the king's spear-maker. Ends with the ring of the first bank pegged out in the snow and Muireall and the
children brought down from the high valleys.
*The valleys come in (1); the first judgement on the hill (1); Muireall and Gormshuil brought to the hill (2); Dòmhnall born, the
first child born after the grove (2).*

**3. Of Gormshuil at the Gate** (5,000) · Gormshuil nic Ailein · GE 2 – 106 · I-0082, I-0083, I-0084.
Gormshuil at 8 watches the bank dug (GE 9); word comes from the west of Beathag Ruadh holding a red hill on the lake shore
(18). Her mother Muireall dies (24); her father marries Sorcha and rides beyond the valleys each season. At 33 she holds the hill
for him; the hard winter and the herds driven to the warm springs, none lost (27). Coinneach, grey, teaches her the Line of the
Mason and dies (55). She greys; her father comes in at the gate each autumn a young man; her grandchildren take him for her son.
Turn: on her deathbed she asks him whether it hurts to stay young; he does not answer. Ends with the king sitting a night and a
day by her, never speaking of her after, and the kin understanding that the long years came from the grove; Dòmhnall, 74 and
looking twenty, takes the hill.
*First riding season beyond the valleys (12); Muireall dies (24); the king weds Sorcha (30); Somhairle born, in his father's
62nd year (38); Iain Dubh born (45); Coinneach dies, first teller of the king's deeds (55); Gormshuil dies (76); the kin learn the
long years are the grove's (76); Dòmhnall Clachach holds the hill (76).*

**Book 2 (W02): "Of Stones, Palisades and Strokes", GE 107 – 590**

**4. Of the Stones in the Valley and the Chair of Birch** (4,000) · Dòmhnall Clachach · GE 107 – 227 · I-0085, I-0086, I-0087, I-0088.
Dòmhnall sets the boundary stones with his own hands to stop the grazing quarrels (107) and learns they only move the quarrels to
the stones. Cogaidhean nan Gleann: the king brings in valleys beyond, riding with few; Fearchar lamed in a fight the king was not
at (189). The first named storm (147) wrecks the hill; traders bring word the Old Ones' shell causeway at Seann Warr is broken
and not mended. Somhairle, a boy of 160 who looks 15, asks to live among Clann na Ceiste and takes no share. Turn: Dòmhnall asks
for more than the hill; the king sends him north to hold what has been won there. Ends GE 227: Dòmhnall riding north, Fearchar
carried to the gathering in the birch chair.
*A grazing quarrel judged by the stones (115); the storm on the hill (147); a valley household stands against the king (160);
Fearchar lamed (189); Somhairle goes to the high valleys (200); Dòmhnall sent north (227).*

**5. Of the Palisade that Burned Twice** (4,500) · Uilleam Faire, watchman of the Red Hill · GE 227 – 312 · I-0089, I-0089a.
The Red Hill: an old line of the lake shore, not of the king's blood. Uisdean ditches the neck and sets the palisade (267). The
king camps under the hill at the end of a summer (289) with two hundred spears and sends no word; the palisade burns with a low
dark light; Uisdean rebuilds with green oak through autumn and winter while the king waits. **Reconcile the dates:** the second
burning comes on the unseasonable frost of early an t-Ògmhios that the lake-shore still calls the king's frost, and on the
morning after (7 an t-Ògmhios, GE 290) Uisdean comes down. The shingle scene verbatim in substance. Ends with the Red Hill
sworn to the king and not to Dùn ìseal, and Uilleam, who threw the water, never sure it was the king.
*Uisdean holds the Red Hill (250); the king camps on the lake shore (289); the first burning (289); the king's frost and the
second burning (290); the Red Hill swears to the king, not to the hill (290); Uisdean dies (312).*

**6. Of the Strokes on the Midden Stones** (3,500) · Dùghall Ceannaiche, hill-folk carrier · GE 290 – 388 · I-0090, I-0091.
Dùghall carries hides and hill cheese to the Old Ones' coasts and trades by gesture; he never learns a word of their tongue. He
sees stones set at the midden edges with single strokes (308), then one cut the morning after a burial, and understands they are
counting their dead (348). Keep the Old Ones opaque: no POV inside them, no cause of their dying. Ends with Dùghall, old, taking
his grandson to the coast and finding fewer hearths lit.
*Hill carriers trade at the Old Ones' coasts (300); a stroke cut after a burial (340); Dùghall dies (352).*

**7. Of the Tally Wall and the Half-Cut Stone** (4,500) · Somhairle (351–513 years) · GE 389 – 590 · I-0092 – I-0096.
Somhairle goes east with his father to Seann Warr and sees the tally wall (389); Seann Tarr's house over its own dead (428); the
strokes thicken on three coasts (470), and the king stands a long while before the wall and will not speak of it. "What did you
see?" "A people counting." At Seann Chwen Somhairle finds the half-cut stone, the chisel marks stopping mid-groove (510). Silence
(551). Ends with the coasts empty of smoke, and Somhairle adding nothing to the Line but the fact.
*Somhairle at Seann Warr (389); the king before the tally wall (470); Somhairle finds the half-cut stone (512); the last smoke
seen at Seann Tarr (530); the hill-folk find the Old Ones' coasts empty (560); Somhairle weds Cairistìona (580).*

### Part Two: Salt and the Handful (Books 3–4, GE 591 – 1,273)

**Book 3 (W03): "Of the Old Names and the Salt Shore", GE 591 – 901**

**8. Of the Old Names** (4,000) · Marsaili nic Iain, settler · GE 591 – 671 · I-0097, I-0098.
Hill-folk put "Seann" before the Old Ones' names and change nothing else (591). Marsaili's household comes down the Abhainn naomh
to the empty river harbour at Seann Skell (628) and uses the old mole without mending it; they will not enter the tower. The turn:
a winter storm, the mole cracked, and still they will not touch the Old Ones' stones. Ends with the first boats of the hill-folk
put out from a harbour they did not build. Beathan's birth (Somhairle's son) comes as tidings from the high valleys.
*Beathan born (591); the hill-folk first say "Seann Skell" (600); first hill-folk boats at Seann Skell (640); the first
hill-folk burial by the sea (660).*

**9. Of Mòrag the Hard** (4,500) · Mòrag Chruaidh (~380), holder of the hill · GE 672 – 719 · I-0099 – I-0103.
The hill has no salt and the south does not own the king. Mòrag raids the salt-scrapes of Baile ghorm and carries the salt off;
seven die on her side and she keeps their names (672). **Reconcile:** the raid takes the old boiling-hearths; the clay basins
behind the shingle bar are the salt-folk's answer (683). The king neither blames nor praises: "The south is not mine yet." He takes
her west, where Seonag nic Uisdein (whom the Red Hill list sets "after her father", as it sets every holder of his line) weighs
her pebble bag before him (694). Pots at Doire shean (705), the oak dug-out at Cidhe Beag (716). Ends with Iain Dubh, her uncle
who looks younger than she does, asking how she did it.
*The seven dead of the raid named at the hill (672); the salt-folk raise a bank behind the pans (683); Mòrag at the Red Hill with
the king (694); Iain Dubh holds the hill (719).*

**10. Of the Shingle at Baile ghorm** (5,000) · Màiri nan Làn, salt-raker · GE 727 – 728 · I-0104, I-0104b.
Iain Dubh comes down to raid; Tadhg Garbh's spear kills him on the shingle, and he is carried home (727). A year later the king
walks alone along the dunes; the master scene in full from Màiri's eyes (the man who cannot throw, the double handful of salt,
"mercy or something worse"). Tormod, Iain's son, at the cairn. Ends with Màiri raking again and the salt shore the king's.
*Iain Dubh's cairn raised at the hill (727); the king comes home from the north (727); the salt shore sworn (728); Tadhg Garbh
rows out alone and does not return (729).*

**11. Of the Sand on the Hearths and the Opened Ground** (4,000) · Seasaidh · GE 770 – 901 · I-0105, I-0106, I-0107.
Seasaidh at seven survives the wave at Cnoc chaol, sand a hand deep on the hearths (777). Married inland, at fifty-seven she sees
the ground open across the valley and the spring move downhill (827). The king comes, looks, forbids nothing. In old age she lays
the white stones (847). Ends with no house within a stone's throw of the crack.
*The survivors build higher on the slope (778); the king at the crack (830); Seasaidh dies (852).*

**Book 4 (W04): "Of the Handful and the Ford", GE 902 – 1,273**

**12. Of the Unlit Hearth and the Basket** (4,500) · Anna nic Coinnich of Achadh dhìreach · GE 902 – 1,070 · I-0108 – I-0111.
Opens as a hearth-telling: the unlit hearth, laid and left (902), peat over it (956); the handful takes hold nowhere written (1,011).
Anna's neighbours burn a whole basket (1,066); three die, the hearthstone runs with colour. The next day the king stands by it
and lays his hand on it; the colour goes out; he says the handful is a good custom (the only law of the age, made by agreeing).
Ends with Anna cupping her hand for her grandchildren: *Làn na glaice, 's gun tuilleadh.*
*The king at Achadh dhìreach (1,066); the cracked hearthstone set in a wall (1,067).*

**13. Of the Watchers of the Slab** (4,500) · Beathan (529) · GE 1,071 – 1,174 · I-0112.
Clann na Ceiste in the high valleys: Somhairle keeping the lines, the slab with its stain. Families near the crack begin to sit
by it a household at a time from Samhain (1,120); Beathan sits with herdsmen who do not know what he is. A herdsman's wife grows
old across his winters. Turn: a herdsman asks him what they are watching for; he does not know either, and says so. Ends with the
king coming up once and sitting a night by the slab among them, saying nothing.
*Beathan first sits by the slab (1,121); Somhairle gives Beathan the Line whole (1,140); the king sits a night at the slab (1,160).*

**14. Of Salt for Wool** (4,500) · Raghnall Ceannfhionn (~415), grandson of the king · GE 1,174 – 1,273 · I-0113 – I-0115.
Raghnall asks the king to settle salt with the Red Hill; "They are both mine. I will not do their talking." The wading scene at the
ford (1,175). The meeting becomes a yearly gathering; first marriage agreed there (1,208). Murchadh Gearr walks the salt road to be
seen (1,247). Ends with the trodden ground, and Raghnall realising the gathering will outlast everyone on it but him and the king.
*Raghnall asks the king (1,174); first marriage made at the ford between hill and Red Hill (1,190); Murchadh Gearr holds the
Red Hill (1,240).*

### Part Three: The Far Shores and the Name (Books 5–6, GE 1,274 – 2,327)

**Book 5 (W05): "Of the Far Shores and the Grey Winter", GE 1,274 – 1,746**

**16. Of the Rope's End** (4,000) · Iseabail, sister of Eachann Ròpa · GE 1,441 – 1,540 · I-0120 – I-0122.

*No one goes down Toll-dubh again (1,453); Eachann dies untold (1,499).*

**17. Of Beitris and the Grey Winter** (5,000) · Beitris, weaver of Muileann leathan, the king's wife · GE 1,541 – 1,746 · I-0123 – I-0127.

*The king weds Beitris (1,690); the grey winter (1,700); Murchadh Mòr born (1,705); Beitris dies (1,744).*
