# Age I volume plan: An Teine sa Chloich, *The Fire in the Stone*

The era legendarium of An Aois Àrsaidh, the Ancient Age (Linn na Fèithe, VE 1 – 5,324).
Architect's plan: instructions to the chapter writers, not prose.

## 1. Title, epigraph, premise, question

- **Title:** *An Teine sa Chloich*, "The Fire in the Stone" (from the words on Carragh Cnoc bheag, I-0069/I-0070).
- **Volume epigraph** (front.json): `> *Cuimhnich an teine.*` / `> Remember the fire.` / `> — cut on Colbh Cnoc chiar`
- **Premise.** A mason splitting rock for a well breaks into a vein of coloured coal, and the lightning lights it. For
  five thousand three hundred years after, the island has no king, no letters, no war and no count of years but the
  count of fathers. The mason's people cover the vein with a slab, carry its fire to every hearth, and keep one
  question alive: why is there colour in the coal? A spirit of smoke answers with the Telling of the Making and a
  riddle, and the Mason's line spends forty lifetimes finding, one coal at a time, that each colour wakes only in its
  own element: gold on the sea, white in the air, black in the ground, red-brown in blood, silver in the river, fire in
  the snap of a hand. Along the coasts another people, the Old Ones, build moles, steps, kilns and a script no one
  reads, go up into the hills, build forts that face inland, and come down again to the shore. In the hills the
  hearths learn fear (the Sea-Watcher, the Hollow Man), leave woods uncut, raise stones and say they were always
  there. The age ends with a young man of the line sitting among seven burning coals in an oak grove.
- **The question the volume answers:** why did it take five thousand years to light seven coals, and what kind of
  people did the asking make?

## 2. Point of view and voice

- The high recollection voice of the master Books (legendarium/book/age_I.md is the model), told as the island's own
  tellings: the hearth of the Mason's line on the night of the lightning, the tellers of the shore and the hills.
  Roughly half the text close POV scenes (sight, cold, smell, speech), half recollection. Dialogue in plain English;
  Ròdais lines only where the plan or NAMES gives them or where you have checked them with the engine.
- **A relay of witnesses.** No one lives long in this age. Each chapter is centred on one or two people (section 5),
  and each Part on a person of the Mason's line. Recurring through-lines carry the reader across the gaps:
  1. **The Mason's line** (Clann a' Chlachair; from ch 32 Clann na Ceiste): the named fathers of the Line, and the
     women of the line who keep *Poit an Teine*, the clay fire-pot said to hold the mason's first fire, and the
     leather bag of coloured pieces broken from the vein before it was covered.
  2. **The Old Ones** (na Seann-Dhaoine): a relay of **unnamed** people of the shore, always told close and
     wordless-ish (their speech, if any, rendered sparingly in English; no Old Ones' word or name is ever given).
  3. **The other hearths of the hill-folk:** the north shore (seals, boats, the Watcher), the Doire uaine hills (the
     Hollow Man, the covered hearth), the east (deer, dogs, the stag, the uncut woods), the south shore, the lake shore.
- **Hard rules of the age** (breaking any contradicts canon):
  - The Old Ones and the hill-folk **never meet on the page**. Whether they shared the island "no telling knows"
    (I-0065). Things may pass between them (the chip at Bral, I-0068: "by gift, by trade or by theft"; the two hands
    on Carragh, I-0070), unexplained. Never say where the Old Ones came from, how they got fire, what their shafts
    took, why the outpost faces inland, who walled the gate or fouled the cistern, or where the hulls went.
    **Show the deed; withhold the one fact the canon withholds.** Old Ones' POVs never see colour in coal.
  - No king, ruler, list, war, battle, earthwork or defended place of the hill-folk (the first are in Age II). No
    named storm (the first is I-0086). No tally or counting marks by anyone before I-0011's net-weights, and no tally
    stones at all (Age II). The hill-folk cut no script: **never show anyone cutting or reading letters**; the words
    on the stones are known by being said at them.
  - Before I-0066 (VE 4,475) **no household outside the Mason's line burns coloured coal**; hearths burn wood and
    peat, lit from the mason's fire. No house is built beside the slab (Age II).
  - Firsts are fixed: first crossing of open water on purpose I-0008 (551); first buildings for a trade I-0018; first
    dog graves I-0054; first telling of the white stag I-0050; sick first brought for healing I-0057; first loss
    laid at the Watcher by name I-0042; panther roads first marked I-0078. Nothing like them earlier.
  - The white stag has **no god** in this age (I-0050); the Keeper named Flidais in the Telling, but no one ties them.
  - Only this age and earlier: no later names, eras, dates, towns, titles; no "Dia-thìrich" (the people are the
    hill-folk, and from ch 31 Clann nan Dè / the godkin); no Library, reckoners, scholars, Church, humans, an Tìr
    Thall, Stone Kings, crowning, long years or the Keeper's promise. The narrator may call the island Dia-thìr;
    characters say "the island". No "Ailean Mòr" (he is Ailean son of Suibhne).
- **Continuity key on the Seven Coals.** The Keeper told Diarmad the whole Telling (legendarium/book/creation.md),
  including which colour holds which element. What no one understood was how each is *woken*: the line tried the
  coals in hearths, in sea-water carried home in skins, in rain, all seven together. Aodh was the first to take them
  to the sea itself, and threw all seven to see which the sea would take. Each later lighting is the same kind of
  step: taking the coal to the element's own body. Lit coals burn out; the line relights from the bag of pieces, and
  from ch 12 on takes new pieces from where the vein shows in a stream bank above Tobar dhìreach, a handful a life.
- **Place names** (future_check flags names of towns founded later): use only
  - Old Ones' landings by their **bare root** in this age (Skell, Dunn, Cwen, Bral, Morn, Brenn, Toll, Vell, Warr,
    Tarr; spec era names; the hill-folk do not yet say *Seann*); markers and landmarks as NAMES.json gives them
    (Làrach an Dùin-fhaire, Toll-dubh, Carragh Cnoc bheag, Colbh Cnoc chiar, Calbh Cnoc ghorm, Tuam Cnoc dhìreach,
    Coille Naomh Cnoc bheag, Imrich nam Pantar, Binnean a' Chlachair from ch 16, Fuarain Theth Muileann ruadh,
    Slighe-mhara àrsaidh, Uilebheist na Mara, Marbh-bheò); islands, rivers and Loch chrom by map name;
  - hill-folk places founded in Age I: Tobar dhìreach, Achadh dhìreach, Àth gharbh, Cnoc fhionn, Ros ruadh;
  - Doire ghlas (the grove's own name) and "the Doire uaine hills" (the hills' own name): allowed, as in the master;
  - era names from PLAN_names.json for the later towns' sites: Na Teallaichean Falaichte (Doire uaine hamlet),
    Achadh a' Chalbh (the column's gathering-ground), Bruthach nan Teaghlach (the hill households in the south-east
    valleys), Bothan a' Chaolais (the strait camp), Creag Ruadh (the lake-shore hill);
  - **describe everything else**: "the black well" and "the young well" (not Tobar dhubh/òg), "the northern shore",
    "the mud below the north-western point", "the warning fire on the north-western headland", "the holy wood of the
    east" (the stag forest), "the spur above the southern lowland" (outpost). Never "where X now stands".
  - Never name the mist (no Ceò Mhanannain, no "Manannan's mist"); describe "the grey on the northern and eastern sea
    that never lifts". For the west use Tìr nan Òg, Magh Meall, Taigh Dhuinn (Telling names).
- **Geography** (0.14 mi/unit; measure before writing): Tobar dhìreach lies on the north side of the central massif.
  Achadh dhìreach ~10 mi W; Àth gharbh ~4 mi; the northern shore ~16 mi N (a day); Binnean a' Chlachair ~10 mi SSE;
  the Doire uaine hills ~12 mi S; the warm springs ~14 mi S; the Dùn ìseal valleys ~18 mi SE; Làrach an Teampaill ~23 mi
  ESE; Colbh Cnoc chiar ~23 mi WNW; the column (Calbh Cnoc ghorm) ~29 mi SW; the holy wood of the east ~26 mi E;
  Tuam Cnoc dhìreach ~38 mi S on the south coast; Carragh Cnoc bheag ~43 mi W; Doire ghlas ~51 mi NW (three days on
  foot); Loch chrom and the lake shore ~50 mi WSW; Skell ~70 mi W; the north-western horn and the Watcher's water ~90
  mi WNW. Eilean dhubh and Eilean fhada lie off the north coast, Eilean mhin off the south, Eilean dhearg off the
  south-east, Eilean ghorm off the west. Mild, wet, windy; snow on the tops in winter; the dry north prizes springs.
- **People numbers:** a few thousand at VE 1, some ten thousand by VE 2,500, some 18,000 at the close (of whom the
  Old Ones perhaps a third). Skell, the largest place, ~300; Tobar dhìreach ~90; most hearth-kindreds 20–80. A crowd
  at the column is "some hundreds".

## 3. Structure

Six Parts, 38 chapters, one chapter per file `book/NN_slug.md` (NN = chapter number, two digits). Each file:
`# <chapter title>`, the date line `*VE a – b*`, an epigraph only where given, then `## I. Of …` sections (2–5).
A chapter that opens a Part puts its Part epigraph under the date line and, after it, the line
`*The <First…Sixth> Part: <Part title>*`. Target words in brackets. Chapters move strictly forward in time.
"Events" = new era events for the chapter's events sidecar (propose more as the story needs; year = era year).

### The First Part: Of the Mason's Fire, and the Covering of the Vein (VE 1 – 820)
Part epigraph (ch 1): `> *Bha teine sa chloich.*` / `> There was fire in the stone.`

**1. Of the Mason and the Lightning** · Neachdan · VE 1 (20 am Faoilleach) · I-0001 · [5,200]
The world before fire: households of the high valleys eat cold, sleep in the lee of rock, and winter is the one
enemy. The spring of Neachdan's valley has failed in a dry year, and he, the best splitter of stone, is sinking a well
with wedge and oak maul. Sleet, split knuckles. The seam, the black break, the colours; he fills his tunic because they
are beautiful. Storm at dusk; lightning into the well; the vein glows and breathes; he weeps with warmed hands. He
carries a burning piece home on a flat stone under his cloak. Sorcha, the boy Conall (six) and the little ones: the
first hearth. He feeds it the coloured pieces; all burn dark and alike, the colour goes out of them; sorry a little,
glad all the same. Turn: the thing eats and can die. They learn it takes heather and wood; Sorcha burns her hand and
learns its bite. Ends at dawn, the household red-faced round it, Neachdan saying it must not go out.
Events: the first hearth (1, same day); Sorcha burned (1); Neachdan finds the vein still burning under the rain (1).

**2. Of the Fire Given Away** · Conall, Ailbhe · VE 1 – 71 · (no master) · [5,000]
The vein burns on in the well-cut for years, low and dark in the rock. The hearth goes out one night; Conall runs to
the vein with a clay pot and relights it: fire can be carried. Neighbours beg fire and Neachdan gives it freely.
Ailbhe, born by the hearth (the first child born in firelight), grows up to be the fire-bearer, walking the fire in
pots to hearths as far as the northern moor. A hut burns (the first fire loss); Conall's rule: fire on a stone, never
on the heather. Neachdan dies by the hearth (38). Conall sits by the burning vein through the dark half of every
year, keeping children off it. Ends with Conall old, the vein still burning, the watch passed to his son.
Events: Ailbhe born (3); fire first carried in a pot (5); the first hut burned (12); fire reaches the northern moor
(25); Neachdan dies (38); Conall's rule of the stone (40); Conall dies (71).

**3. Of the Three Wells and the Slab** · Tadhg, Muirne · VE 72 – 226 · I-0002, I-0003 · [5,000]
The fame of the fire brings claims: the households of the black well and the young well each say the mason's well was
theirs (I-0002; Tadhg of the black well, big-voiced, carries off a scorched stone as proof). The mason's household will
not argue. Muirne, of Conall's line, grows up the keeper of the vein-fire and watches it sink year by year. In a wet
spring the last ember dies (151). Some want to dig for more burning stone; Muirne says the fire came out of the sky and
they will not dig for it. They lay a flat stone over the broken vein (Leac na Fèithe), draw no water beside it, cut
the well again a stone's throw off (I-0003). Turn: they find a wonder and cover it. Ends with Muirne's palm on the cold
slab, and the new well giving water.
Events: Tadhg's scorched stone (80); the young well's telling (95); the vein-fire burns low (130); the last ember (151,
eve); the new well cut (152); Muirne dies (181).

**4. Of the Snow on the Mountains and the Oak in the North** · Conn, Eachna, Luag · VE 227 – 505 · I-0004, I-0005,
I-0006, I-0006a · [5,200]
A triptych across two centuries, recollection linking three close scenes. Conn the herder follows the summer grazing up
the Abhainn uaine as the snowfields shrink; the meltwater cuts the bed; his hearth-stones on the gravel terraces are
left at three heights as the water rises (I-0004). Eachna lays the first hearths at Achadh dhìreach with fire from the
Mason's line: pine and heather charcoal, grey flint, red deer; the line gives fire and never coloured coal (I-0005).
The oak and hazel come in over the northern heath as the birch goes (I-0006), told as grandmothers' memory. Luag walks
to the north-western horn and finds no tree and no hearth; his fire will not live in that wind; he looks west at the
open sea where the weather comes from (I-0006a). Turn: the island is found to have edges. Ends on Luag's return.
Events: Conn's hearths on the terraces (230); the flood that drove them up (240); night hunting of deer by fire (320);
the first autumn store of roasted hazel-nuts (390); Luag's return (452).

**5. Of the Drowned Neck and the First Boats** · Gràinne, Oisean, Scàire · VE 506 – 750 · I-0007, I-0008, I-0008a,
I-0009 · [5,000]
Gràinne's hearth on the low neck to Eilean dhubh: each spring tide comes further; she will not leave till the sea is in
the hearth; the deer are cut off (I-0007). Oisean of the south shore stitches a hide boat and paddles to Eilean mhin to
take seals, the first crossing of open water on purpose; camps a season at a time (I-0008). Scàire of the north shore
goes out in the autumn gales to the skerries off Eilean fhada; a hard year is counted by the boats the gales kept
ashore; from the skerries she sees the grey on the northern sea that never lifts (I-0008a; the-mist). Hazel weirs on
the Abhainn ìseal's side channels, stakes renewed in the same holes (I-0009). Turn: the hill-folk take to the water.
Ends with great-grandchildren driving stakes into the old holes.
Events: Gràinne leaves the neck (506, same day); the deer of Eilean dhubh smaller (560); Scàire sees the grey bank
(620); the hard year of the kept boats (628); the salmon run first feasted at the weirs (700).

**6. Of the Shell Mound and the Three Strokes** · the Old Ones (unnamed) · VE 751 – 820 · I-0010, I-0011 · [4,600]
The first chapter of the shore, in its own mode. A woman of the Old Ones at Dunn on the east coast: limpets, seal bone,
burnt weed on the heap (they have fire; no one says whence); the mound laid down over lifetimes until it stands taller
than a man; their tools shaped otherwise than the hill-folk's (I-0010). At Cwen a net-maker notches every sinker with
the same three strokes, and her daughter learns them: the first mark made again and again on purpose (I-0011); show the
hand, never the meaning. Turn: a people who leave marks. Ends with the child's first three strokes.
Events: the Dunn mound stands a man's height (790); the Cwen nets first set across the bay (805).

### The Second Part: Of the Question in the Grove, and the Gold on the Sea (VE 821 – 1,442)
Part epigraph (ch 7): the Keeper's riddle, as in creation.md.

**7. Of the Boy Who Asked** · Diarmad, Fionnghuala · VE 821 – 889 · (no master) · [5,000]
Tobar dhìreach, the line's hearth; Fionnghuala keeps Poit an Teine; the leather bag of coloured pieces the children play
with. Diarmad at seven asks why the stones have colours; everyone laughs. He asks the old, the fire-bearers,
travellers; he holds the rainbow piece in sun and rain. "Diarmad's question" becomes the valley's jest; his mother
defends him. A herder of the north moor speaks of an oak grove where the wind talks. Turn: at twenty-seven he takes the
rainbow piece and walks three days north-west. Ends at the grove's edge in a gale.
Events: Diarmad born (862); the jest of Diarmad's question (875); Fionnghuala takes the fire-pot (880); Diarmad sets out (889).

**8. Of the Keeper in the Grove** · Diarmad · VE 890 (17 an Gearran) · I-0011a · [5,400]
The master scene (keep the master's lines verbatim): the oak-twig fire in the lee of a fallen trunk, the smoke figure
still in the gale, "What are you?", Coimhdeach na Fine. The Telling: do **not** retell creation.md; give Diarmad's
night of hearing it in fragments and sensations (sparks become stars, the island born of drops of fire, grass-people,
his shame at the forgotten coal, Caoran's laughter). Then the riddle and "That is no answer." It goes. He stays two days
trying the riddle alone and fails. Turn: he has the story and a riddle, not an answer. Ends walking home saying the
words over so as not to lose them. (Doire ghlas is uninhabited oak grove.)
Events: Diarmad's two days in the grove (890); his return to Tobar dhìreach (890).

**9. Of the Counting of the Fathers** · Diarmad, then Deirbhile · VE 890 – 950 · (no master) · [5,000]
Diarmad says the Telling at the hearth; mockery turns to silence; his wife Niamh will not believe at first. He goes
hearth to hearth among the kin gathering the fathers' names back to Neachdan and Conall, and fixes the recital of the
Line, said standing on Oidhche an Dealanaich, the night of the lightning (Sloinneadh a' Chlachair). The Telling goes
from the line to other hearths. The line tries the riddle and fails (coals in rain, in wind, all seven in one fire, in
sea-water carried home in skins). Diarmad dies (931) sure the answer is near. His daughter Deirbhile keeps the Telling
word for word and teaches it. Ends with the Telling said at an eastern hearth by someone three mouths from Deirbhile.
Events: the first recital of the Line (895); Niamh believes (900); the rain and sea-water trials (905); the Telling
reaches the eastern hearths (920); Diarmad dies (931); Deirbhile teaches the Telling (940).

**10. Of the Mole and the Steps** · the Old Ones; one hearth interlude · VE 951 – 1,169 · I-0012, I-0013, I-0014 · [5,000]
Opens on the mole: at Skell on the Abhainn naomh boulders rolled on logs out into the current, an old man judging the
lie of each stone; a dozen boats in its lee (I-0012). Bral on the far west: 212 steps cut from shingle to grazing over
lifetimes; a child runs up them first (I-0013). Interlude at Tobar dhìreach: the recital kept, the riddle idle. Vell
of the south-east: the dead rowed to Eilean dhearg and laid in stone boxes facing the mainland, looking back at the
living (I-0014). Turn: two ways of keeping the dead. Ends with the fortieth box.
Events: the mole's first winter (960); the steps begun (990); a boat lost in the river flood at Skell (1050); the line's
recital kept without asking (1120).

**11. Of the Road on the Marsh and the Signs on the Cliff** · the Old Ones · VE 1,170 – 1,330 · I-0015, I-0016, I-0017 · [4,600]
Toll by the Abhainn uaine: a channel through a rock shelf, a slot for a wattle gate, a pool emptied by hand when the
river falls (I-0015). Warr on the west shore of Loch chrom: a road of crushed shell over a mile of reed marsh, a forearm
high; a girl walks it first (I-0016). Tarr: rows of signs cut into the cliff, the first script; the cutter's care; never
a meaning (I-0017). Turn: they begin to write, and no one will read it. Ends with rain on the new-cut signs.
Events: the Toll gate first dropped (1175); the reed-marsh flood (1220); more rows cut at Tarr (1320).

**12. Of Aodh and the Northern Sea** · Aodh, Muirgheal · VE 1,331 – 1,442 · I-0017a (14 an t-Sultain 1370) · [5,200]
Aodh grows up on the recital: sky, earth, sea; he has never seen the sea, a day's walk north. At twenty-six he carries
the seven coals over the moor in autumn, stands on the shingle, wades to the knee, throws them one by one; six sink; the
gold takes fire on the swell and rides the waves gold until a breaker rolls it under. Home; the old women weep
(Muirgheal): nearly five hundred years since the grove. The riddle is true, and each coal answers its own element.
Aodh throws the six at every shore and hilltop after, to nothing, and dies (1412). The name *gual òir* is said at the
recital. Ends with the line taking new pieces from the stream-bank where the vein shows.
Events: Aodh born (1344); his first sight of the sea (1369); the telling at the hearth (1370); Muirgheal dies (1381);
Aodh dies (1412); new pieces from the stream-bank (1420).

### The Third Part: Of the White Coal on the Height, and the Walls of the Old Ones (VE 1,443 – 2,678)
Part epigraph (ch 13): `> *Dhìrich Fearghas gu mullach an eilein, agus ghabh an gual sneachda teine san adhar.*` /
`> Fearghas climbed to the top of the island, and the snowflake coal took fire in the air.` / `> — from the Line of the Mason`

**13. Of the Kilns, the Ramps and the Houses on the Water** · the Old Ones; lake folk · VE 1,443 – 1,669 · I-0018,
I-0019, I-0020 · [5,000]
Opens on the kilns: seven stone-lined pits with short flues at Morn drying the catch over peat smoke, the first
buildings made for a trade; a kiln-keeper's day (I-0018). Brenn on the north coast: ramps of flat stone wide enough for
hulls greater than any hide boat; a hull framed and launched; the POV stays ashore as it goes (I-0019; never say where).
Loch chrom: platforms on oak piles joined to the shore by plank walks; a child on the planks; which people built them
stays unsaid (I-0020). Turn: the shore grows bold. Ends with a sail going small.
Events: kiln smoke seen from inland (1450); the first hull down the ramps (1520); eels taken from the platforms (1600).

**14. Of the Whale, the Round Houses and the Tower** · the Old Ones · VE 1,670 – 1,950 · I-0021 – I-0024 · [5,000]
A whale strands below the southern Dunn; its ribs raised as frames for three turf-roofed round houses, set up again
twice before they rot (I-0021). Vell of the south-east: fourteen dry-stone round houses, sunk hearths, drains under the
walls (I-0022). The north-western Skell: one hollow-walled round tower with a stair in the wall, looking west over open
water (I-0023; no Watcher yet). North Toll: stone huts in a dune's lee; a sandstorm buries them whole; the people get
out; the sand keeps the huts (I-0024). Turn: shelter against sky and sand. Ends with the dune over the roofs.
Events: the ribs raised again (1720); the first night in the tower (1810); the sand-storm at Toll (1879, same year).

**15. Of the Lean Years of the Line** · Gormal; the Old Ones · VE 1,951 – 2,097 · I-0025, I-0026, I-0027 · [4,800]
Opens on the Old Ones' eastern Bral landing and kilns (I-0025). At Tobar dhìreach six hundred years have passed since
the gold. Gormal keeps the fire-pot and the recital as a branch of the kin tires of the riddle and goes down to the
eastern farms with no coals. She reasons with the young: sky means air, and the line has thrown coals from hilltops but
never from the highest; no one agrees which is highest. Shore sections: the south Morn shell beds with their huge
oysters (I-0026); a second Brenn in the north-east with the same ramps (I-0027). Gormal dies (2004) with the question
passed on. Ends on the twin landing.
Events: Gormal's rule that the recital be kept (1960); the branch leaves (1980); Gormal dies (2004).

**16. Of Fearghas on the Height** · Fearghas, Mathan · VE 2,098 – 2,240 · I-0027a (2 an t-Sultain 2170) · [5,200]
Fearghas reasons that the sea took one by water and the sky will take one by air, at the highest ground. He and his
brother Mathan walk the ridges south and argue peaks; they climb the summit: bare moor, wind to make teeth ache, the sea
shining at every edge. He drops each coal from arm's height over the steep side; the fifth, flecked with white stars,
takes fire halfway down and burns white like a star in daylight, lying lit on the stones below. The summit takes the
kindred's name, Binnean a' Chlachair; herders begin to leave a stone on the top. Turn: second lighting. Ends at the
recital, the name said standing.
Events: Fearghas born (2138); the climb begins (2170, eve); the first stone left on the summit (2171); Fearghas dies (2199).

**17. Of the Cliff-House and the Curved Mole** · the Old Ones · VE 2,241 – 2,523 · I-0028 – I-0031 · [4,800]
Tarr in the north-east: a long house built into a cleft, the living rock its back wall, black with soot to a man's
height; a family through a hard winter (I-0028). Vell on the Abhainn bheag: four houses and a boat-shed, the furthest
north-east (I-0029). North Skell: a second breakwater curved against the open-sea swell, stone by stone as at the other
(I-0030). West Vell: pits dug in the dune, clay-lined, slab-lidded, for the lean season (I-0031). A growing unease;
eyes turn inland (no reason given). Ends with the pits sealed for winter.
Events: the hard winter at Tarr (2250); the curved mole finished (2382, master); fish-bones in the sealed pits (2460).

**18. Of the Outpost and the Three Rings** · the Old Ones · VE 2,524 – 2,678 · I-0032, I-0033, I-0034 · [5,000]
For the first time the Old Ones go up from the shore. On a spur above the southern lowland a walled outpost, stones
dressed on the outer face only, one gate facing inland (I-0032). On the height near the south-east coast a fortress of
three rings, a cistern cut in the rock, room for four hundred and their beasts (I-0033). A kerbed track paved with slabs
toward both (I-0034). Use **different** POVs at outpost and fortress: never show one crew building both. Turn: a people
turn their faces from the sea. Ends with the last slab of the track laid, pointing toward the mountains.
Events: the outpost's gate hung (2530); the cistern first full (2590); cattle first driven inside the rings (2600).

### The Fourth Part: Of the Black Coal in the Ground, the Watcher and the Hollow Man (VE 2,679 – 3,407)
Part epigraph (ch 19): `> *Na las teine far am faic e thu.*` / `> Light no fire where it can see you.`
