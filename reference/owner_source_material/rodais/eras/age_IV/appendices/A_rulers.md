# The Kings, the Queens and the Houses

For the eighteen centuries of a' Scaradh Dia-thìr kept one crown at Cathair dhearg, one vein-house above Muileann dhearg, and between them Na Seachd Taighean that answered to both and were, for most of the age, the stronger. This appendix gives the crowned rulers as Clàr nan Rìghrean recopied in LE 829 and Leabhar a' Chùraim give them, with their years and their ages; Na Seachd Taighean and those of their heads the record names; the masters of Taigh na Fèithe; the scribes who kept the harbour-house at Seann Skell; and the end of the crown. Years are counted from the first year of the age, as the rolls counted them after the crown took up Donnchadh's count; ages are given at the crowning and at the death, as Leabhar a' Chùraim sets them down. Na Bliadhnachan Fada of the old blood had thinned to an ordinary span by An Aois Naomh, and in this age no one, crowned or not, is entered as living past five-and-ninety.

## The crowned rulers

**Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill**, of the house of Dòmhnall, daughter of Mòr nic Coinnich. Crowned at fifteen in FE 2,953, eight years before the first year of the age; she was three-and-twenty when the three crews came to An Talla Dearg. She gave the word that the crown would send no one over and keep no one back, struck her sister Mòrag and Mòrag's children from the succession in the ordinary hand in LE 46, and wore undyed wool for her sister from the thirteenth year of the count. She died at Cathair dhearg in LE 55, at seventy-six, after one-and-sixty years crowned, holding a boundary petition to its end the morning she took to her bed. Her consort Uilleam mac Iain died in LE 29.

**Raghnall mac Uilleim**, her son, born LE 8. Crowned in LE 55 at seven-and-forty, with a strip of undyed wool at his wrist beneath the crowning colours. He died in LE 92 in his eighty-fifth year, four-and-eighty years old, after seven-and-thirty years crowned.

**Raghnall mac Raghnaill**, his son, born LE 45. Crowned in LE 92 at seven-and-forty, grey already at the temples. He died in LE 125 at eighty, three-and-thirty years crowned.

**Ìomhar mac Raghnaill**, his son, born LE 92. Crowned in LE 125 at three-and-thirty with the same cup and the same oath as his father. His only son, Raghnall Beag, died of a fever in LE 170. Ìomhar died in LE 180 at eight-and-eighty, five-and-fifty years crowned and without an heir, and the line of Beathag ended with him.

**Fearchar mac Lachlainn**, of Clann Fhearchair, born LE 157 at Baile Mòr dhomhain, the elder son, bound seven years to the galleries as a boy. Chosen by the heads of Na Seachd Taighean in the yard of An Talla Dearg in LE 182, when his younger brother Iain, head of the house, turned the choosing aside and named him; he drank his first oath from the cup of Clann Mhuirich, the crown having none ready. Crowned at five-and-twenty. He kept his court part of each year at Baile Mòr dhomhain. He died in LE 211 at four-and-fifty, nine-and-twenty years crowned, and asked that no more coal be burned for him than the ordinary handful.

**Fearchar Donn**, his son, born LE 188. Crowned in LE 211 at three-and-twenty. He died in LE 256 at eight-and-sixty, five-and-forty years crowned; the heads of Na Seachd Taighean held the hall in his daughter's name for two years.

**Sìleas nic Fhearchair**, his daughter, born LE 243. Crowned in LE 258 at fifteen. She married Dùghall mac Ìomhair of Clann Ìomhair, set the firlot of Cathair dhearg as the one measure of the island, put a tally-house in every royal granary, chartered the full-moon market of Cathair fhada and widened the bridge of the capital. She died in LE 324 at one-and-eighty, six-and-sixty years crowned, the longest reign An Talla Dearg had then seen.

**Uisdean mac Dhùghaill**, her son, born LE 270 in the twelfth year of her reign. Crowned in LE 324 at four-and-fifty. He died in LE 352 at two-and-eighty, eight-and-twenty years crowned.

**Uisdean mac Uisdein**, his son, born LE 322. Crowned in LE 352 at thirty. He had his son taught his letters by a tutor, Mòr nic Nèill, the first heir so taught. He died in LE 394 at two-and-seventy, two-and-forty years crowned.

**Aonghas mac Uisdein**, his son, born LE 372. Crowned in LE 394 at two-and-twenty, with less dispute than any crowning in living memory. He took up Òrd Mhacha' rite of Macha and made it the crown's custom, and half his household took it up after him. He died in LE 450 at eight-and-seventy, six-and-fifty years crowned.

**Aonghas Òg mac Aonghais**, his son, born LE 400. Crowned in LE 450 at fifty. He died in the winter of LE 484 at four-and-eighty, four-and-thirty years crowned, the first king carried to his grave by the rite of Macha.

**Iain mac Aonghais**, his son, born LE 435. Crowned in LE 484 at nine-and-forty. He had three sons, the youngest, Dùghall, by a second wife. He died in LE 496 at one-and-sixty, twelve years crowned: the third king the hall had buried in a single generation.

**Tormod mac Iain, Tormod Bàn**, his eldest son, born LE 460. Crowned in LE 496 at six-and-thirty. His two sons were drowned together when the ferry on the Abhainn fhionn overturned in LE 534, and his brother Aonghas mac Iain had died four years before. He named no heir. He died in LE 547 at seven-and-eighty, one-and-fifty years crowned, and Na Seachd Taighean held the hall in common while three claims stood unanswered.

**Catrìona nic Aonghais, Catrìona Bhuadhach**, daughter of Aonghas mac Iain and Mairead nic Ailein, niece of Tormod Bàn, born LE 526 at Inis thais. She won Cogadh nan Trì Tagraichean and was crowned in LE 562 at six-and-thirty. She bound every claimant, house and king by Riaghailt an Teallaich, laid Rathad na Banrighinn in stone to her own gate, and honoured Seann Warr's gates. Her husband Iain mac Eachainn died in LE 594. She died in LE 604 at eight-and-seventy, two-and-forty years crowned, the last ruler who had fought in the war.

**Lachlann mac Iain**, her son, born LE 565. Crowned in LE 604 at nine-and-thirty; the seven heads swore to him within a single day of his mother's burial. He had Leabhar nan Trì Tagraichean read into the record and forbade that any copy of it be made shorter than the one before. He died in LE 654 at nine-and-eighty, fifty years crowned.

**Anna**, his daughter, born LE 600. Crowned in LE 654 at four-and-fifty. She died in LE 685 at five-and-eighty, one-and-thirty years crowned.

**The fourth of the house of Catrìona**, Anna's child, born LE 635. Crowned in LE 685 at fifty; died in LE 722 at seven-and-eighty, seven-and-thirty years crowned. Clàr nan Rìghrean as recopied keeps the years and has lost the name.

**The fifth of the house of Catrìona**, child of the fourth, born LE 680. Crowned in LE 722 at two-and-forty; died in LE 769 at nine-and-eighty, seven-and-forty years crowned. The name is likewise lost.

**Eilidh Bhàn**, granddaughter of the fifth, born LE 751. Crowned in LE 769 at eighteen. She sent Iain mac Thormoid against the sea-thieves of the small islands and had Clàr nan Rìghrean recopied onto new hide in LE 829. She died in LE 841 at ninety, two-and-seventy years crowned, the oldest ruler of the age, and without an heir.

**Dòmhnall Ruadh**, of Clann Raghnaill, a custodian of three galleries at Muileann chrom, born LE 790. Named by Na Seachd Taighean after five years of sittings at Taigh na Fèithe, and crowned at Cathair dhearg in LE 846 at six-and-fifty. In LE 859 the crown was joined to the mastery of Taigh na Fèithe, and from LE 865 the custody's seal, the trellis and the hollow stone, went on the crown's letters. He died in LE 871 at one-and-eighty, five-and-twenty years crowned, and was buried at his own wish in a worked-out gallery under the mountain with an unlit lamp at his head.

**Raghnall Òg**, his son, born LE 825. Crowned in LE 871 at six-and-forty, more at ease with a boundary plea than with his own coronation. The record gives no year for his death, and Clàr nan Rìghrean for the century and a half after him keeps reigns that this record does not number.

**Mòrag Ruadh**, a girl of Clann Raghnaill, born LE 1,000. Named by Na Seachd Taighean on the death of the old king and crowned in LE 1,019 at nineteen. She kept her own ledger, clasped in iron at her own asking, from the first month; she judged that the thousandth year of the count deserved one handful burned on Cidhe an Diosail and no more, and rode down to see it burned. Her daughter was born in LE 1,040. She reigned sixty-three years; the ledger's last sum is of LE 1,082.

**Beathag Fhionn**, her daughter, born LE 1,040. Sat at her mother's right hand for years before she was crowned in LE 1,082 at two-and-forty, and closed the coast road against the swallowing cough within the month. She died in LE 1,129 at nine-and-eighty, seven-and-forty years crowned, asking whether the road stood open.

**Ailean**, her son, born LE 1,070. Crowned in LE 1,129 at nine-and-fifty. The record gives no year for his death.

**Ailean mac Raghnaill**, born LE 1,200. The record gives no year for his crowning. He reigned long enough that few at the hall could remember another king's seal, and died in LE 1,266 at six-and-sixty, leaving two sons by different mothers and a burial roll with a blank where the heir's name should stand.

**Iain Ciar**, his son, born LE 1,240. He won Cogadh nam Bràithrean against his half-brother Alasdair Bàn and made the peace at An Taigh-seinnse Mòr with two loaves left uneaten on the board. Crowned in LE 1,276 at six-and-thirty. He kept his brother's lordship of Seann Chwen vacant after Alasdair's death in LE 1,291, made the grain law of LE 1,299, and chartered the road-houses of the mining roads in LE 1,319. He died in LE 1,326 at six-and-eighty, fifty years crowned.

**Catrìona Bheag**, of Clann Raghnaill, born LE 1,400. Crowned in LE 1,429 at nine-and-twenty, the first queen in more than three centuries; the record notes she was small, and that no one at her court said so twice. She had the seven heads swear Riaghailt an Teallaich afresh in LE 1,449.

**Sìleas Òg**, born LE 1,505. Crowned in LE 1,529 at four-and-twenty. She rode a round of every shire seat on the island, the first ruler to stand in all of them, entered the smaller Cunntas nan Làmh of LE 1,539 without a word, and presided when the council refused the first of Athchuingean an Airgid in LE 1,559.

**Ailean mac Raghnaill, the fourth of the name**, born LE 1,550. Crowned in LE 1,589 at nine-and-thirty; dead within the year at forty, the shortest reign Clàr nan Rìghrean sets down. Leabhar a' Chùraim does not say what ailed him. His sister had the years of his crowning and death cut on one stone.

**Mòrag nic Raghnaill**, his sister, born LE 1,556. Crowned in LE 1,590 at four-and-thirty. She reigned forty years, to LE 1,630, and her clerk set down under her no death by war, no famine and no quarrel of Na Seachd Taighean.

**Tormod mac Ruairidh**, of Clann Raghnaill, born LE 1,680. Crowned in LE 1,709 at nine-and-twenty. The record keeps of his reign chiefly its roads and its bridges. He governed his last years from a chair by the hearth and died in LE 1,766 at six-and-eighty, seven-and-fifty years crowned, the longest-lived king Leabhar a' Chùraim had entered since Clàr nam Bliadhnachan; he told his daughter the last night that he had never found the courage to ask Taigh na Fèithe the forbidden question.

**Gormshuil nic Thormoid**, his daughter, born LE 1,715. Crowned in LE 1,766 at one-and-fifty. She rode a progress of every shire seat from LE 1,771 and had Leabhar nan Gearan written at each. In LE 1,795, eighty years old and nine-and-twenty years crowned, she rode the coal-road to Taigh na Fèithe for the winter burning, as she had every winter of her reign, and walked her own horse up the last iced mile. Nothing more is told.

## The empty chair

From the winter after the queen went up, the custodians of Na Seachd Taighean sat at An Talla Dearg in her place, sending their word up the coal-road by the riders who had carried the crown's business, and from LE 1,798 they sealed their letters with the custody's trellis and hollow stone and no name above the seal. No clerk was asked to explain the blank. In the seventeenth winter, LE 1,812, the heads, meeting at Cathair dhearg by the chance of their riders' errands, called themselves Comhairle a' Chùraim, the council of the custody, and named Tormod mac Ailein, eight-and-thirty, a custodian's son of no house among the seven, Ceann a' Chùraim to speak for them with one voice. He was offered the queen's chair at the head of the board and kept his place at its foot. The chair stood empty at every sitting after, and by the last winter of the age no clerk living could say with certainty whose absence it marked.

## Na Seachd Taighean

The houses were made at Taigh na Fèithe in LE 55, when a fifth of the coal-right's households had gone with the fleet and the old apprenticeship could no longer serve the galleries. The remaining custodian households were gathered into seven kindreds, their heads sworn before Crom's stone, and from that year the right to the coal passed by house: Clann Mhuirich, Clann Fhearchair, Clann Ìomhair, Clann Raghnaill, Clann Thormoid, Clann Choinnich and Clann Lachlainn. In LE 109 each head drew a marked stone from a covered bag for the galleries, and the bag hung on its peg in Taigh na Fèithe hall for the rest of the age. From LE 149 the heads wore the silver neck-rings of Muileann chiar.

**Clann Mhuirich.** Its head, a woman, lent the house's own cup for Fearchar mac Lachlainn's first oath in LE 182. The record names no other head of it.

**Clann Fhearchair**, of Baile Mòr dhomhain. It gave the crown Fearchar mac Lachlainn and his line, and its head **Iain mac Lachlainn**, born LE 160, head from LE 179, kept the house's hall while his brother reigned, and died in LE 219.

**Clann Ìomhair**, of the eastern hills about Dùn dhearg. It walled Dùn dhearg in LE 261 without the crown's leave and gave Queen Sìleas her consort, Dùghall mac Ìomhair. **Beathag nic Uisdein**, its head, set her mark last and longest to Riaghailt an Teallaich in LE 565 and knelt first to Lachlann mac Iain in LE 604.

**Clann Raghnaill.** Its head **Raghnall Ruadh**, born LE 505, was chosen master of Taigh na Fèithe in LE 540 over two men of longer service, claimed the crown after Tormod Bàn, refused the measured handful to the shires that stood against him in LE 558, and fell below Muileann chrom in LE 560. The house gave the crown Dòmhnall Ruadh and, from Mòrag Ruadh on, most of the rulers of the age's second half.

**Clann Thormoid.** An old woman of the house, who had given a brother and two nephews to the roll, turned the sitting of LE 54 by saying she would sooner see her right folded into a house that could walk its galleries than stand alone over ones it could not serve.

**Clann Choinnich.** Its head, a woman, argued three winters that the coal be burned beyond the handful to smelt the silver of Muileann chiar, and the house brought the first of Athchuingean an Airgid in LE 1,558 and the second in LE 1,776; both were refused, and the house asked a third time at the first sitting of Comhairle a' Chùraim and was set aside. At the renewed oath of LE 1,449 its head asked whether the oath changed the measure or only the manner.

**Clann Lachlainn.** **Aonghas mac Lachlainn**, born LE 1,720, sat on the council that refused Athchuingean an Airgid and turned his own vote against it at the board without a reason; he died at Cathair dhearg in LE 1,804 at four-and-eighty, the last of that council, and kept his reason.

By the last Cunntas nan Làmh in LE 1,791 three of the seven held more than half the whole right between them, and one of the four smaller houses was eased with a neighbour's unused right.

## The masters of Taigh na Fèithe

**Eòghann mac Mhuirich**, master at the opening of the age, who made the first count of the departing households in LE 3 and saw the measures for the fleet sealed; he died in LE 30, very old. Leabhar a' Chùraim leaves the masters after him unnamed until the war. **Raghnall Ruadh** of Clann Raghnaill was chosen in LE 540 and held the mastery until he fell in LE 560. From LE 859 the king was master of Taigh na Fèithe, and the crown and the custody kept their records on one shelf.

The scribes of Leabhar a' Chùraim the record names are **Donnchadh mac Thormoid**, born LE 14, given to Taigh na Fèithe at nine, maker of the fair copy of the ruling of the coal at fifteen and of the count from An Diosal at thirty, who died at Muileann dhearg in LE 109 at five-and-ninety; **Murchadh Beag**, who took a coal-breaker's son from the face for a scribe's stool and died about LE 728; and **Eòghann mac Pheadair**, born LE 700, who settled the two counts of An Diosal with the crown's scribe **Mòrag nic Ìomhair** in LE 739 and died about LE 770. Under them **Eòghann mac Dhùghaill**, master of the store in the war, kept Raghnall Ruadh's list of refused hearths face to the wall and never scraped it, and his son **Dòmhnall mac Eòghainn** let Seònaid nic Coinnich copy it.

## The harbour scribes of Seann Skell

**Calum mac Nèill**, born FE 2,930, opened Rolla nan Deònach in LE 2 and kept the boards six-and-fifty years; he died in the room above the harbour-house in LE 58. **Niall mac Dhòmhnaill**, his grandson, born LE 29, who counted the hulls with a hazel stick at eleven, kept the boards from LE 58 to his death in LE 104 and was buried with the stick in his hand; his father **Dòmhnall mac Chaluim** kept the trade rolls beside him and died in LE 66. **Murchadh mac Raghnaill** was harbour-master in Niall's time and made the count of half trade. **Ailean mac Mhurchaidh** kept the harbour-house when Seònaid nic Coinnich came to read the old boards in LE 612, and **Donnchadh mac Ailein**, scribe of the harbour-court, would not enter Manannan's blessings beside the tallies of hulls. **Eòghann mac Nèill**, born LE 1,763, kept the book at Cidhe an Diosail from LE 1,792 to the end of the age, counted the eleven at the last Deas-ghnàth a' Chalaidh Fhalaimh, and wrote the number, the weather and the tide in the same plain hand every Là an Diosail after.

## The queen's scribes

**Murchadh mac Eòghainn**, born FE 2,925, wrote the three tellings on one leaf in the first year, sealed the queen's word, and entered Mòrag's line struck in LE 46, sanding the leaf as he had sanded every entry for forty years; he died in LE 49. **Seònaid nic Coinnich**, born LE 549, came to An Talla Dearg at seventeen from the charity of Cathair mhòr, served Catrìona Bhuadhach and Lachlann, and wrote Leabhar nan Trì Tagraichean, finished in LE 624; she died in LE 631 at two-and-eighty. **Pàdraig mac Ruairidh** wrote Leabhar nam Bràithrean in Iain Ciar's praise and died about LE 1,320. **Pàdraig mac Ìomhair**, steward, set his sums before the girl Mòrag Ruadh from the evening she found his tally wrong.
