# Age IV plan: An Aois Scaraidh, the Age of Sundering (LE 1 – 1,819)

## 1. The volume

**Title.** *Solas air an Uinneig*: A Light on the Window.

**Epigraph** (front.json):
> *Solas air an uinneig,*
> *solas gus an tig iad dhachaigh.*
> A light on the window,
> a light till they come home.
>
> — the children's verse of the Seann Skell waterfront

**Premise.** A coast is sighted over the open sea, and one household in five of those who hold the right to the coal
chooses to go. The island builds a fleet it has never had the craft for, sees it go once sunwise round the river pool
at {{place:burg:489}}, and never hears clearly from it again. The novel follows the ones who stayed for eighteen
centuries: a queen who would neither send nor keep back her own sister; the harbour scribes who cut the names and
counted the hulls; the vein-house that turns the right to the coal into an inheritance; the orders of the Old Faith
taking shape; two wars over a crown; a master of the vein who lets hearths go cold; the slow forgetting of keel, stars
and far coast; and at the last, eleven people on a green quay, an empty crown, and a mist that lies thinner each winter.

**The question.** What does a people keep, and what does it let go, when part of it leaves? (Answer the book gives by
its end: it kept the vein, the Law and the lamps for a long while; it let go the keel, the stars, the far coast and at
last the lamps; and it never agreed whether the leaving was a wound or a mercy.)

## 2. Point of view and voice

The high recollection voice of the master Book of the age (`legendarium/book/age_IV.md`), opened out into scenes: speech,
weather, hands, smells, what a person saw. Each chapter has one central figure and is told close to them for its
turning points, framed by recollection ("It is told at Seann Skell that…", "So ended…") for the years between. Keep it
grave, concrete and light on archaism. Later chapters may be drier and quicker, as the master Book is.

**Relay of centres.** The age is 1,819 years long, so it is carried by a relay. Each Part has a centre:

| Part | Chapters | Years | Centre |

| Two: The Sunwise Turn | 7–12 | LE 18–67 | Queen Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill |

| Six: The Last Lamps | 33–39 | LE 1,159–1,819 | Beathag nic Thòmais, who gathers the old copies (with the last harbour scribe and the fisher who sees through the mist) |

**The running spine: the harbour-house of Seann Skell.** Every Part has at least one scene at {{place:burg:489}}: the
boards of the roll, the night of lamps, the berth renamed, the thousandth-year burning, the keel-song's hull, the
eleven. The office of harbour scribe is the one line that runs the length of the book (Calum mac Nèill → Niall mac
Dhòmhnaill → … → Eòghann mac Nèill).

## 3. Rules for every chapter (read before writing)

- **Master canon never moves.** Every master event of `age_IV_master.json` keeps its date, place and meaning. Masters
  are dated by the build; write `{{date:III-00NN}}` / `{{year:III-00NN}}`, never typed dates. Prose uses the
  moon-months (Samhnas, Dubhlas, Faolach, Gearras, Sìolach, Bearnach, Bealltach, Ògach, Teasach, Lùghnas, Pailteach,
  Damhrach, the between-moon Eadrach) and the four feasts, never civil month names (the civil months come later).
- **Nothing ahead of the age.** No humans, no an Tìr Thall, no Church, no Company, no coal-blood, no Library, no
  kingdom-to-come. Never echo the forward-looking phrases in some master bodies (III-0027 "the seed of the two peoples
  that came after"; III-0031 "used again … for a very different leaving"). The Keeper's promise is not quoted. The
  thinning (III-0029) is told only as what the island came to believe, in pieces; the two landfalls and the 19/21
  hulls stay unresolved (no count, seal, list or stick may settle them); the half-year gap between sailing and turn in
  the custody-book (Appendix D §III) stays unexplained; how the grain strife was settled (III-0018) and how the
  leaders were chosen (III-0015) stay untold; what the quarryman saw in Toll-dubh (III-0149) stays untold; why the
  claimants left Coille Naomh Cnoc bheag (III-0091) stays untold.
- **The end of the crown.** App. A: "Of her death, of any heir, and of how the crown fell vacant, nothing is told."
  So Queen Gormshuil nic Thormoid goes up to the vein-house for the winter burning (LE 1,795) and does not come down
  to Cathair dhearg again; the council sits in her place; no death, heir or act of vacancy is ever written. This is the
  book's one "Of this nothing is told." Tormod mac Ailein (master, Age V) is named Ceann a' Chùraim near the end.
- **Rulers' ages (the owner's rule, RULERS.json).** Every crowned ruler now has a birth, an accession and a death in
  eras/RULERS.json and in PLAN_people.json; keep to them, and state a ruler's age where the telling wants it. No one in
  this age lives past 95. The master's long reigns are carried by more rulers than the old king-list showed: Raghnall
  mac Uilleim (55–92) and his son Raghnall mac Raghnaill (92–125), whose son is Ìomhar; Uisdean mac Dhùghaill (324–352)
  and his son Uisdean mac Uisdein (352–394), whose son is Aonghas mac Uisdein (394–450, crowned at 22, a boy taught his
  letters by Mòr nic Nèill); Aonghas mac Uisdein's son Aonghas Òg (450–484), the first king buried by Macha's rite, whose
  son is Iain mac Aonghais. The house of Catrìona has six rulers, not five: Catrìona, Lachlann, Anna, two the annals do
  not name, and Eilidh Bhàn, crowned at 18 and reigning 72 years. Sìleas nic Fhearchair is crowned at 15 and reigns 66
  years; Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill was crowned at 15 at FE 2,953 (the master's year).
- **Owner's line.** Fionnlagh (Line of Aisling no. 17) appears only standing on the quay at the sailing and not going.
  No other member of that line is named in this age. Donnchadh mac Thormoid the vein-house scribe of LE 44 shares a
  name with a later man and is no kin of his: never link them.
- **Scale.** Island ~300,000 at LE 1, ~570,000 at the close. Towns are hundreds (Seann Skell of the west ~1,200 at the
  close, Cathair dhearg ~500, Cathair mhòr ~1,700, the largest). The fleet's people are never totalled: the rolls count
  households. Deaths and crowds as the master gives them.
- **Things the age has not got.** No coin (barter, tally-sticks, salt as the north-east's measure, silver neck-rings);
  no paper before LE 1,674 (boards, hide, skin leaves, tally-sticks); no ship out of sight of land after the fleet
  (coasters of sewn plank); no standing host (house followings).
- **Geography** (guide §13; measured on the map): Seann Skell–Cathair dhearg 24 mi; Cathair dhearg–Muileann dhearg
  39 mi; Seann Skell–Muileann dhearg 60 mi; Seann Skell–Doire fhionn 42 mi; Seann Skell–Ceann mhòr 20 mi; Seann
  Skell–Cuan ruadh 100 mi (the far east coast); Muileann dhearg–Dùn ìseal 18 mi; Muileann chrom–Muileann dhearg 8 mi.
  The fleet went north past the north cape into Manannan's mist on the eastern sea; the Otherworld of the tellings is
  west. There are three Seann Skells (489 the western river port, 132 on the north coast's Hawk bay, 431 the south) and
  two Àth sheans (67 the granary town, 32 the northern ford): always use `{{place:burg:N}}`.
- **Names.** Dia-thìris only, from `eras/NAMES.json` and `age_IV/PLAN_names.json`; add new ones to PLAN_names.json.
  Human names: none in this age.
- **Files.** Each chapter is `book/NN_slug.md` (NN = chapter number, two digits), opening `# <chapter title>`, the
  date line `*LE a – b*`, and, if it opens a Part, the Part's epigraph; its sections are `## I. Of …`, `## II. …`.
  `told_in` anchors: `book/NN_slug.md#i-of-…`.

## 4. Continuity

**Opening state** (where Book III closes): the Holy Age is over; no family lives longer than another; Queen Beathag
nic Dhòmhnaill, daughter of Mòr nic Coinnich, holds the red hall at Cathair dhearg (her mother dead a few years); the
stone cup stands on the threshold of Talla na Lasrach at Dùn ìseal where Brìde's flame burns; the custody of the vein
is kept from the vein-house above the diggings at Muileann dhearg, heir of the Hall, with the custody-book; the
Small-Burning Law measures the handful hearth by hearth, unchanged since Fionnlagh Dall; the orders of the Old Faith
sit apart (dawn-hawk, wolf-sworn stone, sting-oath of Manannan at Seann Skell, the schools of the stag, the
wood-watchers of Macha), with the Old Spirits beside them; the island has only sewn-plank coasters and has never
wished for the sea; the shares of the Roinn live only in boundary stones.

**Closing state** (where Book V opens): the winter of LE 1,818–19. The crown is empty in all but name; the Council of
Custodians, the heads of the seven houses, sits at Cathair dhearg in its place with Tormod mac Ailein as its head;
the kingdom seals with the vein-house's trellis and hollow stone; the Small-Burning Law still holds, three houses hold
more than half the right, the silver petition lies unanswered; Manannan's priests at Seann Skell pray the sea shut at
every tide and write that the mist lies thinner each winter; the rite of the empty harbour is kept by a handful, the
lamps are out; the north-west is nearly empty; on a still morning a fisher off the north-west headlands can see the
open sea through the grey. Nothing is said of ships. The book ends before the mist lifts.

### PART ONE: THE ROLL OF THE WILLING (ch 1–6, LE 1–18). Centre: Mòrag nic Dhòmhnaill.
Part epigraph (ch 1): *Cha chuir mi duine thairis, agus cha chùm mi duine air ais.* / I will send no one over, and I
will keep no one back.

**1. Of the Three Crews** [5,200] — POV Queen Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill. LE 1. Masters III-0001, 0002, 0003.
Open on the settled house of the island (opening state), in the red hall at Cathair dhearg, Samhain coming. Eachann
mac Lachlainn's crew of Cuan shean walks up the river road with a tale of a coast across open water; over the season
a Ros bheag crew (skipper Oighrig nic Raghnaill) and a Ceann mhòr crew (Tòmas mac Uilleim) come too. The three are
heard one after another at the board: different days' sailing, different weather, different shapes of shore; each is
asked if he was first and none claims it. The scribe Murchadh mac Eòghainn writes them and adds at the foot that they
agree on the coast and on nothing else. Mòrag, the queen's sister, at the lower board, asks the only practical
question: how many days' water. Beathag notices. Through the winter the talk goes down to the hearths and quays; the
Choosing begins as a thing with no rule, a season and not a day. Turn: Beathag, from her window, watches Mòrag in the
yard walking the Cuan shean skipper to the gate, and understands her sister before her sister does. End on the queen
telling Murchadh to write nothing of it.
New events: the tale on the quays of Cuan shean (LE 1, burg:282); the Ros bheag crew's telling (1, burg:63); the
Ceann mhòr crew's telling (1, burg:143); Murchadh's leaf of the three tellings (1, burg:19); the Choosing talked at the
west-coast hearths (1); Mòrag goes down to Cuan shean to hear the skipper again (1); households at Inis àrsaidh say
aloud they will go (2, burg:55).

**2. Of the Boards in the Harbour-House** [4,600] — POV Calum mac Nèill, harbour scribe of Seann Skell. LE 2.
Masters III-0004. Taigh a' Chalaidh, its boards, knife and lamp; Calum's craft. Show the roll opening: why by
household (the vein-house reckons hearths, and a household that went half would leave a cold half-hearth), the first
entries (fishers of Inis àrsaidh, farm wives from up the Abhainn naomh with children on the hip), the strikings (a
man back grey-faced before the moon is out; a widow who enters and strikes three times). The harbour-house becomes the
town's confessional. Calum's son Dòmhnall mac Chaluim, his clerk, argues that the boards should be closed; Calum will
not close what the queen has not closed. Turn: the second household to strike out asks Calum to scrape the name so no
one will know it was ever there; he refuses, and the rule that a struck name stays visible is born. End at night:
Calum's wife asks whether he would go; he says the boards need a keeper, and she hears that he wants to.
New events: the boards made ready (2, burg:489); the rule of the whole household (2); the first household on the roll,
fishers of Inis àrsaidh (2); the first striking-out (2); the rule that a struck name is cut through, not scraped (2);
the harbour-house lamp kept late for the roll (2).

**3. Of the Queen's Word and the Queen's Sister** [5,400] — POV Mòrag nic Dhòmhnaill. LE 2–3. Masters III-0005,
III-0006. The sisters alone in the queen's bower: Mòrag asks her sister to forbid or command; Beathag answers that the
crown has never sent anyone anywhere and will not begin, nor hold back anyone who is free. The rider carries the word
to Seann Skell; Calum reads it at the door; the harbour songs turn it into the line. Mòrag talks it through with her
husband Aonghas mac Fhionnlaigh (he says yes before she finishes); she rides the 24 miles in rain, enters her
household, spells it, watches the knife (the master scene, from her side), thanks the scribe and goes out. Second half
at the vein-house above Muileann dhearg, 60 miles east: the custodian Fearchar mac Ruairidh and the master of the
vein-house Eòghann mac Mhuirich count the households of the coal-right on the copied roll: nearly one in five; they
count twice because nobody believes the first figure. Turn: Fearchar's own household is among those counted; he says
so only after the second count. End on Eòghann writing the figure and not looking up.
New events: the rider with the queen's word reaches Seann Skell (2); the queen's word read at the harbour-house door
(2); the harbour songs set the queen's word (2, burg:489); Mòrag's household entered (2, as part of III-0005; tell,
don't duplicate); the roll copied for the vein-house (3, burg:491); Fearchar mac Ruairidh enters his household (3);
the custodians dispute whether the right to the coal goes with those who go (3, burg:491).

**4. Of the Oaks of Doire fhionn** [5,000] — POV Alasdair mac Iain, Alasdair Saor. LE 4–5. Masters III-0007,
0008, 0009, 0010. Alasdair is a boat-wright of the Seann Skell strand who has only ever sewn planks; nobody on the
island has built a hull to keep the open sea. He walks 42 miles north to Doire fhionn; the grove-keeper Eilidh nic
Ruairidh walks him through the oaks and makes him name each tree he will take. The felling: the grove's silence after;
the ox-sleds hauled south for weeks. On the river strand below Seann Skell he lays down the first deep keel by eye and
by argument. He binds the wrights of the strand into one fellowship, and a custodian comes down to swear the
apprentices seven years after the manner of the vein; the boy Coinneach mac Fhearghais is first sworn. Turn: Eilidh
tells him that the grove will not grow again and the village will keep its name anyway; he has no answer. End with the
first frames standing on the strand like the ribs of a beast.
New events: Alasdair walks to Doire fhionn (4); Eilidh nic Ruairidh gives the oaks (5, burg:261); the ox-sleds on the
road south (5); the saw-pit dug on the strand (5, burg:489); woodcutters settle at the wild oak wood by Ros dhomhain to
fell for the fleet: founding of Doire fhiadhaich (5, burg:35); Coinneach mac Fhearghais sworn first apprentice (5).

**5. Of the Hull that Lay Down** [5,200] — POV Alasdair Saor, then Iain Ròpaire of Cuan shean. LE 6–9. Masters
III-0011, 0012, 0013. An Fhaoileag goes down the Abhainn naomh past Ros àrsaidh; Alasdair on the headland with his
hands under his arms; the following sea; she lies down; eleven drowned, his sister's son Tormod Beag among them (the
master scene, extended to the shore and the wake at Inis àrsaidh). The driftwood fire: he redraws the keel in the sand
and changes its setting; the change lives in his wrights' hands. The island labours: Cuan shean's rope-walks on the
harbour front (Iain Ròpaire walking backward twisting strands; the wrights teach the rope-men the names of a keel's
parts so they know what their rope is for, and the walkers set the names to their pace); the sail-cloth levy in
Siorrachd Baile àrsaidh and the tallies at the shire seat. Turn: households strike themselves from the roll after the
drowning, and as many enter. End with the second hull laid to the new pattern.
New events: An Fhaoileag launched (6, burg:489); the eleven named on a board at Inis àrsaidh (6, burg:55); the wake
at Inis àrsaidh (6); households struck after the drowning (6, burg:489); the second hull laid to the new pattern (7);
the rope-makers learn the names of a keel's parts from the wrights (8, burg:282); the tallies of the levy kept at
Baile àrsaidh (9, burg:420).

**6. Of the Star-Steersmen of Cuan ruadh** [4,800] — POV Catrìona nic Iain, a young pilot of Ros bheag. LE 11–18.
Masters III-0014, III-0015. No one in the west can hold a course by night. Six pilots cross the island (some 100
miles, a week on foot by the road through the mountain country) to the old steersmen of Cuan ruadh. Seumas Reultach
teaches on the cliff at night: the steering star (Reul na Stiùire), the wheel of the sky, the smell of the dark; part
of it he has set down on hide, most of it he will not. He dies before they leave, and the pilots carry his lore west
in their heads. The first night-steering trial off Ros àrsaidh. Then LE 18: Catrìona reaches Seann Skell late, after
the households of the roll have met; she finds Mòrag nic Dhòmhnaill and Fearchar mac Ruairidh standing on the quay as
the chosen leaders (the choosing itself is not shown). Mòrag asks her to be pilot of her hull, An Ròn Glas. End on
Catrìona naming the star to Mòrag on the quay.
New events: six pilots sent east (11, burg:63); the pilots reach Cuan ruadh (12, burg:119); part of the star-lore set
on hide at Cuan ruadh (13); Seumas Reultach dies (16, burg:119); the pilots return west (16); the first
night-steering trial off Ros àrsaidh (17, burg:122); Catrìona nic Iain made pilot of An Ròn Glas (18, burg:489).

### PART TWO: THE SUNWISE TURN (ch 7–12, LE 18–67). Centre: Queen Beathag nic Dhòmhnaill.
Part epigraph (ch 7): *Dòrlach airson an teallaich ùir, / is an seann teallach fhathast blàth.* / A handful for the new
hearth, / and the old hearth still warm.

**7. Of the Handful for the New Hearth** [4,600] — POV Donnchadh mac Thormoid, boy-clerk of the vein-house (b. 14, so that he dies at 95 in LE 109; his first entry at sixteen).
LE 19–31. Masters III-0016, III-0017. Fearchar mac Ruairidh petitions the vein-house that the departing may carry the
coal; the custodians argue through a winter burning (the coal has never left the island; the Law measures a hearth,
not a ship). The ruling: one sealed measure a ship, a single household's season. Donnchadh writes the fair copy, his
first entry, and notices it gives no blessing. The measures are sealed "for each hull that shall be ready", never
counted. Beathag, who sends no one, lends the capital's masons "for the harbour's trade": Ailean mac Nèill and his
gang lengthen the west quay with dressed stone. Beathag rides to see the stones laid (so she has seen the fleet
building) and speaks to her sister on the new quay about nothing that matters. Turn: the queen's consort Uilleam dies
(31); Beathag keeps the funeral small. End on the masons' last stone and Donnchadh sealing measures by lamplight.
New events: Fearchar's petition for the coal (19, burg:491); the custodians' debate at the winter burning (22); the
fair copy of the ruling, Donnchadh's first entry (25); the sealed measures made (26); the queen lends masons for the
quay (28, burg:19); the queen at Seann Skell to see the stones laid (30); death of Uilleam mac Iain, the queen's
consort (31, burg:19).

**8. Of the Grain Strife and the Forty-One Barges** [5,000] — POV Tormod mac Coinnich, reeve of the granaries at
Àth shean (burg:67). LE 35–39. Masters III-0018, 0019, 0020, 0021. A lean harvest in the south shires; the council
of the red hall divides on how much stored grain the fleet may take. Tormod, who remembers a dearth year from his
boyhood, bars the store-houses and sets a watch; petitioners and riders come; the watch stands a season. One morning
the word comes and he opens the doors (how the strife was settled is never said). He holds no office afterward, and
walks home. The barges go down the Abhainn dhomhain; Mairead nic Iain, tally-keeper on Drochaid Cathair dhearg,
notches forty-one; on by the coast to Ros àrsaidh and up the Abhainn naomh. Interleave Seann Skell: Calum cuts his
own daughter Mairead's household (her husband Dùghall mac Iain's) onto the roll. Spring LE 38: the fleet lies
complete, nineteen hulls in the custody-book, twenty-one in a later margin (show the hulls, never a count). End with
Calum's grandson Niall mac Dhòmhnaill, a boy, running along the new stones counting hulls and losing count.
New events: a lean harvest in the south shires (35); the council of the red hall divided on the grain (36); Calum's
daughter's household entered (37, burg:489); petitioners at the Àth shean granaries (37, burg:67); Mairead nic Iain's
tally of the barges (38, marker:3); the fleet's stores laid in at Seann Skell (38); the last hulls rigged (39).

**9. Of the Night of Lamps and the Sunwise Turn** [5,800] — POV Niall mac Dhòmhnaill (eleven), with sections for
Mòrag and for Beathag. LE 39–40. Masters III-0022, 0023, 0024. Beathag comes to Seann Skell in Sìolach, sees the
hulls at the quay, and parts from her sister on the stones without a ceremony (last meeting; the queen goes home
before the sailing, as her word requires). The last night: lamps on every sill; Niall on the west quay wall with his
knees up, pitch and wet hemp and sheep's fat, Calum's hazel stick and knife; his aunt Mairead aboard. Mòrag's
section: her last walk along the waterfront, her grown children aboard, Catrìona at the steering oar of An Ròn Glas.
On the quay a man stands by his cart and does not go aboard: Fionnlagh (name him; nothing more). Grey before sunrise:
the hulls come off the quay and go once round the river pool sunwise; Niall cuts notches and does not look long at
faces; they straighten and go down the Abhainn naomh; Calum's pen lifts. Beathag's section: at Cathair dhearg that
morning she hears a plea about a boundary ditch as on any day. End with the harbour songs' mist behind the last hull,
and Niall putting the stick inside his shirt; no one asks him.
New events: the queen's last meeting with her sister at Seann Skell (39, burg:489); Niall given the stick and knife
(40); Mairead nic Chaluim's household goes aboard (40); Fionnlagh stands on the quay and does not go (40, burg:489,
thread line-of-aisling); the queen hears a boundary plea on the morning of the sailing (40, burg:19); the empty
berths walked that evening (40, burg:489).

**10. Of the Count from the Turn** [5,000] — POV Donnchadh mac Thormoid, then Beathag. LE 40–46. Masters III-0025,
0026, 0027, 0028. The custody-book enters the turn under a day after Samhain (Donnchadh copies it so; the gap stays
unexplained). In LE 44 he begins to number the years from the turn, the years of the Diosal, for his own use; nobody
asks him to. That year tidings of the fleet reach Seann Skell (the road and the mouth are never said); old Calum
copies them in a shaking hand; the copy lacks its end. Two coasts reached, not one. The copy goes by rider to the
vein-house and the red hall. Beathag reads it; the court argues over which hulls went where; she says nothing. LE 46:
between a grain ruling and the mending of a bridge, she strikes Mòrag and Mòrag's children from the succession "as no
longer on Dia-thìr", and writes her sister's name in the ordinary way. End on Murchadh, old, sanding the leaf.
New events: the custody-book's entry of the turn after Samhain (40, burg:491); Calum's copy of the tidings sent to
Cathair dhearg and the vein-house (44); the tidings read at the hearths of Inis àrsaidh (45, burg:55); the grain
