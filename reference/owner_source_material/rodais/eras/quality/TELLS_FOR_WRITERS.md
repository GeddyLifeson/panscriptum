# Tells to avoid

These habits make prose read as machine-made. `python quality/tells_scan.py <K>` finds most of them in your files;
run it before you hand in. The full reasoning is in the TOLKIEN and BOOK_STYLE briefs.

1. **The flip.** "It was not a war but a quarrel." "They did not come; they were here." Say the true thing once.
2. **The summing-up line.** "which is to say", "in other words", "This matters because", "It is a fair summary of",
   "In short,". Stop when the thing is told.
3. **Hedging about the record.** "the chronicles do not say", "no record explains", "nothing is known of",
   "the history knows it as". Once in an age, where the silence is the point, said plainly: "Of this nothing is told."
4. **Modern words.** framework, process, key (role, figure), crucial, significant, notably, ensure, landscape,
   navigate, delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, underscore, robust, arguably, essentially, ultimately,
   interestingly, "a sense of", "serves as", "plays a role", "in terms of", "the realm of".
5. **Participle tails.** ", marking the end of…", ", reflecting…", ", highlighting…", ", cementing…". End the
   sentence, or give the thing its own sentence with a real verb.
6. **The same opener three times running.** He… He… He…; The council… The council… The council….
7. **Dashes as a habit.** "X — and Y". Use a comma, a semicolon or a full stop. Keep em-dashes rare.
8. **Pet words.** quiet, simply, merely, indeed, truly, deeply, utterly, perhaps, faint, softly, stillness, echo,
   weight, itself, very, moment. The scan lists any you use more than 6 times in 10,000 words.
9. **Tidy triplets and balanced pairs** for rhythm alone, and abstractions where a thing would do. Name the thing:
   the ford, the ledger, the burnt roof.

Write instead: concrete nouns, strong old verbs, sentences of different lengths, plain statement. "In that year…",
"Thereafter…", "It is told that…", "So ended…". Keep the archaism light.
