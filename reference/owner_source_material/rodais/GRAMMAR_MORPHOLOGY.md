# Word forms

This chapter sets out the forms of Dia-thìris words: the initial mutations, the article, nouns and
their cases, adjectives, pronouns and prepositional pronouns, the verb, the copula, numerals, and
word-formation, ending with the kennings by which Dia-thìris names new things. Paradigm tables come
first, then the rules that produce them, then the exceptions.

**Other chapters.** *The grammar in brief* is the short statement of the language; where it and
this chapter differ, this chapter gives the fuller form and says why. Sentence-level matters (word
order, the progressive and perfect built on *bi*, relative clauses, the passive with *rach*,
clefting with the copula, compound prepositions in the clause, questions and answers, subordinate
clauses) belong to *Sentences*, cited below as **Sentences §** with its section number. *The
sounds* covers pronunciation, and *the Dictionary* gives the gender, plural, root and verbal noun
of every word.

## 0. Conventions, and the fuller forms

### 0.1 The fuller forms

Dia-thìris writes long vowels with the grave (*mòr, sìth*) and never with an acute, and writes the
cluster /sk/ as *sc* (*uisce, scoil, sceul, loisc, faisc, measc, seascad*). Three things about its
word forms are best known from the start:

1. **Dia-thìris keeps the fuller forms.** Spoken for many centuries on an island apart, Dia-thìris has not
   worn its forms down: the full dative and genitive singular, the strong genitive plural (*nam
   balach, nam bròg*), the dual after *dà* (*dà làimh, dà bhròig*), the synthetic verb forms in
   *-inn* and *-eamaid* and the synthetic imperatives of all persons, the relative future (*a
   sheasas*), the impersonal forms of every tense, and the older verbal nouns (*fantainn,
   labhairt, cantainn*). These are the plain speech of the island, not the stiff forms of books.
   Where the older tongue had a form that throws light on a Dia-thìris one, it is noted.
2. **Dia-thìris names new things from old roots.** It took few of the humans' words for the things
   they brought after the Crossing, and for most of them made names of its own (§10). The examples
   in this chapter are old native words: *oide* "tutor", not *tidsear*; *long, curach, bàta*, not
   *càr*.
3. **Some usages are its own, and settled.** Dia-thìris counts by tens as *ceathrad còig*, writes the
   vocative particle before a vowel (*a Aisling*), lenites the qualifier in every place name, and
   spells *aisde*. Each is noted where it comes up.

### 0.2 Notation

- Forms are in *italics*; glosses in "quotes".
- *L* = lenition; *h-*, *t-*, *n-* = the prefixed consonants (§1.4–1.6).
- m., f. = masculine, feminine; nom., gen., dat., voc. = the four cases; sg., pl., du. = singular,
  plural, dual.
- **Broad** vowels: *a o u à ò ù*. **Slender**: *e i è ì*. A consonant is broad or slender by the
  vowel beside it.
- The law *caol le caol, leathan le leathan* ("slender with slender, broad with broad") governs
  every ending in this chapter: a consonant cluster inside a word has vowels of the same class on
  both sides, so almost every suffix has a broad and a slender form (*-adh/-eadh, -aidh/-idh,
  -an/-ean, -as/-eas*). The last vowel of the stem decides between them. The exceptions (the
  participle in *-te*, the suffix *-mhor*, and the words *esan, iadsan, seo, eadar, airson*…) are
  noted where they come up.

## 1. Initial mutations

Dia-thìris words change their first sound according to the word before them. There is one full
mutation, **lenition**, and a set of **prefixed consonants** (*t-, h-, n-*), which are what is left
of older mutations and of final consonants the tongue has since lost. The old nasal mutation,
eclipsis, is **not** written on the word it changes: the spelling shows it only as the final
*-n/-m* of the word before (§1.6).

### 1.1 Lenition: the rule

Lenition turns a stop into a fricative, and is written by adding *h* after the first consonant.
In short:

    b > bh    c > ch    d > dh    f > fh    g > gh    m > mh    p > ph    s > sh    t > th    / #_

| plain | lenited | sound of the lenited form | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| b | bh | /v/ | *bàta* → *mo bhàta* "my boat" |
| c | ch | /x/, slender /ç/ | *cù* → *a chù* "his dog" |
| d | dh | /ɣ/, slender /j/ | *duine* → *droch dhuine* "a bad man" |
| f | fh | silent | *fear* → *an fhir* "of the man" |
| g | gh | /ɣ/, slender /j/ | *gaoth* → *a' ghaoth* "the wind" |
| m | mh | /v/, nasalised | *màthair* → *a mhàthair* "his mother" |
| p | ph | /f/ | *piuthar* → *do phiuthar* "your sister" |
| s | sh | /h/ | *sùil* → *mo shùil* "my eye" |
| t | th | /h/ | *taigh* → *mo thaigh* "my house" |

A lenited proper noun keeps its capital: *Dunn* → *a Dhunn*, *Fionn* → *a Fhinn*.

### 1.2 What does not lenite

| class | examples | note |
|---|---|---|
| vowels | *athair* → *mo athair* is written *m' athair* | elision, not lenition (§1.3) |
| *l, n, r* | *mo làmh, mo nighean, mo rìgh* | lenited in speech (fortis → lenis), never in writing |
| *sc, sp, st, sm* | *mo scian, do sporan, a stòl, glè smachdail* | the *s* before a stop or *m* is protected; so is *sk-* in old names (*Seann Skell*) |
| already lenited | *mo chù* stays *mo chù* after a further trigger | lenition never applies twice |
| *s* + *l, n, r*, vowel | these **do** lenite: *mo shlat, a shneachd, glè shàmhach* | only *sc, sp, st, sm* are protected |

### 1.3 Lenition before a vowel and before *f*

A vowel cannot lenite, so where a lenited form is required before a vowel something else marks it:

- **possessives** *mo, do* elide: *m' athair, d' athair*; before *fh* + vowel the lenited *f* is
  silent and they elide too: *m' fhàrdach, d' fhàrdach*;
- **the verb** takes *dh'* in the past and conditional: *dh'òl, dh'fhàg, dh'èisteadh*;
- **the infinitive particle** *a* becomes *a dh'*: *airson a dh'òl* "in order to drink";
- **the negative** *cha* becomes *chan*: *chan òl, chan fhaic* (§6.8).

### 1.4 The dental rule (homorganic block)

After a word ending in a dental or alveolar consonant (*n, d, t, s, l*), a following **d, t, s**
does not lenite: the two coronals are made at the same place, and the lenition is absorbed. This
is the rule behind:

| trigger | lenites | but keeps *d, t, s* plain |
|---|---|---|
| the article *an* (f. nom./dat., m. gen./dat.) | *a' bhean, a' chathair* | *an deoch, an taigh* (gen.), *an t-sràid* (s → *t-*, §1.5) |
| *aon* "one" | *aon bhàta, aon chù* | *aon taigh, aon duine, aon sagart* |
| *seann* "old" | *seann bhean, seann chù* | *seann duine, seann taigh, seann sagart* |
| *a' chiad* "first" | *a' chiad bhliadhna* | *a' chiad duine, a' chiad turas* |
| *gun* "without" | *gun chiall, gun bhiadh* | *gun teas, gun duine, gun sìth* |
| the contracted articles *san, den, don, bhon, mun, fon, tron* | *den bhàta, bhon bhaile* | *den taigh, fon talamh* |
| *cha* "not" | *cha bhris, cha sheas* | *cha dèan, cha tèid* (only *d, t*: *s* lenites after *cha*) |

The first six rows block all three dentals; *cha* blocks only *d* and *t*. The lenited *s* after
*cha* is old and sure: *cha sheas poca falamh* "an empty sack won't stand" is the proverb.

**A Dia-thìris fixed name breaks the rule.** The free phrase *seann daoine* "old people" keeps the
dental plain, as in the tale *Mar a Dh'fhalbh na Seann-Dhaoine* (*na seann daoine glic aca*); the
hyphenated proper name of the vanished people is *Seann-Dhaoine*, lenited through the dental, as
close compounds often are (§9.6). The island's oldest place names follow the free rule: *Seann Dunn,
Seann Tarr, Seann Toll* beside *Seann Chwen, Seann Bhral*.

### 1.5 The prefixed *t-*

*t-* is hyphenated to a following word in four places, all after the article:

| context | before | example |
|---|---|---|
| masc. nom. sg. | a vowel | *an t-uisce, an t-iascair, an t-each-uisce, an t-Ògmhios* |
| fem. nom./dat. sg. | *s* + vowel, *sl, sn, sr* | *an t-sùil, an t-sràid, air an t-slait* |
| masc. gen. sg. | *s* + vowel, *sl, sn, sr* | *taigh an t-sagairt, solas an t-saoghail* |
| masc. dat. sg. | *s* + vowel, *sl, sn, sr* | *anns an t-sabhal, air an t-sliabh* |

Where the article would lenite an *s*, it prefixes *t-* instead, and the *s* goes silent: *an
t-sùil* /ən̪ˠ ˈt̪uːlʲ/. The *t* is the article's own old final consonant, kept where the *s* has given
way to it. *sc, sp, st, sm* take nothing: *an sporan, an stòl, an sceul*.

### 1.6 The prefixed *h-* and *n-*; traces of eclipsis

**h-** stands before a vowel after certain words ending in a vowel, and keeps the two vowels apart:

| trigger | example |
|---|---|
| *na* (gen. sg. fem., all plural cases but gen.) | *na h-eaglaise, na h-eileanan, na h-aibhne* |
| *a* "her" | *a h-athair, a h-ainm, ga h-ithe* |
| *gu* (adverbs, "to") | *gu h-àrd, gu h-obann, gu h-iomlan* |
| *le, ri* before a vowel-initial noun (the older written practice Dia-thìris keeps) | *le h-aoibhneas, ri h-ùine* |
| the counting particle *a* | *a h-aon, a h-ochd, a h-aon deug* |
| the negative imperative *na* | *na h-òl sin!, na h-ith e!* |
| *a h-uile* "every" | *a h-uile duine* |

**n-** stands before a vowel after *ar* "our" and *ur* "your (pl.)": *ar n-athair, ur n-eilean*.

**Traces of eclipsis.** The older tongue had a full nasal mutation, eclipsis, after words that had
once ended in *-n*: the genitive plural article, the possessives *ar, ur* and *an* "their", the
interrogative *an*, the conjunction *gun*, and the preposition "in". Of it Dia-thìris keeps only these
traces:

| trace | written | example |
|---|---|---|
| the *-n/-m* of the trigger itself, *m* before a labial | *nan / nam, an / am, gun / gum, ann an / ann am* | *nam bàtaichean, am bàta aca, gum faic, ann am bàta* |
| *n-* before vowels after *ar, ur* | *n-* | *ar n-òran, ur n-eich* |
| the nasalised stop in speech | not written | *an cù* is said [əŋ ˈɡuː] with voiced *g*; *nam bàtaichean* [nə ˈmaːʰt̪ɪçan], with the nasal in place of *b* |

The assimilation of *n* to *m* before *b, f, m, p* is written everywhere a particle ends in *-n*:
*am* (article, interrogative, "their"), *nam* (gen. pl. article; unreal "if"), *gum* ("that"),
*ann am* ("in").

### 1.7 Summary of triggers

| trigger | effect | see |

| *mo, do, a* "his" | *L* (*m', d'* before vowel) | §5.3 |

## 2. The article

Dia-thìris has a definite article and no indefinite one: *cù* "a dog", *an cù* "the dog". The article
agrees with its noun in gender, number and case, and it mutates the noun. The noun's own case form
(*balaich, bròige, mnà*) belongs to the noun and is given in the Dictionary; the article adds the
mutation.

### 2.1 Forms of the article

| | masculine | feminine | plural (both genders) |
|---|---|---|---|
| nom. | *an*, *am* (labials), *an t-* (vowels) | *an* + *L*, *a'* + *L* (labials and gutturals) | *na*, *na h-* (vowels) |
| gen. | *an* + *L*, *a'* + *L* (labials and gutturals) | *na*, *na h-* (vowels) | *nan*, *nam* (labials) |
| dat. | *an* + *L*, *a'* + *L* (labials and gutturals) | *an* + *L*, *a'* + *L* (labials and gutturals) | *na*, *na h-* (vowels) |

The **lenited pattern** (fem. nom. and dat., masc. gen. and dat.) works by the first sound of the
noun:

| noun begins with | lenited pattern | example (fem. nom.) | example (masc. gen.) |
|---|---|---|---|
| *b, m, p* | *a'* + *L* | *a' bhean, a' mhàthair, a' phìob* | *taigh a' bhàird, ceann a' mhanaich* |
| *c, g* | *a'* + *L* | *a' chathair, a' ghaoth* | *cas a' choin, ceann a' ghille* |
| *f* | *an* + *fh* | *an fhìrinn, an fheamainn* | *ainm an fhir* |
| *s* + vowel, *sl, sn, sr* | *an t-* | *an t-sùil, an t-sràid, an t-slat* | *taigh an t-sagairt* |
| vowel | *an* | *an oidhche, an abhainn* | *ceann an eilein, fad an uisce* |
| *d, t, l, n, r, sc, sp, st, sm* | *an* | *an deoch, an leabaidh, an nighean* | *doras an taighe, bàrr an locha* |

The **masculine nominative** pattern is:

| noun begins with | form | example |
|---|---|---|
| *b, f, m, p* | *am* | *am bàta, am fear, am monadh, am poll* |
| vowel | *an t-* | *an t-uisce, an t-each, an t-òran* |
| anything else | *an* | *an cù, an gille, an sagart, an taigh* |

### 2.2 The article with prepositions

Most simple prepositions fuse with the article. The fused forms take the dative and the lenited
pattern above:

| preposition | + *an* | example |
|---|---|---|
| *ann an* "in" | *anns an*, *anns a'*; short *san, sa* | *anns a' bhàta, sa mhadainn, san taigh* |
| *do* "to" | *dhan, dhan a'*; older *don* | *dhan bhaile, dhan mhuir, don mhuir* |
| *de* "of, off" | *den* | *den bhradan, den taigh* |
| *bho, o* "from" | *bhon, on* | *bhon chathair, on àird an iar* |
| *mu* "about" | *mun* | *mun chùmhnant* |
| *fo* "under" | *fon* | *fon talamh, fon bhòrd* |
| *tro* "through" | *tron* | *tron choille* |
| *ro* "before" | *ron* | *ron oidhche* |
| *le* "with" | *leis an, leis a'* | *leis a' chloinn* |
| *ri* "to, against" | *ris an, ris a'* | *ris a' ghaoith* |
| *à* "out of" | *às an, às a'* | *às a' chnoc* |
| *aig, air, gu, eadar* | *aig an, air an, gus an / chun an, eadar an* | *aig a' chladach, air a' bhruaich* |

*Chun* (from *gu* + article, historically *gus an*) takes the **genitive**: *chun a' chladaich, chun
na mara* (*Sentences* §8.4). The plural article with a preposition is
*na*: *air na creagan, dha na daoine, bho na bailtean*.

### 2.3 Where the article stands

The article is used, where English has none, with abstract and generic nouns (*tha an t-acras
orm* "I am hungry", *an fhìrinn*), the names of tongues (*an Dia-thìris*), seasons and months (*an
t-earrach, an Giblean*), many place names (*an Àird, an Cuan Siar*), and before a noun that has a
definite genitive (*taigh a' bhàird*, never *\*an taigh a' bhàird*). These are syntax (*Sentences* §8.2–8.3); the forms are those above.

## 3. Nouns

A Dia-thìris noun has a **gender** (masculine or feminine), a **number** (singular, plural, and a
dual that survives after *dà*), and a **case** (nominative, genitive, dative, vocative). Case is
marked by changes to the end of the word (slenderising, broadening, suffixes) and by the
mutations the article and prepositions cause. Dia-thìris keeps all of it in speech, the dative
singular of feminine nouns, the genitive after verbal nouns and the strong genitive plural among
the rest (§0.1).

### 3.1 Gender

Every noun is masculine or feminine; the Dictionary gives the gender of each of its 11,965
nouns. Gender shows in the article (*am balach, a' bhean*), in adjective lenition (*balach mòr,
bean mhòr*), in the pronoun that refers back (*e, i*), and in the genitive and dative forms.
Natural gender mostly wins (*bean, màthair, piuthar, nighean* f.; *fear, athair, bràthair, mac*
m.), with well-known exceptions: *boireannach* "woman" is masculine (by its ending), *caileag*
"girl" feminine and *gille* "lad" masculine as expected, *clann* "children" feminine.

**Rules of thumb by ending.** These predict gender well but not perfectly; the Dictionary gives each noun's gender.

| ending or class | gender | examples |
|---|---|---|
| diminutive *-ag* | f. | *caileag, uinneag, cuileag, bileag, craobhag* |
| abstract *-achd, -eachd* | f. | *rìoghachd, eachdraidh* (f.), *bàrdachd, sìorrachd, fàisneachd* |
| abstract *-e* formed from adjectives | f. | *gile* "whiteness", *òige* "youth", *doimhne* "depth", *àirde* "height" |
| most nouns ending in a slender consonant after a single syllable of long vowel | f. (tendency) | *sùil, sràid, cathair, beinn, abhainn, fìrinn* |
| names of most rivers, many countries and languages | f. | *an Abhainn mhòr, an Dia-thìris* |
| diminutive *-an* | m. | *lochan, bothan, cnocan, balachan* |
| agent nouns *-(a)ir, -adair, -aiche, -iche* | m. | *iascair, sealgair, scrìobhadair, suathaiche, stiùiriche* |
| abstract *-as, -adas* | m. | *sonas, dorchadas, maitheas, càirdeas* |
| verbal nouns in *-adh, -eadh, -amh, -ail* | m. | *briseadh, seasamh, togail, cadal* |
| nouns in *-ach* denoting persons | m. | *balach, Dia-thìreach, Tuathach, boireannach, coigreach* |
| compounds | the gender of the **head** (first element) | *taigh-òsta* m., *bean-phòsta* f., *inntinn-iarainn* f. |

Adjectives used as nouns take the gender of what they refer to: *am Bàn* "the fair-haired man",
*a' Bhàn* "the fair-haired woman".

### 3.2 The four cases, and what marks them

| case | use (details in *Sentences* §8.2, §8.4, §9.3) | typical marking |
|---|---|---|
| **nominative** | subject, object, predicate; citation form | base form |
| **genitive** | "of"; after verbal nouns (*a' sireadh arain*); after compound prepositions (*ri taobh na h-eaglaise*) and *chun, thar, rè, trìd, tarsainn, timcheall, air feadh* | m.: slenderising (*balach → balaich*); f.: *-e* and slenderising (*bròg → bròige*); some *-a* (*muir → mara*); some add a consonant (*cathair → cathrach*) |
| **dative** | after simple prepositions (*air a' bhròig*) | m.: = nominative; f.: slenderised (*bròg → bròig*) |
| **vocative** | address, after the particle *a* + *L* | m.: = genitive (*a bhalaich!*); f.: = nominative (*a bhròg!*) |

**The old stem classes.** These patterns are what remains of the stem classes of the older
tongue, which the grammarians name after the vowel or consonant that once closed the stem. The
masculine *o*-stems (*fear, gen. fir*) slenderise in the genitive; the feminine *ā*-stems (*bròg,
gen. bròige, dat. bròig*) add *-e* in the genitive and slenderise the dative; the *i*- and
*u*-stems (*muir, sùil, fuil, loch*) take *-a*; the consonant stems keep in the genitive a
consonant lost elsewhere (*cathair, cathrach*; *athair, athar*; *abhainn, aibhne*). The declension
classes below follow these families.

### 3.3 Declension classes

| class | gender | gen. sg. | dat. sg. | voc. sg. | old stem class | models |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **I** | m. | slenderised (sometimes with vowel change) | = nom. | slenderised + *L* | *o*-stems | *balach, bàrd, fear, each, iasc, cnoc, eun, ceann, mac, sagart, òran* |
| **II** | f. | slenderised + *-e* | slenderised | = nom. + *L* | *ā*-stems | *bròg, cas, làmh, clach, caileag, long, craobh, sràid, uinneag* |
| **III** | m. and f. | *-a*, with broadening | = nom. | = nom. + *L* | *i*-, *u*-stems | *muir, sùil, fuil, feòil* (f.); *loch, sruth, sneachd, ceòl* (m.) |
| **IV** | m. and f. | = nom. (ends in a vowel) | = nom. | = nom. + *L* | *io*-, *iā*-stems | *duine, baile, bàta, gille, cridhe, teine* (m.); *oidhche, bliadhna, coille, cìste* (f.) |
| **V** | m. and f. | adds a consonant (*-ach, -ar, -ann, -n*) or syncopates | = nom. | = nom. + *L* | consonant stems | *cathair, lasair, caora, abhainn, màthair, piuthar* (f.); *athair, bràthair* (m.) |
| **irregular** | | | | | | *bean, cù, bò, taigh, dia, leabaidh, sliabh, latha* |

| nom. | gen. | change | nom. | gen. | change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| *dorus* | *dorais* | *u → ai* | *rathad* | *rathaid* | *a → ai* |

Class II feminines add *-e* after slenderising; the dative is the slenderised stem alone:

| nom. | gen. | dat. | nom. | gen. | dat. |

### 3.5 Genitive singular: the rules in brief

1. **Masculine, consonant-final**: slenderise (§3.3 class I). Broad final vowel clusters change:
   *ea → i* (*fear, fir*), *ia → è* (*iasc, èisc*), *ìo → ì* (*lìon, lìn*), *o →
   ui* (*cnoc, cnuic*; *port, puirt*), *eu → eòi* (*eun, eòin*; *feur, feòir*).
2. **Feminine, consonant-final**: slenderise and add *-e* (class II); polysyllables in *-ach*
   slenderise only (*gealach, gealaich*; *cailleach, cailliche* is the exception with *-e*).
   Already-slender feminines add *-e*: *sràid, sràide*; *fìrinn, fìrinne*; *beinn, beinne*.
3. **Class III** nouns take *-a* and broaden if needed: *muir, mara*; *sùil, sùla*; *fuil, fala*;
   *loch, locha*; *sneachd, sneachda*; *sruth, srutha*.
4. **Vowel-final nouns** do not change (class IV).
5. **Consonant stems** add *-ach*, *-ar*, *-n* or syncopate (class V): *cathair, cathrach*;
   *lasair, lasrach*; *caora, caorach*; *athair, athar*; *abhainn, aibhne*.
6. **Verbal nouns** have a genitive in *-idh/-aidh* for the *-adh* class (*briseadh, brisidh*;
   *dealachadh, dealachaidh*) and in *-ain* for *-amh* (*seasamh, seasaimh*):
   *àm an dealachaidh* "the time of parting"; *ionad-feithimh* "waiting-place" (from
   *feitheamh*). Kennings use these genitives constantly: *seòmar-dìridh* "ascent-room",
   *muileann-nighe* "washing-mill", *cead-stiùiridh* "steering-leave" (§10).

**Lenition of an indefinite genitive.** After a **feminine** noun an indefinite genitive is often
lenited, most regularly when the two form a fixed phrase: *fuil-ghuail* "coal-blood", *anail
gheamhraidh* "a winter breath", *lèine-shamhraidh* "a summer shirt". After a masculine noun it is
not: *pìos arain* "a piece of bread", *guth-sreinge* "a string-voice", *carbad-sluaigh* "a crowd
chariot". This is the pattern of the kennings (§10.3).

### 3.6 The dual

After *dà* "two" a noun stands in the **dual**: the **singular**, lenited, and for feminine nouns
the **dative singular** form. It is the old dual of the tongue, whose feminine was the same as the
dative, and Dia-thìris uses it throughout: *dà làimh*, *dà chois*.

| | masculine | feminine (class II) | feminine (other classes) |
|---|---|---|---|
| "two ___" | *dà bhalach, dà chù, dà bhàta* | *dà làimh, dà chois, dà bhròig, dà chloich, dà chaileig* | *dà shùil, dà mhnaoi, dà chathair* |
| with the article | *an dà bhalach* | *an dà làimh* | *an dà shùil* |
| gen. (masc.) | *ceann an dà bhalaich* | — | — |

- *dà* does **not** lenite after the article: *an dà chù*, not *\*an dhà*.
- An adjective after a dual is **plural and lenited**: *dà bhalach bheaga* "two small boys", *dà
  làimh fhuara* "two cold hands".
- For a feminine noun the form after *dà* is the dative, lenited: *dà làimh*, from *làimh*.
- Counting the dual onward: *dà ... dheug* "twelve" (*dà chù dheug*), *dà fhichead* "forty" in the
  vigesimal count (§8.2).

### 3.7 The vocative

The vocative follows the particle *a*, which lenites. Before a vowel the particle is still written
(*a Aisling!*, *The grammar in brief* §9).

| | masculine | feminine |
|---|---|---|
| singular | *a* + *L* + slender (= gen. sg.): *a bhalaich!, a bhàird!, a fhir!, a Fhinn!, a Chiain!* | *a* + *L* + nom.: *a bhean!, a mhàthair!, a Mhàiri!* |
| vowel-initial names | *a Aonghais!* (slenderised) | *a Aisling!* (unchanged) |
| vowel-final nouns | *a dhuine!, a ghobha!, a ghille!* (unchanged but lenited) | *a bhò!* |
| plural | strong plurals: nom. sg. + *-a*: *a fheara!, a bhalacha!, a bhàrda!*; weak plurals: nom. pl. + *L*: *a chàirdean!, a dhaoine!* | *a mhnathan!, a chaileagan!* |

*Cian* → *a Chiain*, *Dubhan* → *a Dhubhain*: Dia-thìris slenderises every masculine name in the
vocative, by the class I rule (*The grammar in brief* §9). The vocative plural in *-a* (*a fheara!*)
is the old form; Dia-thìris uses it in formal address and in tales, and the weak plural (*a dhaoine!*)
everywhere else.

### 3.8 The genitive plural

Dia-thìris has two genitive plurals, and both are old:

| | form | when | examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **strong** | = nominative singular | nouns whose plural is made by slenderising (class I), and old class II and III nouns with *-an* plurals | *nam balach, nam fear, nan con, nam ban, nam bròg, nan clach, nan làmh, nan sùl, nan Tuathach* |
| **weak** | = nominative plural | nouns with plurals in *-ichean, -tean, -annan, -achan*, agent nouns, most loan-era and derived nouns | *nam bàtaichean, nan creachadairean, nam màthraichean, nan oidhcheannan, nan cathraichean* |

**Indefinite** genitive plurals are **lenited**: *làn dhaoine* "full of people", *triùir
chloinne* "three children" (gen. sg. of the collective), *fuaim bhròg* "a sound of shoes", *each
bhalach* "a boys' horse". In kennings, lenition of an indefinite genitive plural is the rule even
after a masculine head: *nimh-bhiastan* "beastie-bane", *cladhaire-chnàmh* "bone-digger",
*seanchas-dhaoine* "people-lore" (§10.3).

**Old dative plural.** The older tongue had a dative plural in *-(a)ibh*. Dia-thìris keeps it only in
fixed expressions: *air beulaibh* "in front of" (lit.
"on mouths"), *air cùlaibh* "behind" (lit. "on backs"), *fo chasaibh* "underfoot", and in verse:
*anns na beanntaibh*.

### 3.9 Plural formation classes

Each noun's plural is given in the Dictionary, but plurals fall into classes:

| class | rule | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **internal (slenderising)** | slenderise the final consonant, with the class I vowel changes | *balach → balaich, cat → cait, each → eich, fear → fir, cù → coin, mac → mic, ceann → cinn, eun → eòin, bàrd → bàird, iasc → èisc, cnoc → cnuic, Dia-thìreach → Dia-thìrich, Tuathach → Tuathaich* |
| **-an** | after a broad final | *craobh → craobhan, làmh → làmhan, cas → casan, caileag → caileagan, uinneag → uinneagan, eilean → eileanan, loch → lochan, bròg → brògan* |
| **-ean** | after a slender final | *sràid → sràidean, sùil → sùilean, seachdain → seachdainean, iascair → iascairean* |
| **-tan / -tean** | adds *t*; often syncopates or changes the vowel | *beinn → beanntan, baile → bailtean, teine → teintean, coille → coilltean, cùil → cùiltean, buaile → buailtean, sliabh → slèibhtean* |
| **-aichean / -ichean** | nouns in a vowel, and many in *-ar, -air* with syncope | *bàta → bàtaichean, bliadhna → bliadhnaichean, leabhar → leabhraichean, litir → litrichean, obair → obraichean, cathair → cathraichean, màthair → màthraichean, athair → athraichean, abhainn → aibhnichean, tobar → tobraichean* |
| **-achan / -eachan** | vowel-final nouns, many masculine | *cridhe → cridheachan, talla → tallachan, co-ogha → co-oghaichean* |
| **-annan / -eannan** | | *oidhche → oidhcheannan, muir → marannan, leabaidh → leapannan, àm → amannan* |
| **-(e)an with syncope** | a medial unstressed vowel drops | *muileann → muilnean, ubhal → ùbhlan, seòmar → seòmraichean, dorus → dorsan, tùr → tùir* |
| **irregular** | | *bean → mnathan, duine → daoine, bò → bà, piuthar → peathraichean, bràthair → bràithrean, latha → làithean, taigh → taighean, dia → diathan, sluagh → slòigh* |

**Older plurals.** Dia-thìris keeps its strong plurals and has not let *-an* take their place, as the
Dictionary records: *cnuic* (not *cnocan*, which Dia-thìris keeps for the
diminutive "hillock"), *eòin, cinn, èisc, coin*. A noun with two plurals keeps them apart in
meaning: *cnuic* "hills" beside *cnocan* "hillocks"; *lochan* is both the plural of *loch* and a
noun of its own, "a small loch" (pl. *lochain*).

**Compounds** pluralise the head: *carbad-iarainn → carbadan-iarainn, eun-iarainn →
eòin-iarainn, taigh-freiceadain → taighean-freiceadain, each-cuibhle → eich-chuibhle* (the
describer lenites after the slender plural, §4.1 rule 4).

**Nouns after numerals** take the plural from 3 to 10 (*trì coin, còig brògan*), follow the unit
in the teens (*trì bàtaichean deug*), and take the singular after *fichead* and the other tens, *ceud*
and *mìle* (*fichead cù, ceud bliadhna*). A small old set keeps the singular after every numeral,
relics of the old counted forms: *bliadhna* and *latha*: *trì bliadhna, còig latha*. *Uair* is
singular after *aon* and *dà* (*dà uair* "twice; two o'clock") and plural from three (*trì uairean*).
See §8.

## 4. Adjectives

Attributive adjectives follow their noun and agree with it in gender, number and case, by
lenition and by endings. Predicative adjectives (*tha an taigh mòr*) are uninflected (*Sentences* §1.3). A small set of adjectives precedes the noun and forms a close compound with it
(§4.5).

### 4.1 Lenition of the attributive adjective

The adjective is lenited:

1. after a **feminine singular** noun in the nominative and dative: *bean mhòr, a' bhean mhòr,
   air a' bhròig mhòir*;
2. after a **masculine singular** noun in the genitive and vocative: *taigh a' bhalaich bhig, a
   bhalaich bhig!*;
3. after a **masculine singular** noun in the dative **with the article**: *air a' bhalach mhòr,
   anns an taigh bheag* (without the article it stays plain: *air balach mòr*);
4. after a **plural** noun ending in a **slender** consonant (the strong plurals): *balaich
   bheaga, fir mhòra, eich dhubha, coin bhàna*; not after a broad plural: *brògan mòra, taighean
   geala, ballachan dearga*;
5. after a **dual**: *dà bhalach bheaga, dà làimh fhuara*.

Everywhere else it is plain: *balach mòr, am balach mòr, taighean mòra*. The ordinary lenition
exceptions apply (§1.2): *bean làidir, bean rìoghail, sràid stàiteil, clach scaoilte*.

**Place names.** In place names the qualifier is lenited after every generic, masculine or feminine:
*Baile ghorm, Cnoc bheag, Dùn thais, Cathair mhòr*. In speech an adjective after a masculine
noun follows rule 3 above (*baile gorm*). Dia-thìris keeps the always-lenited form as a fixed naming
pattern (*The grammar in brief* §4; *Names*).

### 4.2 Case and number endings

Monosyllabic adjectives decline like nouns: slenderised in the masculine genitive, slenderised
with *-e* in the feminine genitive, slenderised in the feminine dative, and with *-a/-e* in the
plural. Polysyllabic adjectives, and those ending in a vowel, show only lenition (*brèagha,
àlainn, toilichte, dìleas, uaine*), with a few polysyllables in *-ach* slenderising (§4.3).

**Paradigm: *mòr* "big" with a masculine and a feminine noun**

| | masculine: *balach mòr* | feminine: *bròg mhòr* |
|---|---|---|
| nom. sg. | *balach mòr / am balach mòr* | *bròg mhòr / a' bhròg mhòr* |
| gen. sg. | *balaich mhòir / a' bhalaich mhòir* | *bròige mòire / na bròige mòire* |
| dat. sg. | *air balach mòr / air a' bhalach mhòr* | *air bròig mhòir / air a' bhròig mhòir* |
| voc. sg. | *a bhalaich mhòir!* | *a bhròg mhòr!* |
| dual | *dà bhalach mhòra* | *dà bhròig mhòra* |
| nom./dat. pl. | *balaich mhòra / na balaich mhòra* | *brògan mòra / na brògan mòra* |
| gen. pl. | *bhalach mòra / nam balach mòra* | *bhròg mòra / nam bròg mòra* |

**Case forms of common monosyllables**

| adj. | meaning | m. gen., f. dat. | f. gen. | plural | comparative (§4.4) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *mòr* | big | *mòir* | *mòire* | *mòra* | *motha* |
| *beag* | small | *big* | *bige* | *beaga* | *lugha* |
| *geal* | white | *gil* | *gile* | *geala* | *gile* |
| *dubh* | black | *duibh* | *duibhe* | *dubha* | *duibhe* |
| *dearg* | red | *deirg* | *deirge* | *dearga* | *deirge* |
| *glas* | grey-green | *glais* | *glaise* | *glasa* | *glaise* |
| *bàn* | fair, white | *bàin* | *bàine* | *bàna* | *bàine* |
| *òg* | young | *òig* | *òige* | *òga* | *òige* |
| *ùr* | new | *ùir* | *ùire* | *ùra* | *ùire* |
| *fuar* | cold | *fuair* | *fuaire* | *fuara* | *fuaire* |
| *àrd* | high | *àird* | *àirde* | *àrda* | *àirde* |
| *trom* | heavy | *truim* | *truime* | *troma* | *truime* |
| *crom* | bent | *cruim* | *cruime* | *croma* | *cruime* |
| *fada* | long | *fada* | *fada* | *fada* | *fhaide* |
| *glic* | wise | *glic* | *glice* | *glice* | *glice* |
| *math* | good | *maith* | *maithe* | *matha* | *fheàrr* |

So *oidhche na gealaich ùire* "the night of the new moon", *air bruaichean na h-Aibhne mòire*,
*airson a' mhargaidh mhòir* "for the big market", *fo bhallachan dearga a' chaisteil* "under the
red walls of the castle", *eich bhrèagha ghil* "(in the shape) of a beautiful white horse" (gen.
sg. m., both lenited, in the tale of the water horse: *ann an riochd eich bhrèagha ghil*).

**The older declension.** The older tongue declined its adjectives fully, like nouns of the same
stem class. Dia-thìris still declines the monosyllables in the genitive and dative as a matter of
course, as the tales do.

### 4.3 Polysyllabic adjectives

Adjectives of two or more syllables take no plural ending and, mostly, no case ending: *daoine
toilichte, clann bhrèagha, na h-òrain àlainn*. They still lenite by §4.1: *bean bhrèagha, na
balaich bhrèagha*. Adjectives in *-ach* slenderise in the masculine genitive and the feminine
dative and genitive: *fearann creagach* "rocky land", gen. *fearainn chreagaich*; adjectives already ending slender
(*fiadhaich*) do not change: *bean fhiadhaich, fearainn fhiadhaich*.

### 4.4 Comparison

The **comparative form** is made by slenderising and adding *-e* (after a slender vowel *-e*
alone), so that for regular adjectives it is identical with the feminine genitive singular:

| positive | comparative | positive | comparative |
|---|---|---|---|
| *luath* "fast" | *luaithe* | *domhain* "deep" | *doimhne* |
| *òg* "young" | *òige* | *ìseal* "low" | *ìsle* |
| *geal* "white" | *gile* | *soilleir* "bright" | *soilleire* |
| *sean* "old" | *sine* | *cruaidh* "hard" | *cruaidhe* |
| *fuar* "cold" | *fuaire* | *fliuch* "wet" | *fliche* |
| *blàth* "warm" | *blàithe* | *glic* "wise" | *glice* |
| *àrd* "high" | *àirde* | *brèagha* "beautiful" | *brèagha* (vowel-final: unchanged) |

**Irregular comparatives**

| positive | comparative | note |
|---|---|---|
| *math* "good" | *fheàrr* | *nas fheàrr, as fheàrr, na b' fheàrr*; *is fheàrr leam* "I prefer" |
| *dona* "bad" | *miosa* | |
| *mòr* "big" | *motha* (older *mò*) | |
| *beag* "small" | *lugha* | |
| *fada* "long" | *fhaide* | *na b' fhaide* (tale of the water horse) |
| *furasta* "easy" | *fhasa* | |
| *duilich* "difficult" | *dorra* (also *duilghe*) | |
| *teth* "hot" | *teotha* | |
| *goirid* "short" | *giorra* | |
| *làidir* "strong" | *treasa* (also *làidire*) | |
| *faisc* "near" | *faisce* | |
| *ionmhainn* "dear" | *annsa* | old; in verse and in *is annsa leam* "I love best" |
| *tric* "frequent" | *trice* | |
| *cumhang* "narrow" | *cumhainge* | |

The *fh-* of *fheàrr, fhaide, fhasa* is written in all positions. It is the mark of an old
lenition: *nas* and *as* go back to a phrase meaning "a thing that is", which lenited the word after
it.

**Degrees and particles**

| | present, future | past, conditional |
|---|---|---|
| comparative ("bigger") | *nas motha* | *na bu mhotha*; *na b'* before a vowel or *fh*: *na b' fheàrr, na b' òige* |
| "than" | *na*: *nas motha na taigh*, *nas òige na esan* | *na bu mhotha na an taigh* |
| superlative ("biggest") | relative *as*: *an taigh as motha*, *an nighean as òige* | *a bu*: *an taigh a bu mhotha*, *an rubha a b' fhaide an iar* |

The superlative is a relative clause of the copula ("the house that is biggest"), so it takes the
copula's tenses (§7): *as* in the present and future, *a bu* + *L* in the past and conditional.

**The equative.** "As ... as" is *cho* + positive + *ri* (+ *ris* before the article): *cho socair ri
caora* "as gentle as a sheep", *cho domhainn ris an talamh* "as deep as the earth"; with a clause,
*cho ... 's a*: *cho luath 's a b' urrainn dha* "as fast as he could". *Cho* does not lenite: *cho
mòr, cho beag*. Before a following clause of result, *cho ... 's gun*: *bha e cho ciùin 's gun
deach sianar dhiubh air a mhuin* "it was so gentle that six of them got on its back".

**Nouns of degree.** Beside the comparative, most common adjectives have a noun of degree, used
for "how big", "its length": *meud* "size" (*a mheud*), *fad* "length" (*fad na h-oidhche* "all
night"), *leud* "breadth", *àirde* "height", *doimhne* "depth", *luaths* "speed", *teas* "heat",
*fuachd* "cold". *Cia mheud?* "how many?" is at root "what amount?".

**Intensifiers.** *glè* "very" + *L* (*glè mhath, glè bheag, glè thoilichte*: *glè* lenites every consonant, dentals included); *ro* "too" + *L* (*ro fhadalach, ro
mhòr*); *fìor* "truly" + *L* (*fìor mhath*); *gu math* "quite" (*gu math fuar*); *uabhasach*
"terribly" (*uabhasach fuar*).

**Adverbs** are made from adjectives with *gu* (*gu h-* before a vowel): *gu math* "well", *gu luath*
"quickly", *gu cùramach* "carefully", *gu h-àrd* "up above", *gu h-obann* "suddenly".

### 4.5 Adjectives before the noun: the preposed set

A small closed set precedes the noun, forms a close compound with it (hyphenated with some
nouns and in fixed terms), and lenites it. The noun keeps its own gender and case; the preposed
adjective does not inflect.

| adjective | meaning | mutation | examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| *seann* (pred. *sean*) | old | *L*, dental block | *seann bhean, seann chù, seann taigh, seann duine, seann sagart*; pl. *seann daoine, na seann bhailtean* |
| *deagh* | good | *L* | *deagh dhuine, deagh bhiadh, deagh shìde* |
| *droch* | bad | *L* | *droch dhuine, droch shìde, droch bhliadhna* |
| *sàr* | excellent, supreme | *L* | *sàr-bhàrd, sàr-ghaisceach, sàr-mhath* |
| *fìor* | true, very | *L* | *fìor charaid, fìor-uisce* "spring water", *fìor mhath* |
| *prìomh* | chief | *L* | *prìomh-bhaile* "capital town", *prìomh-chathair* "capital city" |
| *àrd* | high, chief (in compounds) | *L* | *àrd-rìgh* "high king", *àrd-bhàrd* |
| *ath* | next, re- | *L* | *an ath bhliadhna, an ath sheachdain, an ath latha* |
| *dearbh* | very, self-same | *L* | *an dearbh bhean* "the very woman" |
| *leth* | half, one of a pair | *L* | *leth-uair, leth-bhreac* "twin", *leth-inbheach* "half-grown" |
| *corra* | odd, occasional | *L* | *corra uair* "now and then" |
| *iomadh* | many | none; singular noun | *iomadh bliadhna, iomadh duine* |
| *gach* | each | none | *gach feascar, gach latha* |
| *a h-uile* | every | none | *a h-uile duine, a h-uile nì* |

*Seann* before a noun and *sean* after the copula or *tha* are the same word, *seann* being the old
form that leans on the noun after it: *seann duine* "an old man", *tha e sean* "he is old".

### 4.6 Adjective compounds

Two adjectives, or an adjective and a noun, may fuse, the second lenited: *dubh-ghorm* "dark
blue", *liath-ghlas* "grey-green", *ceann-mòr* "big-headed" (noun + adjective), *cas-rùiscte*
"barefoot". These count as one word for agreement: *bean dhubh-ghorm*.

## 5. Pronouns, possessives and prepositional pronouns

### 5.1 Personal pronouns

| | plain | emphatic | reflexive/intensive (+ *fhèin*) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. "I, me" | *mi* | *mise* | *mi fhèin* |
| 2 sg. "you" | *thu*, *tu* | *thusa*, *tusa* | *thu fhèin* |
| 3 sg. m. "he, him, it" | *e* | *esan* | *e fhèin* |
| 3 sg. f. "she, her, it" | *i* | *ise* | *i fhèin* |
| 1 pl. "we, us" | *sinn* | *sinne* | *sinn fhèin* |
| 2 pl./polite "you" | *sibh* | *sibhse* | *sibh fhèin* |
| 3 pl. "they, them" | *iad* | *iadsan* | *iad fhèin* |

There is no separate object form: *Chunnaic mi e* "I saw him", *Chunnaic e mi* "he saw me". *Tu*
replaces *thu* after verb forms ending in *-dh* or *-s* (*bidh tu, cuiridh tu, sheasadh tu, an
robh thu?* but *bhios tu*) and after the copula (*is tu*).
*Sibh* is plural and the polite singular (*The grammar in brief* §9).

The emphatic forms carry contrast and stand alone: *Is mise Eilidh* "I am Eilidh"; *na blais
thusa dheth* "don't *you* taste it". *Esan* and *iadsan* stand outside *caol le caol*, as the
emphatic suffixes do (§5.2).

**Other pronouns.**

| | forms |
|---|---|
| demonstrative | *seo* "this", *sin* "that", *siud* "that (yonder)"; after a noun with the article: *an t-sràid seo, an loch sin*; as pronouns: *seo an t-airgead, Bha siud ann uair* |
| interrogative | *cò* "who", *dè* "what", *cia mheud* "how many", *cò aca* "which (of them)", *ciamar* "how", *càite* "where", *carson* "why", *cuin(e)* "when" |
| indefinite | *cuideigin* "someone", *rudeigin* "something", *duine sam bith* "anyone", *nì sam bith* "anything", *càch* "the others", *a chèile* "each other" |
| pronominal nouns | *fear* "one (m.)", *tè* "one (f.)": *tè dhiubh* "one of them (f.)", *fear às dèidh fir* "one after another" |

*A chèile* takes possessive mutations after prepositions: *ri chèile, le chèile, bho chèile, eadar
a chèile*. *Cuideigin, rudeigin* also stand outside *caol le caol*.

### 5.2 Emphatic suffixes

Emphasis on a pronoun that is fused into another word (a prepositional pronoun, a possessed noun,
a synthetic verb form) is carried by a suffix:

| person | suffix | after a prepositional pronoun | after a possessed noun | after a verb |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. | *-sa* / *-se* | *agamsa, leamsa, dhòmhsa* | *mo thaigh-sa* | *sheasainn-sa* |
| 2 sg. | *-sa* / *-se* | *agadsa, ortsa, dhutsa* | *do chù-sa* | — |
| 3 sg. m. | *-san* | *aigesan, airsan, dhàsan* | *a bhàta-san* | — |
| 3 sg. f. | *-se* | *aicese, oirrese, dhìse* | *a bàta-se* | — |
| 1 pl. | *-e*, *-ne* | *againne, oirnne, dhuinne* | *ar taigh-ne* | *sheasamaid-ne* |
| 2 pl. | *-se* | *agaibhse, oirbhse, dhuibhse* | *ur taigh-se* | — |
| 3 pl. | *-san* | *acasan, orrasan, dhaibhsan* | *an taigh-san* | — |

The suffix is written solid after a prepositional pronoun and hyphenated after a noun. Like
*esan*, the suffixes stand outside the *caol le caol* law; in the older tongue they were words of
their own, and they keep the freedom of separate words still. Where a solid form would clash, the hyphen is written (*leis-san, ris-san,
às-san, dheth-san*); the traditional solid forms *aigesan, airsan, leathase, bhuaithesan,
uimesan, thuigesan, dhaibhsan* are correct Dia-thìris, and stand outside the law as *esan* and
*iadsan* do. The texts use them freely: *is ann
dhutsa a bha am bradan an dàn, agus chan ann dhòmhsa* "it was for *you* the salmon was destined,
not for *me*".

### 5.3 Possessives

| | before a consonant | before a vowel | before *f* + vowel | example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *mo* "my" | *mo* + *L* | *m'* | *m' fh-* | *mo mhàthair, m' athair, m' fhàrdach* |
| *do* "your (sg.)" | *do* + *L* | *d'* | *d' fh-* | *do phiuthar, d' athair, d' fhàrdach* |
| *a* "his, its (m.)" | *a* + *L* | *a* | *a fh-* | *a mhàthair, a athair, a fhàrdach* |
| *a* "her, its (f.)" | *a* | *a h-* | *a f-* | *a màthair, a h-athair, a fàrdach* |
| *ar* "our" | *ar* | *ar n-* | *ar f-* | *ar màthair, ar n-athair* |
| *ur* "your (pl.)" | *ur* | *ur n-* | *ur f-* | *ur màthair, ur n-athair* |
| *an* "their" | *an*, *am* (before *b, f, m, p*) | *an* | *am f-* | *am màthair, an athair, am fàrdach* |

The three *a*'s ("his", "her", and the vocative particle) are told apart by their mutations
alone: *a chù* "his dog", *a cù* "her dog", *a choin!* "O dog!".

**Traces of eclipsis.** *ar n-, ur n-* and *am/an* "their" are the old eclipsing possessives; of
their mutation only the *n-* before vowels and the nasal assimilation of *an → am* are written in
Dia-thìris (§1.6).

**Possessive or *aig*?** Inalienable things (body, kin, mind, name) take the possessive: *mo
cheann, mo mhàthair, m' ainm*. Alienable possessions usually take the article + *aig*: *an taigh
agam* "my house", *na craicnean aca* "their skins" (*Sentences* §5.1).

**Possessives with verbal nouns.** A pronoun object of a verbal noun is a possessive before it,
fused with *aig* in the progressive: *gam fhaicinn* "seeing me", *gad fhaicinn*, *ga fhaicinn*
"seeing him", *ga faicinn* "seeing her", *ga h-ithe* "eating it (f.)", *gar faicinn*, *gur
faicinn*, *gan faicinn*, *gam bualadh* "hitting them";
after other particles the possessive is written in full: *a dhèanamh* "to do it", *airson a
shlìobadh* "in order to stroke it", *mo chuideachadh* "to help me". See *Sentences* §2.5–2.6.

forms; it takes *às aonais* + possessive: *às m' aonais* "without me".

an t-acras orm*; liking *is toil leam*; ability *is urrainn dhomh*; ownership *is leamsa e*;
necessity *tha agam ri falbh*; "there" *tha e ann*; naming *ris an canadh daoine Loch Dubh*
"which people used to call Loch Dubh".

### 5.6 Preposition + possessive

The possessives fuse with some prepositions. Dia-thìris writes these contractions as single words
without an apostrophe, and the tales use them so:
*na mhuing* "in its mane", *nam bheul* "in my mouth", *na fhuil* "in his blood", *leum iad dhan
uisce nan ròin* "they leapt into the water as seals (lit. in their seals)".

| possessive | *ann an* + poss. | *aig* + poss. (with verbal noun) | *do* + poss. | mutation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| my | *nam* | *gam* | *dhom* | *L* |
| your (sg.) | *nad* | *gad* | *dhod* | *L* |
| his | *na* | *ga* | *dha* | *L* |
| her | *na* | *ga* | *dha* | *h-* before vowel |
| our | *nar* | *gar* | *dhar* | *n-* before vowel |
| your (pl.) | *nur* | *gur* | *dhur* | *n-* before vowel |
| their | *nan*, *nam* | *gan*, *gam* | *dhan*, *dham* | none |

*Ann an* + possessive is also the predicative "as": *bha i na bean-phòsta chòir* "she was a kind
wife (lit. in her kind wife)", *tha e na iascair* "he is a fisherman". See *Sentences* §1.3 and §2.7.

With the other prepositions Dia-thìris writes the possessive in full, as the tales do (*fo a chòta*
"under his coat", *le a òrdag* "with his thumb", *bho mo sheanmhair* "from my grandmother", *gu a
màthair* "to her mother"); in speech these run together as *fom, lem, bhom, gum*, and that
pronunciation may be written in dialogue:

## 6. Verbs

### 6.1 The shape of the verb

A Dia-thìris verb is cited by its **root**, which is the second person singular imperative (*tog!*
"lift!", *bris!* "break!"), and its **verbal noun** (*togail, briseadh*), which is lexical. The
Dictionary gives both.

The finite verb has three simple tenses (**past, future, conditional**), an **imperative**, a
**relative future**, and an **impersonal** (passive) form of each. There is no simple present:
the present and all aspectual tenses are periphrastic, built on *bi* + a particle + the verbal
noun (*tha mi a' togail* "I am lifting", *tha mi air togail* "I have lifted"); those belong to
*Sentences* §2.

Every tense has an **independent** form, used when the verb begins its clause or follows *ma* or
the relative *a*, and a **dependent** form, used after the particles *cha, an, nach, gun, mura*
and the like. It is the old distinction of **absolute** and **conjunct** forms, which Dia-thìris keeps
in the future (*togaidh / tog*) and in the irregular verbs (*chunnaic / faca*), and marks in the
past with the particle *do*.

Person is marked by a following pronoun (**analytic** forms: *thog mi, thog thu*), except in a
set of **synthetic** forms where the ending carries the person: the first person singular and
plural of the conditional (*thogainn, thogamaid*) and of the imperative (*togam, togamaid*).
The older tongue had synthetic forms throughout, and these few are what is left of them.
**Dia-thìris uses the synthetic forms as the norm** and does not add a pronoun to them: *thogainn* "I would lift", not *\*thogainn mi*.

### 6.2 The regular verb: full paradigms

Four models: *tog* "lift, build" (broad), *bris* "break" (slender), *òl* "drink" (vowel-initial)
and *fàg* "leave" (*f*-initial). The ending is broad or slender by the last vowel of the root;
the initial is lenited in the independent past and conditional, with *dh'*
before a vowel and before *fh* + vowel (*dh'fhàg*); before *fh* + consonant the lenited form stands
alone (*fhreagair* "answered", as in the tale of Fionn; *fhliuch* "wetted").

**Past**

| | *tog* | *bris* | *òl* | *fàg* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| independent | *thog mi, thog thu, thog e, thog i, thog sinn, thog sibh, thog iad* | *bhris mi …* | *dh'òl mi …* | *dh'fhàg mi …* |
| dependent (after *cha, an, nach, gun, mura*) | *do thog*: *cha do thog, an do thog?* | *do bhris* | *do dh'òl* | *do dh'fhàg* |
| impersonal | *thogadh* "was lifted"; dep. *do thogadh* | *bhriseadh*; *do bhriseadh* | *dh'òladh*; *do dh'òladh* | *dh'fhàgadh*; *do dh'fhàgadh* |

**Future** (also the habitual present: *òlaidh mi uisce gach latha* "I drink water every day")

| | *tog* | *bris* | *òl* | *fàg* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| independent | *togaidh mi, togaidh tu, togaidh e …* | *brisidh mi …* | *òlaidh mi …* | *fàgaidh mi …* |
| dependent | *tog*: *cha tog, an tog?, nach tog, gun tog* | *bris*: *cha bhris, am bris?* | *òl*: *chan òl, an òl?* | *fàg*: *chan fhàg, am fàg?* |
| relative | *a thogas* | *a bhriseas* | *a dh'òlas* | *a dh'fhàgas* |
| impersonal | *togar* (indep. and dep.): *cha togar* | *brisear* | *òlar* | *fàgar* |

**Conditional**

| | *tog* | *bris* | *òl* | *fàg* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. | *thogainn* | *bhrisinn* | *dh'òlainn* | *dh'fhàgainn* |
| 2 sg. | *thogadh tu* | *bhriseadh tu* | *dh'òladh tu* | *dh'fhàgadh tu* |
| 3 sg. | *thogadh e, i* | *bhriseadh e, i* | *dh'òladh e, i* | *dh'fhàgadh e, i* |
| 1 pl. | *thogamaid* | *bhriseamaid* | *dh'òlamaid* | *dh'fhàgamaid* |
| 2 pl. | *thogadh sibh* | *bhriseadh sibh* | *dh'òladh sibh* | *dh'fhàgadh sibh* |
| 3 pl. | *thogadh iad* | *bhriseadh iad* | *dh'òladh iad* | *dh'fhàgadh iad* |
| impersonal | *thogtadh* | *bhristeadh* | *dh'òltadh* | *dh'fhàgtadh* |
| dependent | *togainn, togadh, togamaid, togtadh* | *brisinn, briseadh, briseamaid, bristeadh* | *òlainn, òladh, òlamaid, òltadh* | *fàgainn, fàgadh, fàgamaid, fàgtadh* |

The conditional is also the **habitual past**, the tense of the tales: *sheasadh i aig an doras*
"she would stand at the door", *thigeadh e às an loch* "it would come out of the loch".

**Imperative**

| | *tog* | *bris* | *òl* | *fàg* |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 sg. "let me …" | *togam* | *briseam* | *òlam* | *fàgam* |
| 2 sg. | *tog* | *bris* | *òl* | *fàg* |
| 3 sg. "let him/her …" | *togadh e, i* | *briseadh e, i* | *òladh e, i* | *fàgadh e, i* |
| 1 pl. "let us …" | *togamaid* | *briseamaid* | *òlamaid* | *fàgamaid* |
| 2 pl./polite | *togaibh* | *brisibh* | *òlaibh* | *fàgaibh* |
| 3 pl. | *togadh iad* | *briseadh iad* | *òladh iad* | *fàgadh iad* |
| impersonal "let it be …" | *togtar* | *bristear* | *òltar* | *fàgtar* |
| negative | *na tog, na togadh e, na togamaid, na togaibh* | *na bris* | *na h-òl* | *na fàg* |

The past impersonal and the conditional third person are spelled alike (*bhriseadh* "was broken"
and "would break"); context and the dependent forms (*do bhriseadh* against *briseadh*) tell them
apart. The relative takes
*dh'* like the past before a vowel or *fh* + vowel: *a dh'òlas, ma dh'èisteas tu* (tale of the Old
Ones), *nuair a dh'fhaighnicheas* (tale of the Tuathaich).

### 6.3 Stem types

**Verbs in *-ich*** are slender throughout: *ceannaich* "buy": *cheannaich, ceannaichidh,
cheannaicheadh, cheannaichinn, cheannaicheamaid, a cheannaicheas, ceannaichear, cheannaichteadh*.
Their verbal noun is usually *-achadh* (§6.6), and their participle *-ichte*.

**Syncopating verbs.** Roots of two syllables whose second vowel is short and unstressed lose it
before a vowel-initial ending, and the consonant cluster then takes the class of the first
vowel:

| root | meaning | future | conditional | 2 pl. imperative | verbal noun |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| *caidil* | sleep | *cadlaidh* | *chadladh, chadlainn* | *cadlaibh* | *cadal* |
| *foscail* | open | *fosclaidh* | *dh'fhoscladh* | *fosclaibh* | *foscladh* |
| *innis* | tell | *innsidh* | *dh'innseadh* | *innsibh* | *innse* |
| *bruidhinn* | speak | *bruidhnidh* | *bhruidhneadh* | *bruidhnibh* | *bruidhinn* |
| *freagair* | answer | *freagraidh* | *fhreagradh* | *freagraibh* | *freagairt* |
| *tachair* | happen | *tachraidh* | *thachradh* | — | *tachairt* |
| *labhair* | speak (formal) | *labhraidh* | *labhradh* | *labhraibh* | *labhairt* |
| *iomair* | row | *iomraidh* | *dh'iomradh* | *iomraibh* | *iomradh* |

These eight and a dozen more
(*ceangail, fuascail, seachain, coisinn, siubhail, tadhail…*) syncopate before every
vowel-initial ending, and keep the full root in the past, the 2 sg. imperative and the
consonant-initial impersonals (*chaidil, caidil, chaidilteadh*).

**Verbs in a long vowel or *-gh*** add endings without change: *suidh* "sit": *shuidh, suidhidh*;
*nigh* "wash": *nighidh, nigheadh*; *dìrich* "climb" is an *-ich* verb.

### 6.4 The impersonal (autonomous) forms

Every tense has an impersonal form, "one lifts / it is lifted", with no subject; the agent, if
named, follows *le*. They are the old passive of the tongue, and Dia-thìris uses them in every
register: *Chan fhacas iad a-riamh tuilleadh* "they were never seen again"; *cha d'
fhuaireadh sceul orra* "no trace of them was found"; *rugadh mi ann an Seann Dunn* "I was born in
Seann Dunn"; *gum faicte ròn mòr glas* "that a big grey seal would be seen".

| tense | ending | *tog* | *bris* |
|---|---|---|---|
| past | *-adh / -eadh* + *L* | *thogadh* (dep. *do thogadh*) | *bhriseadh* (dep. *do bhriseadh*) |
| future | *-ar / -ear* | *togar* | *brisear* |
| conditional | *-tadh / -teadh* + *L* | *thogtadh* (dep. *togtadh*) | *bhristeadh* (dep. *bristeadh*) |
| imperative | *-tar / -tear* | *togtar* | *bristear* |

The pronoun object of an impersonal stays a pronoun: *thogadh e* "it was lifted", *bhriseadh
iad* "they were broken". The periphrastic passive with *rach* (*chaidh a dhèanamh* "it was made")
and with *bi* + *air* (*tha e air a dhèanamh* "it has been made") are in *Sentences* §4.

### 6.5 The relative future

After the relative particle *a* ("who, which, that"), after *ma* "if", and after the conjunctions
built on the relative (*nuair a* "when", *mar a* "as", *cho fad 's a* "as long as"), the future is
the **relative future** in *-as/-eas*, lenited: *an duine a sheasas* "the man who will stand",
*ma thogas tu* "if you lift", *nuair a dh'fhaighnicheas clann nan Tuathach* "when the children of
the Tuathaich ask", *cho fad 's a bhios gual fon talamh* "as long as there is coal under the
ground". The irregular verbs use their independent future (§6.9): *a nì, a chì, a thèid*.
The older tongue had relative forms in *-as* in the present; they have lived on in the future, and
Dia-thìris uses them wherever a relative future is possible; the plain future after *a*
(*\*an duine a seasaidh*) is not Dia-thìris.

### 6.6 Verbal noun formation

The verbal noun names the action and does the work of the non-finite verb (*Sentences* §2).
It is lexical, but most verbs fall into these classes:

| class | formation | examples |
|---|---|---|
| **-adh / -eadh** | root + *-adh/-eadh*; the largest class, and the default for new verbs | *bris → briseadh, pòs → pòsadh, glan → glanadh, bual → bualadh, scrìobh → scrìobhadh, loisc → loscadh, dùisc → dùscadh, dùin → dùnadh, las → lasadh* |
| **-amh / -eamh** | root + *-amh/-eamh* | *seas → seasamh, dèan → dèanamh, caith → caitheamh, feith → feitheamh* |
| **-ail / -eil** | root + *-ail/-eil* | *fàg → fàgail, tog → togail, gabh → gabhail, cùm → cumail, leig → leigeil, tilg → tilgeil* |
| **-tainn / -tinn** | root + *-tainn/-tinn* | *cluinn → cluinntinn, lean → leantainn, fan → fantainn, seall → sealltainn, can → cantainn, tuit → tuiteam* (*-eam*, an old variant) |
| **-sinn** | root + *-sinn* | *tuig → tuigsinn, creid → creidsinn, ruig → ruigsinn, trèig → trèigsinn* |
| **-inn / -ainn** | root + *-inn/-ainn* | *faic → faicinn, faigh → faighinn, feuch → feuchainn, tig → tighinn* |
| **-achadh / -eachadh** | *-ich* verbs: *-ich → -achadh/-eachadh* | *cruinnich → cruinneachadh, smaoinich → smaoineachadh, ionnsaich → ionnsachadh, tòisich → tòiseachadh, coinnich → coinneachadh, sàraich → sàrachadh, bàsaich → bàsachadh, soilleirich → soilleireachadh* |
| **-ach, -eachd, -igh** | *-ich* verbs, older short forms | *ceannaich → ceannach, fuirich → fuireach, falaich → falach, coisich → coiseachd, èirich → èirigh, dìrich → dìreadh* |
| **-achd / -eachd** | | *èist → èisteachd, marcaich → marcachd* |
| **-e** | root + *-e* | *ith → ithe, nigh → nighe, suidh → suidhe, guidh → guidhe, innis → innse* |
| **-aidh / -airt / -al / -ad** | smaller classes | *iarr → iarraidh, freagair → freagairt, tachair → tachairt, labhair → labhairt, caidil → cadal, ceangail → ceangal, gluais → gluasad* |
| **zero** | verbal noun = root | *òl, ruith, fàs, snàmh, seinn, leum, dìon, gairm, cluich, falbh, ràn* |
| **irregular** | | *rach → dol, thig → tighinn, abair → ràdh, thoir → toirt, beir → breith, bi → bhith, dèan → dèanamh, faic → faicinn, faigh → faighinn* |

**Older verbal nouns.** Where a verbal noun might have been levelled to a commoner pattern, Dia-thìris
keeps the older one: *fantainn* "staying" (from *fan*), *cantainn* "saying" (from *can*), *labhairt*
"speaking" (from *labhair*), *sealltainn* "looking", *trèigsinn* "forsaking". *Fuireach* (from
*fuirich*) is the usual word for "living, dwelling"; *fantainn* is "waiting, remaining".

**The genitive of the verbal noun** is used after another noun and as the object of a verbal noun:
*-adh → -aidh/-idh* (*àm a' bhrisidh*; *seòmar-dìridh* "ascent-room", from *dìreadh*),
*-achadh → -achaidh* (*àm a' chruinneachaidh*), *-amh → -aimh* (*ionad-feithimh*), *-ail*
unchanged (*obair-togail* "building work"). See §3.5.

### 6.7 Participles

The **past participle** ("broken, lifted") is the root + *-te* (*-ta* after some broad roots),
with *-ichte* for *-ich* verbs. It is an adjective: it lenites after a feminine noun and does not
take a plural ending.

| root | participle | root | participle |
|---|---|---|---|
| *bris* | *briste* "broken" | *pòs* | *pòsta* "married" |
| *dùin* | *dùinte* "shut" | *dèan* | *dèanta* "done, made" |
| *caill* | *caillte* "lost" | *reòth* | *reòta* "frozen" |
| *loisc* | *loiscte* "burnt" | *toll* | *tollta* "holed" |
| *scrìobh* | *scrìobhte* "written" | *abair* | *ràite* "said" |
| *foscail* | *foscailte* "open" | *croch* | *crochte* "hanged" |
| *ceannaich* | *ceannaichte* "bought" | *sàraich* | *sàraichte* "oppressed" |
| *tog* | *togte* "built" | *cruinnich* | *cruinnichte* "gathered" |

*An cnoc loiscte* "the burnt hill" (tale of the coal-blood), *bean phòsta* "a married woman", *bròg
thollta* "a holed shoe" (hyphenated, *bròg-thollta*, the kenning for "flat tyre"). The ending is written *-te* after
a broad consonant too (*togte, pòsta* beside *loiscte*); the ending stands outside *caol le caol*.

Two other adjectival forms of the verb:

- **Gerundive** with *ri* + verbal noun: *ri fhaicinn* "to be seen", *ri reic* "for sale", *ri
  dèanamh* "to be done".
- **Potential** with *so-* "easy to" and *do-* "hard/impossible to" (§9.5): *so-dhèanta* "feasible",
  *do-dhèanta* "impossible", *so-thuigsinn* "easy to understand".

### 6.8 Preverbal particles and their mutations

The particles are syntax (*Sentences* §3.3–3.4); their effect on the verb's form is
morphology:

| particle | meaning | takes | mutation | examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| *cha, chan* | not | dependent | *L*, but not *d, t*; *chan* before a vowel and lenited *f* | *cha bhris, cha sheas, cha dèan, cha tèid, chan òl, chan fhaic, cha do thog, cha tuirt* |
| *an, am* | question | dependent | none (*am* before *b, f, m, p*) | *an tog?, am bris?, am faic?, an do thog?* |
| *nach* | negative question; "that … not" | dependent | none | *nach tog?, nach eil?* |
| *gun, gum* | that | dependent | none | *gun tog, gum bris, gun do thog* |
| *mura, mur* | if not | dependent | none | *mura tog, mura do thog* |
| *ma* | if | independent (relative future) | *L* | *ma thogas tu, ma dh'èisteas tu, ma chaidh* |
| *nan, nam* | if (unreal) | conditional, dependent | none | *nan togadh tu, nam biodh* |
| *a* | who, which | independent (relative future) | *L* | *a thogas, a bhios, a thàinig* |
| *na* | don't | imperative | *h-* before vowel | *na tog, na h-òl* |
| *mus, gus* | before, until | dependent | none (*gus an* + dependent) | *mus tig an stoirm, gus an do shoilleirich an latha* |

### 6.9 The irregular verbs

Eleven verbs are irregular: *bi* and ten others. Most of them build their tenses on more than one
stem, old roots of different shape brought together under one meaning (*faic*: past *chunnaic*,
future *chì*). The tables below give every
form: past, future, conditional (1 sg., 1 pl. and other persons), relative, the imperative of
every person, and the impersonals.
In each table, **indep.** is the independent form and **dep.** the dependent.

**bi "be"**

## 7. The copula

The copula *is* links two noun phrases and fronts a stressed element; *bi* is used for location,
state and existence (*Sentences* §1.2–1.4). The copula has two tense forms: a **present**
(also used for the future) and a **past** that is also the conditional. It is unstressed and has
no verbal noun or imperative. The older tongue kept past and conditional apart; Dia-thìris has one
form, *bu*, for both.

### 7.1 Forms

| | present, future | past, conditional |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | *is*, *'s* | *bu* + *L*; *b'* before a vowel and *fh* + vowel |
| negative | *chan* (before vowels, pronouns and most nouns), *cha* before a consonant in fixed phrases (*cha toil leam*) | *cha bu* + *L*; *cha b'* |
| question | *an*, *am* (before labials) | *am bu*, *am b'* |
| negative question | *nach* | *nach bu*, *nach b'* |
| "that" | *gur*, *gur e* | *gum bu*, *gum b'* |
| "that … not" | *nach* | *nach bu* |
| "if" | *mas* (*mas e*), *ma 's* | *nam bu*, *nam b'* |
| "if not" | *mur*, *mura* | *mura bu* |
| relative (affirmative) | *as*, *'s* | *a bu*, *a b'* |
| relative (negative) | *nach* | *nach bu* |
| "because" | *a chionn 's gur* | *a chionn 's gum bu* |

### 7.2 The copula with pronouns

When the predicate is a definite noun or a pronoun, the copula is followed by a third-person
pronoun (*e, i, iad*) that anticipates it: *Is i Aisling an ceannard* "Aisling is the leader"; *Is
e seo an sceul* "this is the tale"; *Chan e mise a rinn e* "it was not I who did it". With an
emphatic personal pronoun the copula takes it directly: *Is mise Eilidh*, *Is tusa an t-oide*.

| | present | past/conditional |
|---|---|---|
| affirmative | *is e, is i, is iad; is mise, is tusa* | *b' e, b' i, b' iad; bu mhise, bu tusa* |
| negative | *chan e, chan i, chan iad* | *cha b' e, cha b' i, cha b' iad* |
| question | *an e?, an i?, an iad?* | *am b' e?, am b' i?* |
| answers | *'S e.* / *Chan e.* | *B' e.* / *Cha b' e.* |

### 7.3 Fixed copula phrases

Many common predicates are an adjective or noun with the copula and a preposition. Their tense
is carried by the copula alone:

| present | past/conditional | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| *is toil leam* | *bu toil leam* | I like / would like |
| *is fheàrr leam* | *b' fheàrr leam* | I prefer / would prefer |
| *is urrainn dhomh* | *b' urrainn dhomh* | I can / could |
| *is aithne dhomh* | *b' aithne dhomh* | I know (a person, a place) |
| *is fheudar dhomh* | *b' fheudar dhomh* | I must / had to |
| *is còir dhomh* | *bu chòir dhomh* | I ought |
| *is leamsa e* | *bu leamsa e* | it is mine / was mine |
| *is math sin* | *bu mhath sin* | that is good / would be good |
| *is dòcha* | *bu dòcha* | perhaps / it was likely |
| *is annsa leam* | *b' annsa leam* | I love best |

*Bu toil leam* keeps *toil* plain by long usage (*bu thoil leam* is also heard). The
superlative of adjectives is a relative copula clause (*as motha, a bu mhotha*, §4.4). Clefts
with *is ann* (*is ann dhutsa a bha am bradan an dàn*) and *is e … a* belong to *Sentences* §1.6.

## 8. Numerals

### 8.1 Cardinals: the decimal count

The numerals have a **counting form**, used alone (in counting, arithmetic, telling the time),
and an **attributive form**, used before a noun. The counting form of 1–19 takes the particle *a*
(*a h-* before a vowel).

| 4 | *a ceithir* | *ceithir* | plural |

| 9 | *a naoi* | *naoi* | plural |
| 10 | *a deich* | *deich* | plural |

| 12 | *a dhà dheug* | *dà … dheug* | *dà bhàta dheug* (*dheug* is lenited after the dual) |
| 13–19 | *a trì deug …* | *trì … deug* | *trì bàtaichean deug* (see below) |
| 20 | *fichead* | *fichead* | singular: *fichead bàta* |
| 30 | *trithead* | *trithead* | singular |

| 100 | *ceud* | *ceud* | singular: *ceud bliadhna* |
| 1000 | *mìle* | *mìle* | singular: *mìle long* |
| 1,000,000 | *millean* | *millean* | singular |

**Tens and units.** Dia-thìris joins a ten and a unit without a conjunction: *fichead aon* 21,
*ceathrad còig* 45, *naochad naoi* 99. No *'s* "and" stands between them, as it does in the count
by twenties (§8.2); the two are simply set side by side. The noun follows the whole numeral in the singular: *ceathrad còig bàta*.

**13–19.** A noun between the unit and *deug* stands in the plural in careful speech and in
writing, as it has been of old: *trì bàtaichean deug, còig coin deug*.
The singular (*trì bàta deug*) is heard in everyday speech, and is the only form for the nouns that
stay singular after every numeral, such as *bliadhna, latha*.

**Hundreds and thousands.** *Ceud* and *mìle* are nouns: *dà cheud* 200 (lenited after *dà*),
*trì ceud* 300, *ochd ceud* 800; *dà mhìle* 2000, *trì mìle*. Compound numbers run from largest
to smallest: *mìle ochd ceud ceathrad ochd* 1848. A noun after them is singular: *trì ceud
bliadhna*.

### 8.2 The vigesimal count

The older count by twenties is understood everywhere and used for ages, in old texts and in
tales (*The grammar in brief* §10). Twenty is the unit, and the units above it join with *air* + lenited
*fhichead* ("on twenty"):

| | vigesimal | with a noun |
|---|---|---|
| 20 | *fichead* | *fichead bliadhna* |
| 21 | *aon air fhichead* | *aon bhliadhna air fhichead* |
| 25 | *còig air fhichead* | *còig bliadhna fichead* (noun inside) |
| 30 | *deich air fhichead* | *deich bliadhna fichead* |
| 40 | *dà fhichead* | *dà fhichead bliadhna* |
| 50 | *dà fhichead 's a deich* | *dà fhichead bliadhna 's a deich* |
| 60 | *trì fichead* | *trì fichead bliadhna* |
| 80 | *ceithir fichead* | |
| 100 | *ceud* (older *còig fichead*) | |

*Tha e trì fichead bliadhna 's a deich a dh'aois* "he is seventy years old". *Bliadhna* and *latha*
stay singular after every numeral (§3.9).

| May | *an Cèitean* | *den Chèitean* | November | *an t-Samhain* (f.) | *den t-Samhain* |
| June | *an t-Ògmhios* | *den Ògmhios* | December | *an Dùbhlachd* (f.) | *den Dùbhlachd* |

A date is the ordinal + *latha* + *de*: *an ceathramh latha den Ghiblean* "the fourth of April";
*Diluain, an treas latha deug den t-Samhain* "Monday the thirteenth of November". A year: *anns a'
bhliadhna mìle ochd ceud ceathrad ochd* "in the year 1848", as the old count of the Diosal
wrote it. A year of one of the eras now in use takes the era after it in the genitive: *anns a'
bhliadhna fichead seachd de Linn an Dubhain* "in the year 27 of the Dubhan Era", written *DE 27*. Genitives of the months appear in *mìos na Samhna* "the month of November",
*Latha Bealltainn* "May Day".

Time of day uses *uair* "hour": *aig uair* "at one o'clock", *aig dà uair*, *aig trì uairean*,
*leth-uair an dèidh ceithir* "half past four", *cairteal gu còig* "a quarter to five".

## 9. Derivational morphology

Dia-thìris builds words from its own stems with its own suffixes and prefixes, and by compounding.
Every derived word obeys *caol le caol* inside itself, so suffixes come in broad and slender
pairs chosen by the last vowel of the stem.

### 9.1 Agent nouns

All are masculine; a woman is named with *ban-* (§9.5) or with *bean-*.

| suffix | from | examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-air / -(e)ir* | nouns | *iascair* "fisherman" (*iasc*), *sealgair* "hunter" (*sealg*), *dorsair* "doorkeeper" (*doras*), *clàrsair* "harper" (*clàrsach*) |
| *-adair / -eadair* | verbs and nouns | *scrìobhadair* "writer", *creachadair* "raider" (*creach*), *sealbhadair* "owner" (*sealbh*), *dealbhadair* "painter", *lìonadair* "filler" |
| *-aiche / -iche* | verbs, esp. *-ich* verbs | *suathaiche* "grazer" (*suath*), *stiùiriche* "steerer" (*stiùir*), *rannsaiche* "searcher" (*rannsaich*), *cuiriche* "sower" (*cuir*), *bàthaiche* "drowner" (*bàth*) |
| *-aire / -eire* | verbs | *slugaire* "swallower" (*sluig*), *cladhaire* "digger" (*cladhaich*), *snìomhaire* "twister" (*snìomh*) |
| *-ach / -each* | places, qualities | *Dia-thìreach, Tuathach, coigreach* "stranger" |
| periphrastic | *fear-, bean-, neach-, luchd-* + genitive or participle | *fear-pòsta* "husband", *bean-phòsta* "wife", *neach-cuibhrinn* "stakeholder", *luchd-turais* "travellers" |

*Fear-* and *neach-* compounds pluralise as *luchd-* (*luchd-turais*) or by the head (*fir-phòsta*).
The agent suffixes are the most productive in the kennings (§10): *suathaiche-nèimh,
slugaire-solais, bàthaiche-teine, cuiriche-òir, cladhaire-chnàmh*.

### 9.2 Abstract nouns

| suffix | gender | from | examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| *-as / -eas* | m. | adjectives | *sonas* "happiness" (*sona*), *donas* "evil" (*dona*), *gliocas* "wisdom" (*glic*), *maitheas* "goodness" (*math*), *ceartas* "justice" (*ceart*), *càirdeas* "friendship" (*caraid*) |
| *-adas / -eadas* | m. | adjectives | *dorchadas* "darkness" (*dorcha*), *bòidheachas* "beauty" |
| *-achd / -eachd* | f. | nouns, adjectives, verbs | *rìoghachd* "kingdom" (*rìgh*), *bàrdachd* "poetry" (*bàrd*), *sceulachd* "storytelling, tale" (*sceul*), *fàisneachd* "prophecy", *coiseachd* "walking", *marcachd* "riding" |
| *-e* | f. | adjectives (= the comparative form) | *gile* "whiteness", *òige* "youth", *doimhne* "depth", *àirde* "height" |
| *-(e)achas* | m. | nouns | *iascachas* "fishery", *tuathanachas* "farming" |
| *-alachd / -eileachd* | f. | adjectives in *-ail, -ach* | *càirdeileachd* "friendliness", *feumalachd* "usefulness" |

### 9.3 Adjective-forming suffixes

| suffix | meaning | examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-ach / -each* | having, full of | *creagach* "rocky" (*creag*), *salach* "dirty" (*sal*), *feumach* "needy" (*feum*), *gaolach* "loving" (*gaol*), *craobhach* "wooded" |
| *-ail / -eil* | like, pertaining to | *fearail* "manly", *càirdeil* "friendly", *rìoghail* "royal", *làitheil* "daily", *bliadhnail* "yearly", *dùthchasail* "native" |
| *-mhor* | abounding in | *lìonmhor* "numerous" (*lìon* "fill"), *brìghmhor* "full of substance", *neartmhor* "mighty", *ceòlmhor* "musical", *cumhachdmhor* "powerful" |
| *-te / -ta* | participle (§6.7) | *briste, pòsta, loiscte, tollta* |
| *so- / do-* | prefix: easy / hard to (§9.5) | *so-dhèanta, do-dhèanta* |

*-mhor* is written the same after either class of vowel and stands outside *caol le caol*; it was
once a word of its own, *mòr* "great", and keeps the freedom of one.

### 9.4 Diminutives

| suffix | gender | examples |
|---|---|---|
| *-an / -ean* | m. | *lochan* "small loch", *bothan* "hut, cottage" (*both*), *cnocan* "hillock", *sruthan* "brook", *leabhran* "booklet", *balachan* "little boy" |
| *-ag / -eag* | f. | *caileag* "girl" (old *caile*), *bileag* "leaflet, ticket" (*bile* "leaf"), *clachag* "pebble", *cuileag* "fly", *duilleag* "leaf, page" |

The diminutive takes its own gender regardless of the base: *loch* m. → *lochan* m., *clach* f. →
*clachag* f., but *bile* m. → *bileag* f. Names take *-an* as an endearment and a patronymic
suffix: *Dubhan* (*dubh*), *Fionnan* (*fionn*), *Ciaran*. The kennings use *bileag* for
papers of every kind (*bileag-turais* "ticket", *bileag-lùghdachaidh* "coupon").

### 9.5 Prefixes

All the productive prefixes lenite what follows (with the usual exceptions, §1.2) and are
hyphenated, except a few fused long ago (*banacharaid* "female friend", *aineolach*
"ignorant", *comhairle* "counsel").

| prefix | meaning | examples |
|---|---|---|
| *mì-* | un-, mis-, bad | *mì-thoilichte* "unhappy", *mì-fhortan* "misfortune", *mì-chinnteach* "uncertain", *mì-rùn* "ill will" |
| *neo-* | not, un- | *neo-chiontach* "innocent", *neo-bhàsmhor* "immortal", *neo-ar-thaing* "no thanks needed"; *neoni* "nothing, zero" (fused) |
| *ath-* | re-, again, second | *ath-bhreith* "rebirth", *ath-shealladh* "second sight, déjà vu", *ath-ghleusadh* "re-tuning", *ath-bheothachadh* "revival" |
| *ban-* | female | *ban-rìgh* "queen", *ban-dia* "goddess", *ban-ghaisceach* "heroine", *ban-tighearna* "lady" (dental block after *n*) |
| *co-, comh-* | together, co- | *co-ogha* "cousin", *co-dhùnadh* "decision", *co-chomhairle* "consultation", *comh-fhreagairt* "correspondence" |
| *so-* | easy to, -able | *so-dhèanta* "feasible", *so-thuigsinn* "easily understood", *so-ruighinn* "accessible" |
| *do-* | hard to, un-…-able | *do-dhèanta* "impossible", *do-thuigsinn* "incomprehensible" |
| *fìor-* | true, genuine | *fìor-uisce* "spring water", *fìor-ghlan* "pure" |
| *ro-* | fore-, pre-; too | *ro-ràdh* "preface", *ro-shealladh* "foresight", *ro-mhath* "very good" |
| *fo-* | under, sub- | *fo-cheàrd* "under-craftsman", *fo-bhrìgh* "hidden meaning", *fo-thalamh* "underground" |
| *eadar-* | inter-, between | *eadar-theangachadh* "translation", *eadar-mheadhanach* "intermediate" |
| *dì-* | privative, de- | *dì-cheannadh* "beheading", *dì-mheas* "contempt" |
| *eu-, ea-* | negative | *eu-comasach* "impossible", *eu-dòchas* "despair" |
| *àrd-, prìomh-, sàr-, leth-* | chief, principal, excellent, half | §4.5 |

### 9.6 Compounding

Dia-thìris has two kinds of compound, and both are old.

**1. Close compounds (modifier first).** An adjective, a preposed noun or a prefix comes first and
lenites the head: *àrd-rìgh* "high king", *prìomh-bhaile* "chief town", *mòr-shluagh* "multitude",
*garbh-chrìochan* "rough bounds", *dubh-ghorm* "dark blue", *deagh-ghean* "goodwill". The head
(second element) gives the gender and takes the endings: *na h-àrd-rìghrean*, *prìomh-bhailtean*.
The dental block applies loosely: free phrases keep it (*seann taigh*), fixed names may not
(*Seann-Dhaoine*, §1.4).

**2. Loose compounds (head first).** A noun is followed by a genitive, a verbal noun in the
genitive, or an adjective, and the pair is hyphenated once it names one thing: *taigh-òsta* "inn"
(house of hosting), *each-uisce* "water-horse", *bean-phòsta* "wife", *craiceann-ròin* "seal-skin"
(pl. *craicnean-ròin*), *clach-chuimhne* "memorial stone", *taigh-freiceadain* "watch-house". This
is Dia-thìris's main word-building pattern and the pattern of every kenning (§10).

- The **head** (first element) gives the gender and takes every inflection: *an t-each-uisce, an
  eich-uisce* (gen.), *na h-eich-uisce*; *stad a' charbaid-iarainn* "the stop of the train".
- The **describer** is fixed in its genitive (or adjective) form, but it follows the lenition
  rules of §3.5, §3.8 and §4.1 for the head: after a feminine head an adjective is lenited
  (*cèir-chruaidh*) and a genitive often is (*clach-chuimhne, fuil-ghuail*); after any head an
  indefinite genitive plural is lenited (*seanchas-dhaoine*); after a slender plural head the
  describer lenites (*eich-chuibhle*).
- **Hyphens** join the elements of a compound that names one thing; a free phrase is written
  apart (*clach mhòr* "a big stone", *clach-mhòr* would be a name). *Caol le caol* binds
  each hyphenated part separately, since it holds only inside one word.
- **Stress** in a loose compound falls on both elements, the heavier on the describer
  (*taigh-ÒSTA*); in a close compound on the first (*ÀRD-rìgh*). See *The sounds*.

## 10. The kennings: how Dia-thìris names new things

### 10.1 How the kennings came about

Dia-thìr had no word for most of what the humans brought when they came, in the first years of the
Anchor Era, and later the kingdom needed words for things of its own making. Dia-thìris took few of the
humans' words for these things (*The grammar in brief* §13). For most of them its speakers made a
name as the language had long named the *each-uisce* and the *taigh-òsta*: a loose compound of old
roots that says what the thing does or what it is like. Such a name is called a kenning. The
Dictionary lists 1,634 of them, each with its literal sense.

In its forms a kenning is an ordinary **head-first loose compound** (§9.6): a head noun, a
describer in the genitive (or an adjective, a genitive plural, a prepositional phrase),
hyphenated. It inflects through its head, pluralises through its head, and takes the head's
gender. A small number of heads and describers recur, each carrying a fixed meaning, and this is
what makes a new kenning easy to understand the first time it is heard.

### 10.2 How a new kenning is made

1. **Old roots.** Speakers build from words long in the language, the words of the tales and of
   the bards (*nèamh, speur, teine, gual, iarann, guth, dealbh, cuimhne, siubhal, suain, sceul*),
   and from old borrowings for things known before about AE 20 (*leabhar, cofaidh*). Roots that
   came in with the humans' learning (*tele-, fòn-, eileactr-, meatair*) are not used.
2. **Head first, then the describer**, hyphenated, the describer in the genitive where the grammar
   wants it.
3. **Two elements, three at most.** Three-element kennings use a preposition or the article
   inside: *teine-gun-lasair, gille-nan-soithichean*.
4. **Each part obeys *caol le caol*.**
5. **Mutation by the compound rules** (§9.6): the describer is lenited after a feminine head when
   it is an adjective (and optionally when it is a genitive singular), and after any head when it
   is an indefinite genitive plural.

### 10.5 Inflecting a kenning

A kenning is a noun, and declines by its head (§3). Taking *carbad-iarainn* m. "train" and
*inntinn-iarainn* f. "computer":

| | *carbad-iarainn* | *inntinn-iarainn* |
|---|---|---|
| nom. sg. | *an carbad-iarainn* | *an inntinn-iarainn* |
| gen. sg. | *a' charbaid-iarainn* (*stad a' charbaid-iarainn* "the train stop") | *na h-inntinne-iarainn* |
| dat. sg. | *air a' charbad-iarainn* | *air an inntinn-iarainn* |
| nom. pl. | *na carbadan-iarainn* | *na h-inntinnean-iarainn* |
| gen. pl. | *nan carbadan-iarainn* | *nan inntinnean-iarainn* |
| with a possessive | *mo charbad-iarainn* | *m' inntinn-iarainn* |
| with an adjective | *carbad-iarainn mòr* | *inntinn-iarainn mhòr* |

A kenning can itself become a head or a describer. *Carbad* gives *stiùiriche-carbaid* "driver"
and *bothan-carbaid* "garage"; *dealanach* gives *carbad-dealanaich* and *tobar-dealanaich*
"socket". So the kennings have become a living way of making words. The heads and describers of
§10.4 are as good as suffixes with fixed meanings, and a speaker names a new thing by choosing a
head for what it is and a describer for what it does.
