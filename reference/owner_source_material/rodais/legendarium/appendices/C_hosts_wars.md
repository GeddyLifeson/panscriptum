## Appendix C — Hosts, Wars and Calamities

Here are gathered out of the annals every war and armed struggle of which they tell, the hosts that stand on the map today, and the calamities that have fallen on the island, whether they came from the sea, the ground, the weather or the vein. Nothing is added that the annals do not hold. Where the annals disagree, the entries are given as they stand; where the record is sealed, or was never made, that is said, and no more.

Much is missing, and the reader should know how much. The Age of Ailean cut its dead on tally stones and did not say what killed them. The Age of Sundering left two histories of its wars, and one was written to praise a king. For the Age of Strangers the custodians' rolls, the Mission's lists and the Residency's letter-books seldom agree in their numbers. The complete record of the Long War is sealed at Leabharlann Muileann chaol until the fifty years run out ({{date:VI-0128}}).

### Part I. The Wars

### Before the wars

The oldest deed of force that is remembered was no war, and who did it is not known. On {{date:I-0058}} the single gate of {{place:marker:31}} was blocked with rubble from the inside, by people who left by another way or did not leave. More than a century later the cistern of {{place:marker:32}}, which had served four hundred people, was found choked with the bones of whole cattle ({{date:I-0060}}). Both were works of the Seann-Dhaoine. What was fought over there, and by whom, no chronicler has ventured to guess.

The first war in either of the oral king-lists is **the salt-flats raid**. On {{date:I-0099}} Mòrag Chruaidh, fifth of the Stone Kings, led the hill-folk down to the salt-pans at {{place:burg:170}} and carried the salt away. Seven of the hill side died, and the telling keeps the number. Her successor Iain Dubh went down to repeat the raid and was killed on the shingle ({{date:I-0104}}). The list says only that he was brought home. Of the other wars the king rode to over the island, the lists keep no count, only that he came home from all of them but the last.

**Blàr Àth na Fala**, the Battle of the Ford of Blood, closed the Age of Ailean and opened the Holy Age. The households of {{place:burg:422}} and the east, who had sworn to owe nothing to Dùn ìseal or its king, came against Ailean Mòr at the ford below Dùn dhearg, and he held it alone three days and three nights, until the dead of the host dammed the river. He fell on the third night, on {{date:I-0098a}}, worn down by the whole host; no one man overcame him. His kin, camped about Dùn ìseal and quarrelling over the shares they looked to have of him, did not come. One son, Ìomhar Dearg, came late, on the morning after, and fell on the spent host and took Dùn dhearg; and so the first share of the kingdom was won with a sword before any share was given.

### Aimhreit nan Oighrean, the strife of the heirs

*Cause.* At the king's death some seven hundred heirs of his blood stood up to claim at Dùn ìseal ({{date:II-0001a}}). They bound the vein in common and gave it to no one of the blood ({{date:II-0001}}), and parted the rest of the kingdom by *Roinn na Rìoghachd* ({{date:II-0001b}}): nine great shares for the nine eldest lines, a portion of the dead out of all nine for the lines of the king's dead children, and the Red Share of Ìomhar Dearg at Dùn dhearg let stand as he had taken it. Every share was parted again into countries, provinces, counties and holdings, and many holdings owed oaths to two lords, some to three ({{date:II-0001d}}). At every death of a holder the share was parted again. No part of the settlement was ever wholly agreed, and every part of it was a cause.

*Sides.* First the line of Murchadh Mòr, who held the great share of the north, against the line of Ìomhar Dearg; afterward nearly every line of the king's blood against some other, and the lines changed sides by marriage. The Hall at Dùn ìseal took no side in any of it, and its ground was the one place no feud could follow a man.

*Course.* The first blood was shed at {{place:burg:77}}, on the line between the north and the Red Share, when the men of two great-grandsons of the king met over a boundary stone and Aonghas Bàn mac Mhurchaidh was killed ({{date:II-0001c}}). The strife ran after that for some five hundred and fifty years, in raids, burnings and killings between kin, few of them large and none of them forgotten, for the men who began a feud might live a thousand years to remember it. Ìomhar Dearg himself was killed by a cousin at a ford in the Hall's second century. The waypoint houses made the road-peace against it ({{date:II-0038}}). The last and worst of it was **Cogadh an Dà Mhionn**, the War of the Two Oaths, which began when a marriage between Eithne nic Ailein's line at {{place:burg:23}} and the line of Ìomhar failed, and each side called on every holding sworn to both to fight for it. It went on in fits for some forty years. It ended at {{place:burg:395}}, where Fearghas Dà-Mhionn, lord of the granary fields and sworn to both sides, burned his own harvest rather than feed either host, and died in his hall ({{date:II-0064b}}). The middle of the island went hungry that winter.

*End.* The lords of the shares swore *Sìth an Stairsnich*, the Peace of the Threshold, on the threshold of Talla na Lasrach at {{place:burg:315}}, beside the stone cup, with the Keeper for witness ({{date:II-0064c}}). No share was to be parted again at a death, no one was to swear to two lords, and the boundary stones were to stand where they stood. Eithne nic Ailein, last living of the nine who had taken the great shares, swore first. After the Peace the shares began to gather again, and the first to gather them was Goraidh mac Ìomhair ({{date:II-0076a}}), who became the first rìgh after Ailean ({{date:II-0077}}).

### The War of the Roads

*Cause.* In the Holy Age the carriers' road ran from the mountain to the sea, and its tolls went to the rìgh of Dùn dhearg. Lachlann Ciar, head of the leading family of the harbour town of {{place:burg:1}}, began to keep the southern tolls for himself and send none of them to Dùn dhearg ({{date:II-0091}}). Iain Garbh, son of Sìleas Dhonn, took the hall at {{place:burg:422}} and demanded the western tolls, and Lachlann Ciar sent him back a basket of fish-bones ({{date:II-0092}}).

*Sides.* On the one side stood Iain Garbh of Dùn dhearg, with the road-guards and the lowland levies; on the other Lachlann Ciar and the men of Caol leathan. The Hall at Dùn ìseal refused to bless either side.

*Course.* On {{date:II-0093}} Iain Garbh marched west and burned the toll-house at {{place:burg:151}}, which Lachlann Ciar's people had taken. The annals call this the first war on the island since the Peace of the Threshold, and the first fought over a road; every war since the Roinn had been fought over a share. At the ford of {{place:burg:500}} the men of Caol leathan held Iain Garbh's levies for a day before they gave way ({{date:II-0095}}). Forty lay dead of the two sides together, and long afterward the carriers called it the ford of the forty. Falling back, Lachlann Ciar's men burned {{place:burg:45}} so that the levies would find no grain there ({{date:II-0096}}). Its people walked to Cathair dhomhain, and many never came home. {{place:burg:106}} shut its gate against both armies and fed the burned-out people of Baile ghlas through the winter ({{date:II-0097}}). Neither side forgave it, and neither attacked it. Iain Garbh then closed Caol leathan from the landward side for a season, but its boats brought in fish and he could not close the harbour. His levies drifted home for the harvest, and the siege ended without an assault ({{date:II-0098}}).

*End.* The two leaders met under the roof of the waypoint house that would later be called {{place:marker:5}}, where by the road-peace neither of them could draw ({{date:II-0099}}). Caol leathan kept half its tolls and sent the other half to Dùn dhearg, and Lachlann Ciar swore to the Law with the Keeper as witness. So the war ended in a house built to feed carriers. Muileann òg was rebuilt as a market town, and from its fair came {{place:marker:38}} ({{date:II-0130}}).

### Lesser strife of the Holy Age

The **Silver Feud** ({{date:II-0151}}) was a closing of the roads, and no war. For three seasons no silver left {{place:burg:419}}. The road-guards went unpaid and the eastern tolls went uncollected. It has always been called a feud, though no one was killed in it. At sea, outlaw boats from the eastern islets began raiding the fishing strands. Sliochd Raghnaill armed its boats at Seann Tarr and burned two of them ({{date:II-0175}}), and the rest were never caught. Late in the age raiders from the north-western waters took a season's dried fish from the north-western strands. The sting-sworn merchants of Ros bheag paid for two armed boats to watch the shore, and the raiders moved north ({{date:II-0201}}).

### The War of the Three Claimants

*Cause.* King Tormod Bàn's two sons were drowned together when a ferry overturned on Abhainn fhionn ({{date:III-0102}}). The king died at Cathair dhearg without naming an heir ({{date:III-0103}}), and three claims were laid before the houses at once.

*Sides.* The claimants were Catrìona nic Aonghais of Inis thais, the late king's niece; Dùghall Garbh of Baile Mòr dhomhain, his half-brother; and Raghnall Ruadh, head of Clann Raghnaill and master of the vein-house above Muileann dhearg ({{date:III-0104}}). The seven houses divided among the three. {{place:burg:64}}, the western port nearest the capital, shut its gates to all three and declared for none ({{date:III-0105}}). It was twice besieged in the war, and taken by neither claimant.

*Course.* Dùghall's host broke Raghnall's at the ford of {{place:burg:32}} on {{date:III-0106}}. The custody-book counts some three hundred dead, the most of them drowned in the ford. Raghnall's men burned {{place:burg:316}} and its granary as they fell back ({{date:III-0107}}), and the villagers spent the winter at Cathair mhòr on the charity of Macha's priests. Catrìona besieged {{place:burg:26}}, which had declared for Dùghall, and took it after forty days, when the dead of the siege lines had poisoned its wells ({{date:III-0108}}). She let the garrison go free on their oath. Raghnall, who held the vein-house, then refused the measured handful of coal to every hearth and temple in the shires that stood against him ({{date:III-0109}}). Hearths that had burned coal for generations went cold. By the judgement of the annals this deed turned more against him than any battle did. Catrìona and Dùghall joined their hosts against him and met Raghnall below {{place:burg:354}} on {{date:III-0110}}. Raghnall was killed on the field, and his own custodians opened the vein-house the next day.

*End.* Catrìona was crowned at Cathair dhearg on {{date:III-0112}} and was known afterward as Catrìona Bhuadhach. The war had lasted some ten years. Her first great law bound the custodians themselves: no claimant, house or king could withhold the handful from a lawful hearth ({{date:III-0113}}). Seann Warr's neutral gates were later honoured by the crown ({{date:III-0115}}). Seònaid nic Coinnich's *Book of the Three Claimants* ({{date:III-0117}}) is the oldest history on Dia-thìr told as a single tale, and not as a list of entries.

### The sea-thieves of the north-west

Boats working out of the small islands off the north-west coast began to take fish and grain from coasting traders ({{date:III-0131}}). The thieves were Dia-thìrich, men of the north-west shore, and the harbour rolls give some of their names. A force sent by Queen Eilidh Bhàn landed on {{place:feature:3}} and burned their boats on the strand ({{date:III-0133}}). Their leader, Uilleam Ruadh, was taken to Cathair dhearg and hanged.

### The Brothers' War, Cogadh nam Bràithrean

*Cause.* King Ailean mac Raghnaill died at Cathair dhearg leaving two sons by different mothers, Iain Ciar and Alasdair Bàn, and each of them had been told that he was the heir ({{date:III-0176}}).

*Sides.* The seven houses divided four to three between the brothers.

*Course.* Alasdair Bàn's men seized {{place:burg:318}}, and Iain Ciar took it back on {{date:III-0177}}. The war is named from that battle. Alasdair's ships burned the waterfront of {{place:burg:30}}, which held for his brother; the fire took the rope stores and the granary, and the town had to beg its bread from Cathair mhòr ({{date:III-0178}}). Iain Ciar defeated Alasdair below {{place:burg:360}} on {{date:III-0179}}. Alasdair was taken alive, and his captains were allowed to go home.

*End.* The brothers made peace at {{place:marker:10}}, on neutral ground between their lands ({{date:III-0180}}). Alasdair gave up his claim and was given the lordship of Seann Chwen for life. Iain Ciar was crowned. Alasdair kept the peace until his death ({{date:III-0181}}), and his lordship went back to the crown. Iain Ciar, having seen Ceann mhin beg for bread, ordered every shire seat to keep a year's grain in store against the next war ({{date:III-0182}}). The only surviving account of the war was written by his clerk Pàdraig mac Ruairidh ({{date:III-0183}}). It praises the king at every turn, and later readers have found it a trial.

### The humans' campaigns and the forerunners of war

For the most part of the Age of Strangers the fighting was at sea. Raiders stripped a Company brig off the north-west coast ({{date:IV-0050}}) and burned a coal ship off the east ({{date:IV-0108}}). The Company's first armed reply was a gunboat that burned every boat on the shingle of a cove near {{place:burg:126}} ({{date:IV-0109}}). Most of them belonged to the town's own fishermen, and the raiders were not found.

On land the Administration kept no standing force until Charles Merriman brought a company of armed constables ({{date:IV-0259}}). They broke the dockers' strike at Ros dhomhain ({{date:IV-0263}}). After the Rending the roll tells of the first bloodshed between the two peoples ({{date:IV-0270}}), which its fuller entry sets at Muileann chrom ({{date:IV-0274}}): a gallery drowned seven when the pumps were stopped to save fuel, the crews stoned the Company's agent, and the constables fired. Four Dia-thìrich and one constable died. Merriman answered the second petition by tripling the constabulary and fortifying the stores of four mining towns ({{date:IV-0282}}). The roadside houses became waypoints for a militia with no name ({{date:IV-0290}}). The first stoppage held in concert across the pits was broken by the closing of the Company stores ({{date:IV-0308}}). After the confiscation edict ({{date:IV-0316}}) the silver guild began buying rifles from the eastern pirates ({{date:IV-0320}}), a council for the mining regions met in the sacred forest at Cnoc bheag ({{date:IV-0322}}), and Strake ordered its arrest ({{date:IV-0323}}).

### The War of the Hills

Here it is called the War of the Hills, after the country where it began. Later rolls call it the Severance war, after the manner of its ending.

*Cause.* On {{date:IV-0324}} the Dia-thìrich of several mining regions rose together for the first time, and grievance became open war. The grievances are those told above, and the one most often named is the confiscation edict.

*Sides.* With the Dia-thìrich stood the council of custodians and crew-leaders under Eilidh nic Raghnaill, with the crews of the camps, the Lamp-Watch, the silver guild of Muileann chiar and its riflemen, the riflemen of Cnoc leathan ({{date:IV-0340}}), the company of the Hawk order's faithful mustered at Seann Skell ({{date:IV-0349}}), and the towns that declared for the council one after another. With the Administration stood Commissioner Strake, the constabulary, the Company's guards, and the Company's steamships and coasters. Some towns held aloof. The human town of {{place:burg:476}} shut its gates and raised a militia that it sent to neither side ({{date:IV-0346}}). The concession town of {{place:burg:54}} closed its road against both sides for the rest of the war ({{date:IV-0347}}). Ros dhomhain, the Dia-thìrich city below the Residency, neither joined the rising nor stood against it until late in the war.

*Course.* The war opened in the hills. Crews under Mòrag nic Iain rushed the store at Muileann chrom at first light, and its eleven constables surrendered without a shot ({{date:IV-0325}}). Crews sank two barges across the mouth of the coal quay at {{place:burg:351}} ({{date:IV-0326}}). At {{place:burg:44}} the constables held their walled pithead for eleven days and surrendered on the twelfth, when their water ran out, and Eilidh nic Raghnaill sent them to the coast unharmed ({{date:IV-0327}}). The northern pits rose ({{date:IV-0328}}), and {{place:marker:10}} shut its gates across the coal road, which stayed cut ({{date:IV-0329}}). The capital rose next and barricaded Drochaid Cathair dhearg with coal-carts ({{date:IV-0331}}), and Strake drew the Administration into the Residency above Ros dhomhain ({{date:IV-0334}}).

The first relief column, under Major Peter Ashdown, was met at the ford of {{place:burg:163}} on {{date:IV-0335}}. Ashdown lost forty men and his baggage. The rebels lost nineteen, and the council's roll names every one. Dùn chrom shut its gates behind the retreating column and nursed the wounded it left ({{date:IV-0336}}). In the east the silver town rose ({{date:IV-0337}}), and the post at {{place:burg:315}} surrendered after one exchange of fire ({{date:IV-0338}}).

The humans struck back from the sea. A Company steamship came up the Abhainn uaine and shelled the coal stage at {{place:burg:313}} and burned the town behind it, killing twenty-three ({{date:IV-0342}}). On {{date:IV-0344}} fishing boats from {{place:burg:36}} and Ros ruadh burned a Company coaster at anchor. The keepers of the southern beacons put out their fires, and two Company ships were lost on the reefs ({{date:IV-0350}}).

Through the first winter neither side could come at the other ({{date:IV-0352}}), and the capital went hungry ({{date:IV-0354}}). While the war stood still the rebels burned the tithe ledgers in three raids, at Ros dhomhain, Ceann àrsaidh and Muileann chrom ({{date:IV-0355}}). The Administration's last sally toward the mining country was driven back at {{place:burg:392}} on {{date:IV-0359}}. The Ros dhomhain harbour quarter opened its gates to the council ({{date:IV-0360}}), Strake left the Residency by water ({{date:IV-0362}}), and {{place:burg:63}} became the last seat of the Administration ({{date:IV-0363}}), where the human families went hungry within months ({{date:IV-0365}}).

*End.* The last ship recorded from an Tìr Thall came to Ros bheag with a sealed letter and left on the next tide ({{date:IV-0368}}). Envoys met at {{place:burg:304}} and agreed a truce ({{date:IV-0369}}). The humans still on Dia-thìr would go into the north-west under the council's safe-conduct, and the council would leave the concession towns standing. The columns went north ({{date:IV-0371}}) and camped a night at {{place:burg:346}} ({{date:IV-0373}}), where several of the humans wrote down the name of the place. Strake sailed for an Tìr Thall in the last ship able to make the crossing ({{date:IV-0374}}). On {{date:IV-0377}} the war ended, and the crossing itself was closed. That is the Severance. The war-companies were stood down, but no act ever abolished them ({{date:V-0002}}).

### Raiders in the Age of the Kingdom

After the Severance, ships flying no flag, some crewed by men who had served the Administration, raided the coast off Cathair gheal ({{date:V-0055}}). The council sent no help. In the end the fishermen armed their own boats and drove the raiders off, and were fined for bearing arms without licence ({{date:V-0085}}). Not until the patrol boats were refitted for dubhan could they outrun the raiders ({{date:VI-0043}}).

### An Cogadh Fada, the Long War

*Cause.* Some of the northern towns would not take the place made for them in the new trade ({{date:VI-0054}}), and went on working the last coal seams sooner than hang on one man's fuel. When the Mine Board's northern revenue fell by a quarter ({{date:VI-0063}}), it declared the western seams Crown property and ordered the pits closed. Enforcers were posted at {{place:burg:489}} ({{date:VI-0065}}), the first armed company sent north since the Severance, and there followed skirmishes too small to name ({{date:VI-0068}}). Ruth Calder's offer to pay the Crown's fifth in coal went unanswered ({{date:VI-0071}}). The order to muster raised the regiments again ({{date:VI-0075}}) under Somhairle mac Lachlainn ({{date:VI-0076}}), and the Moot's last petition was filed ({{date:VI-0077}}).

*Sides.* For the kingdom, in the reign of Cathal, were the Board's enforcers, the regiments raised again, and later the fleets, all running on dubhan. Against them were the holdouts of five western towns, Àth leathan, Muileann àrsaidh, Cnoc thais, Àth naomh and Tobar dhearg, with Ruth Calder and Tom Varley named among them. The Moot stood apart and fed the hungry.

*Course.* Arms went west by the coal line ({{date:VI-0078}}). The hewers gathered at Àth leathan ({{date:VI-0079}}), and the regiments marched west ({{date:VI-0080}}). The **Battle of Àth leathan**, fought on {{date:VI-0081}} at {{place:marker:18}}, was the first great battle of the war. The open record counts only the kingdom's dead. The Tuathaich tell of the holdouts' dead in the hundreds, and Ruth Calder's name is not found in the open record again ({{date:VI-0082}}). The holdouts fell back into the hills ({{date:VI-0083}}). The regiments besieged Muileann àrsaidh, which held for weeks on stored coal and potatoes ({{date:VI-0091}}). {{place:burg:269}} was taken after its road was cut ({{date:VI-0098}}), and {{place:burg:372}} and Tobar dhearg opened their roads without a siege ({{date:VI-0109}}). In the autumn of the next year a column of several hundred holdouts came down out of the hills and went south-east toward the capital ({{date:VI-0111}}). Why it went is written only in the sealed archive. The Tuathaich name its leader Owen Barrow; the open record names no one. It made camp by {{place:burg:360}}, a day's walk from the capital ({{date:VI-0113}}), and the first and third regiments were brought down the fair road to meet it. The **Battle of Muileann ghlas**, fought on {{date:VI-0115}} at {{place:marker:17}}, was the second and last great battle of the war. The open record gives no count of the column's dead ({{date:VI-0116}}). It was the second battle fought below Muileann ghlas of which the annals tell; the first was fought there in the Brothers' War.

*End.* When word of the battle reached the hills, the last holdouts came down to the seventh regiment's posts and were disarmed and sent home ({{date:VI-0117}}). An Cogadh Fada ended on {{date:VI-0118}}, and nine regiments and four fleets remained on their stations. By the Board's order the western seams were flooded and capped ({{date:VI-0122}}). The wartime tithe ({{date:VI-0086}}) was carried on in the Treasury's accounts, though no minute of the council shows that anyone decided to keep it ({{date:VI-0131}}). Tuathaich families built a cairn on the field of Àth leathan ({{date:VI-0130}}). The kingdom raised a stone with the regiments' dead at Cathair mhòr ({{date:VI-0126}}).

### Part II. The Hosts

### The older hosts

Until the Long War the island kept no standing host. In the strife of the heirs the lords of the shares fought with their households and sworn men, seldom more than a few score to a side, and a holding sworn to two lords sent its spears to whichever called first. Later in the Holy Age the rìgh of Dùn dhearg fought with road-guards and with levies raised from the lowland farms, and those levies went home at harvest whether the war was done or not ({{date:II-0098}}). The claimants and the brothers of the Age of Sundering fought with the followings of the seven houses. The queen who burned the sea-thieves' boats sent a force raised for that errand alone ({{date:III-0133}}). In the Age of Strangers the only standing force on the island belonged to the humans: Merriman's constables and the Company's guards and gunboats. The Dia-thìrich fought the War of the Hills with companies raised by the council, the silver guild and the hawk-houses. Those companies were stood down in {{year:V-0002}} and kept only as names on a roll. For ninety years the kingdom's regiments stood on paper alone, and its coast was guarded by patrol boats. The first armed company it sent north after the Severance was a company of Board enforcers ({{date:VI-0065}}).

### The regiments and fleets

The thirteen hosts on the map were all raised in {{year:VI-0084}}, the first year of An Cogadh Fada, and none has been stood down ({{date:VI-0120}}). Each takes its name from the town where it is stationed. The numbers do not follow the order of raising: the seventh regiment was raised first and the ninth last, and the fleets in the order first, fourth, second and third. Why they were so numbered is not told. The rolls of the Severance companies were read out when the regiments were raised again ({{date:VI-0076}}), and the numbers may have come from those rolls; but the open record is silent. Two of the towns named Seann Skell have hosts. The seventh regiment stands at the western one. The ninth regiment and the second fleet stand at the one on the southern coast. The regimental clerks tell them apart by river and coast ({{date:VI-0110}}).

The regimental rolls of the present year count 1,673 foot, 905 riflemen and 696 horse in the nine regiments, with twenty-three guns. The first regiment is the largest, at 545 men, and the eighth and ninth are the smallest, at about 255 each. The horse are kept for the hill roads of the west and the north, where lorries cannot go and the dubhan depots are far apart. The four fleets are small. The first and second have two hulls each and the third and fourth one each, with about a hundred men to a hull.

### Part III. The Calamities

### The five stricken districts

The map shades five districts, each for the calamity that gave it a name. All five fell in the Age of Strangers or after, and in each of them the annals show that the same ground had been struck before.

**A' Phlàigh Uaine, the Green Death.** *Struck:* {{place:burg:87}}, {{place:burg:450}}, {{place:burg:25}}, {{place:burg:479}}, {{place:burg:336}}, {{place:burg:20}}, {{place:burg:274}}, {{place:burg:223}}, {{place:burg:371}}, all but the last two in the shire of Cnoc bheag. From {{date:IV-0152}} the Green Death went through the mining camps. The coal dust was blamed, but no inquiry into it was ever brought to an end. At {{place:burg:135}}, where it was first noticed, forty were buried in the first month in a field still called the green acre ({{date:IV-0153}}). It spread along the coal roads to these nine towns and to twenty-six settlements in all, and the custodians' roll leaves a blank line after the last ({{date:IV-0154}}). Àth ghlas burned the huts of its dead and was free of it soonest ({{date:IV-0156}}). No reckoning of all the dead survives. After it came the first strike, at Baile thais, over the galleries where the sickness had been worst ({{date:IV-0166}}).

**Gorta Baile chrom, the Great Hunger.** *Struck:* {{place:burg:305}}, {{place:burg:472}}, {{place:burg:143}}, {{place:burg:445}}, {{place:burg:335}}, {{place:burg:242}}, all Tuathaich towns today, in the shires of Ceann mhòr, Muileann àrsaidh, Baile chrom and Cnoc thais ({{date:IV-0195a}}). The oldest hunger on Dia-thìr with a place to it was here, in pots of boiled bark and doubled grave-rows ({{date:I-0134}}), and a dearth emptied the same shore in the Age of Sundering ({{date:III-0134}}). The Great Hunger began on {{date:IV-0194}}, when the harvest failed in thirty-four settlements; and it was the worse because the levy had taken so many that too few were left to work the land. It was worst at Baile chrom, whose people could see the new beacon from their empty fields ({{date:IV-0195}}); the Administration did not dispute the count. At Seann Bhrenn the grain-pits of the Holy Age were opened and held only dust ({{date:IV-0198}}). The Levy was suspended too late for the harvest and never repealed ({{date:IV-0200}}).

**Tiormachd Achadh shean, the Long Drought.** *Struck:* {{place:burg:411}}, {{place:burg:344}}, {{place:burg:13}}, {{place:burg:78}}, in the shires of Achadh shean and Doire fhionn. The springs of Achadh shean failed in the Age of Ailean ({{date:I-0135}}), and dry years came again in the Holy Age ({{date:II-0237}}) and in the Age of Sundering ({{date:III-0200}}); each time the shared grain brought the district through. The Long Drought began on {{date:IV-0217}} and held three years. Fewer than half of Achadh shean's cattle reached water ({{date:IV-0218}}), and the custodians counted ten districts as drought country while the Administration kept no count ({{date:IV-0219}}). The fever of bad water that followed killed seventeen at Baile Mòr ruadh ({{date:IV-0224}}). When dry years returned in the Age of the Kingdom the council sent grain up the coal line, and none died of hunger ({{date:V-0154}}).

**Scàineadh Cill ghlas, the Rending.** *Struck:* {{place:burg:123}}, {{place:burg:228}}, {{place:burg:389}}, in the shires of Muileann dhearg, Muileann chiar and Muileann dhomhain. The ground here has moved in every age: a crack across the valley ({{date:I-0106}}), cracked wells ({{date:III-0082}}), a fallen gallery with fourteen names on a stone at its mouth ({{date:III-0144}}). The Rending came on {{date:IV-0269}}, while a common working was being driven with blasting-powder. It killed dozens, swallowed the old cutting and its shrine ({{date:IV-0271}}), and closed the district for a generation behind the Company's fence ({{date:IV-0272}}). The displaced built Achadh chaol on the edge of the fenced ground ({{date:IV-0273}}). The district was reopened ({{date:V-0067}}), and on {{date:V-0141}} a gallery under Muileann dhearg gave way along a line the surveyors had called sound, and thirty-one hewers died. It was, the annals note, the same fault.

**Tonn Mhòr Cnoc chaol, the Great Wave.** *Struck:* {{place:burg:364}}, {{place:burg:190}}, {{place:burg:150}}, {{place:burg:90}}, on the coasts of the shires of Seann Vell, Cnoc naomh and Ros gharbh. Sea sand a hand thick lies over a village of the Age of Ailean at Cnoc chaol ({{date:I-0105}}), a swell took two children there in the Age of Sundering ({{date:III-0126}}), and Cuan dhearg was twice flooded before it built its sea-wall ({{date:III-0229}}). The Great Wave struck without warning on {{date:V-0017}} and wrecked the three towns in one night. It was the first calamity the new kingdom met by its own means. The relief parties counted a little over forty dead ({{date:V-0018}}). The wave ran on up the gulf to Ros gharbh and broke its quays, though the town on its headland lost few ({{date:V-0018}}). Cuan dhearg's harbour was gone and its boats lay in the fields. The shepherds of {{place:feature:14}} did not go back ({{date:V-0022}}).

### Other calamities of the annals

**Plagues.** A coughing sickness killed some ten at {{place:burg:191}} ({{date:III-0054}}); a spotted fever some forty at {{place:burg:92}}, where the Fianna of the Stone nursed the sick and many of them died of it ({{date:III-0118}}); the swallowing cough near a third of {{place:burg:466}} ({{date:III-0158}}). The strangers' fever came ashore with a crowded ship and killed ninety-one Dia-thìrich and eleven humans ({{date:IV-0034}}). The spotted pox at Ros dhomhain killed four hundred and six by the Mission's count, and more by the custodians' ({{date:IV-0096}}). The grey fever ({{date:IV-0276}}) killed the young and strong more readily than the old, and in the north-west put Dia-thìrich and humans in the same wards for the first time ({{date:IV-0287}}).

**Famines.** The first hunger of the Holy Age with a place is the winter after the burning of Achadh mhòr ({{date:II-0064b}}), whose dead the Hall counted with the dead of the war. The first famine known to have been turned aside ({{date:II-0179}}) was the first trial of the shared-meal custom, and the custom held. Beyond the Great Hunger the annals record hunger in the capital and in Ros bheag during the War of the Hills ({{date:IV-0354}}, {{date:IV-0365}}), and the north's first year alone, when Tuathaich memory puts the dead at Doire ghlas above two hundred and no Dia-thìrich count was made ({{date:V-0004}}).

**Storms, floods and fires.** The first storm given a name is remembered only for the naming ({{date:I-0086}}); the oldest whose name survives is A' Ghaoth Dhubh ({{date:I-0159}}). Am Fuath Mòr drowned eighteen at Seann Toll ({{date:III-0066}}), a storm drowned twenty-three at Baile ghorm ({{date:III-0227}}), and another eleven Tuathaich off Cuan bheag ({{date:V-0082}}). The Abhainn ìseal cut the coal line for most of a year ({{date:V-0130}}). Fire burned Ceann mhin twice ({{date:III-0207}}) and the fair at Muileann òg once ({{date:V-0146}}).

**The vein's own dead.** The mountain has killed those who worked it in every age: from the basket burning at Achadh dhìreach ({{date:I-0111}}) to the first deaths laid to the humans' pace of work, eleven of them, entered as an accident ({{date:IV-0071}}).
