## Appendix G — The Goods and Markets of Dia-thìr

Here is set down what the island grows, digs, catches and makes; the sixteen market towns through which nearly all of it is bought and sold; the chief roads of trade between them; and the dues the Treasury takes on that trade. The account of the present day is drawn from the Treasury's market roll for the last full year, which the clerks of the market-due keep in each of the sixteen market towns and send to Cathair dhearg, and from the books of the fair-court at Muileann òg. The older history comes from the annals, and where a trade has a first date in them the date is given.

The roll counts goods in the measures the markets use: grain, fish, clay and sand by the wain; timber by the pile; stone by the pallet; coal by the wagon; silver and gold by the bar; cattle, sheep, horses and oxen by the head; oil, dubhan, wine, honey and tar by the barrel; salt and dyes by the bag; cloth by the bolt; books by the volume. Prices are given in the realm-coin.

### I. The Countries of the Island and What They Grow

Dia-thìr is a wet and windy island, mild for so northerly a place, and most of it is forest and moor. Its husbandry follows the lie of five countries.

**The high bare north.** Along the northern coast from Ceann mhòr to Seann Tarr, and inland over the moors behind the Hawk bay, the ground is bare and windswept, with snow on the high moor in winter. Yet it grows more than its look promises. Barley and oats are sown in every sheltered hollow, hemp grows in the valley bottoms, and the heather feeds bees and sheep. The shore is the richer half. The northern and north-western waters hold the island's herring grounds and its whales, and amber is gathered from the wrack on the strands of the Hawk bay and the north-west. The market towns of this country are {{place:burg:143}}, {{place:burg:132}}, {{place:burg:113}}, {{place:burg:160}} and {{place:burg:34}}.

**The birch and pine hills.** The largest single country on the island is the belt of birch, pine and hill pasture that runs from the western plain across the middle of Dia-thìr into the eastern hills. It is cattle and sheep country before all else, and it gives besides timber, venison, honey and a dark wine pressed from blaeberry and bramble. Almost all of the island's coal-bearing ground lies in these hills: the silver of {{place:marker:2}} and of the hills of Cuan ghlas, the copper and tin of Ceann àrsaidh, Cnoc bhàn and Doire fhionn, the gemstones of the Muileann dhearg hills, the marble of Baile Mòr dhomhain, the old spoil-heaps where dubhan is made for the forges, and the charcoal woods that fed the forges before it. Copper was little worked before the Age of the Kingdom. The humans shipped theirs in for the first coinage ({{date:IV-0053}}), and the first great working is the copper-town of Àth fhiadhaich ({{date:V-0206}}). The market towns of the hills are {{place:burg:273}}, {{place:burg:76}}, {{place:burg:315}}, {{place:burg:136}} and {{place:burg:63}}.

**The wet western and southern woods.** South and west of the capital the forest is oak, alder and hazel, dripping for half the year. Timber, game, clay, white sand, hemp and honey are its chief goods, with barley on the cleared ground. The potteries and glass-houses stand in this country because its clay and sand are the best on Dia-thìr, and the dyers of the south take their colours from its plants. Its market towns are {{place:burg:204}}, {{place:burg:297}}, {{place:burg:233}} and {{place:burg:15}}.

**The oak country of the capital and the east.** Around the capital and along the eastern road the woods are broad-leaved and the soil is deeper. This is the island's best mixed farming: cattle and sheep, grain, wine, hemp, and the horses of the Doire chaol country. Its farms sell through {{place:burg:26}}, {{place:burg:22}} and Dùn ìseal.

**The eastern plain and the mosses.** The open grassland east of Dùn ìseal, where the herders of the Age of Ailean drove their cattle to the warm springs ({{date:I-0224}}), is thin pasture and bees. The wet mosses of the west coast and the western plain give peat, reeds and wildfowl, and at {{place:burg:170}} the salt-pans the Stone Kings fought over ({{date:I-0100}}).

Some goods grow in only one or two places. Flax grows only in the lake-shore fields of Seann Warr, and its seed is pressed there for linseed oil, in which the road-houses were frying their oatcakes as early as {{year:III-0073}}. Tobacco grows only on the old concession farms behind Ceann mhòr, where the humans first planted it ({{date:IV-0122a}}). Gold is washed only from the streams below {{place:burg:384}} and Cnoc dhìreach, and the pearl-mussels lie only in the shallows of the south-eastern coast off Cnoc bhàn and Caol ruadh.

Dia-thìr grows no spices, tea, sugar, silk or dates, and has no camels or elephants. Before the Severance the ships of an Tìr Thall brought small amounts of spice, tea and sugar to the Company's stores. No ship has come from the west since the last one sailed from Ros bheag ({{date:IV-0368}}), and those goods are not sold on the island now.

The vein itself no longer appears in the roll. Coal-fuel from the Sloc Mòr was sold at the fair by the cartload until {{date:VI-0005}}, and the western coal seams sold their coal to the north until the Board closed them. The western seams were flooded and capped in {{year:VI-0122}}, the upper galleries of the Sloc Mòr were closed in {{year:VI-0144}}, and the few baskets of altar-coal cut each year under the Rite's custody are not sold in any market. Dubhan, the fuel that took the coal's place ({{date:VI-0001}}), is a wrought good, and the only one the roll counts among the raw goods. It is made from the spoil of the old workings ({{date:V-0207}}) at the works of Seann Skell on the southern coast and in the east and north ({{date:VI-0154}}). The works sell it by the barrel into the markets for the forges, kilns and glass-houses, which burned charcoal from the hill woods before it, as the charcoal-burners of the eastern woods had burned it since the Age of Sundering ({{date:III-0199}}). Lamps, engines and lorries buy it at the works' own depots and pumps and at the inns that keep a pump ({{date:VI-0029}}), and the wartime tithe on it is taken at the pump by a clerk of the Treasury ({{date:VI-0087}}).

Dubhan is also the one good of Dia-thìr sold off the island. In the present year Dubhan, who made the first working fuel, won from the council the right to export it ({{date:VI-0154b}}), the first leave for any good to go beyond the kingdom's waters since the Severance. The export is held to a measure the Treasury sets each year and is loaded only at the quay of Ros dhomhain, where a clerk of the Treasury takes the tithe on every barrel and the custodians of Òrd Bhrìde seal every hold, so that nothing of the vein goes with it. The kingdom's own depots and the Moot's are served first. In this year dubhan goes abroad under that charter and under no other.

### II. The Wrought Goods

The greater part of the worth that passes through the markets lies in goods made in the towns. In the order of their worth in the last roll they are these.

**Salted and smoked food.** Salt fish, salt meat and smoked meat are made in almost every town and are the largest trade on the island by value. Salting is as old as the fish-kilns of Seann Mhorn ({{date:I-0018}}) and the salt road of the Age of Ailean ({{date:I-0193}}); the mining shires have eaten salt fish in the lean season since {{year:III-0085}}. The biggest salting towns are Cathair mhòr, Dùn ìseal, Seann Dunn and the harbours of the gulf.

**Arms and field-guns.** The forges of the old vein country, of Àth naomh in the west and of Caol ìseal on the north coast smelt bog-iron and bronze into arms, and a few of them cast field-guns. Every market in the kingdom sells arms back to its towns, where the regiments and fleets raised in An Cogadh Fada are stationed. The trade is young. It grew from the Crown's contracts for the regiments ({{date:VI-0132a}}), and arms are now the dearest common goods in every market. The forge-town of {{place:burg:491}}, where Uilleam Gobha made the rebels' grenades ({{date:IV-0330}}), stands in the same hills.

**Books, paper and ink.** Dia-thìr is a country of copyists. Paper has been made from rags and hemp at Muileann chaol since {{year:III-0218}} and at Tobar dhìreach since {{year:V-0077}}, and books are bound and sold in more than eighty towns. The largest trade in books is at Dùn ìseal, Cathair dhomhain, Baile Mòr ruadh, Inis àrsaidh and Dùn chrom.

**Soap and candles.** Soap is boiled from tallow and seal-oil, and candles are dipped from beeswax and tallow, in most towns of the north and the gulf. The wax lights of the ancestor shrines were the first of this trade ({{date:III-0119a}}).

**Ships, sails and rope.** Seagoing ships are built at nine yards: the two Seann Dunns on the east coast, Inis thais, Seann Skell of the Hawk bay, Ros fhionn, Inis ìseal, Ceann leathan, the royal quays of Cathair dhearg and Inis mhòr. Their sails are cut from hemp cloth in the same towns, and their rope comes from the rope-walks of Baile ghlas and Seann Tarr. The first deep keels since the Sundering were measured from the humans' hulls at Inis mhòr ({{date:IV-0017}}).

**Glass and pottery.** Glass is blown from the white sand of Cuan ruadh, Ceann mhòr and the southern Seann Skell in some seventy towns, most of all at Cathair mhòr on the north road, Baile Mòr dhìreach and Seann Vell. The first glass-house on Dia-thìr was built at Baile Mòr dhìreach in {{year:IV-0142a}}. Pottery is older. The red ware of the western road was fired at Baile ghorm and Cnoc àrsaidh in the Holy Age ({{date:II-0243}}, {{date:II-0244}}). Today the largest potteries are at Dùn fhiadhaich, Ros dhìreach and Àth bheag, and Cathair fhada sells their ware to the north.

**Cloth, garments and leather.** Wool is spun and woven in every sheep country. The chartered weavers are those of Baile chiar ({{date:V-0065}}) and the mills of Doire ghlas ({{date:V-0202}}). Hides are tanned at Baile ghorm ({{date:V-0211}}) and Muileann dhomhain ({{date:V-0181}}), and boots and harness are made in most of the market towns.

**Scent.** Scent waters are distilled from bog-myrtle, meadowsweet and heather honey and sold in small glass flasks. This trade belongs to the capital shore and the western plain: Seann Vell, Caol fhiadhaich, Cnoc àrsaidh, Baile ghlas and Doire shean. It began at Seann Vell in the first years of the restored kingdom ({{date:V-0107a}}).

**Drink and cheese.** Barley spirit, the fire-water of the northern road ({{date:II-0196}}), is distilled in some forty towns. Ale is brewed for sale only at Ceann chaol, Muileann fhada and Seann Bhral in the south-east. Cheese is made in the cattle towns, most of all at Ceann leathan and Cathair fhada.

**Coin.** The realm-coin is struck only at the mint at Cathair dhearg ({{date:V-0024}}). The blanks it is struck from are refined in the villages of the eastern hills from the silver of Muileann chiar and the gold of Baile dhìreach ({{date:V-0137a}}), and the Treasury counts those blanks among the goods of the Dùn ìseal market.

The market town holds the market-court, weighs goods on the Crown's weights and keeps the clerk of the market-due. **Ceann leathan** is the largest and dearest market on the island. Its reach runs from the gulf shore up into the old vein country of Muileann chrom, Muileann dhearg and Muileann dhomhain, whose forges now make arms where their pits once sent up coal. It became the market of the southern fishing in the Holy Age ({{date:II-0068}}), and its harbour-master's notched tallies are the oldest tallies kept in the Library. Today the crowds of the Còmhrag keep its prices the highest on Dia-thìr. Grain, timber, honey and wine all cost it nearly twice what they cost on the south coast. It sells nothing to other markets and buys from eight of them: scent from the capital, amber from the Hawk bay and the north-west, pearls from Cnoc bhàn, cattle, sheep and venison from the northern hills, coin-blanks from Dùn ìseal, oxen and linseed oil from the western plain, and seal-oil, whale-oil and dyes from Ceann mhòr.

**Cathair dhomhain** is the market of the capital shore: the three harbour towns of Cathair dhomhain, Ros dhomhain and Baile ghlas, the royal precinct of Cathair dhearg, and the farms of Baile chiar and Àth shean behind them. Its right to its own tolls dates from the rivalry with the new royal seat ({{date:II-0138}}). Its scent-makers and glass-houses make it the chief seller of scent to the rest of the island, and the horses of Doire chaol go from it to three other markets.

**Ros fhionn** has the most towns of any market, fifty-three, from Muileann chaol and the library in the south to Caol bheag on the north coast. The salt that made the town ({{date:III-0079}}, {{date:III-0147}}) left it when the harbour silted ({{date:III-0166}}), but the market-court the salt-masters had set up stayed on the headland ({{date:III-0168a}}), and the hill towns still bring their goods to it. The forge-towns of Baile Mòr uaine and Baile Mòr dhìreach make it the first market in arms. It has no goods of its own to spare for other markets and buys scent, pottery, pearls and amber from them.

**Seann Skell of the Hawk bay** gathers the north-central coast from Doire ghlas and Muileann leathan to Seann Toll. The amber of its strands has been prized since the Age of Ailean ({{date:I-0116a}}), and it is the one good the market sends out, mostly to Ceann leathan. Whale-oil, honey and sheep are its other raw goods. The Tuathaich towns of the Doire ghlas country are in its reach and sell their cloth through it.

**Ros bheag** is the market of the west coast between Cuan shean and the western Seann Skell. Its harbour court was swearing trade oaths on the scorpion's sting by {{year:II-0160}}. Its clay, honey and the bog-iron of Àth naomh are worked into pottery and arms, and its surplus cattle and sheep go north to Seann Skell.

**Seann Tarr** is the smallest of the northern markets in people. It began as the feast-market of the ancestor shrine ({{date:III-0119a}}) and is still busiest at the great feasts. Honey and wax, the dyes of Baile fhiadhaich and the hemp of Doire uaine and Achadh naomh are its goods. The hemp goes to the rope-makers chartered there in {{year:V-0182}}.

**Dùn ìseal** holds the market of the eastern hills under the old Hall ({{date:II-0064a}}). Its reach takes in the silver town, the gold stream of Baile dhìreach, the copper-town of Àth fhiadhaich ({{date:V-0206}}) and the mills of Muileann bhàn and Muileann gheal. It sends coin-blanks and copper to Baile Mòr dhìreach, Ceann leathan and Seann Skell.

**Baile Mòr dhomhain** is the market of the northern hills ({{date:II-0106a}}) and the island's second exporter of livestock. When the coal trade fell away its market day drew more carts than the fair at Muileann òg ({{date:V-0198}}). Cattle, sheep, venison, horses, the marble of its hills ({{date:III-0181a}}) and glass go from it to five other markets.

**Baile Mòr dhìreach** gathers the east coast from Seann Tarr's southern country to Àth bheag. Its shipyards at the two Seann Dunns build more hulls than any other market's, its glass-houses are the oldest on Dia-thìr ({{date:IV-0142a}}), and the dyers of Baile Mòr ruadh and the furriers of Àth ruadh sell through it. It buys cattle, sheep, horses and coin-blanks from the hill markets.

**Baile Mòr fhionn** is the chief market of the western plain. Wool was the measure of every bargain there from the Holy Age ({{date:II-0134a}}) until the second currency reform ({{date:IV-0177}}). Its country grows grain, wine and cattle and breeds the island's horses and draught oxen. The old salt-pans of Baile ghorm and Seann Warr and the flax-fields of Seann Warr are in its reach, and its scent-makers send scent to Cathair fhada and Seann Skell.

**Cathair fhada** is the market of the south. Its charter dates from Queen Sìleas's tally-houses ({{date:III-0070a}}), and its merchants have run the fair at Muileann òg since the fair was first held in the restored kingdom ({{date:V-0088}}). It sends the pottery of Dùn fhiadhaich north to Ros fhionn and Seann Skell. The Crown grain-store built there in {{year:V-0125}} is the reserve of the south.

**Ceann mhòr** is the market of the Tuathaich north-west. It was chartered when the humans' harbour officers had gone ({{date:V-0014c}}), and it keeps its court in the old harbour store. It has the most varied goods of any market: grain, fish, whale-oil and seal-oil ({{date:II-0198}}), amber, dyes, the tobacco of the concession farms and the tools of Cathair gheal. It sends out more kinds of goods than any other market and buys none.

**Àth fhionn** is the market of the north coast from Inis mhòr's country to Caol mhin. The north coast's fish, whales and sheep come to it, and the whale-boats that go out from it learned their trade from the humans ({{date:IV-0127b}}). Its own market dates from the Age of Sundering ({{date:III-0135a}}).

**Muileann fhionn** began as a fort and mill of the human administration and became a Tuathaich market town after the Severance ({{date:V-0044a}}). It is the island's greatest cattle market. Its droves of cattle and sheep go south to Ceann leathan, Cnoc bhàn, Seann Tarr and Caol ruadh, and it buys arms from Ceann mhòr.

**Cnoc bhàn** is the market of the south-eastern farms above the long mill-race of Muileann fhada ({{date:III-0156a}}). It sells pearls, clay and amber to the other markets. Its forge-towns make tools and field-guns, and its towns are the only ones on Dia-thìr that brew ale for sale.

**Caol ruadh** is the youngest and smallest market. It was chartered after An Cogadh Fada to victual the garrisons and the second fleet at the southern Seann Skell ({{date:VI-0120a}}). The glass of Seann Skell and the venison of the strait's woods are its goods.

The **Fèill Muileann òg** is not one of the sixteen. It is a yearly fair with a court of its own ({{date:V-0089}}), held on the old ford-ground where salt was first traded for wool ({{date:I-0113}}). Traders from every shire come to it, and the northern day set aside in {{year:V-0190}} is the only place where Tuathaich goods are sold under their makers' own marks.

### IV. The Roads of Trade

In the last roll the market-courts recorded 8,058 dealings. In 6,235 of them a market sold goods to one of its towns. In 1,759 a town sold its goods into its market. Only 64 were sales from one market to another. Nearly all the trade of Dia-thìr is near at hand. The dealings between markets are few, yet they carry the goods one country has and another lacks, and six roads of them stand out.

1. **Livestock south from the northern hills.** The largest trade between markets is in cattle, sheep, horses and venison. It runs from Muileann fhionn, Baile Mòr dhomhain and the capital shore south and east to Ceann leathan, Baile Mòr dhìreach, Seann Tarr, Cnoc bhàn and Cathair fhada. Its drove roads are the ones the drovers of Àth chiar first marked with cairns ({{date:III-0093}}). The dubhan lorries have now replaced the droves on most of them ({{date:VI-0024}}).
2. **Scent from the capital shore and the western plain.** Scent is the costliest good that goes between markets. It goes from Cathair dhomhain and Baile Mòr fhionn to Ceann leathan, Ros fhionn, Seann Skell and Cathair fhada.
3. **Amber, pearls and whale-oil to the gulf.** The costly things of the coasts go to the dearest market. Amber goes from the Hawk bay and Ceann mhòr, pearls from Cnoc bhàn and Baile Mòr dhìreach, and seal-oil and whale-oil from the north-west, and Ceann leathan takes most of all of them.
4. **Coin-blanks and copper from the eastern hills.** Dùn ìseal sends the blanks of the silver and gold refiners, and the copper of Àth fhiadhaich, to Baile Mòr dhìreach, Ceann leathan and Seann Skell.
5. **Pottery and clay.** Cathair fhada's pottery goes north to Ros fhionn and Seann Skell, and the clay of Cnoc bhàn and Baile Mòr dhìreach goes to the kilns of the north.
6. **Arms from the north-west to the north-western interior.** Ceann mhòr's smiths sell arms to Muileann fhionn, the only road of the arms trade that crosses between markets.

Two things are sold everywhere and seldom carried far: hemp, the cheapest good in every market, and grain. Grain is dearest at Ceann leathan and cheapest at Caol ruadh; hemp is cheapest at Cnoc bhàn.

### V. Taxes and the Treasury

The Crown takes two dues, both set by the council in the first weeks of the kingdom ({{date:V-0015a}}) and collected by the maoir of the shires (Appendix H). The Treasury reckons them in purses of the realm-coin.

**The market-due.** Seventeen in every hundred of the price is taken on every sale a town makes into its market and on every sale between markets. Sales from a market back to its own towns are not charged. The Treasury fixed the due at that rate after An Cogadh Fada and set a clerk in each of the sixteen market towns to take it and keep the market roll ({{date:VI-0131a}}). It is kept apart from the wartime tithe on every sale of dubhan ({{date:VI-0086}}), which is still taken at the pumps ({{date:VI-0087}}) and which the Treasury carried forward after the war as a line of its own under the regiments' pay ({{date:VI-0131}}). In the last roll the towns' sales into their markets were worth about 18,580 purses, the markets' sales back to their towns about 9,700 and the sales between markets about 1,200, and the market-due brought the Treasury 3,193 purses.

**The head-due.** Each household pays the head-due for every head in it. It was first collected on the count of {{year:V-0034}} ({{date:V-0036a}}) and stands at eighteen purses on every hundred thousand heads. It brought in 6,375 purses in the last roll, two parts in three of the Crown's money.

The Treasury's roll for the present year comes to 9,568 purses ({{date:VI-0154a}}). The towns' own chests hold about 12,660 purses together, and the towns' yearly product is about 11,460.

**Earlier taxes and currencies.** The first toll on Dia-thìr was Goraidh Mòr's road-toll of one fish in twenty ({{date:II-0080}}). The silver weights of Muileann chiar gave the carriers' roads a common measure ({{date:II-0064}}), and the Hall's clerks first paid in stamped silver rings ({{date:II-0127}}). In the Age of Sundering Queen Sìleas fixed the firlot of Cathair dhearg as the measure of grain ({{date:III-0069}}) and set up the tally-houses, the first account of trade kept across the whole island ({{date:III-0070}}). Ros fhionn salt was the measure of value in the north-east ({{date:III-0079}}), and wool was the measure on the western plain. The humans' mark and its hundred pence were struck at Ros dhomhain ({{date:IV-0053}}), and three currency reforms pushed barter out ({{date:IV-0052}}, {{date:IV-0176}}, {{date:IV-0253}}). The Company's tithe on coal rose without any matching return ({{date:IV-0130}}) and was extended to grain ({{date:IV-0132}}). The rebels burned the tithe ledgers ({{date:IV-0355}}). The restored kingdom struck its own realm-coin ({{date:V-0023}}) and pegged it to dubhan in {{year:VI-0049}}.
