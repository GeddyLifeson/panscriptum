# Names

Here is set down how the places and peoples of Dia-thìr are named in Dia-thìris: the generic nouns that
stand at the head of a place name, the lenition that marks the word after them, the agreement of
broad and slender vowels, the older names of the Seann-Dhaoine, and the names the peoples bear
themselves. The rest of the grammar is in *The grammar in brief*, *Word forms* and *Sentences*.

## The look of a Dia-thìris name
A name of the island is known at sight. Its consonants are softened with a written *h* (*bh, mh, dh,
gh, ch, sh*); it has the diphthong *ao*; it writes the cluster *sk* as *sc*; and it marks a long
vowel with the **grave** accent (*à è ì ò ù*) and with no other mark, for Dia-thìris has no acute. These
few letters are the face of the island's names on every map and in every roll, and a traveller who
has learned them can tell a Dia-thìris name from any other before he can say it.

## Lenition in names
The second word in a place name is *lenited*: its first consonant is softened, as it is after most
of the generic nouns in ordinary speech. Written as sound changes at the start of a word:

```
m > mh / #_        b > bh / #_        c > ch / #_
p > ph / #_         t > th / #_        d > dh / #_
g > gh / #_          s > sh / #_        f > fh / #_
```

`#_` means "at the start of a word." So *mòr* ("big") becomes *mhòr* after a generic noun:
**Cathair mhòr**, "big city." Three further points hold:

- **Lenited *f* is spelled *fh*, and stays written although it is silent.** *Fiadhaich*
  (wild) lenites to *fhiadhaich*, not *hiadhaich*.
- ***S* does not lenite before *c, p, t, m* or *k*.** The clusters *sc-, sp-, st-, sm-* resist
  mutation, and so does the *sk-* of the Old Ones' names: a word beginning *Sk-* stays *Sk-* and
  never becomes *Sh-*. That is why the name is **Seann Skell**.
- **A lenited proper noun keeps its capital letter**, with the *h* written straight after it:
  *Dunn*, where it is lenited, becomes *Dhunn*, not *dhunn* (after *seann* it is not lenited at
  all; see below).

**"Always lenited" belongs to names.** In everyday Dia-thìris an adjective after a masculine noun is
not lenited (*baile gorm*). Place names such as *Baile ghorm, Cnoc bheag, Dùn thais* keep the
always-lenited form as a fixed usage of naming, handed down from old time (see *The grammar in
brief* §4).

## Caol le caol, leathan le leathan
*Slender with slender, broad with broad* is the first law of Dia-thìris spelling. Vowels fall into two
classes:

- **Broad**: *a, o, u, à, ò, ù*
- **Slender**: *e, i, è, ì*

Inside a single word, any consonant (or cluster of consonants) with a vowel on each side must have
those two vowels of one class. For this reason the endings of the language so often go in pairs, a
broad form and a slender, chosen by what they are joined to. The ending of the small rivers' names
works in the same way: *eas* (a word for a waterfall or rapids) is the slender form, *as* the
broad, and the last vowel of the root decides between them. *Ciar* with the ending gives
**Ciaras**, not *Ciareas*: though the word begins with a slender-looking *i*, the vowel that touches
the ending is the *a* of *-iar*, and *a* is broad.

The law governs only two vowels with a consonant *between* them. Two vowels side by side with
nothing between, a diphthong such as the *ai* in *Àrsaidh*, are free of it.

**Two-word names are exempt.** *Cathair mhòr* need not keep the law, for it binds only within one
written word and says nothing of the meeting of two words in a phrase.

Every one of the island's 157 river names keeps it.

## The names of the Old Ones
Before the Dia-thìrich an older people lived on the island: the **Seann-Dhaoine**, "the Old Ones."
Their names that survive are marked with **Seann**, the old, irregular form of *sean* ("old") that
stands before a noun and lenites it, as in *seann bhaile*, "old town." So a settlement of the Old
Ones reads **Seann Chwen, Seann Bhral**, "old Chwen," "old Bral." The roots themselves (*Bral-,
Cwen-, Tarr-, Vell-, Morn-, Skell-, Brenn-, Toll-, Warr-, Dunn-*) are not Dia-thìris. They are all that
is left of a tongue that came before and did not outlive its speakers except in names; the word
that marks them as old is Dia-thìris, the Dia-thìrich's own way of speaking of them.

**Seann does not lenite *d*, *t* or *s*.** After a word ending in *n* the dentals stay plain, as
in *seann duine*, *seann taigh*. So the names are **Seann Dunn, Seann Tarr, Seann Toll**, while
*Seann Chwen* and *Seann Bhral* are lenited; and by the rule for *sk-* above, **Seann Skell**. The
shires named after these towns take the same forms.

About one port in four still carries this older layer in place of a full Dia-thìris name of generic and
qualifier. The Dia-thìrich did not translate these names; they took them over whole. When fishers
resettled Seann Mhorn in the Holy Age they kept the name they found there, and said that the old
name came with the stones. So it has been with all of them since: kept as they were found, and
never put into Dia-thìris.

## Generic elements (place-name heads)
A Dia-thìris place name is built from a small closed set of generic nouns and a qualifier that
describes: *Cathair dhearg, Muileann chaol, Àth fhada*. The generic follows the kind of settlement
that is named:

| Role | Generics used |
|---|---|
| Capital | Cathair, Dùn |
| City | Cathair, Baile Mòr, Dùn |
| Town | Baile, Àth, Cnoc, Muileann |
| Village | Cill, Tobar, Achadh, Doire |
| Hamlet | Tobar, Cill, Carraig |
| Port (any size) | + Ros, Ceann, Cuan, Caol, Inis |

*(Cathair* = stone fort/city · *Dùn* = fortified hill · *Baile* =
settlement · *Àth* = ford · *Cnoc* = hill · *Muileann* = mill · *Cill* =
holy cell, shrine · *Tobar* = well · *Achadh* = field · *Doire* = oak grove ·
*Carraig* = rock · *Ros* = headland · *Ceann* = headland/point · *Cuan*
= harbour · *Caol* = strait · *Inis* = island.)*

## Qualifiers
Words of colour, size and character, always lenited when they follow a
generic: *mòr* (big), *beag* (small), *dubh* (black), *geal/bàn*
(white), *dearg/ruadh* (red), *uaine/glas* (green-grey), *fada* (long),
*domhain* (deep), *ìseal* (low), *fiadhaich* (wild), *naomh* (holy),
*fionn* (fair), *ciar* (dusky), *gorm* (blue), *sean* (old), *òg*
(young), *garbh* (rough), *min* (smooth), *caol* (narrow), *leathan*
(wide), *crom* (bent), *dìreach* (straight), *àrsaidh* (ancient).

## Rivers
The great rivers (those at or above the middle length of the island's rivers) take **Abhainn**
("river") with a lenited qualifier: *Abhainn mhòr, Abhainn dhubh*. The small streams take a root
and the *-as/-eas* ending, broad or slender to match, as told above: *Fionnas, Ciaras,
Àrsaidheas*.

## Shires
A shire takes the name of its seat, so that a shire's name is the name of its seat town:
*Siorrachd Cathair mhòr* is simply "the shire of [the city] Cathair mhòr." The two island shires
that have no town, Eilean dhìreach and Eilean ìseal, take the names of their islands. *Siorrachd*
is the Dia-thìris word for a shire. The island's 123 shires, 505 towns and 157 rivers are all named after
the patterns of this chapter.

## The state and its peoples
- **Dia-thìrich**: the greater part, the people of Dia-thìr. **The coal-blooded, the *fuil-ghuail*, are
  born among them**: Aisling, Cian, Lorccan and Fionnan are Dia-thìrich. The name is made in the
  ordinary Dia-thìris way, a root with the people-ending *-aich*, written *-ich* after a slender
  vowel ("the people of X"). They hold the capital, *Cathair
  dhearg*.
- **Tuathaich**: the northern minority, **ordinary humans**, Dubhan's people, mortal and at the
  edge of things: without power, pushed to the northern rim of the island, and holding no part of
  the capital. The name is from *tuath*, "north," by the same people-ending as Dia-thìrich; on the page the
  two names stand side by side like brothers, though their fortunes do not.
- **Seann-Dhaoine**: "the Old Ones," the people who held the coasts before the Dia-thìrich and are now
  all but vanished; from them come the coastal names marked *Seann*, told of above.
- The state's full title is **Rìoghachd Dia-thìr**, "the Kingdom of Dia-thìr," which bears the
  island's own name in its own tongue.
- The capital is **Cathair dhearg**, "the red city."
