## PREFACE — THE FOUR LITERACIES

The Panscriptum does not sort its readers by title, station, or magnitude. It sorts them by **fluency**, and it recognizes exactly four:

| Literacy | Called | Can do | Speaks | Reads | Writes | Amends |
|----------|--------|--------|--------|-------|--------|--------|
| First | **the Guest** (layperson) | *Find* things | Vernacular | Headers | — | — |
| Second | **the Reader** (student) | *Read* fluently | Vernacular | Both registers | — | — |
| Third | **the Hand** (scholar) | *Write* and cite | Both | Both | Formal | — |
| Fourth | **the Custos** (sage) | *Amend* and adjudicate | Both, deliberately | The grammar itself | Formal & doctrine | Yes |

The difference is not accumulation but *kind*. A Guest uses the library; a Reader understands it; a Hand contributes to it; a Custos is responsible **to** it — for what its grammar can and cannot say. Each Part of this volume is one literacy's complete curriculum, and the same worked example — the formal citation of Son Goku — is re-read at the end of every Part, so you can watch a single page deepen four times.

**The Four Questions** (the placement self-test, asked at every registry desk):

1. *Can you find the volume where Rocket League lives?* (If yes: you are at least a Guest.)
2. *Read aloud: 𝔄 M4.31 ± 0.30, Six Paths epoch, Transcribed.* (If you can, in both registers: at least a Reader.)
3. *Write a one-line claim about the Night of Broken Veils that would survive review.* (If it carries its event-code: at least a Hand.)
4. *A ⟦↑17⟧ event acquires a new contradicting witness. What, exactly, is now true?* (If your answer begins "the record and the event are different objects" — sit down, Custos, we have work for you.)

# PART I — THE LAY READING
## *The Guest's grammar: finding things, and not embarrassing yourself*

### I.1 The one-sentence map

Everything in the library is one of three things: a **Law** (how reality works), a **Deed** (something that happened), or a **Digest** (an index of deeds, organized for lookup). If you want *how ki works*, you want a Law shelf (Collection VII). If you want *what Goku did*, you want the Deeds (the Chronica Annex — the enormous wing, you cannot miss it). If you want *who Goku is*, you want a Digest (the Registry of Persons). Ask yourself which of the three you're after, and you have already done half the librarian's job.

### I.2 Finding a thing in four moves

1. Open the **Acquisitions Index** (back of the Master Charter): every source, one line, one code.
2. Read the code left to right: **II.A.3** means *Collection II* (Worlds), *Set A* (a shelf of related worlds), *Series 3* (one world). Big to small, always.
3. Letters are normal. One collection is lettered **W** and shelved sideways — that is on purpose; it touches everything.
4. The huge wing with its own doors is the **Annex** — all history. Its shelves are called Canons. If your question starts with "when" or "why did," go there first.

### I.3 Reading an entry header without training

You will see something like: **𝔄 M4.44 ± 0.20**. You need to know three things. The **M-number is the shelf of power** — bigger is scarier, and the difference between M3 and M4 is a *category*, not a step. The **decimals are for clerks**; you may ignore them. The **± is honesty** — a wide one means the librarians are still arguing, and you should hold the value loosely. That is genuinely all a Guest needs.

### I.4 Shelf-talk: the starter lexicon

The library's informal register is a real dialect with real rules. The essentials:

| Say | Meaning |
|-----|---------|
| "a solid three" / "a deep seven" / "a shallow four" | Position within a power band, in thirds. Decimals are never spoken. |
| "at the ceiling," "on watch" | Nearly strong enough for the next band; under review |
| "shelved up" | Recently promoted a band |
| "argued" | Carries a wide interval; the experts disagree |
| "seen / told / guessed" | How good the evidence is, best to worst |
| "local" / "off-shelf" | From this universe / from a different resonance-family entirely |
| "deep-shelf" | From rung 15 or above; change the subject |
| "unaddressed" | The ⌀ things. Said quietly. |
| "the megafauna" | The six giant franchul— *ahem*, the six giant Series |
| "the Alexandria" / "the Babel" | The main collections / the Annex |
| "the Landlord" | The polite omniversal usage for the Abrahamic Throne |

### I.5 The three courtesies

Never **quote someone's exact decimal aloud** — it is called *reading the worksheet at them*, and it is either a boast or a threat. Never ask a registry clerk **about the missing volume**; they will stop being helpful. And never open **the Outer Register** alone; this is not superstition, it is policy, and the policy has a casualty list.

**First reading of the worked example.** A Guest sees the Goku page and correctly concludes: *a deep-shelf name, a solid seven, seen with my own eyes says a librarian, and argued about at the top.* That is a fully successful Lay Reading. Nothing about it is wrong; everything about it is shallow.

# PART II — THE READER'S READING
## *The Student's grammar: the complete notation, made fluent*

### II.1 Spine codes (where a book lives)

**Form:** `Collection.Set.Series[.Volume]` — always big to small. Collections are numbered 0–XI, plus **W** (the Weave, crosswise). Inside the Annex, the Set-level units are **Canons**: `VIII.9.3` = Annex, Canon of the Great Wars, third volume. A code is also a *claim of kind*: anything filed under III is being treated as divine; anything under X is being treated as measurable; anything under XI, you were warned.

### II.2 Shelfmarks (where a thing exists)

**Form:** rung glyphs, top down, `Ω › H? › X? › Mt.ASC › Mv.DRG › U-7 › G.North › P.Earth`. Rules a Reader must have cold: **?** means uncharted and may never be guessed; **U-codes** are permanent universe registrations (after first citation you may abbreviate to the U-code and below); **⌀** marks pervasives — things that exist *as* a rung rather than *at* an address — and a Reader recognizes the short list on sight (the Warp, the Pale, the Creeper, the Eternities).

### II.3 Assay notation (how strong, how sure, and when)

**Full form:** `𝔄 = M7 + 0.62 ⟨w-composite; worksheet X.3 §G-114⟩ ; CI ± 0.41` — value, derivation pointer, interval. **Short form:** `𝔄 M7.62 ± 0.41 (MUI epoch)`. A Reader internalizes three disciplines: the **epoch tag** is part of the value (power is a curve; a number without a date is a different claim); the **interval** is where disputes are stored, not decoration (both signed readings live inside that ±); and two special values are not numbers at all — **DECLINED** (a domain refusal, for ground-of-being claimants) and **MΩ.x** (the Omega Band: the strength of an *argument*, never a being).

### II.4 Attestation, and its collapse into verbs

Formal grades: **Witnessed → Instrumented → Transcribed → Reconstructed → Disputed** (plus **Redacted**, and one volume's unique **Witnessed-and-Scripted**). Vernacular collapse: *seen* (first two), *told*, *guessed*, *argued*. A Reader moves between the five-grade and four-verb systems without thinking, and knows the mapping is lossy on purpose.

### II.5 Event-codes and ascension marks (how history is addressed)

**Form:** `E-0902-VEILS ⟦↑17⟧` — era-date, mnemonic, and the **ascension mark**: how far up the Chain of Record the event has been ratified. ⟦↑17⟧ is settled omniversal history; ⟦↑9⟧ is true at a cluster archive and still climbing; a pending mark on an old event *is information* (ask why it stalled). Dates are One Calendar, **AS** = After the Sundering.

### II.6 Threads, doors, and hands

**⌁ Threads** are the citation web — spine codes, shelfmarks, event-codes; following them is how the library is actually read. **✦ Three Doors** end every entry: three adventure hooks at three scales, always exactly three. And the **marginalia hands** are part of the text: AVAR corrects, QUILL testifies, MOTH computes, and the UNNAMED HAND warns — a page's argument is often *in the margin*, and a Reader reads margins first.

**Second reading of the worked example.** The Reader parses every line of the Goku citation: anchor M7 by hegemonic feat; composite +0.62 with a recomputable worksheet at X.3 §G-114; interval ±0.41 *containing two signed readings* (so the vernacular "argued deep" is literally encoded); MUI epoch, so earlier epochs are different measurements; Witnessed, dual-signed. The Reader now knows not just *what* the page says but *how it would change* — a new feat moves the curve; a new contest tightens the interval.

# PART III — THE HAND'S READING
## *The Scholar's grammar: writing things that survive review*

### III.1 The Five Absolutes of the Formal Register

1. **No naked values.** An Assay appears with interval, epoch, and worksheet pointer, or it is a rumor.
2. **No guessed rungs.** Unknown is `?`. Guessing is lying with confidence.
3. **No silent averaging.** Divergent readings are cited with both signatures.
4. **No loose scale-terms.** *Stellar* ≠ *planetary*; *multiversal* ≠ *metaversal*. Formal prose is taxonomically exact.
5. **No unanchored claims.** Per the Doctrine of Derivation: every factual sentence cites a **Law** (by Part or spine code) or a **Deed** (by event-code). A sentence with neither is decoration and will be struck.

### III.2 Writing an entry

Follow the template (Charter, Part Seven) field by field. The learned skills: **The Record** is written in Custodial voice — declarative, sourced, no adjectives doing load-bearing work (Moth: "'mighty' is not a measurement"). **Contradictions** is not a bug ledger; it is where you *file* what honest witnesses dispute, by event-code, without adjudicating above your literacy. **For the Table** is Moth's register: mechanics, DCs, build pointers into VII.8 — a Hand keeps fiction and mechanics in their marked lanes. **Three Doors**: one hook at home Magnitude, one a tier up, one crossing a rung. Exactly three. The discipline is the point.

### III.3 Citation forms

**Full citation** (first appearance, Ledgers, treaties): the complete block, as in the Goku example. **Short form** (running text): `𝔄 M3.49 ± 0.12 (post-Raoh; X.3 §K-77)`. **Thread form** (margins and indexes): bare codes — `II.A.5 · E-0000-SUND ⟦↑17⟧ · W.1`. Using a heavier form than needed is pedantry; lighter than needed is malpractice; the Hand's craft is knowing which.

### III.4 Procedure: contradictions, errata, ratification

To **file a contradiction**: identify the shared event-code, attach both attestations with pedigrees, do not resolve — resolution is a Custos matter, and the Compact of Mirrors (E-0604-MIRROR) is the controlling precedent for "both are true at their addresses." To **propose an erratum**: recompute under current doctrine, show the delta, file as *proposed* pending countersign — the model filing is **Erratum 1** (X.2 Fundamenta §10), which moved a published Assay from M3.52 to M3.49 and is cited here chiefly to teach the tone: the system was not embarrassed; the system was *working*. To **countersign**: verify recomputability ("can a stranger, given your citations, get your number?") — if yes, sign; if no, return with the deficiency named. Ratification is peer review with a ladder attached.

### III.5 Register conversion

Formal → vernacular is lossless and always permitted (7.62 → "solid seven at the deep line"). Vernacular → formal is **forbidden as inference** — shelf-talk is never evidence; you go back to the worksheet. A Hand who cites gossip has stopped being a Hand.

**Third reading of the worked example.** The Hand reads the Goku citation as *reviewable work product*: checks that the worksheet pointer resolves; that the interval covers both signed readings; that the epoch is the feat's epoch and not the filing date; that "Ascendant, Gyre-law" is the correct Class taxonomy. The Hand could now *write* this page — and could be held responsible for it, which is the actual definition of the third literacy.

# PART IV — THE CUSTOS'S READING
## *The Sage's grammar: why the language is shaped like this, and how to change it without breaking it*

### IV.1 Notation as epistemology

At the fourth literacy you stop reading marks and start reading *doctrines wearing marks*. Every glyph is a philosophical commitment made mandatory: the **±** is the doctrine of honest error (a value without an interval claims omniscience); the **epoch tag** is the doctrine of becoming (beings are curves; a number is a moment); the **?** is epistemic humility enforced at the level of syntax; the **⌀** is the admission that address itself has limits; **DECLINED** is the type-theoretic wall (the instrument does not measure its manufacturer); the **⟦↑n⟧** is objectivity-as-treaty (the record is what has been countersigned, not what has been seen); and the requirement of *derivation pointers* is the whole Doctrine of Derivation compressed into a bracket. A sage can reconstruct the library's philosophy from its punctuation. That is not a metaphor; that is the design requirement, and it is why the grammar must never be extended casually.

### IV.2 The deep structure, one page

Three strata (Law, Deed, Digest — and the Annex is the only book; the rest are its indexes). One chain (scribe to registry to Absolute; accession as ratification; history arrives old). One treaty at the top (omniscience does not abolish perspective; the ratified record is an *agreed instrument*, which is why even ⟦↑17⟧ events keep Contradictions registers). One paradox above that (choosing what the record says is bias; refusing to choose is also a choice — the Custodial Paradox, permanently assigned one desk up). And four honesty theorems below it all (no lossless scalar; measurement is touch; the measured system contains its measurers; the ground of being is outside the domain — Fundamenta H1–H4). A Custos holds all of this at once *while shelving a book about monkeys fighting balloons with equal seriousness*, because seriousness of method is precisely what makes room for absurdity of subject.

### IV.3 The amendment power

Only the fourth literacy may extend the grammar, under the three meta-rules. **Declared:** a new mark is published with its doctrine before use (Axiom M3 applies to language itself). **Derivable:** it must pass the Moth test — *can a stranger recompute you?* — a mark that encodes a judgment no one else could re-derive is an opinion in costume. **Reversible:** every extension ships with its erratum procedure; a grammar that cannot correct itself will eventually lie. The recent history of the library is a case study in lawful amendment: the Wing class, the Annex class, the Omega Band, event-codes, ascension marks — each arrived as *doctrine + notation + procedure*, which is the only shipping container the library accepts.

### IV.4 Adjudication

A Custos sitting a Contradictions case asks, in order: *Same referent?* (Identity Rule — if two witnesses describe the same thing, merge and file the dispute). *Same stage?* (Continuity Rule — shared stage, shared history). *Same address?* (If not: both true, shelfmark-scoped, Compact of Mirrors). *Ratifiable?* (If sibling registries will not co-sign: the event remains testimony, and *that is a finding, not a failure* — see the Testimony Problem, III.7). The sage's discipline is the last step: writing "unresolved" in a steady hand.

### IV.5 The sage's mark

The fourth literacy is finally recognizable by one behavior: **knowing what the system cannot say, and saying it anyway — in prose, labeled as prose.** The system cannot say who sits in the Seat. It cannot rank two incomparable champions. It cannot make the Pale give anything back. A Custos states these limits without flinching and without pretending the limits are answers. The grammar ends; the librarian continues; the margin exists for exactly this.

**Fourth reading of the worked example.** The sage reads the Goku citation and sees none of the things the first three readers saw, or rather sees through them: the page is a *treaty* — between two witnesses who disagree inside one interval, between a mortal chain and the desks above it, between a boy who kept exceeding every instrument and an institution that kept rebuilding its instruments rather than deny what it saw. The entry is not about how strong he is. It is about what it costs to *agree about anything*, written small. That is the Lay Reading's "solid seven," read at depth. Same page. Four libraries.

## APPENDIX A — THE READER'S LADDER (summary card)

**Guest:** finds by index; speaks shelf-talk; honors the three courtesies. **Reader:** parses all six notations (spine, shelfmark, assay, attestation, event-code, thread); reads margins first. **Hand:** writes under the Five Absolutes; files contradictions and errata; countersigns what a stranger could recompute. **Custos:** amends under the three meta-rules; adjudicates by the four questions; writes "unresolved" in a steady hand.
