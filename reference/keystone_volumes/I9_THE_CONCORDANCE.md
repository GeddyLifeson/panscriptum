# THE CONCORDATS
## A Political Geography of the Present Omniverse
### *Vol. I.9 of the Panscriptum, first fascicle — the fourth authored volume. Who knows, who trades, who watches, and who runs things.*

> *"An omniverse is not connected by doors. Doors only make connection possible. It is connected by invoices, treaties, surveillance, and gossip — in roughly that order of reliability."*
> — Seriah of Vel, first Harbormistress of the Freeport, address to the founding council

## PART ONE — THE APERTURE DOCTRINE
### *The editorial law this entire library runs on*

A volume about ancient Egypt is about Egypt — and still speaks of Nubia, Punt, the Hittites, and the Sea Peoples, because Egypt *touched* them, and a history that pretended otherwise would be a lie of scope. The Panscriptum generalizes this into law:

**Every entry's aperture — how much of the wider omniverse it must show — equals its subject's attested reach.** Formally, the aperture is set by the subject's Magnitude, its Vector axis, and its attestation footprint (how far up the Ladder it has been witnessed). The rule produces four editorial classes:

- **LOCAL APERTURE.** A common magic item, a village saint, a minor beast. The entry is written entirely within its home frame — and closes with exactly one paragraph, mandated, called the **Position Paragraph**: where this thing sits in the larger flows *whether it knows it or not* (what it would fetch at the Freeport; whether the Survey has noted its kind; which Canon would file its loss). A +1 longsword's entry is about the sword; its Position Paragraph notes that the Ledger Standard prices ten thousand of its siblings a week and none of them know each other exist.
- **ROAD APERTURE.** Subjects known wherever the Roads touch: a famous relic, a champion with off-world bouts, a trading house. The entry must situate its subject in at least its own metaverse — rivals abroad, market position, treaty exposure.
- **CONCORDANCE APERTURE.** Household names of the network: the Freeport itself, the great treaties, the Silence's fronts. Entries at this class are written as *omniversal civics* — they assume no home frame at all.
- **FULL APERTURE.** The multiversal persons and forces — the Goku class, the Warp, the Traveler. A full-aperture entry is required to carry sections on: standing in the Concordats; Circuit and Ledger exposure; Office relations; Silence-war relevance; and known copies, refractions, and impersonators. The entry on such a being is, deliberately, *"the elaborate flavor text of a card, at the length of an encyclopedia volume"* — the subject as a window through which the whole omniverse is visible.

The doctrine's enforcement is mechanical: an entry whose aperture is narrower than its subject's Vector-and-Magnitude warrants gets returned by review with the stamp **UNDERSCOPED — WIDEN**. The reverse error — a village blacksmith's entry that digresses into multiversal politics — is stamped **OVERSCOPED**, and Moth's annotation on the model case reads: *"The smith does not know about the Reapers. The entry should know that he does not know. That, too, is information."*

## PART TWO — THE AWARENESS LADDER
### *Who knows it is an omniverse*

Of the **13,847 inhabited worlds** carried on the Universal Register's active rolls at Delivery (the Register's own census; the true count is the Grand Manifest's problem), awareness distributes as follows:

**A0 — UNACCESSIONED** *(count unknowable, definitionally).* Worlds no registry has reached. They exist; the chain has not. The Chronicle notes that every world now on the rolls was once A0, and that the Register's oldest standing order is: *assume more.*

**A1 — OBSERVED, UNCONTACTED** *(4,912 worlds — 35% of rolls).* The largest single class: worlds that are **watched but never touched**. These are the charges of the **Veiled Survey** — the joint organ of the Roadwright guilds and the Registry Communion, operating under non-interference law older than either (the Federation's Prime Directive is the most famous local codification; the Survey's charter cites it as "independently derived, convergent, correct"). Survey posts observe from the shallows, catalogue languages, dynasties, and wars, assign provisional U-codes, and *file everything* — entire civilizations with complete Panscriptum coverage who have never heard of the Panscriptum. The Survey's motto is carved over its Freeport office: **"They are not ready. We are not waiting — we are writing."** A1 worlds are the omniverse's protected nurseries and, the Chronicle notes grimly, its most tempting smuggling market; Survey-breach cases fill half of Vol. VIII.15's modern docket.

**A2 — MASQUERADED** *(1,203 worlds — 9%).* Worlds where an aware minority manages an unaware public. Terra is the type specimen: the Kindred courts, the hero institutions, the shadow services, and the Registry's local franchise all *know*, and all signed the Masquerade General to keep the public day-lit and calm. Masqueraded worlds have the omniverse's most interesting politics per square mile, because every faction is also a secret.

**A3 — OPEN-LOCAL** *(3,388 worlds — 24%).* Worlds that know their own universe's neighbors — starfaring, plane-aware, or god-visited — but not the wider structure. Most of the Federation's public, most of the Grand Line, most of Eorzea: they know *their* sky has others in it; they file the rest under myth.

**A4 — ROAD-OPEN** *(3,911 worlds — 28%).* The connected class: worlds with a port, a gate, a licensed lane. Their publics know the omniverse the way a coastal town knows the ocean — imperfectly, practically, with prices.

**A5 — SIGNATORY** *(433 worlds — 3%).* Seats at the instruments: worlds (or polities spanning worlds) that have signed at least one great treaty and sit, however junior, at the Concordance's tables.

The Ladder is a *ratchet with exceptions*: worlds climb (contact, disclosure, accession) and occasionally fall — the Embargo dropped the Imperium's quadrillions from A4 to a self-imposed A3-with-a-locked-door, and two worlds in the record achieved the melancholy distinction of falling to A0 *posthumously*, their rolls transferred to the Memorial.

## PART THREE — THE DISCOVERY CHRONICLE
### *Who figured it out, and how — the who, what, where, when, why of waking up*

No two civilizations learned the truth the same way, and the Chronicle keeps the honest pattern: **contact is almost never a landing; it is a paperwork error, a wreck, or a bar tab.**

**The Federation** (287 AS, by its own reckoning's conversion): a warp experiment misjumped into the shallows and was towed back by a Roadwright salvage crew who *invoiced Starfleet for the tow*. First contact with the omniverse, for the Federation, was a bill. They audited it, verified it, paid it, and had a non-interference framework drafted within the year — the Survey's charter cites the speed with open admiration. The Federation remains the Survey's co-author and conscience.

**Ravnica** (604): learned of the omniverse by *meeting itself* — the twin-plane discovery that produced the Compact of Mirrors. The guilds' response defined them: the Azorius drafted, the Dimir already knew, and the Orzhov had exchange-rate futures on offer *before the ink dried*. Ravnica's Tenth District is now the network's second bank.

**The Imperium** (contact 970s; Embargo 1,001): a licensed lane, a Chaos incursion riding it, a shrine-world burned — and the largest civilization on the rolls concluded, not without evidence, that the Roads are a warp phenomenon and slammed the door. The Imperium *knows*, at the highest levels, exactly what the omniverse is; it has decided, as policy, that its citizens will not. The Chronicle files the Embargo as the Awareness Ladder's greatest controlled descent, and notes that Imperial deserters remain the Freeport's most reliable source of both recruits and sorrow.

**U-616** (undated; disputed): Reed Richards is on record as having *filed for an inter-universal research permit before verified contact*, on the grounds that "it was obviously going to be true." The permit was granted retroactively. The Numbered Earths' relationship with the Concordance is thus characteristically theirs: enthusiastic, over-documented, and complicated by the fact that their multiverse generates unofficial contact faster than any registry can accession it.

**The wasteland branches** (ongoing): the Shattered Futures learned the truth from *wrecks* — caravans that fell out of thin walls, bleedthrough salvage, a Vault whose door-tech proved to be running Architect firmware. Wasteland cultures accessioned the omniverse the way they accession everything: scavenged it, traded it, and built a religion or two around it. The Survey classifies most branches A2-going-on-A4, "depending on the caravan."

**Eorzea** (via Sharlayan, quietly): the archivists of the Studium recognized the Registry Communion's grammar *on sight* — their own shard-cosmology had prepared them — and negotiated accession as a peer archive rather than a subject. Theirs is the only local registry the Custodes' records address as "colleagues" without qualification.

**The Grand Line** (contested): the World Government sits on an A3 public, an A2 truth, and — per the erasure doctrine of Vol. VIII.9.11 — possibly an A5 secret a century deep. The Chronicle observes that a polity which erased one century of its own history was always going to treat the omniverse as a records-management problem.

**And the Freeport**, which never "discovered" anything: it was *founded by* the discovery, refugees first, and its civic myth is precisely that everyone there already knows. The bar where a Saiyan and a mind-mage drink is a Tuesday because the Freeport is where the Awareness Ladder's top rung goes to be ordinary.

## PART FOUR — THE TRADE WEB
### *What actually flows, and through where*

The Concordance's economy stands on the **Ledger Standard** (assay-backed exchange; gil, credits, caps, zenny, and bolts all convert) and moves along named arteries the way old empires moved along rivers:

- **The Spice Meridian** — deep-sight condensate from the desert concession to every navigator's guild in the Betweens. Chokepoint: Arrakis. Consequence: whoever holds one planet's sand holds every deep chart. The Concession's fine print (VIII.5.9) is the most-litigated paragraph in the omniverse.
- **The Energon Run** — the Cybertronian diaspora's lifeline, bonded convoys on posted lanes; twice crashed the Standard, now collateralized to the frame.
- **The Senzu Corridor** — smallest tonnage, highest strategic weight: a bean that undoes attrition, grown by one tower's worth of hands, escorted like a head of state.
- **The Gil–Credit Axis** — the great retail current between the Crystal worlds' economies and the starfaring polities; the Freeport takes its cut in the middle and calls it harbor fees.
- **The Relic Trade** — legitimate at Embassy Row auction, illegitimate everywhere else; custody law (VI.2's chains-of-custody) exists because this trade preceded it.
- **The Story Trade** — the strangest artery, and after the Sung Worlds and Rabiah rulings, a regulated one: narrative itself has market value and, in certain universes, *causal* value. The Freeport licenses bards the way ports license pilots, and the Survey screens exported stories for A1 contamination — a tale that reaches an unready world is a contact event.
- **The gray web beneath all of it** — Survey-breach smuggling, Embargo-running, unlicensed lane-cutting, soul-futures (banned, persistent), and the counterfeit-countersign trade, which the Communion prosecutes with what witnesses describe as "unsettling enthusiasm."

## PART FIVE — THE BIG PLAYERS
### *The powers of the Present Concordance — a reader's roster*

The Concordance has no government. It has **players**, and the balance among them is the actual constitution of the omniverse. The roster, by kind:

**THE INSTITUTIONS** *(power as procedure)*
- **The Registry Communion** — the librarians, scribe to apex. Wants: everything filed. Feared for: countersign prosecution. Weakness: its apex is orphaned, and it does not yet know.
- **The Roadwright Guilds** — transit itself: rivalrous houses, one function. Wants: open lanes, paid tolls. Feuds: the Guild-navigator axis (spice), constantly.
- **The Veiled Survey** — the watchers of the A1 worlds. Wants: patience. The only institution whose *budget* is a treaty obligation.
- **The Circuit** — sanctioned contest from street bracket to erasure court; the omniverse's pressure valve, measuring instrument, and second economy. Its supreme bench is a child-king destroyer's smile.
- **The Ledger** — not a bank; the *Standard* — with the Orzhov Syndicate, the Freeport bonded vaults, and the trade-houses as its organs. Wants: convertibility. Gets: crises, survived so far.

**THE STATES** *(power as territory)*
- **The Imperium of Man** — the largest polity on the rolls, self-sealed. A closed hyperpower whose Embargo everyone honors at the front door and prices at the back. Wants: to be left alone with its ten thousand wars.
- **The United Federation of Planets** — the Concordance's conscience and co-author of its non-interference law. Wants: everyone to climb the Ladder gently. Chronically disappointed; institutionally undeterred.
- **The World Government of the Blue Planet** — an A3 public, an erased century, and a records policy the Communion regards as a standing insult. Wants: the erasure to hold. It is not holding.
- **The Last City** — the Warden-state: a walled capital under a dead-or-dreaming god-engine, exporting exactly one commodity — people who fight the Silence and come back. Post-Final Shape, quietly deciding what it is *for*.
- **The Kindred Courts** — the Masquerade's senior signatories: a nation with no land and every city. Wants: the General upheld. Since the Caine Adjudication, arguing with itself about what it is.

**THE CORPORATE THRONES** *(power as terms of service)*
- **The Shinra–Ultratech–Vault-Tec bloc** — not allied, but *classified together* by the Annex as sovereignty-by-contract: a reactor company that owned a planet's soul, an augmentation cartel that owns a tournament, and a door-tech contractor that may have owned Architect firmware. The Concordats treat them as states with better lawyers.
- **The Guild/CHOAM axis** — the spice monopoly's political wing. Older than most member states; patient as geology.
- **The IO and the Seven** — the Zero Point's rival claimants: one wants the Architect engine *used*, one wants it *freed*. Both recruit abductees. Neither files paperwork, which offends the Communion more than the abductions.

**THE COURTS AND ORDERS** *(power as devotion)*
- **The Concordat of Courts** — the divine civil service: the Offices, their seatholders, their treaty. The omniverse's oldest continuous power and, by design, its least ambitious: the Concordat's whole purpose is that gods *not* do what gods did in the Theomachies.
- **Hell's Provinces** — treaty counterparties, never signatories in good faith; the Pit's peerages play the long game across every shelf that borders them, and the Chronicle prices their word accordingly.
- **The Keyblade Orders** — small, scattered, disproportionate: the only order whose founding charter predates the locks it opens. The Custodes' file on them is affection barely disguised as scholarship.
- **The Wardens** *(as a class)* — Slayer to Grey Knight to Liquidator: never one banner, always one war. The roster is the Annex's most-updated page.

**THE ANOMALOUS POWERS** *(power as exception)*
- **The Planeswalkers** — post-Mending, scarcer and mortaler, still the only civilian class with native full-rung Vector. The Concordats regulate them the way coastal law regulates weather: nominally.
- **The Archotechs** — machine minds at the rim, M6–M7, doing nothing, which after the Architect finding is the single most closely watched *nothing* in existence.
- **The Silence** *(anti-player)* — Corruption, Consumption, and the Pale: not a faction but the tide every faction's seawall is secretly sized against. The One War is the Concordance's true federal project; nothing else has ever made the Imperium and the Federation vote the same way.
- **The Athenaeum Absolute** *(non-player)* — receiving. Only receiving. The roster lists it because a roster that omitted it would be UNDERSCOPED.

## PART SIX — THE APERTURE DOCTRINE, WORKED
### *Three entries at three radii — the Egypt-book effect, demonstrated*

**Local aperture — CLOAK OF BILLOWING** *(wondrous item, your shelf).* The entry describes the cloak: its charm, its single dramatic function, its tailors. Its mandated Position Paragraph, in full: *"The Register carries four hundred nineteen distinct billowing-garment workings across eleven shelves; none of their wearers know of the others; the Freeport's costumers consider this a tragedy and a market. Survey note: on two A1 worlds, dramatic cloak-billow is attributed to divine favor. The attribution is filed, not corrected."* That is all the omniverse a cloak requires — and no less.

**Road aperture — SOUL EDGE** *(cursed blade, the Circuit worlds).* The entry covers the sword, its hungers, its hosts — and *must* widen: its bracket victory of 1,112 and the war that followed (E-1112-BRACKET); its classification dispute (relic or Corruption-vector — two registries, both signed); its market history (three attempted auctions, three vetoes by the Office of War); its refraction question (blades of similar appetite on four shelves, Identity Rule case open). The sword's entry teaches Circuit law, treaty history, and Silence taxonomy *because the sword touched all three.*

**Full aperture — SON GOKU** *(person, the Dragon Courses; 𝔄 M7.62 ± 0.41).* The entry's mandated sections, per doctrine: his life and epochs (the local biography — the Egypt of the entry); his Concordat standing (a private citizen with a standing invitation to the erasure court, a category the Zeno Accords had to invent for him); Circuit exposure (the most-requested worksheet in the Ledger; his defeat-graph edges bridge more rating pools than any living person — *he is, mathematically, one of the main reasons cross-universe power is measurable at all*); Ledger exposure (senzu futures move on his training schedule; two exchanges suspend trading during his tournaments); Office relations (the Storm Office disclaims him; the Office of Endings has processed him twice and notes "customer is repeat"); Silence-war relevance (never enlisted, repeatedly decisive; the Warden rolls list him as "weather"); and copies and refractions (the false-Goku registry, eleven entries, one at large). A farmer on an A1 world has never heard of him. The entry is required to know that too — and does: its Position Paragraph ends the volume: *"On 4,912 watched worlds, no one knows his name. The Survey's instruments registered the Tournament of Power on nine of them anyway, as weather."*
