*(This plate is reproduced inside the front cover of every volume.)*

> The books you hold were not written in your universe.
>
> At the far end of a reality now dead, in the last stable place their cosmos had left, an order of archivists built a library and refused to stop writing. **What this actually is, out of character:** a full-scale, in-universe encyclopedia series for a D&D 5e omniverse campaign framework — every franchise, pantheon, and world logged with a consistent address system, a consistent power scale, and consistent 5e hooks, so the DM never has to invent structure on the fly again. The books are written as artifacts; the mechanics live in clearly marked sidebars.

## PART ONE — HOW THE LIBRARY IS ORGANIZED

The Panscriptum uses a four-rank shelving hierarchy, largest to smallest:

**COLLECTION → SET → SERIES → VOLUME**

**COLLECTION** — A whole wing of the library, defined by *kind of knowledge*, not by franchise: Worlds, Gods, Beasts, Persons, Relics, Powers, Measures, History, Practice. There are twelve numbered (Collections 0 through XI), plus one shelved crosswise: **Collection W, the Weave** — the integration collection, letter-coded because it is not a subject but the thread through every subject. A Collection answers the question "what sort of thing am I looking up?"

**SET** — A themed shelf within a Collection. In the Catalogue of Worlds, each Set corresponds to a **metaverse** — a cluster of universes that resonate with one another (all the martial-ascension worlds shelve together; all the space operas shelve together; all the dead Earths shelve together). A Set answers "what neighborhood of reality is it from?"

**SERIES** — A run of one or more volumes on a single subject: one multiverse, one universe, or one tightly bound family of them. "The Gundam Centuries" is a three-volume Series; "The Far Galaxy" is a twelve-volume Series (a Wing — see the Law of Shelf-Load below). A Series answers "which world, specifically?"

**VOLUME** — One physical book, built on the standard Volume Template (Part Six). The atomic unit of the library is the **Entry** (Part Seven); a volume is a bound sequence of entries plus apparatus.

Every volume carries a spine code: `Collection.Set.Series.Volume` — e.g., **II.A.3** is Collection II (Worlds), Set A (the Ascendant Gyre), Series 3 (The Grand Line). This is how cross-references work everywhere in the library: any entry can point at any other entry in three or four characters.

### The Law of Shelf-Load

Universes are not the same size, and the shelving must say so. Every property in the Catalogue of Worlds carries a **shelf-load class**, assigned from its measured entry-mass in the Universal Register — not from affection, not from fame:

- **COMPACT** — shares a volume with kin (the tournament-earths; the twin district-myths). Entry-mass in the hundreds.
- **STANDARD** — one volume. Entry-mass in the low thousands. Most of the library.
- **GRAND SERIES** — 2–8 volumes under one Series name. Entry-mass in the five figures.
- **WING** — 8–12+ volumes; a Series so massive it functions as a collection-within-its-Set, with its own internal anatomy and index. Entry-mass in the six figures. There are six Wings in the library, and the Custodes' shorthand for them is unsentimental: *the megafauna.*
- **ANNEX** — the class above Wing, holding exactly one member: **Chronica**. A collection so massive it constitutes a second library, roughly equal in mass to every other Collection combined, delivered in its own crates and organized into its own internal Sets (called **Canons**). Visiting scholars at the Athenaeum called the rest of the library "the Alexandria" and the Chronica Annex "the Babel." The Custodes never corrected them.

A Wing follows a standard internal anatomy so that every giant reads the same way: cosmography → gazetteer of worlds/regions → factions and orders → chronicle of ages and wars → persons of the realm → arsenal and vessels → power-systems → the Contradictions register (largest in the Wing, always) → synthesis and index. Shelf-load is re-assayed as the Register grows; promotion is routine and demotion has never once occurred, a fact Lector Moth calls "the least surprising finding in the library."

## PART TWO — THE LADDER OF BEING
### *The seventeen rungs, and the shelfmark system built on them*

The Custodes shelve reality itself the way they shelve books. Existence climbs in seventeen rungs, and **every single entry in the Panscriptum carries an address on this ladder** — a *shelfmark* — so that anything, from a tavern to a god, can be located:

| # | Rung | Glyph | What it is |
|---|------|-------|------------|
| 1 | Planet | **P** | A single world |
| 2 | Planetary System | **Ps** | A star and its bound worlds |
| 3 | Stellar System | **Ss** | Formal term for any star-neighborhood, inhabited or dead |
| 4 | Star Cluster | **Sc** | A bound family of stars born together |
| 5 | Stellar Association | **Sa** | A loose, unbound family of stars drifting apart |
| 6 | Nebula | **N** | The clouds that birth and bury stars |
| 7 | Galaxy | **G** | Billions of stars in one bound wheel |
| 8 | Galaxy Group | **Gg** | A few dozen neighboring galaxies |
| 9 | Galaxy Cluster | **Gc** | Hundreds to thousands, bound as one structure |
| 10 | Supercluster | **Sp** | Clusters of clusters |
| 11 | Cosmic Filament / Void | **F / V** | The threads and emptinesses of the cosmic web |
| 12 | Universe | **U** | One continuous spacetime with one set of laws |
| 13 | Multiverse | **Mv** | Universes joined by a shared origin or connective law |
| 14 | Metaverse | **Mt** | Multiverses joined by resonance — theme, law, or mutual recognition. **The Sets of Collection II are metaverses.** |
| 15 | Xenoverse | **X** | Metaverses joined *artificially* — by builders, or by declaration |
| 16 | Hyperverse | **H** | Structures of xenoverses so vast they survive only as myth |
| 17 | Omniverse | **Ω** | The total. Everything. There is no outside. |

### The Shelfmark

A shelfmark is written top-down, omniverse first, using the glyphs above:

**Ω › H? › X? › Mt.ASC › Mv.DRG › U-7 › G.North › P.Earth**
*(read: the Omniverse; hyperverse uncharted; xenoverse uncharted; the Ascendant Gyre metaverse; the Dragon Courses multiverse; Universe 7; the North Galaxy; the planet Earth.)*

Working rules:

1. **Every catalogued universe gets a permanent U-code** in the Universal Register (Vol. I.3) — a short name plus number. After first citation, entries may abbreviate to just the U-code and below: `U-616 › P.Earth › Manhattan`.
2. **Unknown rungs are marked `?`, never guessed.** Most universes' hyperverse position is uncharted; the Custodes considered guessing a form of lying.
3. **Some things refuse an address.** Entities that pervade an entire rung at 13 or above (the Warp, the Blind Eternities, the Pale, the Creeper, certain Outer Gods) are shelved *at* their rung with the notation **⌀** ("pervasive"): e.g., `Ω › Mt.⌀ — THE WARP`. These entries are flagged and cross-referenced heavily, because they are exactly the things a Tier-4 campaign is about.
4. **The metaverse rung is load-bearing.** It is the in-fiction reason wildly different power systems can interact: universes in the same metaverse "recognize" each other's ascension laws. Travel *within* a metaverse is hard; travel *between* metaverses requires xenoverse-grade infrastructure — Apertures, Sparks, the Bifröst, a Digivice, a TARDIS-grade engine, or a god.

## PART THREE — THE REGISTRY OF MAGNITUDES
### *One power scale for everything, keyed to 5e*

Every entry — person, beast, god, relic, polity, or power system — carries a **Magnitude (M0–M10)**. Magnitude is the ladder of *power* laid alongside the ladder of *place*: what rung of reality this thing can meaningfully threaten. Where evidence permits, the whole-number Magnitude is refined to two decimal places by the **Custodial Assay** of Collection X — a derived, time-indexed, error-honest value (e.g., 𝔄 M3.52 ± 0.12) computed from the Nine Measures, so that no number in the library is ever assigned arbitrarily.

| Magnitude | Name | Can threaten… | 5e expression |
|-----------|------|---------------|----------------|
| **M0** | Mundane | A village | Levels 1–4; CR 0–4 |
| **M1** | Heroic | A city or nation | Levels 5–10; CR 5–12 |
| **M2** | Paragon | A continent | Levels 11–16; CR 13–19 |
| **M3** | Worldshaker | A planet | Levels 17–20; CR 20–26 |
| **M4** | Planetary Sovereign | A planetary/stellar system | Level 20 + Epic Boons; CR 27–30; mythic traits |
| **M5** | Stellar | Star clusters, associations, nebulae | Boon-stacked mythics; lair = a star system |
| **M6** | Galactic | A galaxy, group, or cluster | Statted only in aspect/avatar form |
| **M7** | Universal | A universe's fundamental laws | Avatars + narrative mechanics (Vol. IX.2) |
| **M8** | Multiversal | Whole multiverses | Never fully statted; fought via fragments, champions, terms |
| **M9** | Metaversal+ | Metaverses and xenoverses | Plot-scale only; "you do not fight it, you unwrite it" |
| **M10** | Omniversal | Everything | Exactly one class of entry. All disputed. *(See Part Four: the Grand Custodes.)* |

Working rules:

1. **Magnitude is capability, not level.** A Kamurocho street legend and a shinobi jōnin are both M1 even though their universes' cosmic ceilings differ enormously.
2. **Statting stops at M5.** M6+ beings appear in play only as avatars, aspects, herald-fights, or campaigns (Vol. IX.2, "The Magnitude Tables," gives the conversion math).
3. **Every power system gets a Magnitude ceiling** in Collection VII: what the system's known maximum is (Hokuto Shinken caps at 𝔄 M3.52; ki ascends through M7; a Planeswalker's Spark is an M8 *travel* ability with the wielder's own combat magnitude left separate — the scale forces exactly the distinctions DMs need).
4. **Campaign tiers map 1:1**: an M0–M1 campaign is street-level; M2–M3 is classic 5e; M4–M5 is your epic tier; M6+ is endgame content run on the narrative mechanics of Collection IX.

### The Custodial Assay — the decimals

> *"My colleagues catalogue what things* are. *I catalogue what things* can do*, and I refuse to do it with adjectives. 'Mighty' is not a measurement. 'Unstoppable' is not a measurement. When Sister Quill returns from a dead Earth insisting that a man in a torn shirt is 'basically a god,' the Order deserves better than her enthusiasm and my skepticism shouting at each other across a reading room. It deserves a number, a method that produced the number, and an honest statement of how wrong the number might be. Every decimal in this library can be traced back through Collection X to a feat somebody witnessed, an instrument somebody read, or a defeat somebody suffered. Where it cannot, the entry says so — in print, where it embarrasses us."*
> — Lector Moth, Preface to Vol. X.1

Magnitude tells you the shelf; the **Assay** tells you the exact spot on the shelf. Where evidence permits, every entry's Magnitude is refined to two decimals — written **𝔄 M7.62 ± 0.41**, colloquially a "Moth Number" — and every published value is **derived, never assigned**: the output of a printed worksheet, traceable to witnessed feats, instrument readings, or the Chain of Defeats (the Order's transitive lattice of who has beaten whom).

**The Anchor.** The integer Magnitude is fixed by the entity's best-attested *hegemonic* feat — what scale of conflict it can **decide**, not merely what it can break. (This is why Kenshiro anchors at M3 without cracking continents: destructive output is one axis among nine, not the anchor.)

**The Nine Measures.** Eight power axes, each scored 0.0–9.9 *within* the anchor band, plus one evidence grade:

| Measure | Weight | Scores |
|---------|--------|--------|
| Ruin | 0.20 | Peak destructive/effective output |
| Continuity | 0.15 | What it takes to remove the entity from play — durability, regeneration, resurrection, "already dead" clauses |
| Celerity | 0.12 | Speed, reaction, combat tempo |
| Reach | 0.10 | Spatial extent of effect — armspan to acausal |
| Transgression | 0.18 | Violation of local law: time-stop, fate-edit, soul-address, conceptual attack, toon-force. The axis that makes hax legible. |
| Sustain | 0.08 | How long peak holds; resource dependence |
| Vector | 0.07 | Can it get to you — mobility across distances and, at high bands, across rungs |
| Volition | 0.10 | Skill, mastery, battle-intelligence — the multiplier on everything else |
| Attestation | — | Evidence quality (Witnessed → Instrumented → Transcribed → Reconstructed → Disputed). Sets the confidence interval, never the value. |

> **Addendum (filed per the errata regime of Vol. X.2 §10; derivation in Vol. X.6).** The table above is the **combat battery** and keeps its name, the Nine Measures. Vol. X.6 (*Instrumenta Facultatum*) proves the eight power axes do not span the faculties of mind and extends the vector by three operational axes — **Acumen** (cognition: prediction–planning advantage, in bits), **Discernment** (perception and insight: veridicality bits under adversarial concealment), and **Suasion** (social force: bits of other agents' choices set by voice and standing alone — force-backed compliance is Ruin's shadow, compulsion is Transgression, neither is Suasion). The full instrument — eleven axes plus Attestation — is **the Twelve Measures**; its weights are fitted from contest data like the others, and the six-faculty field Instrument (Strength, Dexterity, Constitution, Intelligence, Wisdom, Charisma, 1–30 with Transcendence Grades at M6+) is the deterministic conversion defined in X.6 §6: STR ← Ruin at somatic delivery, DEX ← Celerity, CON ← Continuity + Sustain, INT ← Acumen, WIS ← Discernment, CHA ← Suasion. Anchor, bands, weights-discipline, and every theorem of X.2 are untouched.

**The Formula, in five steps.** *(1) Anchor:* from the best-attested hegemonic feat, fix the integer Magnitude **Mₐ**. *(2) Score:* rate the eight power measures 0.0–9.9 within that band, each score justified by a cited feat — these citations are the worksheet lines. *(3) Compute:* **𝔄 = Mₐ + (Σ wᵢ·sᵢ)/10.** A mid-band entity lands near .50; a .9x is flagged for anchor review ("promotion watch"). *(4) Interval:* from the Attestation grade and source disagreement, print ± the honest error. *(5) Epoch:* stamp it, file the worksheet, update the Ascension Curve.

**Worked derivation — Kenshiro** *(Hokuto Shinken successor; post-Raoh epoch).* Anchor **M3**, hegemonic: the fate of his world is, in practice, decided by this man (Witnessed, Quill).

| Measure | Score | Worksheet note | w·s |
|---------|-------|----------------|------|
| Ruin | 2.1 | Anti-personnel absolute; anti-structure incidental; does not crack continents | 0.420 |
| Continuity | 4.8 | Survives wounds that end M2 careers; no resurrection clause | 0.720 |
| Celerity | 6.5 | Perceives and answers Nanto airborne-blade tempo | 0.780 |

| Vector | 0.8 | Planet-bound; walks | 0.056 |

> **𝔄 = 3 + 5.214/10 = M3.52 ± 0.12** *(Witnessed; Quill, with Avar's counter-signature and one clerical dispute)*

Every number in the Ledger has a page like this one — including the street skater assayed at **M0.44 ± 0.15** in Vol. II.P.3, printed as proof the same machinery resolves the bottom of the scale as finely as the top.

**Three doctrines.** Every Assay is **time-indexed** (an epoch tag on a being's Ascension Curve — *Naruto, Six Paths epoch* is a different measurement from *Naruto, Hokage epoch*); every Assay carries an **honest interval** (the confidence bounds are where the library stores its power-scaling disputes — Avar's and Quill's competing readings of Son Goku are both filed, both signed); and above M8 stands the **Assay Ban** — measurement is touch, and some things notice being touched, so all M8+ values are reconstruction, inference, or prayer.

**Gap doctrine & 5e conversion.** Within a shared band: Δ𝔄 < 0.15 = peers; 0.15–0.49 = clear advantage (advantage on contested checks, +2 effective AC/DC in duels); 0.50–0.99 = decisive (the weaker side needs terrain, allies, or an unanswerable Transgression); ≥ 1.00 = outclassed (resolve narratively or via IX.2's avatar rules). For M0–M5, **CR ≈ band-floor + decimal × (band-ceiling − band-floor)** — so M3.52 ≈ CR 23, and a 9th-level party (treating party level as CR in the same formula) sits near M1.57. The **Rosetta Tables** (Vol. X.4) run the conversion the other way, mapping each universe's native scale (scouter power-levels, psyker grades, Stand statistics, Devil Fruit awakening stages…) onto the Assay, per U-code.

### The Omega Band — 𝔄 MΩ.x, the assay of groundings

One class of entry defeats the Assay entirely: **ontological claimants** — beings claimed to be the ground of existence itself rather than entities within it. Their entries carry the notation **𝔄: DECLINED** (*"the instrument does not measure its manufacturer" — Moth*), and the competing creator-claims across all traditions are filed as **the First Argument**, the library's oldest open case, ruled non-justiciable by the Concordat of Courts. Since the beings cannot be assayed, the Custodes assay the next best thing: **the arguments for them.** The Omega Band scores each tradition's ontological *grounding* on the **Seven Groundings** — Necessity, Aseity (does the regress halt here?), Simplicity, Sufficiency, Scope (does it ground logic and mathematics, or only local physics?), Survivability (against its own tradition's strongest critiques), and Convergence — yielding **𝔄 MΩ.x** with the usual honest interval.

The Band's chief instrument is the **regress test**: run the contingency argument against a claimant's own creation account. Any claimant whose scripture grants them a stage, substrate, or *before* (an egg, a primordial sea, a chaos to shape) is thereby classified a **demiurge** — real, mighty, a First Office seatholder for their address, but not the ground, because the regress passes through them. Only claimants who halt the regress on their own terms assay high, and the finding the Custodes underline without comment: **the top claimants' intervals overlap** — the strongest standing datum for the monist reading of the First Argument.

Two integrity rules govern the Band. **A high MΩ is the strength of a case, not evidence of its subject** — *"We can measure how good the argument is. We cannot measure whether reality was persuaded by it."* And **the Seat/Name gap**: a perfect argument establishes the *Seat* — that a ground exists — never the *Name* of its occupant, and never that the occupant acted in any particular history. Crossing from Seat to Name runs on testimony alone, with all its contradictions intact (**the Testimony Problem**, treated in full in III.7: the one ontological claimant who outsourced His entire documentation to mortal scribes — possibly because a record ratified by omniscients would compel, and the system being documented runs on the freedom to doubt).

### The Two Registers — formal and vernacular reference

The Panscriptum is written in two distinct registers, and the difference between them is the difference between a published measurement and shop-floor talk — between a physics paper and physicists at lunch. They differ in form, in obligations, and in what they are *for*.

**The Formal Register ("High Custodial").** Used in entry headers, the Ledgers, the Registers, worksheets, and all body text. A formal reference is **reproducible**: it must carry everything needed to check it. The canonical citation form:

> ⟦FORMAL CITATION⟧
> **SON GOKU** *(Kakarot, of the Saiyan line of U-7)*
> **Shelfmark:** Ω › H? › X? › Mt.ASC › Mv.DRG › U-7 › G.North › P.Earth
> **Assay:** 𝔄 = M7 + 0.62 ⟨w-composite; worksheet X.3 §G-114⟩ ; CI ± 0.41
> **Epoch:** Mastered-Ultra-Instinct attainment, Tournament of Power
> **Attestation:** Witnessed; dual-signed AVAR/QUILL; readings divergent, both filed
> **Class:** Person (Ascendant, Gyre-law) • **Threads:** II.A.1 · V.4 · VIII.9 · X.3 §G-114

The formal obligations are absolute, the way units and error bars are absolute in physics: a formal Assay **never** appears without its confidence interval, its epoch tag, and its worksheet pointer (a value with no derivation reference is, formally, not a measurement but a rumor); unknown rungs are marked `?`, never guessed; pervasives carry ⌀; a divergent reading is cited *with both signatures*, never silently averaged. Formal prose is exact about scale-terms the way biology is exact about taxa: *stellar system* is not *planetary system*, *multiversal* is not *metaversal*, and an entity is *M4-anchored*, not "about M4."

**The Vernacular Register ("shelf-talk").** Used in marginalia, prefaces, field notes — and by everyone who actually lives in the omniverse. Vernacular reference is **operational**: compressed for speed, honest about being approximate, and it follows its own consistent grammar rather than being sloppy formal speech:

- **Decimals are never spoken.** A band divides into thirds: **shallow** (.00–.32), **solid** (.33–.66), and **deep** (.67–.99). Kenshiro (3.52) is "a solid three"; the Slayer (4.44) is "a solid four"; Goku (7.62) is "a solid seven at the deep line — and argued deep," since Quill's high reading would shelve him there, which is precisely the kind of dispute the vernacular wears on its sleeve. Quoting someone's exact decimal aloud is called *reading the worksheet at them* and is understood everywhere as either a boast or a threat.
- **Place compresses to relation.** "Local" (same universe), "off-shelf" (different metaverse), "deep-shelf" (rung 15 and above), "unaddressed" (the ⌀ pervasives — said quietly).
- **Band-edges have their own words.** A .9x entity is "at the ceiling" or "on watch"; a fresh promotion is "shelved up"; a wide confidence interval makes someone "argued" ("the Emperor's the most argued name in the Registry").
- **Attestation collapses to verbs**: *seen* (Witnessed/Instrumented), *told* (Transcribed), *guessed* (Reconstructed), *argued* (Disputed).

The two registers convert losslessly in one direction only — formal → vernacular always works; vernacular → formal requires going back to the worksheet — which is precisely the relationship between a published figure and the way it gets talked about. Vol. 0.1 prints the full conversion tables; the rule of thumb is that **body text earns its precision, marginalia spend it.**

## PART FOUR — THE ONE OMNIVERSE: THE UNIFICATION DOCTRINE
### *Why Goku and Jace Beleren live in the same reality, and what dissolves the walls between "verses"*

The Collections tell you where everything is shelved. This Part tells you why it is all **one cosmos** — not a hundred sealed franchise-boxes that occasionally cross over, but a single continuous reality whose internal divisions are geographic, historical, and metaphysical, never editorial. The Custodes state the founding principle in one line:

> **"Provenance is not geography."** Stories arrive at the Athenaeum as separate reels. Reality does not. The library's work is not collection — it is *reassembly*.

Two rules operationalize it:

**The Identity Rule.** When two sources describe the same thing — Ravnica in the Eternities and Ravnica on the Great Wheel, Theros twice, two tellings of one god — they are the *same thing witnessed twice*, and the disagreements go to the Contradictions register as scholarship, not to separate shelves as canon. One reality, many witnesses.

**The Continuity Rule.** Worlds that share a stage share a history. Every "modern Earth" is presumed to be *the same Earth* unless its physics forbid it. The walls between universes are real, but they are **scar tissue, not architecture** — wounds left by the Sundering (below), which is why they can be crossed at all.

### The Chord — one substrate under every power

All power in the omniverse is technique applied to a single substrate the Custodes call **the Chord**: the standing resonance of the original creation-Song, still sounding through every rung. (This is why the Sung Worlds can only be entered by music, why Arda's creation *is* a song, and why metaverses group by "resonance" — they are literally harmonic families.) Every power system in Collection VII is a *dialect* of the Chord:

ki is the Chord as **amplitude discipline** — the body as instrument, trained louder. The five colors of mana are its **chromatic theory** — the Chord split by prism into philosophy. Chakra is a **braided dual-tone**; Haki is **assertion**, the will to be the loudest note in the room; Hamon is the Chord carried in breath and sunlight; a Stand is a soul's **overtone made visible**; the Force is not a technique at all but the Chord's own will, *listened to*; psykery is raw, unshielded exposure; the Light and the Darkness are the dyad of its first two intervals; the *culoare adevărată* is the Chord **seen instead of heard**; Getter radiation is its appetite for growth; divine power is the Chord answered by worship at scale. This is not flavor — it is the load-bearing reason the library works: **one substrate is why one measurement (the Assay) applies to everyone**, why ascension is a universal law, and why a Saiyan and a mind-mage are commensurable at all. They are not from "different verses." They are different instruments.

### The Silence — one war, many fronts

Against the Chord stands its negation, which the deepest texts call **the Silence**, in three known strains: **Corruption** (the Warp's Chaos, the Dark Aether — the Chord played *wrong*, on purpose); **Consumption** (the Flood, the Creeper, the Locust, the Rot — the Chord *eaten*, biomass and entropy); and true **Silence** (the Pale — not noise but erasure, the Song forgetting itself; the only strain the Athenaeum ever named its own enemy). Nearly every great conflict on every shelf is a front in this one war, and the scattered figures who fight it — the Slayer, the Guardians, the Keyblade orders, the Getter pilots, the Liquidators of the Rot City, the Grey Knights — are logged collectively as **the Wardens**: never one organization, always one work.

### Terra Mosaica and the Shattered Futures

The library's boldest act of reassembly. The dozens of "modern Earths" are **one Earth — Terra** — with one reconciled past: the pantheon Courts were its antiquity, region by region, exactly as Collection III records them; and its 20th and 21st centuries were the **Age of Masters**, a single crowded secret history in which the shadow wars (Metal Gear's snakes, the Clancy doctrine, XCOM's hidden invasion, the Black Ops numbers, the mirrored Modern Warfares) ran beneath the same decades as the fighting Circuit (Street Fighter's world warriors, the Iron Fist, the Chōjin crown, Ryōzanpaku's masters), while Kamurocho and Shinjuku were, and are, *neighborhoods in the same Tokyo*, and the Kindred kept their Masquerade in the same cities. One Earth. It was always one Earth.

Then the Sundering (below) did something crueler than destroy it: it **shattered Terra's future into branches**. The Great War of 2077 (Fallout), the Death Omen sky (Hokuto), the fuel-death highways (Mad Max), the tunnels of Metro, the green flu, the energy famine — these are not different planets; they are **sibling futures of the same world**, each fully real, radiating from the same shared past. In-lore, pasts converge and futures are plural. A wastelander from the Fury Roads and a Vault dweller are countrymen separated not by space but by *which ending happened to them*. (Worlds with their own skies — Eorzea, Wraeclast, Eternia, the Blue Planet of the Grand Line — remain their own worlds; the Mosaic doctrine consolidates only what shared a stage.)

### The Five Ages — one history

**I. The Singing** — creation; the first sounding of the Chord (the Music of the Ainur is Arda's local memory of it; the Sung Worlds are echoes that never fully condensed). **II. The Age of Courts** — the pantheons real, regional, and treatied; divine history as recorded in Theographia. **III. The Sundering** — the wound: the event that partitioned the rungs, walled the universes, shattered Terra's futures, and let the Silence in. Every "verse boundary" in the omniverse dates to it. **IV. The Age of Roads** — the long reopening: Sparks, Keyblades, gates, hyperlanes, relays, Apertures — every transit system in Vol. I.6 is a stitch across Sundering scar tissue. **V. The Present Concordance** — now: a crossable, trading, quarreling, singly-historied omniverse. *(And one standing implication the Custodes refuse to print outside the Apocrypha: a map of every surviving door is a map of the Sundering itself. That is what the missing volume is.)*

### The Institutions — how strangers sensibly meet

- **The Circuit.** Sanctioned contest as omniversal law, from a street dojo to the Tournament of Power — one continuous ladder of brackets. The legal, honorable, and *common* way beings of different worlds and magnitudes meet without war. Half the martial history on these shelves is Circuit history.
- **The Concordat of Courts.** The gods' own treaty: domains partitioned, syncretism formalized (a storm god traveling abroad is *received* by the local storm court), mortals off-limits to direct annihilation. Why Zeus and Thor and Indra are rivals and colleagues rather than combatants: they signed.
- **The Roadwrights.** Everyone licensed, sparked, or sworn to cross: planeswalkers, Keyblade orders, gate authorities, gummi pilots, the transit tier of every spacefaring polity. Rivalrous guilds, one function — they keep the Age of Roads open.
- **The Ledger Standard.** Trade needs exchange, and exchange needs a unit: value across worlds is denominated against the Assay (Moth's system moonlights as the omniverse's monetary standard — gil, zenny, credits, caps, and bolts all convert through it, to his documented horror).
- **The Masquerade General.** The standing treaty that keeps unawakened M0 populations unaware — the *reason* most people on most worlds have never heard of any of this, and the campaign-ready tension every urban game runs on.

### The Meeting of Powers — the worked example the whole doctrine exists for

**Son Goku** (𝔄 M7.62 ± 0.41) and **Jace Beleren** (𝔄 M2.88 ± 0.25) share a reality because they share the Chord — one plays it as amplitude, the other as mind and passage. The Assay's axes, not its total, say how they sensibly interact: in **Ruin**, Goku outclasses him by five bands and no dice are rolled. In **Vector**, the gap runs the *other way* — Jace's Spark is cross-rung travel Goku cannot follow. In **Transgression**, Jace's mind-edit operates on an axis Goku's training barely defends. So a duel is a foregone conclusion; a chase is Jace's to lose; a game of secrets is not close — and a *partnership* (one man who can hit anything, one man who can find anything and get them both there) is the sensible shape their meeting actually takes, most likely brokered at a Circuit bracket or on a Warden front. **The omniverse is not a ladder. It is a chord.**

### The Great Identifications — where the walls come down entirely

The Identity Rule, applied at full strength, does more than reconcile two Ravnicas. The Custodes' deepest integration scholarship — Collection W, the Weave, shelved crosswise because it is not a subject but the thread through every subject — makes nine load-bearing identifications:

1. **The Betweens are one sea.** Hyperspace, slipspace, the Lanes Between, the Ocean Between, the Blind Eternities, the Astral currents, the Warp — these are not seven transit media. They are **depths of one medium**: the shallow Betweens (hyperspace, slipspace — fast, safe, physics-adjacent), the middle Betweens (the Lanes, the Eternities — where Sparks and gummi ships sail), the deep Betweens (**the Warp is the Betweens' corrupted deep**, where the Silence's strain of Corruption pooled after the Sundering), and the abyssal floor — which is the Pale. Every navigator in the omniverse is sailing the same sea at different depths, which is why a planeswalker, a Warp pilot, and a gummi crew can meet, and why all three fear the deeps for the same reason.
2. **Divinity is held in Offices.** Zeus, Thor, Raijin, and Set's storm-aspect are not rival copies — they are regional seatholders of the **Storm Office**; Ra, Amaterasu, and Apollo of the **Sun Office**; Thanatos, Anubis, Hela, Darksiders' Death, and every reaper of the **Office of Endings**. The Concordat of Courts is, at bottom, a civil service. When a god dies, the Office remains — and posts the vacancy.
3. **There is one Hell, in provinces.** The Pit, the Burning Hells, Argent D'Nur's captor-realm, the Chaos wastes, the Dark Aether — provinces of a single infernal continuum on the border of the deep Betweens. Its wars of succession are why invasions of the material rungs come in waves. The lawful death-lands — Duat, Hades, Helheim, the Land of the Dead — are **not** Hell: they are the Underworld network, a different jurisdiction entirely, administered by the Office of Endings, and offended by the comparison.
4. **The precursors were one firm.** The Forerunners, the Reapers' drowned makers, the archotechs at the rim, the builders of the Zero Point, the door-wrights whose ruins floor half the dungeons in the omniverse — one elder civilization-complex, **the Architects**, whose work predates the Sundering and whose unexplained departure coincides with it. Every setting's "mysterious precursor ruin" is the same firm's abandoned infrastructure. The open case in Vol. W.4: whether the Sundering was done *to* them, *by* them, or *as* them.
5. **The great bloodlines are few.** All dragonkind is one brood in taxonomy — Shenron's wish-serpents, Bahamut's court, Alduin's brood, Tiamat's children: one family tree with a war at its root. All vampirism descends from one Embrace (the Kindred remember the name Caine; other strains remember other names for the same night). All zombie plagues are strains of Consumption, catalogued like influenza. And Terra's martial arts form one lineage tree — the Hokuto succession and the Ansatsuken school are **sibling arts** of a single assassin tradition that split a millennium ago, which every Circuit historian already suspected.
6. **The world-anchors are a network.** Yggdrasil, the God Tree, the crystals of the Crystal Multiverse, the Worldstone, Castle Grayskull, the Traveler — **Chordroots**: physical anchors where the Chord touches matter. They are why their worlds matter strategically, why everyone fights over them, and why destroying one causes a *local sundering*.
7. **The power-substances are condensates.** Senzu, spice, materia, energon, Element 115, eezo, Argent energy, boost — the Chord condensed into matter, one taxonomy, one trade. This is what the Ledger Standard actually prices, and what half the wars in Chronica were fought over.
8. **There is a Freeport.** Where worlds fall to the Silence, their survivors wash up somewhere — and that somewhere is real: the **Freeport of Fallen Skies** (the Keyblade orders' records call it Traverse Town), the refugee-harbor and market of the middle Betweens, with embassy rows, Circuit brackets, Roadwright guildhalls, and a bar where a Saiyan and a mind-mage meeting over drinks is a Tuesday. The default campaign hub of the entire framework.
9. **Meaning travels.** The Chord carries intention the way air carries sound — which is the in-lore answer to the question every crossover quietly skips: *why do they understand each other?* Deep translation is imperfect (the Contradictions register is partly a record of its failures), true names resist it, and the Pale eats it — but a Terran and a Ravnican can haggle. The omniverse has a common tongue because it has a common instrument.

### The Doctrine of Derivation — the Three Strata

The library's deepest structural law, stated after the Annex made it undeniable: the Panscriptum contains only three kinds of writing, stacked.

1. **LAW** — what is true regardless of events: the Ladder of Being, the Chord and its dialects, the Registry of Magnitudes' definitions, the physics of the Betweens. Law is not derived from history; history happens *inside* it. (Even here the Annex reaches in — every law was *discovered* on some date, and the Reckoning Canon records the discovery — but the law itself is timeless.)
2. **DEED** — everything that has ever happened: the Chronica Annex, entire. Every event in it carries a citable **event-code** (the Night of Broken Veils is **E-0902-VEILS**; the Sundering is **E-0000-SUND**), which makes deeds addressable the way shelfmarks make places addressable and Assays make power addressable.
3. **DIGEST** — everything else in the library. And this is the answer to the question of what the other Collections *are*: **they are indexes of the Annex, organized for lookup.** A Registry dossier is a person's deeds, compressed and alphabetized. A Worlds volume is a place's deeds, shelved geographically. A Theographia entry is an Office's deeds, shelved theologically. A treaty in the Armamentarium's world is a deed with signatures. Even the Assay is derived history — every worksheet line cites an attested *feat*, and a feat is an event: the Chain of Defeats is Annex data wearing Moth's notation. The Custodes' formulation, carved opposite the Annex lintel: **"The Annex is the only book. The rest are its indexes."**

The binding rule that makes this real rather than rhetorical: **every claim in every Digest volume must cite either a Law (by Part or spine code) or a Deed (by event-code).** A Digest sentence with neither citation is, formally, decoration — and the Contradictions registers exist precisely because two witnesses to the same event-code may disagree. This is also the production order of the entire project, inverted from what the shelf-order suggests: the event spine is written first, and the entries condense out of it — worlds, persons, beasts, and relics precipitating from history like salt from the sea.

### The Chain of Record — how history ascends

The Doctrine of Derivation implies an institution, and the institution exists: **the Registry Communion** — the federation of every library worth its salt at every rung of the Ladder, all keeping records in the same grammar of shelfmarks, event-codes, and assays. A town scriptorium, a planetary athenaeum, a system registry, a cluster archive, a galactic repository, on up through the universal and multiversal registries to the apex — every one of them a rung-local mirror of the Panscriptum's own structure, because the structure was never the Custodes' private invention. They *propagated* it down the Roads for centuries — and recorded, with some unease, that many worlds adopted it in a single generation, "as if remembering it rather than learning it." The open case file sits in Vol. W.4, where all Architect questions go.

**Accession** works exactly as it should: a scribe witnesses a deed and logs it with a provisional local code. The local registry classifies it and countersigns. It ascends — town to world, world to system, rung by rung — gathering a **countersign pedigree** at each level, until it is accessioned into the Annex at the apex with its full event-code. The notation shows how high an event has climbed: **E-0902-VEILS ⟦↑17⟧** is ratified to the top of the Ladder; an event marked **⟦↑9⟧** is stuck at a cluster archive, still true locally but not yet omniversal history. The Attestation grades gain their real meaning here — *Witnessed* means a Custos bypassed the chain in person; *Transcribed* means the chain delivered it; *Disputed* means two sibling registries ratified contradictory accounts of the same code, which is where half the Contradictions registers come from.

Three consequences worth building on. **History arrives old**: full ascension takes years to centuries, the Canon of the Now runs perpetually behind, and the Apertures were partly the Custodes' shortcut around their own bureaucracy — field verification instead of waiting for the mail. **The chain can be attacked**: countersigns get forged, registries get captured, and the Pale does not merely erode books — it eats *registries*, unwriting events mid-ascension, which is why the Communion fears it above every other strain. And the third consequence requires knowing what actually sits at the top of the chain — because it is not, and never was, the Athenaeum Ultima.

### The Athenaeum Absolute — the Library of the Omniverse

At rung 17 — at Ω itself, where there is no outside — sits the true apex of the Chain of Record: **the Athenaeum Absolute**, the Library of the Omniverse. Its staff are the **Grand Custodes**: omnipotent, omniscient beings doing, more or less, *paperwork*. They are the answer to the Registry of Magnitudes' oldest riddle — the M10 line reads "exactly one class of entry, all disputed," and this is the class: the clerks at the top of everything, disputed *by each other*.

Why omniscient beings keep records at all is the doctrine's philosophical heart, and the Custodes of the Final Shelf understood it well enough to carve it into their own name (the Athenaeum Ultima's order named itself after the true Final Shelf above — aspiration, not arrogance; they were the highest **mortal** registry, a relay near the top of the chain, never the top). The reasoning: **omniscience does not abolish perspective.** To know everything is still to know it *as someone* — total knowledge is still indexed from a standpoint, weighted by a temperament, ordered by a history of attention. Two omniscient beings can agree on every fact in existence and still disagree on cause, weight, and meaning — on which deed *mattered*, on why an event "objectively" happened — and both will be right about all the facts while being irreconcilable about the account. The Grand Custodes know that sooner or later, something is going to start an argument. The Annex-at-the-apex exists so that when it does, there is an agreed instrument to point at. **Objectivity, at rung 17, is not a vision. It is a treaty.** The ratified record is not what any omniscient sees — it is what all of them have countersigned, which is why even a ⟦↑17⟧ event-code can carry a Contradictions register: contradiction is native all the way to the top.

The residue — the question of how a mind that knows everything can *choose* what the record shall say without that choice being bias, when refusing to choose is also a choice — is called **the Custodial Paradox**, and it is formally assigned: it is the sole and eternal work of the office of the **Custos Absolute**, the grandest of the Grand Custodes, whose desk has one item on it. The Panscriptum does not solve it. The Panscriptum cites it.

Two working rules govern the Absolute's relationship with everything below. **It receives; it does not descend.** The chain flows upward only — no ruling, correction, or rescue comes back down, ever, as far as any mortal registry can attest. (The Assay Ban's warning that "some things notice being touched" is understood, at the deepest reading, to be about proximity to their desks.) And **it does not die.** The fall of the Athenaeum Ultima with its universe beheaded the *mortal* chain at its highest relay — but the Absolute abides above it, still receiving. Which sharpens the campaign frame rather than blunting it: the Delivered Collection is the orphaned **mortal** apex, and whatever world it landed on is now the highest surviving relay of the Chain of Record — the grand library of the *mortal* omniverse, terminus of every accession chain still faithfully countersigning history upward. Somewhere, a town scribe logs a deed. It is coming here — and everything that ascends from here goes, next, to desks whose clerks already know its contents and are waiting, with the patience of the omniscient, for the paperwork. One marginal note in the Delivered Collection bears on this. The Custodes denied having a fifth archivist. The Absolute has never denied having a field hand.

### The Stakes Doctrine — why the strong don't simply end every story

Three braided reasons, all in-lore and all usable at the table: **attention** (acting at high magnitude is loud in the Chord, and the Silence orients toward loud things — the same logic as the Assay Ban); **treaty** (the Concordats bind gods and the god-adjacent; the Circuit exists precisely to vent what treaties suppress); and **attenuation** (power is resonance, and resonance is strongest at home — a being operating far off its native harmonic sustains its peak poorly, with For-the-Table rules in Vol. I.10: off-resonance Sustain and Ruin decay the further from home-metaverse an entity fights, which is why an M7 visiting a noir Tokyo is a terrifying *guest* rather than an automatic apocalypse).

## PART SEVEN — THE ENTRY TEMPLATE
*The atomic unit. Everything in the library — a god, a gun, a galaxy — is an Entry:*

> **◈ ENTRY NAME** *(local name; Custodial designation)*
> **Shelfmark:** Ω › … › *(full address, ? for uncharted, ⌀ for pervasive)*
> **Class:** World / Polity / Person / God / Beast / Relic / Vessel / Praxis / Event / Substance
> **Magnitude:** Assay to two decimals with interval and epoch where measured — e.g., 𝔄 M4.31 ± 0.30 *(Six Paths epoch)* — per Collection X; band-only (M0–M10) where unmeasured. Ceiling notation for systems and polities.
> **Attestation:** Witnessed / Instrumented / Transcribed / Reconstructed / Disputed / Redacted *(plus the unique grade Witnessed-and-Scripted, held by one volume)*
>
> **The Record.** The in-fiction body text: what this thing is, in the Custodes' voice.
>
> **Contradictions.** Variant canons, timelines, and retellings, logged as unresolved scholarship — *the device that lets every messy franchise timeline coexist without breaking anything.*
>
> **Marginalia.** Notes in the four recurring Hands (see below).
>
> **▣ For the Table.** The boxed 5e sidebar: stat block or CR guidance, build pointers into VII.8, DCs, treasure.
>
> **⌁ Threads.** Cross-references by spine code, shelfmark, and event-code — every factual claim in The Record anchors to a Law citation or an Annex event-code per the Doctrine of Derivation.
>
> **✦ Three Doors.** Exactly three adventure hooks, always: one at the entry's home Magnitude, one a tier above, one that crosses a rung.

**The Four Hands.** Marginalia throughout the library recur in four handwritings, and tracking them is half the fun of reading it:

- **CUSTOS-PRIME AVAR** — dry, official, corrective. The voice of the institution.
- **SISTER QUILL** — enthusiastic, first-person, clearly *went there*. Frequently contradicts Avar. Occasionally bleeds on the page.
- **LECTOR MOTH** — obsessed with modeling everything as a game; author of every "For the Table" sidebar. The Order thought him eccentric. He was right.
- **THE UNNAMED HAND** — writes only warnings. Appears more often in later volumes. The Custodes deny having a fifth archivist.
