# CENSUS ET PROPAGATIO
## On the Population of a Universe, and the Speed of News
### *Vol. X.7, first fascicle — the third volume of Collection X to be written rather than planned. Filed as a proposed extension, pending re-ratification up the Chain of Record.*

> *"We have spent two fascicles measuring what a single being can do, and never once asked how
> many beings there are. A ledger of champions is not a census. And a library that cannot say
> how long its own news takes to arrive has no business stamping anything ⟦↑17⟧."*
> — Lector Moth, opening the third Fundamenta

## §0 — PROGRAM

Two questions the first two fascicles left standing, both raised by the Owner and both answerable
with the machinery already in hand.

**First: how many.** X.2 measures an agent; X.6 measures a mind; neither counts. The Universal
Register carries 13,847 inhabited worlds on its active rolls (I.9 §2) and admits the figure is
the charted subset — *"the true count is the Grand Manifest's problem."* This fascicle makes the
total derivable rather than deferred, so that a universe entered into the Register tomorrow
arrives with its population already known instead of estimated.

**Second: how fast.** Part Four holds that *"history arrives old"* and that an event's ascension
mark ⟦↑n⟧ records how far it has climbed. But the charter never states how far apart two shelves
*are*, so "that world has not heard" has no length, and any writer may assert it at any distance
without contradiction. This fascicle supplies the metric, and thereby makes the assertion
falsifiable.

It proceeds: the Standard Universe rule and why fixing it settles more than it simplifies (§1);
the census chain, with every term marked measured or declared (§2); the physical-impossibility
constraints and why a census must be refused rather than published when it breaks them (§3); the
Kardashev correspondence and the bridge to the Ruin axis (§4); the propagation metric, its two
clocks, and the observed-mark rule (§5); the honesty theorems this fascicle adds (§6); and the
errata it files against its own first drafts (§7).

The stance is unchanged: **systems before evidence.** Every convention declared before use.

## §1 — THE STANDARD UNIVERSE

**Definition 1 (Standard Universe).** A universe is, by declared convention, **one
observable-universe-equivalent**: the same extent, star count, and matter content as this
reality's observable universe. Departures are declared per-U-code, never assumed.

*Owner ruling, 2026-08-20.* The alternative — sizing each universe to its own source material —
was considered and rejected. Three consequences follow, and the third is the reason:

1. **The census becomes a constant.** Every STANDARD universe holds the same number of stars,
   the same number of worlds, the same number of civilizations. A Register row needs no size
   field; it needs only a flag when a universe is *not* standard.
2. **Population-scale commensurability, for the same reason as rung-scale.** X.2 §8 grounds
   cross-system measurement in the observation that every inhabited system shares a binding
   hierarchy — *"the Ladder is the shared ruler because it is the shared situation."* Definition 1
   extends that: the shared ruler is now the same **length** everywhere.
3. **It removes an inflation vector.** Absent the rule, every shelf's partisans argue their
   universe is larger, and "larger universe" silently becomes a power argument — a claim about
   scale smuggled in without a feat, an axis, or a worksheet. The rule closes that door before
   anyone walks through it.

**Declared exceptions.** The charter already names the class: II.N.3, *"the small universes that
re-run themselves"* — the Basement Loop, the Rot City of ANEURISM IV. A universe departs from
STANDARD only where its own text forbids the standard reading, per the Continuity Rule's own
proviso (*"unless its physics forbid it"*).

| Class | Multiplier | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **STANDARD** | 1.0 | the rule; every U-code unless flagged |
| MINOR | 10⁻⁶ | declared exception: a walled single galaxy |
| POCKET | 10⁻⁹ | declared exception: a closed loop, one stage and no sky |

| **exoplanets** | **3.2 × 10¹⁹** |

**Corollary (the Register's true reach).** Against I.9's 13,847 charted inhabited worlds, the
Register has accessioned **one world in 4.3 × 10¹³** of a single universe's life-bearing planets.
The Grand Manifest's problem now has a magnitude. The Veiled Survey's motto — *"They are not
ready. We are not waiting — we are writing"* — is revealed as considerably more ambitious than it
sounds.

**The four biological terms are the fascicle's weakest point and are marked as such.** Nobody
knows f_life. They are calibrated to an in-fiction observable — the omniverse is demonstrably
crowded, every one of 215 catalogued sources having inhabited worlds — and that calibration is
honest only because it is labelled. Per H5's logic applied to populations: *a declared convention
is not a measurement, and this table must never be cited as though it were.*

## §3 — THE IMPOSSIBILITY CONSTRAINTS

A Kardashev type is defined by the scale it commands. Each therefore carries a hard ceiling set
by how many of that thing exist, and a census violating one is not describing a crowded universe
but an incoherent one.

**Definition 2 (Census admissibility).** A census is admissible only if:

- Type III civilizations ≤ galaxies
- Type II civilizations ≤ stars
- Type I civilizations ≤ habitable-zone worlds
- extant civilizations ≤ life-bearing worlds

**Theorem 2 (Refusal).** An inadmissible census must be refused, not published with a caveat. A
figure asserting six galaxy-spanning civilizations per galaxy is a fabricated number in exactly
the sense of Absolute 1 — it asserts evidence that cannot exist — and the Register would carry it
as fact.

*This is not hypothetical. The first draft of this fascicle set the Type III share at 10⁻³,
producing 1.2 × 10¹² galactic civilizations in a universe holding 2.0 × 10¹¹ galaxies. Caught by
the constraint, not by the author. Filed as Erratum 4 (§7).*

Declared occupancy, satisfying Definition 2:

| Type | Share of extant | Count | Occupancy |
|---|---|---|---|
| K < 1 (pre-planetary) | 0.90000 | 1.08 × 10¹⁵ | — |
| Type I (planetary) | 0.08500 | 1.02 × 10¹⁴ | 1 per 2 × 10⁴ habitable worlds |
| Type II (stellar) | 0.01499 | 1.80 × 10¹³ | 1 per 1.1 × 10⁶ stars |
| Type III (galactic) | 0.00001 | 1.20 × 10¹⁰ | **6% of galaxies host one** |

## §4 — THE KARDASHEV CORRESPONDENCE

Kardashev measures a civilization's power budget in watts; the Assay's Ruin axis measures an
agent's peak deliverable work in joules (X.2 §4). They convert through time.

**Definition 3 (Kardashev value).** Sagan's continuous form, **K = (log₁₀ P − 6) / 10**, P in
watts. Earth at 2 × 10¹³ W sits at K = 0.73.

**Definition 4 (The budget bridge).** A polity commanding P watts commands **P × 3.156 × 10⁷
joules per year**, read against the Ruin band edges by the same clamped log rule as every other
axis.

| | Power | Annual budget | Reaches |
|---|---|---|---|
| Earth, present | 2 × 10¹³ W | 6.3 × 10²⁰ J | M2 |
| Type I | 1 × 10¹⁶ W | 3.2 × 10²³ J | M2 |
| Type II | 4 × 10²⁶ W | 1.3 × 10³⁴ J | **M4** |
| Type III | 4 × 10³⁷ W | 1.3 × 10⁴⁵ J | **M5** |

**Theorem 3 (Command is not threat).** A Type III civilization commands a galaxy's output and
reaches Ruin **M5** — not M6. Its own galaxy's binding energy is ~10⁵⁹ J; at total output it
would need **7.9 × 10¹³ years** to disperse the thing it commands, five thousand times the
present age of the universe.

*The Anchor's founding distinction reappears at civilizational scale without being put there.*
X.2 §3 separated hegemonic decisiveness from destructive output for individuals — Kenshiro
anchors M3 without cracking continents. Definition 4 reproduces it for polities: a galactic
civilization **decides** a galaxy's future while remaining unable to **break** it. The two
readings of threat were never a quirk of persons; they are a property of the measurement.

**Scope limit, load-bearing.** Definition 4 returns the band a budget *reaches*, which is an
**upper bound on Ruin and never an Anchor**. A power grid is not a weapon; deliverable structured
work against a resistant target is a different quantity from gross output, and a civilization's
Anchor is fixed by what it can decide, exactly as an individual's is.

## §5 — PROPAGATION: THE TWO CLOCKS

**The problem.** Part Four asserts that history arrives old and that ⟦↑n⟧ records how far it has
climbed, but supplies no distance between shelves. A writer may therefore claim any world has not
heard of any event, at any remove, and no instrument contradicts them.

**Definition 5 (Shelf distance).** For shelves A and B, let w(A,B) be the inverse-frequency
weighted count of entities both attest — shared furniture, weighted so that a rare shared entity
binds strongly and a ubiquitous one (Earth, the Moon) barely counts. Distance along an edge is
**1 / w**, and **d(A,B)** is the shortest path.

This is not an invented quantity. The Registry Communion propagates along the Roads (VIII.4); the
Roads run through the Betweens; W.1 holds the Betweens are one sea at differing depths. Shelves
that share entities share couriers, and d is the length of the sea between them.

**Definition 6 (The two clocks).** They are independent, and conflating them was the error of
this fascicle's first draft (Erratum 5, §7):

- **Ascension** — how long an event takes to climb **its own** Chain of Record to rung r:
  **T_asc(r) = r^1.35 − 1** years. *Distance does not enter.* A scribe logging a deed in his own
  town waits on no courier; the accession law is local-first by construction.
- **Arrival** — how long before a shelf at distance d hears of it at all:
  **T_arr(d) = 1000 · d** years. Anchored so that the far end of the measured range — Left 4 Dead
  to Dragon Ball Z, d = 1.13 — is roughly a millennium, and a same-universe pair (Alien to
  Predator, d = 0.006) is about six years.

**Definition 7 (Observed mark).** A shelf at distance d, at time t after the event, sees the mark
the event carried at time (t − T_arr(d)); before arrival it sees ⟦↑0⟧, honestly.

**Measured distances**, from the corpus of 211 catalogued sources:

| d | Pair | Arrival |
|---|---|---|
| 0.006 | Alien ↔ Predator | 6 yr — one universe |
| 0.006 | Call of Duty Zombies ↔ Black Ops | 6 yr — one universe |
| 0.037 | Pantheon: Greek ↔ Roman | 37 yr — one pantheon, two registers |
| 0.175 | Pantheon: Greek ↔ Marvel | 175 yr — the Storm Office |
| 0.384 | Black Ops ↔ Pixar | 384 yr, *via Marvel and Roger Rabbit* |
| 0.839 | Minecraft ↔ Warhammer 40,000 | 839 yr, *via Terraria and The Division* |
| **1.126** | **Left 4 Dead ↔ Dragon Ball Z** | **1,126 yr — the furthest measured** |

**Corollary (the amendment rule the Owner asked for).** A source entering the library late does
not require a new arm of the omniverse. It requires a **distance and a date**: compute d to every
existing shelf, and its non-appearance in their records is thereby *predicted* rather than
excused. Where the metric says a shelf **should** have heard and its records are silent, that
silence is a Contradictions entry — a finding, with a number attached, instead of a hand-wave.

## §6 — THE HONESTY THEOREMS, EXTENDED

X.2 §9 and X.6 §8 remain in force. Two are added.

**H8 (The census is a convention, not an observation).** Four terms of Theorem 1 — f_life,
f_complex, f_civilization, f_survives — are declared, not measured, and the census inherits their
status. A population figure derived from them may be *used* (for generation, for scale, for
scoping) and may never be *cited as evidence* about any particular world. The chain is transparent
so that a reader can see exactly where measurement stops and convention begins — which is the only
honest way to publish a number nobody can check.

**H9 (Silence must be predicted, not asserted).** A claim that some shelf has not heard of an
event is admissible only with its d and its date. Unquantified silence is not humility; it is an
unfalsifiable claim wearing humility's clothes, and it is exactly as inadmissible as an
unfalsifiable power claim. *The library may say "they had not yet heard." It may not say it
without saying how far away they were.*

## §7 — ERRATA FILED

**Erratum 4 — the impossible Kardashev mix.** This fascicle's first draft declared the Type III
share at 10⁻³, yielding 1.2 × 10¹² galactic civilizations against 2.0 × 10¹¹ galaxies — six per
galaxy. Repaired by Definition 2's constraints and a revised mix (§3); the constraint check is now
mandatory and refuses publication rather than annotating it.

**Erratum 5 — the conflated clocks.** The first propagation model summed lateral distance and
vertical ascension into a single cost, which made even rung 1 unreachable for any distant shelf:
every pair read ⟦↑0⟧ forever and the model said nothing. The error was of kind, not degree — it
treated a distant observer as though it slowed the event's own ratification. Repaired by
Definition 6's separation into two independent clocks.

**Erratum 6 — the uniform-sphere trap.** U = 3GM²/5R assumes uniform density. It is adequate for
Earth (2.24 × 10³², matching the cited value and anchoring the M3 ceiling) and **wrong by a factor
of three for the Sun**, which is centrally condensed: the formula gives 2.28 × 10⁴¹ where the
integrated density profile gives ~6.9 × 10⁴¹. The M5 floor uses the literature value. Any future
worksheet computing a stellar binding energy from the uniform formula is understating it, and this
erratum exists so the next Hand does not rediscover it the hard way.

## §8 — OPEN PROBLEMS

1. **Calibrating f_life against the rolls.** The four declared biological terms are currently
   chosen for plausibility, not fitted. The Register's 13,847 charted worlds, weighted by Survey
   coverage per rung, are in principle a likelihood — turning the conventions into estimates with
   intervals.
2. **Distance beyond co-attestation.** d is currently a proxy from shared entities. Where a shelf
   attests no shared furniture at all it is unreachable and its distance is infinite, which is
   almost certainly too strong: the Roads reach further than the Register records.
3. **Directed propagation.** News does not travel symmetrically — a Signatory world with an
   embassy hears sooner than it is heard of. d should become directed, weighted by each shelf's
   rung on the Awareness Ladder (I.9 §2).
4. **The population-to-persons bridge.** Theorem 1 gives civilization counts; the Registry of
   Persons needs *people*, with a Magnitude distribution. A population's band distribution is
   presumably a power law anchored at M0, but its exponent is unfitted and unpublished.

## COLOPHON — WHAT HERE IS REAL

*Out of character: the astrophysics is real and cited — Cassan et al. 2012 for planet occurrence,
Bryson et al. 2021 for η⊕, Lauer et al. 2021 and Conselice et al. 2016 for the galaxy count,
Sagan's continuous formulation of Kardashev (1973), and the standard gravitational binding energy
of a uniform sphere with its documented failure for centrally-condensed bodies. The census chain
is the Drake equation restructured to run on populations rather than contact rates, with its
biological terms marked as the conventions they are rather than smuggled in as estimates. The
propagation model is an inverse-frequency-weighted shortest path — the standard construction for
document-similarity graphs — reinterpreted as courier distance. The synthesis, and the two
honesty theorems, are original to this document. Its most useful export, like its predecessors',
is not a number: it is that the library can now be told when one of its own numbers is
impossible, and refuse it.*

*— Vol. X.7, first fascicle. The universe has been counted; the news is in transit; the desk lamp
burns on.*
