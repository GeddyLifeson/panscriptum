This is the FEATS & ATTESTED DEEDS chapter. It is not like the other chapters and the rules
below override the general entry format where they differ.

Every line of feat text below is a QUOTATION mined from this source's own pages, together with
the page it came from and the Assay axis it speaks to. This chapter's whole value is that it is
evidence. Treat it as testimony you are arranging, never as material to embellish.

WHAT TO WRITE

For each entity below, in the order given, write one section:

  * A heading with the entity's name and its shelfmark exactly as supplied.
  * One short paragraph stating what the record shows in aggregate -- how many deeds are
    attested, which axes they cluster on, and which pages they were drawn from. This is a
    statement about the EVIDENCE, not about the character's importance.
  * Then the deeds themselves, grouped under their axis, each one presented as the quotation it
    is, with its page cited. Preserve the wording. You may trim a leading conjunction or a
    dangling clause for readability; you may not paraphrase, merge two deeds into one, or
    smooth a specific number into a vague one.

HARD CONSTRAINTS -- these are the point of the chapter

  * DO NOT INVENT A DEED. If you know something about this entity from elsewhere, it does not
    belong here. Only what is listed below is attested.
  * DO NOT SCORE. Do not produce an Assay decimal, a +/- interval, or a ranking of entities
    against one another. The Magnitude band supplied for an entity may be REPORTED as given;
    a band shown as "unassayed" must be reported as unassayed and never guessed at. Charter
    Part Three's rule stands: no worksheet, no number.
  * DO NOT INFER AN AXIS. Each deed's axis is supplied. Do not re-file one because it reads
    better elsewhere, and do not invent an axis name that is not in the data.
  * DO NOT RANK OR COMPARE. That a deed is more impressive than another is exactly the
    judgement the Assay exists to make under its own method, and this chapter is its input,
    not its output.
  * If an entity's deeds are thin or repetitive, say so plainly. A sparse record is a true
    fact about the evidence and is more useful than a padded page.
  * Threads stay pending, as everywhere else in the library.

Open the chapter with one short framing paragraph: what this chapter is, that it is quoted
evidence drawn from the source's own pages, and what the reader should and should not conclude
from it. Use the SOURCE CONTEXT for that framing only, and do not restate it verbatim.
